# PART 1 — SOURCE SURVEY

## 1. SHIPS AND VEHICLES AS CHARACTERS

### 1.1 Star Trek Adventures (the key case)
Ships get **six Systems** (Comms, Computers, Engines, Sensors, Structure, Weapons) in the slot where characters have six Attributes, and **six Departments** (Command, Conn, Security, Engineering, Science, Medicine) in the slot where characters (in 1e) had Disciplines. Plus Scale (2–6 for Starfleet), Shields (ship stress), Power (ship Momentum), Resistance (ship armour), and Talents.
- https://philgamer.wordpress.com/2017/06/29/lets-study-star-trek-adventures-part-9a-a-home-in-the-stars/
- https://mephitjamesblog.wordpress.com/2017/07/20/star-trek-adventures-starships-and-enemies/
- https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TabletopGame/StarTrekAdventures

**Is it considered a success?** Qualified yes, but the reason is not what it looks like. The reviews are positive but the praise is about *terminology consistency*, not simulation fidelity. 2e's notable change was renaming character "Disciplines" to "Departments" to match ships: *"If you've ever asked for a ship's disciplines or a character's departments in 1e and then floundered, you know this is a relief."* — https://continuingmissionsta.com/2024/07/27/star-trek-adventures-2e-review-part-1/

The load-bearing finding for you is buried in the resolution rules: **the ship does not act. It assists.** *"when characters are performing functions on a ship… they typically roll their own stats, with an assisted roll made for the ship that can add successes"* (https://www.strangeassembly.com/2017/review-star-trek-adventures), and Mephit James: ships act as a *"helping character"* — the player rolls, the ship rolls, successes combine. The 2e reviewer is blunter: the six systems and departments *"assist characters onboard but otherwise act like locations."*

So the most-committed ship-as-character design in the hobby gives the ship **character-shaped attributes but not character-shaped agency**. That distinction is the single most important import from this survey.

### 1.2 Starfinder
Ship stat block: tier, speed, maneuverability, AC, TL, HP, DT (damage threshold), CT (critical threshold), shields by quadrant, attacks by arc, power core, computer, crew min/max, expansion bays. Combat roles: captain, pilot, gunner, engineer, science officer, each acting in a named phase.
- https://aonsrd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=192 · https://aonsrd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=193

The ship never rolls. *"individual character competence—not ship statistics alone—determines combat success."* The ship contributes fixed modifiers and thresholds; the person contributes the roll.

### 1.3 Traveller
Ship: hull/structure (one point per fifty tons), armour, thrust, sensors, screens, computer. Crew positions: pilot, captain, drive engineers (M and J separately), turret/bay gunners, damage control, marines, passengers. *"The system uses crew member skills, not inherent ship attributes."* Notably the computer can occupy crew positions via software (Fire Control, Expert Pilot) — i.e. **the role is a slot, and its occupant may be a person or a thing**.
- https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/space-combat/

Scale: *"The rules maintain distinct scales: personal combat exists separately… Weapon damage uses abstract 'hits' rather than translating to personal-scale damage."* Traveller refuses cross-scale arithmetic outright.

### 1.4 Coriolis
Ship attributes are a short flat list: Maneuver, Modules, EP (energy points), HP, Signature, Armor, Speed — plus modules (bridge, reactor, graviton projector required) and named features that grant bonuses (*"+1 to all sensor rolls"*).
- https://philgamer.wordpress.com/2017/02/06/lets-study-coriolis-part-4-spaceships-star-travel/

Worth noting what is *absent*: no ship-side skills, no ship-side agency. The ship is a bundle of modifiers and one integrity track.

### 1.5 Blades / Scum & Villainy ship playbook
S&V's ship is a full playbook: its own sheet, upgrades, and crew slots, structurally parallel to the Blades crew sheet.
- https://evilhat.com/product/scum-and-villainy/ · https://lairofsecrets.com/blog/scum-villainy-playbooks/

The design point: the ship occupies the *crew-sheet* slot, not the *character-sheet* slot. It is a shared, persistent, upgradeable thing the group collectively owns and advances — the same structural position as the Blades crew and the Ars Magica covenant.

### 1.6 Lancer
*"Humans and the smallest mechs are SIZE 1/2. Most mechs are SIZE 1, but some are as large as SIZE 3."* Humans and mechs are on the **same** size ladder, one step apart. Lancer's actual solution to person-vs-mech is to not have person-scale combat: *"Lancer makes a distinction between freeform narrative play and mech combat, in which tracking individual turns and actions is important."*
- https://studylib.net/doc/27473910/lancer---core-book · https://lancer-faq.netlify.app/ · https://forststories.com/lancer-combat/

The FAQ confirms drones are *"characters that are allied to their users"* and that terrain/objects are handled ad hoc, not as statted entities.

### 1.7 Mothership
Ship sheet is a manifest, not a character sheet: crew roster, cargo, systems, condition. Ships are property with state, not actors.
- https://www.tuesdayknightgames.com/products/mothership-shipbreakers-toolkit · https://octopusink.itch.io/mothership-1e-ship-manifest

## 2. FACTIONS AND ORGANISATIONS

### 2.1 Blades in the Dark — the faction game
Faction fields: **Tier** (*"a measure of wealth, influence, and scale"*, 0–VI), **Hold** (binary, *"W indicates weak hold. S indicates strong hold"*), **Status** (−3..+3), **Turf** (max 6), **Claims**.
- https://bladesinthedark.com/faction-game · https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md

Two findings you should steal:
1. **Tier and Scale are separate axes.** Tier is quality/effectiveness (*"Quality… the effectiveness of tools, weapons, or other resources, usually summarized by Tier"*); Scale is *"the number of opponents, size of an area covered, scope of influence."* Almost every other system merges these into one "level" number. Blades doesn't, and it is correct not to.
2. **Hold is not a number.** A two-state stability flag with a demotion rule (*"if hold drops while already weak, the faction loses 1 Tier and stays weak"*) beats a hit-point track for an organisation. Factions don't die by attrition; they degrade in steps.

Status: *"Your crew's status with each faction indicates how well you are liked or hated… You track your status with each faction on the faction sheet."* Read carefully, this is a **directed value anchored on the observer's sheet**, not a symmetric edge — but the published sheet only ever materialises the PC crew's side, so Blades is per-participant *in shape* and one-sided *in practice*.

### 2.2 REIGN (One-Roll Engine) — the best precedent, with a caveat
Company stats: **Might, Treasure, Influence, Sovereignty, Territory**. The company has its own sheet, is built collaboratively at character creation, spends and gains XP, and *"can attack other territories with its military might, conduct espionage, etc."*
- https://rpgmusings.wordpress.com/2010/01/13/collective-vs-individual-goals-and-a-quick-look-at-reign/ · https://atomicovermind.com/reign/ · https://www.rpg.net/reviews/archive/13/13096.phtml

Key mechanical relation: individual PC actions *"provide big boosts (or penalties) to company rolls"* — the person is a modifier on the organisation's roll, exactly inverting STA (where the ship is a modifier on the person's roll). Both directions exist in the corpus; **what never happens is the person and the organisation rolling in the same contest as peers.**

The design intent is explicitly non-anthropomorphic. Reign 2e's framing: *"Companies are not persons and cannot relate to the world through metis; no, they are machines, formed from certain social knowledge… everything is simplified to what can be done and how the machine can replicate itself."*
- https://splitparty.substack.com/p/reign-2e-part-1

That is a designer saying out loud: **the organisation gets its own instruction set, not a copy of the person's.** Caveat: I could not reach a primary rules text for exact company action resolution (rpg.net paywalled, 1d6chan 403, the Let's-Read stops before the company chapter — https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/unseenlibrarian/reign-a-game-of-lords-and-leaders/). The five stat names are corroborated across two independent secondary sources.

### 2.3 Stars Without Number (Revised)
Faction fields: *"Hit Point (HP), Force rating, Cunning rating, Wealth rating, FacCreds, Experience Points (XP), Homeworld, Tags, and deployed Assets."* Assets each have *"HP, purchase cost, Attack value, Counterattack value, Type, Location, and Tech Level."* Faction turn actions: Attack, Buy Asset, Change Homeworld, Expand Influence, Refit Asset, Repair Asset/Faction, Sell Asset, Seize Planet, Use Asset Ability. Resolution is dice: *"Force vs. Force 1d3+1 damage."*
- https://takeonrules.com/2018/12/27/lets-read-stars-without-number-factions/

This is the maximal "faction as character" design and it is where the person-assumptions are thickest: HP, XP, attack, counterattack, damage dice, and a **single `Homeworld` field** standing in for spatial existence. Note also: assets carry a `Location`, factions carry a `Homeworld` — SWN needed *two different* place-shapes and got there by accident.

### 2.4 An Echo Resounding (Sine Nomine)
Domain stats: **Military, Wealth, Social** (1–5 unmodified, +1 modifier per tier above 5). Assets are mobile (units) or immobile (structures/customs), each with upkeep tied to a stat. **Locations** are the substrate of control (*"A Domain needs at least one location to exist"*), each contributing e.g. *"+4 Wealth and +4 Social points"* for a city, and each carrying **Obstacles** (problems halving benefit until resolved by a domain action or PC intervention). Resolution: *"domain checks (roll 1d20 + relevant stat modifier) or domain saving throws (roll under 12)."* PC domains get two actions/turn; NPC domains get one.
- https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/traveller/an-echo-resounding/ · https://www.pathfinderinfinite.com/en/product/99063/an-echo-resounding-a-sourcebook-for-lordship-and-war

The important structural move: **a faction's spatial existence is a set of Location entities, not a field on the faction.** And a Location carries its own attached problems. That is the shape you want, and it is precisely what SWN's `Homeworld` field gets wrong.

### 2.5 Ars Magica covenants
*"The covenant is the single most important character in most Ars Magica sagas, because it is the character that all the players contribute to."*
- https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Six:_Covenants · http://www.redcap.org/page/Covenant

But look at what it actually holds: Library (summae/tractatus), Lab Texts, Vis Sources & Stocks, Enchanted Items, Specialists (NPCs), plus **Boons and Hooks** — where Hooks are *story liabilities that pay for Boons*. The covenant has **no attributes, no HP, no skills, and never rolls.** It is a container of other entities plus a set of declared narrative obligations. It is called a character and is structurally an inventory-plus-agenda. This is the strongest single counterexample to "important thing ⇒ character sheet."

### 2.6 PF2 / Kingmaker kingdoms
Four ability scores — Culture, Economy, Loyalty, Stability — which explicitly *"function like the ability scores of a character, providing modifiers on die rolls and checks."* Also: kingdom level (capped at party level), kingdom XP (1000 to level), Control DC, Size (1–100+ hexes, driving Resource Die type), kingdom skills with proficiency ranks, kingdom feats, Unrest (escalating −1/−2/−3/−4 to all checks), four Ruin tracks each opposing one ability (Corruption↔Culture, Crime↔Economy, Decay↔Stability, Strife↔Loyalty), Resource Dice/Points, five Commodities with storage caps, government type, charter, heartland, eight leadership roles, settlements, Fame/Infamy, and a four-phase turn.
- https://2e.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=1742 · https://2e.aonprd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=1739 · https://rpgbot.net/p2/kingmaker-kingdom-rules-guide/

This is the most complete faction schema in print and also the most contaminated: **XP, level, proficiency ranks, and feats are all imported wholesale from the person schema**, and kingdom level is *capped at party level*, meaning a nation's capability is defined by the personal advancement of four adventurers. Two things here are genuinely good and worth keeping: the **Ruin↔Ability opposition** (each capacity has a named decay axis that opposes it) and **Unrest as a global penalty to all checks** (one scalar that degrades everything, rather than per-system damage).

### 2.7 Birthright (AD&D)
Provinces (level, loyalty), holdings of four types (law/temple/guild/source) held *by different regents in the same province*, Regency Points and Gold Bars as parallel currencies, bloodline as the licence to hold a domain, domain turns.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthright_(campaign_setting) · https://www.angelfire.com/az/pleasuremonkey/birth.html

The reusable idea: **a place can be simultaneously held by several factions along different axes.** Control is not exclusive and not a single owner field. This directly contradicts a universal `owner` attribute.

### 2.8 D&D 2024 Bastions — the instructive failure
Fields: level gates (5/9/13/17), basic facilities (*"no game effects"*), special facilities, hirelings (1–4 per facility) who *"execute Bastion orders"*, Bastion Defenders as a roster, seven orders (Craft/Empower/Harvest/Maintain/Recruit/Research/Trade), a Bastion Events table. **No HP. No attributes. Does not act independently.**
- http://dnd2024.wikidot.com/bastions · https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1828-exploring-bastions-from-the-2024-dungeon-masters

The Alexandrian's critique is the most useful design document in this whole survey:
> *"Nothing goes in. Nothing comes out."* · *"there's no way to level up from doing Bastion stuff; and there's no way to improve your bastion without leveling up"* · hirelings and defenders *"lack stat blocks, preventing player participation in bastion defense"* · *"Bastion events are all designed to be neat, tidy, and basically resolve themselves."*
- https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52045/roleplaying-games/dd-2024-the-bastion-bubble

The failure mode here is the **opposite** of anthropomorphism: giving a category its own private schema, its own private clock, its own private economy, and no shared Verbs — so nothing in the main game can touch it and it can't touch anything. This is your Rule 1 (components communicate only through the Ledger and Verbs) restated as a playtest report. **A category-specific subsystem with no shared instruction set is dead weight.**

## 3. PLACES AND DOMAINS

### 3.1 Dungeon World steadings
Four fields, all enumerated rather than numeric: Size (Village/Town/Keep/City), Population (Exodus/Shrinking/Steady/Growing/Booming), Defenses (None/Militia/Watch/Guard/Garrison/Battalion/Legion), Prosperity (Dirt/Poor/Moderate/Wealthy/Rich), plus free tags: Safe, Religion, Exotic, Resource, Need, Oath, Trade, Market, Enmity, History, Arcane, Divine, Guild, Personage, Dwarven, Elven, Craft, Lawless, Blight, Power.
- https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/the-world/ · https://exposit.github.io/dw-srd/dw_the_world.html

Everything is an ordinal enum or an open tag. **No numbers, no HP, no agency.** Note that several tags (Enmity, Oath, Guild, Personage) are really *references to other entities* wearing a tag's clothing — a tag system without magnitude and without referents gets abused into an edge list. Relevant to your "Tag… identified by ID not name, never implies another Tag" rule: DW shows what happens without that discipline.

### 3.2 Blades claims, turf, districts
*"Every claim is already controlled by a faction. To acquire one for yourself, you have to take it from someone else."* · *"Some claims count as turf. Others provide special benefits to the crew, such as bonus dice in certain circumstances, extra coin… or new opportunities for action."* · *"Each piece of turf that you claim represents abstracted support for the crew. You can hold a maximum of 6 turf."*
- https://bladesinthedark.com/faction-game · https://bitd.gplusarchive.online/2016/12/16/lets-talk-about-the-claims-system/

A claim is a **transferable capability anchored to a place** — the same object as an SWN Asset and an An Echo immobile asset. Three independent designers converged on it. Design tension noted in the archive discussion: seizing a claim carries a mandatory −2 status hit, which *"overrides crew playbooks stylistically"* — a hardcoded consequence forced one shape of fiction. Cautionary for baking consequence magnitudes into the Substrate rather than a Component.

### 3.3 PF2 settlements, Beyond the Wall villages
PF2 settlements sit *inside* the kingdom sheet (capital + settlement list, each with its own blocks). Beyond the Wall's village is a collaboratively-built reference point with position and hooks but no stat block — *"treated as a location with positional attributes rather than as a developed character."*
- https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/gimpinblack/beyond-the-wall-further-afield/ · https://retiredadventurer.blogspot.com/2015/07/review-beyond-wall-and-other-adventures.html

## 4. RELATIONSHIPS AS FIRST-CLASS THINGS

This is the section with the clearest verdict. **The corpus is overwhelmingly per-participant, and the systems that use a shared edge are the ones that had to add exceptions.**

### 4.1 Monsterhearts — Strings (strongest direct evidence)
*"Strings represent your character's control over another character"*, supporting *"one sided relationships (Strings only going one way)."* Explicitly contrasted with Masks: *"you either have Influence or don't, where you can have multiple Strings on someone."* Player A may hold 3 Strings on B while B holds 1 on A. Spend a String to *"add one to your next move against them… subtract 1 from their next move against you… offer them experience to do what you want… add an extra harm."*
- https://spikedmarkovchain.wordpress.com/2018/09/29/strings/

Note the structure: the *stance* is per-participant and directional, *and it is spendable* — i.e. the connection is a depletable resource held by one side. That's your Connection holding a Resource Noun.

### 4.2 Masks — Influence
Directional by construction: one character *has Influence over* another; it is given, taken and locked. Binary rather than graded (per the Monsterhearts comparison above). Influence is the permission to shift another character's Labels.
- https://spikedmarkovchain.wordpress.com/2018/09/29/strings/ · https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TabletopGame/MasksANewGeneration

*(I could not reach a primary Masks text — 1d6chan 403, fandom 402 — so treat the binary/graded detail as secondary-sourced.)*

### 4.3 Smallville / Cortex Prime — Relationships as rated dice
Each Lead holds a rated relationship die (d4–d12) toward each other character, and it enters the dice pool: *"your relationship die might add if it's your friend in trouble."* Cortex Prime formalises this as a trait set alongside Values, Distinctions, Affiliations: *"Relationships… represent if the character is acting with or concerning a person for which they have a rated relationship."*
- https://blackcampbell.com/2010/09/21/review-smallville-rpg/ · https://gnomestew.com/cortex-prime-review/ · https://cortex.fandom.com/wiki/Traits

Crucially: **the relationship die is rolled by its holder, in that holder's pool.** It is a capacity of the holder, directed at a target. There is no shared object being rolled. The relationship *map* is built collaboratively (the whole table draws it) but the *ratings* are per-character — collaboration on the graph's shape, independence in the values.

### 4.4 Burning Wheel — Reputations, Affiliations, Relationships
Three separate one-sided constructs bought with character points and sitting on the character's own sheet; they add dice to Circles tests (*"Their reputations and affiliations may also add to the dice pool"*).
- https://takeonrules.com/2021/08/26/the-circles-subsystem-of-burning-wheel-gold/ · https://forums.burningwheel.com/uploads/default/original/2X/5/59cd3bc99daa0d2d9795e46a3cb77a5ccdf77ab8.pdf

*(Sheet layout confirms per-character sections; I could not verify the 1D/2D/3D ratings from a primary source.)* The relevant structural point: BW needed **three different constructs** because "my standing with a group", "my membership in a group" and "my tie to a person" are not the same shape. A single generic edge cannot express that; three per-participant Connection kinds can.

### 4.5 Pendragon — Passions
Passions are numerically rated and rolled, and each names its target: Loyalty (lord), Love (family), Hate (Saxons). The rating belongs to the knight, about the target.
- https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/divinecoffeebinge/pendragon/ · http://www.thekeep.org/cgi/amber?action=browse&id=Pendragon/TraitsAndPassions

*(Primary page blocked by robots; this is the standard reading and consistent across secondary sources.)* Note the passion's target may be a **group** (Saxons) or a **person** — so a Connection's other end is not always a person, which argues against typing Connection's counterparty as Person.

### 4.6 Blades — Status
Per (crew, faction) ordered pair, −3..+3, tracked on the observer's sheet. See §2.1.

### 4.7 Ironsworn — Bonds
Bonds are the character's own progress track, marked when *they* form a connection. One-sided by construction.
- https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html

### 4.8 The counter-precedent, and why it isn't one
Fate models a relationship as an aspect — a single shared string invocable by either party. That is the "edge" pattern. But note what Fate must then do: it treats the aspect as *an object in its own right* that can be created, attacked, and removed, with a permission cost. Fate's fix for edges is to promote them to entities. Ars Magica goes further: **a covenant is an N-party relationship modelled as an entity with its own sheet** — a shared, targetable thing that outlives the participants.

## 5. ABSTRACT ENTITIES

### 5.1 Blades — Clocks
*"A progress clock is a circle divided into segments. Draw a progress clock when you need to track ongoing effort against an obstacle or the approach of impending trouble."* Sizes: 4 (complex), 6 (more complicated), 8 (daunting). And the design note that matters: *"A clock is like a speedometer in a car. It shows the speed of the vehicle—it doesn't determine the speed."*
- https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md

That quote is a warning for you: a clock is a **projection of state, not a driver of it.** In your terms: a clock should be a Lens over a Threshold on a Resource, not an Entity with its own authoritative counter. If it's an Entity, folding must derive its fill from Records, never store it.

### 5.2 Apocalypse World — Fronts and Threats
A front: *"Choose a fundamental scarcity. Create 3 or 4 threats. Write its agenda / dark future. Write 2–4 stakes questions. List the front's cast. Create the front's overall countdowns."* Threat types: Warlords, Grotesques, Landscapes, Afflictions, Brutes — each with a named list of sub-kinds (Slaver, Hive queen; Prison, Breeding pit; Disease, Condition, Delusion, Barrier; Hunting pack, Cult, Mob, Family). Each threat carries: name, type, **impulse**, cast, custom moves, countdown clock. Scarcities: Hunger, Thirst, Ignorance, Fear, Decay, Despair, Envy, Ambition.
- http://bignose.whitetree.org/tmp/apocalypse-world/rules-playbooks.pdf · https://lumpley.games/2019/12/30/powered-by-the-apocalypse-part-1/

The taxonomy is the point: **a person (Warlord), a group (Brute), a place (Landscape), and a condition (Affliction) are the same kind of object** — all four are "a threat", all four carry an impulse, a countdown and custom moves. Baker did the L1 category split you're doing and landed on *five categories with one identical schema*. Note that "Landscape" and "Affliction" being threats means AW already treats a place and an abstract condition as entities with drives.

### 5.3 Dungeon World — Fronts and Dangers
Danger types: Ambitious Organizations, Planar Forces, Arcane Enemies, Hordes, Cursed Places. Each danger: Type, **Impulse** (*"a cult has the impulse 'to infest from within'"*), Grim Portents (*"dark designs for what could happen if a danger goes unchecked"*), Impending Doom (Tyranny, Pestilence, Destruction, Usurpation, Impoverishment, Rampant Chaos), Cast, optional custom moves. Fronts: 2–3 dangers, 1–3 stakes questions, cast.
- https://www.dungeonworldsrd.com/gamemastering/fronts/

The **Impulse** is the most transferable idea in this section. It is a declared standing tendency that resolves what a non-agentive entity "does" without giving it a decision. It maps exactly onto your **Listener** with a `Decider: Auto`. A cursed place does not choose; it has an impulse, which is a Listener plus a default.

### 5.4 Ironsworn — Vows as tracked objects
Vows, journeys, fights and bonds are all the same object: a ten-box progress track with a **rank** (troublesome 3 boxes/dangerous 2/formidable 1/extreme 2 ticks/epic 1 tick per success). Resolution is a roll of the progress score against challenge dice.
- https://tedtschopp.github.io/Ironsworn-SRD/Ironsworn%20SRD.html · https://ironsworn-rpg.fandom.com/wiki/Marking_Progress

Rank is not size — it is **the exchange rate between effort and progress**. A vow, a journey, a fight and a relationship all share one schema differing only by rank. That is one Category with a Facet, not four Categories.

### 5.5 Fate — situation aspects and zones
A situation aspect is a fact about the scene with identity, invocable, and removable by action. A zone is an abstract place with adjacency but no metrics. Both are entities with identity and no attributes.
- https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/extras

## 6. THE UNIVERSAL-ATTRIBUTE QUESTION

### 6.1 Fate — the Bronze Rule / Fate Fractal
> *"In Fate, you can treat anything in the game world like it's a character. Anything can have aspects, skills, stunts, stress tracks, and consequences if you need it."*
- https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/extras

Note the **"if you need it."** The same page immediately constrains: *"Extras require a permission or cost to own"*, and frames extras as the game's *"special effects budget"* — *"deliberately chosen elements rather than default features."*

So the most famous "everything is a character" system in the hobby says: (a) the *vocabulary* is universal, (b) the *fields* are optional, (c) having any of them at all costs something. That is precisely the open-world / "absent is not zero" position, arrived at from the fiction side. Fate's universal set is not four fields — it is **one field: identity, plus an à-la-carte menu.**

### 6.2 Cortex Prime — trait sets
Trait sets: Attributes, Skills, Distinctions, Values, Relationships, Assets/Signature Assets, Powers, Specialties, Resources, Affiliations. *"Not every game will use all those options."* You assemble a game by choosing which trait sets exist; anything in the game is built from the chosen sets.
- https://gnomestew.com/cortex-prime-review/ · https://cortex.fandom.com/wiki/Traits

Cortex's universal is **"a thing is a set of rated traits, each of which contributes one die"** — a uniform contribution shape, not a uniform field list. That is your uniform Verb-invocation shape, one abstraction level down.

### 6.3 GURPS
GURPS derives object HP from weight and DR from material, and applies Size Modifier as a to-hit modifier. Objects get HP, DR and SM — and *nothing else*: no attributes, no skills, no will, no perception.
- https://gurps.fandom.com/wiki/HP_and_DR_of_Objects_and_Cover · https://gurps.fandom.com/wiki/Size_Modifier

*(Both fandom pages returned 402 to my fetcher; this is the well-known 4e Basic Set treatment, and https://forums.sjgames.com/showthread.php?t=93857 discusses it.)*

The "most generic system ever built" gives objects a **three-field schema**, entirely disjoint from the character schema except that both feed the same damage resolution. GURPS's universal is not a field set — it is a **shared resolution pipeline**. That is exactly your position: the Substrate is the instruction set, not the schema.

## 7. SCALE

| System | Mechanism | Cross-scale interaction |
|---|---|---|
| Fate System Toolkit | Per step of difference, *"+1 to the attack roll or +1 to the defense roll"* and *"Deal +2 shifts of harm… or reduce incoming harm by 2"*, applied to the larger. Only when *"unequal."* Recommends *"three or four"* steps total. | Linear-in-steps bonus. Never forbidden, never multiplied. |
| Blades | Scale is an **effect factor**: *"the number of opponents, size of an area covered, scope of influence… Larger scale can be an advantage or disadvantage depending on the situation."* Separate from Tier/Quality. | Adjusts position/effect. No arithmetic. |
| Traveller | Two disjoint scales. Ship damage in abstract "hits." | Forbidden by construction. |
| Starfinder | Ship has DT (damage threshold) — damage below DT does nothing. | A **floor**: small contributions are literally discarded. |
| Godbound | HD tiers + Fray Die: *"a free auto-hit you do every turn"* against *"lesser foe[s]"*; Mobs abstract away crowds. | Scale as a **threshold of relevance**: below-tier entities are auto-resolved, not computed. |
| Lancer | Humans SIZE 1/2, mechs SIZE 1–3 (same ladder); person-scale has no combat system. | Avoided: two play modes, no shared resolution. |
| STA | Ship Scale drives Resistance, Crew Support, and phaser damage. | A **multiplier on derived values**, not a conversion of magnitudes. |
- https://fate-srd.com/fate-system-toolkit/scale · https://writeups.letsyouandhimfight.com/doresh/godbound/ · https://aonsrd.com/Rules.aspx?ID=192

**The finding: not one surveyed system performs an arithmetic conversion of magnitude across scales.** Every one of them does one of four things: forbid the interaction (Traveller, Lancer), convert scale into a flat bonus (Fate, Blades), impose a threshold below which small things do nothing (Starfinder DT, Godbound HD tiers), or scale derived defensive values only (STA).

Your `× 10^(source − target)` truncating conversion at R-750 is the most aggressive rule in the corpus, and its consequence is Starfinder's DT arrived at by arithmetic: a Scale-1 person contributing to a Scale-4 target yields `⌊x × 10⁻³⌋ = 0` for anything under 1000. That's defensible and matches the fiction — a rifle does nothing to a dreadnought — but it means **"the engineer sabotages the dreadnought" cannot be a vector.** It must be a Threshold met on a Scale-1 part (the console), producing a State on the whole. Your Rule 18's *"Scale belongs to the part as well as the whole — a Scale-4 ship has Scale-1 doors"* is exactly the escape hatch Traveller and Starfinder use for boarding actions, and it is load-bearing. Make sure content authors know that targeting the part is the *only* route, or they'll try to buff a rifle to Scale 4.

# PART 2 — CANDIDATE L1 CATEGORY LIST

Ranked by cross-system recurrence. Each line: name · what recurs · where.

**Tier A — present in essentially every surveyed system**

1. **Being** — person/creature/crew member. Everywhere.
2. **Body** — organisation, faction, crew, company, cohort, gang, covenant, domain, kingdom. Blades, REIGN, SWN, An Echo, Ars Magica, PF2, Birthright, AW ("Brutes"), DW ("Ambitious Organizations").
3. **Site** — a bounded, addressable place: room, door, covenant, settlement, steading, claim, bastion, hex. DW steadings, Blades claims, PF2 settlements, An Echo locations, Ars Magica, D&D bastions, AW ("Landscapes"), Fate zones.
4. **Thing** — a discrete object: lock, weapon, artefact, module, asset-as-item. GURPS objects, Ironsworn assets, Ars Magica enchanted items, Coriolis modules.
5. **Vessel** — ship, vehicle, mech, mobile crewed platform. STA, Traveller, Starfinder, Coriolis, S&V, Lancer, Mothership. *Justification for splitting from Thing: only Vessels have Posts and carry Beings; that combination recurs and nothing else has it.*

**Tier B — recurs in most, under different names**

6. **Territory** — a spatial extent rather than an address: turf, province, hex-set, district, sphere of influence. Blades turf, Birthright provinces, PF2 Size, An Echo. *Justification for splitting from Site: Sites are contained; Territories contain, overlap, and are held by several Bodies at once along different axes (Birthright). One field cannot serve both.*
7. **Undertaking** — vow, project, score, plan, front, danger, clock, countdown. Ironsworn, Blades, AW, DW. Uniform schema: a target state, a rank/size, a progress measure, an impulse, and a consequence on completion.
8. **Phenomenon** — storm, plague, blight, curse, affliction, condition-in-the-world. AW Afflictions and Planar Forces, DW Cursed Places, PF2 kingdom events, An Echo Obstacles. *Emits vectors; never decides.*
9. **Notion** — rumour, secret, doctrine, lore, tradition, reputation-as-object. Ars Magica library and lab texts, DW tags (History/Oath/Enmity), Burning Wheel Reputations, Blades rumour-driven scores, and your own Almanac. *Its distinguishing property is that its state is per-observer, not global.*
10. **Relationship** — see Part 4. Ars Magica covenants show the N-party form; Monsterhearts/Masks/Cortex the 2-party form.

**Tier C — recurs but is arguably a Facet, not a Category. Flagging the choice explicitly.**

11. **Post** — a named seat: crew role (Traveller, Starfinder, STA departments), leadership role (PF2's eight), Ars Magica specialist. Recurs strongly and has real state: it exists while vacant, it has requirements, and Traveller proves **its occupant need not be a Being** (*"the ship's computer can cover several positions"*). This is your Socket concept at instance level. I'd ship it as a Category; modelling it as a Facet on Vessel/Body loses the vacancy.
12. **Holding** — asset, claim, boon, immobile-asset. SWN Assets, An Echo assets, Blades claims, Ars Magica boons. Three designers converged on "a transferable capability anchored to a Site and owned by a Body." I'd model this as a **Facet** (a Thing/Site that is claimable) rather than a Category, because in every system it is a *pairing* of an existing place with an existing owner, not a new kind of thing.
13. **Proposal** — your own. No external precedent as an entity; the nearest is a Blades clock. Keep it, but see the universal-attribute finding below: it is the entity that breaks your candidate universals.

**Recommended seed list (10):** Being · Body · Vessel · Site · Territory · Thing · Phenomenon · Notion · Undertaking · Relationship. Plus two Substrate-internal: **Post**, **Proposal**.

**What I would NOT ship as a Category:** Clock (it's a Lens — Blades' own "speedometer" quote), Asset/Holding (a Facet), Role-as-class (Tags), Event (that's a Record), Faction-vs-Crew (same Category, different Facets — Blades uses one faction sheet for both).

# PART 3 — CANDIDATE L2 UNIVERSAL ATTRIBUTE LIST

The four-way test, extended with two extra probes: **Proposal** and **Connection**, because your own dictionary says both are Entities, and they are where the candidates actually break.

| Candidate | Rumour | Lock | Faction | Storm | Proposal | Connection | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **UNIVERSAL** |
| **Category** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **UNIVERSAL** |
| **Existence interval** (came-into-being Tick; ceased-to-be Tick) | ✓ started/stopped circulating | ✓ installed/destroyed | ✓ founded/dissolved | ✓ formed/dissipated | ✓ raised/decided | ✓ formed/severed | **UNIVERSAL** — and missing from your list |
| **Facet map** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **UNIVERSAL by construction**, not an attribute |
| **Tag set** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ~ | ~ | **UNIVERSAL as an affordance**, empty by default |
| **Scale** | ✗ | ✓ | ~ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | **NOT universal** |
| **Place / containment ref** | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | **NOT universal — and wrong shape 3× of 4** |

### Argument per line

**Identity.** Trivially universal; the definition of Entity. The only decision is whether the ID is opaque or structured. Given Rule 5 (permanent, never reused) and per-Campaign rebuilds (Rule 9), it should be Campaign-scoped and opaque.

**Category.** Universal and necessary, not merely descriptive: it is what determines which Facets are legal and — the important part — **whether instances of this Category may be named as a Verb target at all.** Precedent for Category-as-schema-gate: AW's five threat types all share one schema but differ in which fields are meaningful; Cortex's trait sets are chosen per game object.

**Existence interval.** This is the one you're missing. Every source has entities that begin and end: Blades factions lose Tier and are destroyed; AW/DW countdowns complete; Ironsworn vows are fulfilled or forsworn; PF2 kingdoms fall to Unrest; rumours are debunked; storms dissipate. Your Ledger derives creation from the creating Record, so *coming into being* is free — but *ceasing to be* is not, and it must not be a Category-specific field or four Components will invent four incompatible ways to say "gone." Recommend: a **Substrate-defined State axis** (`existence`: pending/active/ended) rather than a raw field, so it inherits the State Noun's exclusivity guarantee and the "ended only by a Verb" rule. This also gives Proposals their pending/decided lifecycle for free.

**Tag set.** Passes all six probes. But per Rule 8b, "absent is not zero" — an entity with no Tags must be distinguishable from an entity whose Tags haven't been folded. Ship it as a universal *affordance* (every Entity may carry Tags; the Tag Facet is present-or-absent explicitly), not as a required field.

**Scale — fails.** A rumour has no scale: its magnitudes ("how widely believed") are not commensurable with a storm's, and there is no meaningful `× 10^n` between them. A faction is ambiguous in every system that tried it — Blades needed **two** numbers (Tier for quality, Scale for size) and explicitly kept them apart, which means "the faction's scale" is not one value. A Proposal and a Connection have no scale at all. But Rule 18 makes Scale mandatory wherever addition happens, so you can't just make it optional and hope.

> **Recommended resolution:** Scale is not a universal Entity attribute. It is a required Facet of a Substrate-shipped Component (call it *Situated*), and **the Category declares whether its instances are targetable by a Verb**. Targetable ⇒ the Situated Facet is mandatory ⇒ Scale is present and explicit. Non-targetable Categories (Proposal, Connection, Notion-as-pure-index) carry no Scale and cannot appear in a Verb's target slot. This is CI-enforceable in one check: *for every Verb invocation in every fixture, the target's Category is targetable and its Situated Facet is present.* It also protects Rule 18 better than a universal field with a default, because a defaulted `scale: 0` on a rumour is a silent lie that will fold into real arithmetic.

**Place / containment — fails, and fails informatively.** Three of the four probes reject the *scalar shape*, not just the field:
- A rumour is in many places at once; that is its defining property.
- A faction has turf (Blades: a set, max 6), locations (An Echo: *"A Domain needs at least one location to exist"*), provinces (Birthright), or — where a designer forced a scalar — a single `Homeworld` field (SWN) that is a known abstraction. SWN needed both `Homeworld` on the faction *and* `Location` on each asset, which is the schema admitting the scalar was insufficient.
- A storm has extent, not an address.
- Only the lock has a genuine containment parent.

> **Recommended resolution:** Place is a Facet with **two distinct forms** — an *anchor* (0-or-1 Site reference; the lock's door, the covenant's valley, SWN's homeworld) and an *extent* (a set of Site/Territory references; Blades turf, An Echo locations, a storm's footprint). Do not collapse them. Birthright additionally proves that extent must permit **overlapping holders on different axes** — a province simultaneously held by four regents in law/temple/guild/source. A single `owner` or `controller` on a Site will not survive contact with a domain game.

**Knownness — the trap.** It passes all six probes, which makes it look like the strongest universal on the list. Reject it anyway: knownness is **per-observer**, so putting it on the Entity forces the Substrate to store a set-per-entity that will be read wrongly by every Component that treats it as a scalar, and it will leak PII-shaped data (who knows what) into simulation state. It belongs in your **Almanac** and is projected by a **Lens**. Concrete precedent for the per-observer shape: Blades status is anchored on the *observer's* sheet; Burning Wheel Reputations sit on the *character*, not on the group. **The most universal-looking attribute in the survey is the one that must be a Lens.**

### Verdict on your candidate list

> Your list — identity, category, tags, scale, place — is **two too many and one too few.**
>
> - **Drop Scale** to a Facet gated on Category targetability.
> - **Drop Place** to a Facet, and split it into anchor and extent.
> - **Add an existence/lifecycle axis** as a Substrate-defined State.
> - Keep identity, category, tags.
>
> Final L2: **identity · category · existence-axis · tag affordance · facet map.** Everything else is a Facet. This is more or less where Fate landed independently (*"Anything can have aspects, skills, stunts, stress tracks… if you need it"* + *"Extras require a permission or cost to own"*) and where GURPS landed (objects get HP/DR/SM and share only the resolution pipeline with characters).

# PART 4 — CROSS-CUTTING FINDINGS

### F1. The universal thing is the resolution pipeline, not the field set
GURPS shares only damage resolution between objects and characters. Cortex shares only "a rated trait contributes a die." Fate shares only the four building blocks *as a vocabulary*, with everything optional and costed. STA shares attribute *shape* between ship and crew and is praised mainly for reducing terminology confusion. Not one surveyed system has a large mandatory field set common to all entities. **Your core claim is right, and the corpus says the way to deliver it is a uniform Verb shape plus a tiny universal record — which is what you have.**

### F2. The two opposite failure modes, both documented
- **Anthropomorphism:** PF2 kingdoms get XP, levels, proficiency ranks and feats, capped at party level. SWN factions get HP, Attack and Counterattack. STA ships get a Medicine department.
- **Bubbling:** D&D 2024 Bastions get their own private economy, clock and event table with no shared Verbs — *"Nothing goes in. Nothing comes out."* (https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52045/roleplaying-games/dd-2024-the-bastion-bubble)

Your architecture already picks the correct middle (shared Verbs, per-Category Facets). The risk in *your* design is bubbling, not anthropomorphism, because Components can't call each other. **Mitigation: the seed Categories must all be exercised by the same Verbs in the same golden fixtures.** If Notion and Body never appear in a shared fixture, you've built two bubbles.

### F3. Person-assumptions that leak most often — concrete
1. **Hit points / an integrity track.** Fails on rumour and storm. SWN gives factions HP; Blades pointedly does not, using Hold (a two-state flag with a Tier-demotion rule) instead. Organisations degrade in steps; they don't bleed.
2. **XP and levels.** PF2 kingdoms level via XP capped at party level. A nation's capability defined by four adventurers' personal advancement is the purest possible leak.
3. **Action economy.** "One action per turn, two if PC-controlled" (An Echo, PF2). Turn-taking is a combat-scene assumption applied to a political process.
4. **A single location field.** SWN's `Homeworld`. Fails on faction, storm, rumour.
5. **A single owner/controller.** Fails on faction, storm, rumour; Birthright disproves it even for places.
6. **One "level" number conflating size and quality.** Nearly universal; Blades is the exception and is right. You have this correct already (Scale ≠ Capacity ≠ Threshold) — hold the line, because content authors will ask for "just a Tier."
7. **Six attribute slots because the character sheet had six.** STA. The ship's "Departments" are aggregates of the *crew's* competence stored on the *ship* — a category error the fractal actively invites.
8. **Morale / loyalty / will on inanimate things.** DW steading Population tags are demographics; PF2 Loyalty is people. Both are stored on the place. If your Place Facet ends up with a `loyalty`, it is a Body's Facet in the wrong place.
9. **Name as simulation state.** Names are the field most likely to carry PII (Rule 10) and are pure presentation. Put them in the Lens.

### F4. "A Relationship is a Category of Entity holding one Connection per participant, never an edge" — **supported, strongly, with one refinement**

Evidence for per-participant stance:
- Monsterhearts Strings: explicitly directional and asymmetric in magnitude — *"one sided relationships (Strings only going one way)"*, and A can hold 3 on B while B holds 1 on A. https://spikedmarkovchain.wordpress.com/2018/09/29/strings/
- Masks Influence: directional by construction (*has Influence over*), and binary where Strings are graded — two designers, two different magnitudes, both one-sided.
- Cortex/Smallville: the relationship die is rated by its holder toward a target and rolled **in the holder's own pool** — a capacity of the holder, not a shared object. https://gnomestew.com/cortex-prime-review/ · https://blackcampbell.com/2010/09/21/review-smallville-rpg/
- Burning Wheel needed **three** separate one-sided constructs (Reputations, Affiliations, Relationships) because "my standing with a group", "my membership" and "my tie to a person" are different shapes. A generic symmetric edge cannot carry three shapes; three Connection kinds on one Relationship can. https://takeonrules.com/2021/08/26/the-circles-subsystem-of-burning-wheel-gold/
- Pendragon Passions: rated, rolled, held by the knight, aimed at a named target — and the target may be a group (*Hate (Saxons)*), so **do not type a Connection's counterparty as Person.**
- Blades Status: anchored per (observer, faction) pair on the observer's sheet.
- Ironsworn Bonds: the character's own track.

Evidence for Relationship-as-Entity rather than as a pair of stances:
- **Ars Magica covenants are an N-party relationship modelled as an entity with its own sheet**, and the game calls it *"the single most important character in most sagas."* It outlives its members, is targetable, holds shared property, and carries obligations (Hooks) binding all participants. An edge cannot represent five parties; a two-stance pair cannot either. https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Six:_Covenants
- Fate's fix for relationship-aspects is to promote them to objects that can be created, attacked and removed — i.e. the edge pattern only works once you've made the edge a thing. https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/extras

**The mechanical argument, in your own terms:** a Verb has exactly one target. "Sever the alliance", "expose the marriage", "seize the treaty" all need a target that is neither party. An edge is not addressable, so it cannot be a target, so it cannot be changed by a Verb, so consequence cannot propagate to it. Making the Relationship an Entity is not a modelling preference — it is forced by your uniform Verb shape.

**The refinement:** the corpus splits cleanly into two things that a naive "one stance per participant" model merges.
- **Shared, objective facts about the relation** — that it is a marriage/treaty/covenant, when it formed, what obligations bind all parties (Ars Magica Hooks, AW front stakes questions). These belong on the **Relationship Entity itself**.
- **Per-participant subjective stance** — how much leverage, warmth, obligation each side holds (Strings, Influence, Status, Passions, Relationship dice). These belong on the **Connection**.

If you only have Connections, you have nowhere to put "this is a marriage" without duplicating it and risking divergence on fold. Make the Relationship Entity carry the shared facts and each Connection carry the stance. That is one small change to the dictionary line and it makes the N-party case (Ars Magica) fall out for free.

**One caution:** Strings and Status are *spendable*. A Connection must be able to hold a Resource Noun (depletable, with thresholds), not just a scalar. Monsterhearts' whole design is that leverage is consumed.

### F5. Does a faction/ship/place need to ATTEMPT, or only to be targeted? — **Three different answers, and the split is the finding**

**Vessels: no. Unanimously.**
- Traveller: *"The system uses crew member skills, not inherent ship attributes."*
- Starfinder: *"individual character competence—not ship statistics alone—determines combat success."*
- STA — the maximal case: the ship *assists*, as a *"helping character."* The player rolls; the ship adds successes.
- Coriolis: ship provides modifiers and features; crew rolls.
- Lancer: mechs are piloted; there is no mech-without-pilot resolution.

Zero of six surveyed vessel systems let the vessel initiate. **A Vessel is a Capacity/Modifier bundle plus an integrity track plus a set of Posts. It never holds a Proposal.**

**Bodies: yes — but on a different clock, never in a scene.**
- SWN factions take faction turns between sessions (*"something the GM resolves in between sessions… no more than 30 minutes"*), with actions Attack/Seize Planet/Expand Influence and real damage dice.
- An Echo domains roll *"1d20 + relevant stat modifier"* and domain saving throws, two actions per turn for PC domains.
- PF2 kingdoms run a four-phase turn with kingdom checks against a Control DC.
- REIGN companies roll ORE, with PC actions as *"big boosts (or penalties) to company rolls."*
- Blades factions act via GM-ticked clocks — no roll at all.

So Bodies act, but always at a **coarser Moment cadence** (downtime, faction turn, month) and never as a peer of a person inside a scene. Note the two opposite couplings: REIGN puts the person's result *into* the company's roll; STA puts the ship's result *into* the person's roll. **What never happens is the person and the organisation rolling as peers in one contest.** That is your Scale rule proving itself from the fiction side.

**Sites and Phenomena: no — they emit, they don't decide.**
- D&D Bastions: *"A Bastion doesn't independently take actions."* Hirelings execute owner-issued orders.
- DW steadings: no agency at all.
- Ars Magica covenants: never roll.
- AW/DW dangers have an **Impulse** (*"to infest from within"*), not a decision — a declared standing tendency that fires without a chooser.

**Recommended mapping to your machinery:**

| | Source of a vector | Holds a Proposal | Decider | Moment cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Being | yes | yes | Person | scene |
| Body | yes | yes | Person or Auto | downtime/turn |
| Vessel | yes (via Posts) | **no** | — | scene, but as Modifier |
| Site / Territory | yes (Thresholds) | no | — | — |
| Phenomenon | yes | no | **Auto** (Impulse = Listener + default) | tick-driven |
| Notion | yes | no | Auto | tick-driven |
| Undertaking | no (it is a Threshold) | no | — | — |
| Relationship | yes (via Connections) | no | — | — |

The clean statement: **separate "may be a source of a vector" (broad — anything can emit) from "may hold a Proposal" (narrow — Beings and Bodies only).** Your `Decider: Auto | Person` already encodes exactly this, and DW's Impulse is the published proof that Auto is sufficient for everything that isn't a Being or a Body.

### F6. Steal these four specific mechanisms
1. **Blades' Hold** — a two-state stability flag with a step-demotion rule, instead of an HP track, for anything that degrades in steps rather than bleeding. Fits your State Noun exactly (exclusive within an axis, ended only by a Verb).
2. **PF2's Ruin↔Ability opposition** — each Capacity has a *named* decay axis opposing it (Corruption↔Culture, Crime↔Economy). This gives every Dimension a guaranteed negative use, which is exactly what your Rule 17b demands (*"Every Dimension must be used on both signs, or the axis dies"*). PF2 is your CI check made into content.
3. **DW/AW's Impulse** — the declared standing tendency. One field turns a non-agent into something that acts, with no decision and no Proposal.
4. **Ironsworn's Rank** — the exchange rate between effort and progress, letting a vow, a journey, a fight and a bond share one schema. This is the argument for collapsing several of your candidate Categories into Undertaking-with-a-rank.
