# The two parallel tracks — the worklist

*Brand and legal, and audience. Both startable today, both impossible to compress at the end.*

Neither of these waits for the game. The trademark clock is months long, and an audience is years long. Everything here is small and repeatable, and the reason it belongs in a document is that small repeatable things are what get dropped when the interesting work is elsewhere.

Grounding for every claim here is in `branding-research.md`.

---

# Track A · Brand and legal

## A0 · The two decisions that come first

**Neither is research. Both are yours, and everything else waits on them.**

**Is the ruleset ever a book?** If a printed or PDF core book exists as a saleable object, it inherits the entire art-cost structure *and* a real trademark problem — a title of a single creative work is not registrable. If it is only ever software and web documentation, both largely dissolve, because **software and games are explicitly exempt from that bar.** This is the highest-consequence unanswered question in the project and it can be answered today.

**Is the brand the system, the platform, or the company — and are those one thing or three?** Three is more flexible and three times the filing cost. One is cheaper and fuses everything to a single name. Everything in the naming work depends on it, and it needs one sitting, not research.

---

## A1 · The name

**Step 1 — Generate badly, then filter.** Thirty candidates before judging any of them. Evocative beats descriptive; invented beats generic; two syllables beat four.

**Step 2 — The pronunciation test.** Say it to someone over the phone. If they cannot spell it back, you are choosing a permanent tax in search, in voice, and in word of mouth — in exchange for a distinctive mark. That trade can be worth it. Make it on purpose.

**Step 3 — Clearance, one sitting per surviving candidate.**

- Search the USPTO database directly for the relevant classes.
- Search itch.io, DriveThruRPG, Steam and BoardGameGeek for a game already using it.
- Check the `.com`, and check the handle on Bluesky, Discord, itch and GitHub *as a set* — one available handle across four platforms beats a perfect name on one.
- Search the plain word. If page one is something unrelated and enormous, the SEO fight is unwinnable.

**Step 4 — Sit with the survivors before choosing.** Names that survive being said out loud repeatedly are different from names that win a first sitting.

**Step 5 — File intent-to-use** as soon as one is chosen. First action currently runs several months, and total pendency is longer. There is no reason to wait for a product.

**Get right:** file as **software and a service**, not as a book title. That is where the exemption lives, and it is the single most useful thing in the branding research.

---

## A2 · The position, in one sentence

Write the sentence that goes at the top of the site before there is a site.

**What does not work:** *not D&D*. Positioning against the category leader by negation makes the leader the frame, and the audience most receptive to it is the one least likely to switch.

**What works:** a constraint stated positively. Something the game *is* that nothing else is. The strongest candidates from what exists:

- Play across a week without anyone waiting on anyone.
- Every number, visible, assembled step by step, with nothing hidden behind a screen.
- One system where fire, a lie and a lockpick are the same machinery.

**Write three versions. Say each to someone who does not play RPGs.** The one they repeat back correctly is the one.

---

## A3 · The no-AI position

**State it early, plainly, and permanently**, while it is cheap to say and impossible to fake retroactively.

It is a market credential and not only an ethical one — the audience is currently very alert to it, and a named human who committed early is in a different category from one who announced it after being asked.

**Be precise about the line.** AI in code, research and analysis is a different claim from AI in shipped prose and art. Say which you mean. A vague version is worse than none, because it invites the exact challenge it was meant to pre-empt.

---

## A4 · Visual identity, without an art budget

The systems that solved this solved it with **design, not illustration** — typography and layout led. That is available today and costs nothing but taste.

**And one option nobody else has.** This game computes vectors. Those vectors can be **drawn** — procedurally, deterministically, from the same numbers the engine already produces. A visual language generated by the system itself is unique, free, infinitely extensible, unambiguously not AI, and *nobody in this category is doing it.*

**Concretely, startable now:** pick a typeface pair with a licence that permits use in a paid web app, set a palette, and build one procedural visual from real resolution data. That is a complete identity for a design-led product.

---

## A5 · Licence and openness

Decide what a Component means to a customer, and whether third parties may ever author types. Both are in `open-questions.md` — Q4.3 in Part 4, Q5.3 in Part 5 — and both change the licence choice.

**Do not make an irrevocable licence promise before the business model exists.** An irrevocable promise is the strongest possible trust signal and it cannot be walked back. That is the point of it, and the reason to be sure first.

# Track B · Audience

An audience takes years and cannot be bought at the end. **Starting now, at whatever cadence holds, beats a large push at beta.**

---

## B1 · The newsletter, from month one

The only asset you own across platform collapses. Everything else is rented.

**One post a month is enough.** Two paragraphs on a decision you made and why. The design log *is* the content — and it is unusually good content, because almost nobody shows this part of the work.

**Set it up before there is anything to say.** The friction of starting is the reason most of these never exist.

---

## B2 · Build in public

The interesting thing that exists right now is not a game. It is **a person carefully designing a system in the open**, with the reasoning visible.

Things worth publishing that already exist or nearly do:

- Why fire and ice cancel by arithmetic instead of by a rule.
- Why the Verb list is being closed **last**.
- The four problems an adversarial review found in the designer's own work, and the fixes.
- Why percentages sum instead of compounding, with the worked numbers.

**The last one is the model.** Showing a mistake you found and fixed builds more credibility than showing a feature.

---

## B3 · Channels, and the honest assessment

**Bluesky** — where the tabletop creator community went. Start now, post the design log.

**Discord** — **not yet.** A server opened too early is a public advertisement that nobody cares. Open it when there is something to gather around, around the closed playtest.

**itch.io** — free to list, and the right home for a free playable ashcan long before launch. That artefact is the cheapest possible proof that the system is real and fun.

**Actual play** — do not start a show. Category-wide viewership is a few hundred concurrent, thousands of shows already compete, and it is enormously expensive in time.

**And the one advantage nobody is using:** this game's asynchronous mode produces **readable text as its native output.** A Chronicle is a shareable artefact that no synchronous game generates. That is a content channel that costs nothing because the game makes it.

---

## B4 · The free playable thing, well before launch

A print-and-play or browser subset, free on itch, long before the platform exists.

It is the cheapest proof the system works, the thing that gets the first few hundred followers, and — usefully — **the same artefact as the Phase 2 paper kit.** Build it once.
