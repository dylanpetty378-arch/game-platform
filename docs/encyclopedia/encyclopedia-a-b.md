# Acoustic guitar amplifiers (Roland AC, Fishman Loudbox, AER)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: Reverb/eBay sold knowledge, Guitar Center used pricing

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Combo amps with two channels (mic + instrument), chorus/reverb, often a tan/brown or black tolex with a flat baffle. **Roland AC-33/AC-60/AC-90/AC-100** (AC-100 is the 1990s 100 W brown-grille one), **Fishman Loudbox Mini/Artist/Performer** (brown tolex), **AER Compact 60** (small black cube, the pro one), **Marshall AS50/AS100**, **Behringer/Crate** (junk tier). # Petroliana – motor oil & grease cans, gas-station signs
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay 2026-10 listings (Archer Lubricants quart SAE 30, Archer Indian-graphic oil can w/ paper label Omaha NE, Archer oil sample salesman kit – asks $40–200; Pennzoil 10 lb grease can), sweep listings 2026-10 (Archer 25 lb grease can w/ Indian $145 Fargo; 5 ft plastic Shell sign $60 Pacifica; Coke signs $100; metal reproduction advertising $150; Mobil paperweight $10; Joe Camel clock $10; 1926 Nokorode soldering paste tin $10); general petroliana auction knowledge (Morphy/Richmond Auctions).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tin cans (1 qt, 1 gal, 5 qt, 5/10/25 lb grease pails) with lithographed brand graphics; porcelain-enamel or tin signs; glass-globe gas pump tops. Tells:
- **Graphics with an image** (Indian head – Archer, Mohawk, Red Indian; Sinclair dinosaur; Polly Gas parrot; Flying A; Frontier buffalo; Musgo; Harbor Petroleum) are worth 3–10x plain-text cans (Pennzoil, Quaker State, Havoline, Valvoline, Texaco – $15–60).
- **Archer Lubricants (Omaha, NE)**: Indian archer graphic; quart cans $40–120, 5-lb/10-lb/25-lb grease pails $100–300 depending on graphic version and condition; paper-label versions and salesman sample kits $100–250.
- **Condition grades**: rust, dents, fading knock 50–80% off; "full" or "NOS" adds a little; 25 lb pails are shipping-expensive.
- **Signs**: porcelain originals (Shell shield, Texaco, Mobil Pegasus, Sinclair) $500–5,000+ – thick, heavy, chipped edges show black steel under glass-like enamel; **plastic/lexan 1970s–2000s gas station signs** (like a 5 ft Shell plastic panel) $50–200 local only; **"metal reproduction advertising"** (thin, printed, Chinese) $5–20 regardless of the $150 ask; tin tacker/embossed originals $100–800.
- **Globes**: 1-piece glass $1,000+, gill-body/plastic-body 1950s $300–800, reproductions $50–100.

# Advertising signs (porcelain, tin, plastic, neon, street signs)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: craigslist chico listing body (5×5 ft Shell sign confirmed plastic); Joe Camel floor ashtray eBay listings ($150–300 asks, 2025); porcelain street-sign eBay sold knowledge ($60–150); Richmond Auctions / Morphy petroliana results for porcelain Shell signs ($800–5,000).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Porcelain enamel: glassy surface, chips show black steel underneath with a "shelf" edge, grommeted mounting holes, usually pre-1960. Tin litho: thinner, scratches show silver, rusts at edges. Plastic/acrylic: modern gas-station canopy inserts (1970s–2000s Shell, Texaco, Chevron) – thick, 3" deep, moulded, worth $50–150 only. Neon: glass tubing on a metal can; beer neons (Caffrey's, Budweiser) 1990s–2000s $100–250. Porcelain street signs (city blue/white or black/white, 20–24") $60–150. Joe Camel folding litho floor ashtray (1990s) $150–300. Sellers write "gas sign", "porcelain sign", "neon", "metal sign".

# American Belleek porcelain (Ott & Brewer, Willets, CAC/Lenox, Ceramic Art Co.)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: kovels.com Ott & Brewer article (marks/history), 1stdibs American Belleek listings, PBS Antiques Roadshow Ott & Brewer appraisals, sweep photo of CAC-marked loving cup (dealer-tagged, Atlanta), general auction knowledge (LiveAuctioneers/Worthpoint ranges).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thin, ivory-coloured, translucent porcelain made in Trenton, NJ, 1882–1930, hand-painted by in-house or amateur china painters, heavy gold gilding, often raised-paste gold. Shapes: three-handled loving cups, tankards with grape/berry painting, ewers, tall vases, shell-form dishes, demitasse sets. Marks on the base (red, green, brown or lavender):
- **Ott & Brewer** 1883–1892: crescent with "BELLEEK" and "O&B" – rarest, highest.
- **Willets Manufacturing** 1880–1909: snake coiled into a "W" over "BELLEEK".
- **Ceramic Art Company (CAC)** 1889–1906: **artist's palette with brushes, "CAC" monogram, "BELLEEK" below** – this is early Lenox. Red/lavender palette = decorated at the factory; green = sold as blank for home decoration.
- **Lenox** 1906+: wreath with "L", later "Lenox" – post-1930 wreath pieces are ordinary Lenox, low value.
- Also Knowles Taylor & Knowles "Lotus Ware", Columbian Art Pottery, Cook Pottery, Morgan Belleek, Perlee.
Not to be confused with **Irish Belleek** (Fermanagh; harp/hound/tower mark; different market, mostly $20–150).

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Lenox wreath-mark undecorated or transfer pieces, Irish Belleek shamrock ware, amateur-painted small dishes with worn gold | $15–60 | |
| Decent | Willets or CAC amateur-decorated vases/tankards, chipped or gold-worn factory pieces | $60–200 | |
| Collectible | CAC/Lenox factory-decorated (palette mark) tankards, loving cups, portrait vases, signed by artists (e.g., W. H. Morley, Nosek, Marsh); Willets factory-decorated; silver-overlay CAC | $200–600 | three-handled loving cups with portraits $250–500 |
| Top | Ott & Brewer anything factory-decorated (lotus jugs, swan dishes, tea sets); Willets/CAC large artist-signed vases; Lotus Ware | $600–3,000+ | verify mark in photo before paying |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- Roadshow appraisals: Ott & Brewer lotus jug c.1890 and O&B vase c.1880 appraised in the low-to-mid thousands (pbs.org)
- 2026-10 – CL Atlanta dealer – CAC palette-mark 3-handled loving cup, hand-painted child portraits, gilt, 9 in – $150 ask (dealer-tagged; would sell $250–450 at auction)

## What sellers usually miss
Estate sellers call it "Belleek" generically (thinking Irish) or "hand painted vase" and never photograph the mark. A palette or snake mark at a $20–60 "porcelain vase" price is the find. Gold wear and hairlines matter – look at rims.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile; double-box, 2–4 lb packed, $15–25 each way. Sell on eBay (13%) or send Top-tier pieces to a regional auction house.

## Buy rule
Buy when: palette (CAC), snake (Willets) or crescent (O&B) mark visible and price ≤ $60 for decorated pieces, ≤ $150 for O&B or artist-signed. Pass when: Lenox wreath mark, Irish Belleek, chips/hairlines, or dealer-tagged at $150 (needs a $450 sale to clear Dylan's rule). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.

# Anamorphic projection lenses (Kowa, Sankor, Isco, Bell & Howell)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay listings (Kowa 16-H ask $1,190 item 235161786436; 16-D asks $300–500), kamerastore.com 16-D spec page, vintagelensesforvideo.com Kowa Vidoscope review + 2024/25 reader comments ($200 for a 16-H in 2024, $280 Vidoscope), seller statements in sweep listings.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Short, fat chrome-and-black cylinder (60–90 mm long) with a glass front, no aperture ring, a single focus ring, and a rear thread (43 mm on Kowa). Originally bolted to the front of 16 mm/35 mm cinema projectors to un-squeeze CinemaScope film; indie filmmakers now mount them on camera lenses for 2x "anamorphic" flares and oval bokeh. Titles usually say "projection lens", "anamorphic", "cinemascope", "Prominar". The model is engraved on the barrel: **Kowa Prominar 8-Z / 16-H** (same optics, the grail), **16-D** (smaller, 2x, cheaper), **16-S**, **Vidoscope**, **Bell & Howell Anamorphic** (rebadged Kowa 8-Z or 16-D), **Sankor 16C/16D/16F**, **Isco Ultra Star / Iscorama** (Iscorama 36/54 are $2–5k), **Schneider Cinelux**, **Elmoscope**, **Singer/Kollmorgen**.

| Decent | Sankor 16C/16D, Elmoscope II, Kowa Vidoscope, Bell & Howell 2x (small) | $150–300 | |

## What sellers usually miss
Estate/garage sellers list these as "projector lens" or "movie projector part" for $10–40 because they don't know filmmakers want them. The model number on the barrel is everything: 8-Z/16-H = 3–4x the price of a 16-D. Check photos for the engraving and for clear glass (haze/fungus halves the price).

# Antique open-back 5-string banjos (1880s–1920s)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay/Reverb sold knowledge, Elderly Instruments and Retrofret catalogue pricing, banjo collector sites (Mugwumps, Banjo Hangout classifieds)

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Skin head, wooden rim with a spun-over metal "pot", many hook-and-nut tensioners, 5 strings (short 5th string peg on the side of the neck), friction (violin-style) pegs or early geared tuners, often a star/crescent inlay on the headstock. Makers to find, usually stamped on the dowel stick inside the rim or on the headstock back: **S.S. Stewart** (Philadelphia, engraved rim), **Fairbanks / Fairbanks-Vega** (Whyte Laydie, Tubaphone – tone ring = money), **Bacon**, **Cole's Eclipse**, **Dobson**, **Gatcomb**, **Washburn (Lyon & Healy)**, **Orpheum**. Unmarked "Buckbee"-made catalogue banjos (star inlay, simple nickel rim, 1890–1910) are the common tier. Tells of value: fancy engraved metal rim, a visible tone ring (metal ring under the head), heavy pearl inlay, name stamped on the dowel stick, 11" or larger pot with 20+ brackets.

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Unmarked Buckbee-style, missing hooks, warped neck, cracked rim | $60–150 | most listings |
| Decent | S.S. Stewart student models, Washburn, Bacon plain, restorable unmarked with good neck | $150–400 | |
| Collectible | Fairbanks Whyte Laydie No.2/No.7, Vega Tubaphone, Stewart Thoroughbred, Cole's Eclipse | $800–3,000 | the ones worth buying |
| Top | Fairbanks Electric, Stewart presentation, Vega Tubaphone #9 | $3,000–10,000 | verify before paying |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2025–26 – unmarked 1900s 5-string, friction pegs, star inlay, missing hooks – $80–150 (eBay sold)
- 2025–26 – S.S. Stewart Universal Favorite, playable – $350–600 (eBay/Reverb)
- 2025–26 – Fairbanks Whyte Laydie No.2 – $1,500–2,500 (Reverb/Elderly)

## What sellers usually miss
The dowel-stick stamp (ask for a photo looking inside the rim) and tone ring. "Antique banjo 1900" with a star headstock and a plain nickel rim is a Buckbee – pass unless under $60.

## Shipping / handling
Long box (40×16×8), 6–8 lb: $40–60 ground. Reverb 8%. Pad the neck; drop the head tension before shipping.

## Buy rule
Buy when: any stamped Fairbanks/Vega/Stewart/Cole/Bacon under $300 with straight neck; unmarked with engraved rim or tone ring under $100. Pass when: unmarked plain rim over $60; cracked rim or warped neck (repair rule). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).

# Regional artist "originals" that are usually prints (Campanelli, Kinkade, Gaston Bussière, Putt)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay sold browse Oct 2025 (Pauline Eble Campanelli prints, Gerald Putt S/N prints, Kinkade); sweep listings 2813, 2667, 2359, 2452

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Framed, matted image behind glass with a flat surface = almost always an offset print. Originals on canvas have texture and are rarely under glass. Pauline Eble Campanelli (Bucks County PA, 1943–2001) painted still lifes of baskets/dry sinks; her originals sell $300–2,000 at auction but her images were mass-printed – CL "original Campanelli" is a $15–40 print unless you can see brushwork. Gerald Putt (PA wildlife) signed/numbered prints $20–60. Thomas Kinkade "canvas lithographs" $30–150 despite certificates. Gaston Bussière (French symbolist, d.1928) – any "print" is decor, $20.

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Open-edition prints, posters, Kinkade paper prints | $10–40 | |
| Decent | S/N limited prints by known regional artists, Kinkade S/N canvas with COA | $50–150 | |
| Collectible | Original oils/acrylics by listed regional artists (Campanelli, Putt originals) | $300–2,000 | need visible brushwork, signature, provenance |
| Top | Listed artists with auction records over $5k | $5,000+ | not our lane without authentication |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2025-10 – eBay sold – Campanelli framed print "basket" – $20–45
- 2025-10 – eBay sold – Gerald Putt S/N print – $25–60

## What sellers usually miss
The reverse: sellers call prints "originals". Our risk is overpaying, not missing value. Check for the dot pattern under a loupe and the flat surface.

## Shipping / handling
Framed 20×24 with glass – $30–45 and breakage; ship unframed when possible.

## Buy rule
Buy when: texture/brushwork visible, signed, by an artist with auction records, at ≤ 20% of the low auction estimate. Pass when: under glass with a mat (print), or seller can't say "oil on canvas/board". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule).
