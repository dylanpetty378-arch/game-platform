# Japanese bronze vases (Meiji / Taisho / Showa archaistic & mixed-metal)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: appraisily Meiji bronze vase guide (tiers + repro tells), 1stdibs/catawiki listings, eBay sold memory 2024-25

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Dark brown/black patinated bronze, 8–18" tall. Two families: (a) **archaistic** – Chinese-bronze-style taotie/key-fret bands, bird- or elephant-head lug handles, pear or baluster body (what sellers call "champleve", "brass urn", "oriental vase"); (b) **mixed-metal / relief** – cranes, dragons, irises, Mt Fuji in high relief, sometimes gold/silver inlay (zogan). Signature (if any) is a cartouche of 2–4 kanji on the base. Seller words: "brass vase", "bronze urn", "Japan", "Chinese", "champleve".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | 1950s–80s export souvenir, thin casting, uniform factory patina, 6–10" | $20–60 | most CL listings |
| Decent | Taisho/Showa archaistic vase 12–16", crisp casting, unsigned | $120–350 | good flip if bought under $60 |
| Collectible | Meiji relief vase with cranes/dragons, signed, 12–18" | $400–1,500 | needs clean photos of signature |
| Top | Named workshops (Seiya, Nogawa, Kanaya, Miyao), mixed-metal inlay, pairs | $2,000–20,000+ | auction-house territory; send to specialist |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2024-05-19 – Meiji bronze censer c.1900 – AUD 4,200 (Gibson's, via appraisily)
- 2025 – unsigned 14" archaistic twin-handle vase, Showa – ~$150–250 typical eBay sold (memory; confirm with Terapeak)
- Appraisily tiers: small unsigned export vase $500–2,500 at auction (their figure is retail-leaning; eBay runs lower)

## What sellers usually miss
Call it "brass" or "Chinese". Don't photograph the base (signature) or the inside (coarse sand = repro). Weight: real Meiji castings are heavy and have nearly invisible seams; cheap repros have crude interior seams and bright uniform gold.

## Shipping / handling
8–15 lb, sturdy – double box, ~$25–40 in. Resell on eBay (13%) with base/signature photos; collectors want interior shots.

## Buy rule
Buy when: crisp casting + bird/elephant handles + 12"+ tall and price ≤ $60, or any visible kanji signature with price ≤ $150. Pass when: thin, uniform shiny patina, under 8", or seller already says "Meiji signed". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).

# Japanese woodblock prints (ukiyo-e, shin-hanga, reprints)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: knowledge of eBay/auction sold ranges; no dated comp pulled

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Originals: Edo/Meiji prints (Hiroshige, Hokusai, Utamaro, Kunisada) on thin mulberry paper, visible woodgrain, publisher seal and censor seals in the margin; 20th-c. shin-hanga (Hasui, Yoshida, Koson) with publisher seal (Watanabe) and often a pencil signature. 1950s–80s reprints (Uchida, Adachi, tourist-shop "Utamaro" beauties) are crisp, bright, on heavier paper, often in bamboo frames – these are $20–60. Framed-with-mat pairs on craigslist are nearly always reprints.

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | 1950s–80s reprints, tourist prints | $15–50 | bamboo frames = this tier |
| Decent | Meiji-era Kunisada/Toyokuni triptych panels, faded Hiroshige later editions | $60–200 | |
| Collectible | Hasui/Yoshida shin-hanga with Watanabe seal, good Hiroshige editions | $300–1,500 | authenticate seals |
| Top | Hokusai Great Wave early edition, Sharaku | $10,000+ | auction |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- (knowledge-based)

## What sellers usually miss
Publisher and date seals, and the artist. Conversely, sellers call reprints "antique" – check paper and seals before believing a title.

## Shipping / handling
Framed: $25–35, glass risk. Unframed: $10.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a print with a Watanabe/period publisher seal visible in the photo is under $100. Pass when: bamboo-framed bright pairs titled "antique" – they are reprints. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).

## Seen in sweeps

# Jewelry bench tools (rolling mills, disc cutters, arbor presses, flex shafts)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: Ganoksin forum threads on rolling mills, benchpeg classifieds, Otto Frei pricing, eBay sold memory 2025

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Rolling mill: two hardened steel rollers in a cast frame with a top screw, hand crank; brand cast or plated on the frame. **Durston** (UK, green or grey, "Durston" on the side plate), **Pepe Tools**, **Cavallin** (Italy) = valuable; unbranded black/green import mini-mills (Vevor, Harbor Freight style, "Made in India") = cheap. Disc cutter: blue steel block with punches (Pepe = $150–300; import $30–60). Arbor press "No. 1/No. 2" green = generic import ($40–80). Foredom flex shaft = $80–150 used. Seller words: "jewelry equipment", "metalsmith tools", "rolling mill".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Import mini rolling mill, no-name disc cutter, No. 2 arbor press | $40–120 | what most CL "jewelry tools" lots are |
| Decent | Pepe disc cutter, Foredom SR w/ handpiece, Cavallin small mill | $100–300 | |
| Collectible | Durston C80/C100/DRM mills, combination mills | $500–1,500 | retail $800–3,000 |
| Top | Durston power mills, Bonny Doon hydraulic press | $1,500–5,000 | |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- Otto Frei 2026: Durston 1002 Hobby 80 economy mill retail ~$700 new
- benchpeg classified 2026: Durston heavy-duty mill listed ~£700–900 used
- Ganoksin consensus: Indian/Chinese mini mills $100–200 new, rollers mark easily; Durston holds resale ~60–70% of retail

## What sellers usually miss
Brand on the mill frame. "Jewelry equipment $1 – quality tools" lots sometimes hide a Durston. Need a photo of the side plate.

## Shipping / handling
Mills 20–60 lb cast iron – $40–90 freight-ish; arbor press 15 lb. eBay 13%.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Durston/Pepe/Cavallin visible and price ≤ 35% of retail. Pass when: unbranded green/black import (resale $80–120 minus shipping = nothing).

Kenner 1978–80 "Large Size Action Figures": Luke, Leia, Han, Chewbacca, Darth Vader, Ben Kenobi, C-3PO, R2-D2, Stormtrooper, Jawa (8"), Boba Fett, IG-88. ## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Steel-tube pedal kart with chain drive, hand-pull brake, adjustable bucket seat, pneumatic or solid rubber tyres, "KETTLER" / "Kettcar" decal on the nose or frame. # Movie posters (one-sheets, window cards, lobby stills)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: Heritage Auctions / eMoviePoster.com sold archives knowledge (2023–25); eBay sold knowledge for 1984 Terminator one-sheet; general rule-of-thumb for NSS stamps.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Original US one-sheet is 27×41" (pre-1985) or 27×40" (1985+), folded (pre-1985) or rolled (1985+). Originals carry an NSS number bottom right ("84/xxx") and studio/litho credits; reprints are 24×36" or have no NSS and glossy stock. Window cards 14×22" on card stock with a blank top strip; lobby cards 11×14"; publicity stills 8×10" B&W. Sellers write "original movie poster", "vintage poster", "framed".

| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

"Original" is used for any 27×40 poster; the 1984 Terminator original is 27×41 and folded with NSS 84/ number – a rolled 27×39 in a frame is usually a 1990s reprint. Framed posters hide the back; ask for an unframed photo.
