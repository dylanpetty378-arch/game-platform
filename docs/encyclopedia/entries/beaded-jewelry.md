## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Necklaces, bracelets, earrings and collars built from strung or woven beads (glass, crystal, faux pearl, stone, shell, amber, seed beads). Value hides in the beads' material and age and in a few signed makers. Photo tells:
- **Miriam Haskell / Haskell-style:** clusters of tiny seed pearls and glass beads hand-wired onto a gilt filigree base, "Russian gold" warm finish, ornate box or hook clasp with an oval "MIRIAM HASKELL" plaque.
- **Old Venetian / African trade beads:** chunky millefiori (mosaic flower canes), chevron beads (layered star pattern visible at the ends), "white heart" red beads (red over white core), worn uneven beads strung on raffia or cord.
- **Czech pressed glass 1920s–30s:** faceted, molded or two-tone beads with brass caps and spacers, barrel clasps, sometimes "Czechoslovakia" stamped on the clasp.
- **Peking glass, real amber, coral, jade:** see material tests below.
- **Native American beadwork:** loomed or peyote/brick-stitch seed beads, often on hide or with leather ties; older pieces use smaller, slightly irregular beads, sinew or cotton thread, period colors (greasy yellow, white heart red, Cheyenne pink).
- **1950s beaded collars:** detachable "Peter Pan" collars of faux pearls/beads sewn to satin or felt, hook-and-eye closure; Japanese and Hong Kong made, often sold under Baar & Beards "Top Hit" or Glentex labels.
- **Modern imported pieces:** perfectly uniform seed beads, bright aniline colors, plastic button or lobster clasps, polyester thread, glued felt backing.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Miriam Haskell (NY, 1926–present).** Unsigned before ~1947 (paper hang tags only); horseshoe plaque 1947–50; oval "MIRIAM HASKELL" plaque with reversed impression on the back early 1950s–late 1970s; flat-backed oval plaque ~1980 on. Every element hand-wired, never glued (antiquesknowhow).
- **Other signed costume beadwork:** Robert (Original by Robert), DeMario, Vendome (Coro), Hobé, Hattie Carnegie, Kenneth Jay Lane, Joan Rivers (modern), Stanley Hagler, Lawrence Vrba, Francois. Signature on a plaque or clasp.
- **Native American:** Navajo, Lakota/Sioux, Kiowa, Apache, Comanche, Cheyenne, Crow, Ojibwe; contemporary artists sometimes sign or tag. Most "Native-style" loom bracelets on Craigslist are imported (Asia/Mexico, or crafted from craft kits).
- **Venetian (Murano) and Bohemian (Czech) glass:** trade beads made 1800s–1930s for Africa; chevrons, millefiori, eye beads, white hearts.
- **Collars:** Baar & Beards "Top Hit" (NY importer), Glentex (scarves/accessories importer), Hong Kong and India hand-sewn collars.
- **Natural stones/pearls:** hematite (very heavy, metallic gray), turquoise (natural vs dyed howlite/magnesite), coral (natural red vs dyed bamboo), amber (Baltic), jet (Whitby), cultured pearls (Mikimoto clasp).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Faux pearls:** glass base with pearlescent coating (Japan, Majorica) — coating flakes to show glass. Plastic pearls are light and the coating peels in sheets.
- **Real pearls:** gritty when rubbed on teeth, knotted between each pearl, gold clasp marked 14K/18K.
- **Glass vs plastic beads:** glass is cold, heavy, clicks sharply; plastic is warm and light. Mold seams on cheap plastic.
- **Hematite:** extremely heavy for size; modern "magnetic hematite" (hematine) is synthetic.
- **Turquoise:** natural shows matrix that sits flush; dyed howlite/magnesite shows white under scratches and dye pooling in cracks; plastic block turquoise is uniform.
- **Amber:** light, warm, floats in salt water; plastic copies sink. Pressed amber has swirl patterns.
- **Native beadwork age:** pre-1930 beads (size 13–16/0 Italian/Czech) vary in size and shape; older thread is sinew or cotton (opaque), modern is nylon/polyester (translucent) (coyotesgame); hide backing and brain-tanned leather are old tells.
- **Clasp dating:** box clasps and screw barrels are older; lobster clasps date most pieces to 1980s or later; spring rings 1930s on.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Broken or restrung strands (new thread, new clasp) — restringing is acceptable to most buyers if beads are original, but a new clasp on a "signed" piece kills authenticity.
- Faux pearl coating loss ("peeling"), verdigris on brass wiring (Haskell — normal in moderation), missing beads in clusters.
- Native beadwork: loose rows, broken warp threads, moth damage to hide.
- Stringing done by an owner who added modern beads.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Fake Haskell plaques soldered onto unsigned pieces; Haskell's own modern reissues are signed but lower value.
- Imported "Native American" beadwork sold as Native-made — a federal crime under the Indian Arts and Crafts Act if misrepresented; never describe a piece as Native-made without evidence.
- Modern reproduction "African trade beads" (new Indonesian/Ghanaian glass) are common; new chevrons have crisp, unworn ends.
- Dyed stones sold as natural turquoise/coral; plastic sold as amber.

## Market
- Huge volume, low prices; eBay and Etsy are flooded with modern beadwork. Signed Haskell and true trade beads sell within weeks; everything else languishes.
- Buyers: costume-jewelry collectors (signature-driven), bead collectors (material-driven), boho/vintage fashion buyers.
- Etsy outperforms eBay for 1950s collars and pretty unsigned vintage.

## What sellers usually miss
- Unsigned pre-1947 Haskell (wired-on-filigree construction without a plaque) is sold as "costume junk."
- Old trade-bead strands from estates ("African beads") — chevrons and millefiori are worth checking.
- Real Baltic amber and natural coral in grandma's jewelry box, sold by the strand as "beads."
- Signed clasps (Czechoslovakia, Haskell, Vendome) hidden at the back — ask for a clasp photo.

## Shipping / handling
- Pocket-size; padded mailer or small box, $5–6 (USPS Ground Advantage under 8 oz). Insure Haskell and anything over $100.
- Coil strands in a bag so beads don't rub; protect faux-pearl coating.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Name the materials you can prove (glass, faux pearl, natural amber tested by salt water) and say "unsigned" or show the signature.
- Measure length, bead size (mm), weight.
- Close-up of clasp and any signature, plus a scale photo.
- Never call a piece "Native American made" or "Navajo" without a maker's tag or provenance; "Southwestern style" is the honest term.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$6) − ship to Dylan (~$6) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Because ordinary beaded jewelry sells $10–40, a buy must be clearly mid-tier or above: signed Haskell plaque (or obvious Haskell wiring on filigree), old trade beads, real amber/coral, pre-1960 Native beadwork on hide, or Czech 1920s–30s glass with original clasp.
- Pass on uniform modern seed beads, plastic clasps, dyed stones, mall "crystal."
- Native beadwork claims and high-end Haskell are authentication-sensitive — buy only at prices that still work if it's "Haskell-style" or "Native-style."
- Fits the size rule always.
