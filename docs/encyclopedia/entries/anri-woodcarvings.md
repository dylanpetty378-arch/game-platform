## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small painted carved-wood figures made in Val Gardena (Ortisei / St. Ulrich, South Tyrol, Italy), the Black Forest (Germany) and Brienz (Switzerland), 1930s–1980s, sold as souvenirs and barware.
- **Mechanical bottle stoppers** (1940s–70s): a cork base on top of which a carved figure moves when a lever or string at the back is pressed — man tips his hat, couple kisses, drinker raises a glass, woman beats a man with a broom, man lifts a woman's skirt (risqué). Mostly ANRI; stamped "ANRI" or "Italy" on the base, or a foil sticker.
- **Big Mouth figures** (1950s–60s): figures with a wide open mouth (toothpick, cigarette or match holders), sometimes bottle openers.
- **ANRI figurines** (1960s–2000s): Juan Ferrandiz (children and animals), Sarah Kay (Australian illustrator), Disney characters, nativities, Kristin; marked "ANRI" with an artist's name, sometimes a limited edition number. 
- **Black Forest** (1860s–1920s): carved bears, hall stands, nutcrackers — much more valuable but larger.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **ANRI** (Anton Riffeser, Ortisei, founded 1912; peak exports 1950s–80s; resin "ANRI" figures later): stamped "ANRI ITALY", sometimes a gold/green foil label, painted in thin translucent washes on maple/linden.
- **Other Val Gardena workshops**: unmarked "Hand Carved Italy" pieces of similar look.
- **Swiss Brienz** and **German Oberammergau** souvenirs: more naturalistic, often unpainted.
- **Japanese copies** (1950s–60s): moulded composition or wood-flour pieces painted to look carved, "Japan" stamped — less value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- ANRI: crisp tool marks, translucent paint over visible grain, glass-like varnish, cork base (stoppers), hidden lever on the back.
- Copies: uniform moulded surfaces without knife facets, opaque paint, seams.
- Risqué actions and multi-figure actions are the premium stoppers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or stuck levers, missing strings, dried/crumbling cork bases, missing hats or arms (glued back), paint loss and alcohol stains, cracks in the wood. Test that the action works.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Japanese composition copies (see above). Modern Chinese resin "carved look" stoppers and toothpick holders. ANRI made resin figures from the 1990s — lower value than wood.

## Market
Steady barware/kitsch demand; single stoppers sell slowly in a crowded market; lots sell at a per-piece discount (~$5–9 each). Holidays (Nov–Dec) bump barware.

## What sellers usually miss
- The ANRI stamp on stoppers listed as "carved cork".
- Risqué stoppers mixed into lots.
- Reverse: sellers price stoppers at $10–20 each ($120 for 12 in our sweep), above what lots sell for.

## Shipping / handling
Light (stoppers 2 oz each). Lot of 12 in a small box with bubble wrap, $10–14. Protect levers/arms.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Count, list each action, which levers work, ANRI stamps (photo), height, cork condition. Lot listings with a group photo and individual action photos.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). A lot selling for $90 nets ~$78 before ~$12 shipping each way: buy price must be ≤ ~$10 for $50 profit. So: stoppers ≤ ~$1–2 each in large lots, Big Mouth holders ≤ $5 each. Large ANRI Disney/Ferrandiz limited editions only at ≤ 25% of sold and only if they fit a passenger seat. No testing issues beyond the lever.
