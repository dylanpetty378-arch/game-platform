## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Souvenir/decor models** (1950s–present, Spain, Italy, Taiwan, India): solid or shallow-bored barrel, decorative crests ("coat of arms", "Toledo"), cast metal carriages with spoked wheels, slotted/Phillips screws, uniform dark patina with brass high spots. The example is this type.
- **Signal / yacht cannons** (1850–1920): heavy bronze barrel with a real bore (1/2"–1"), touch-hole, wooden naval truck carriage or iron carriage, often with maker marks or proof marks; can be functional black-powder pieces (antique-firearm rules apply to pre-1899).
- **Big-Bang carbide cannons** (Conestoga Co., 1912–present): cast-iron/grey-painted barrels with "Big-Bang" cast in, plunger-igniter, calcium carbide ("Bangsite") fuel; models 6F, 10F, 15F, 25F, 60mm M60 tank, "Big Bang" field cannons.
- **Cast-iron toy cannons** (1890s–1930s): Grey Iron, Kenton, Ives, J&E Stevens cap cannons – painted iron, cast spoked wheels, firing caps.

## Makers, brands and variants
Conestoga / Big-Bang (Bethlehem PA), Grey Iron Casting Co. (Mount Joy PA), Kenton Hardware (Ohio), Ives, J&E Stevens (cap cannons), Bradley & Hubbard (bronze desk models), Penncraft (1970s decor), Italian "Fabbrica d'armi" souvenir models, Spanish Toledo souvenirs, Indian brass reproductions.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Heavy, finely chased bronze, real bore and vent, wooden carriage with iron fittings = better. Lightweight brass-plated pot metal, screwed-together carriage, decorative crests and fake touch-holes = souvenir.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing wheels/axle nuts, broken trail, cracked pot metal, missing ramrod. Big-Bang: missing igniter parts.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian-made "antique bronze signal cannons" are common; many marked "Napoleon" or with fake proof marks. Conestoga still makes Big-Bang cannons (date by casting details and paint).

## Market
Decor models: plentiful, slow. Big-Bang: steady collector demand (around July 4th). Signal cannons: auction market.

## What sellers usually miss
Big-Bang cannons that sellers think are cast-iron toys; real bored bronze signal cannons sold as decor.

## Shipping / handling
1–4 lb, small box $10–15; heavy bronze 5–15 lb more.

- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (Big-Bang demand around July 4th; toy cannons to cast-iron toy collectors); auction for bored 19th-c. bronze signal cannons. Decor models local or in lots.
- **Price position:** at the sold median ($50–200 Big-Bang/Grey Iron/Kenton; $300–2,000 signal cannons), accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days; decor $20–50 firm.
- **Photos:** cannon side on with carriage; maker marks on barrel/carriage; muzzle showing bore or solid; touch-hole; breech; wheels and carriage joints; firing (video) for Big-Bang; next to a ruler.
- **State:** material, overall and barrel length, bore present or solid, carriage material and original, marks transcribed, working (Big-Bang tested) or "decorative, non-functional", chips and repairs by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox, 1–4 lb $10–15, heavy bronze 5–15 lb double-boxed; carriage removed or immobilized, wheels wrapped.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Big-Bang Conestoga 15FC] carbide cannon, Bethlehem PA [1950s], fires with carbide and water (tested); the brass desk souvenirs sell for [$] and working Big-Bangs bring [$] every July."

## Buy rule
Decor desk cannons resale $20–50 → profit negative at $60. Pass. Big-Bang at ≤ $25 (resale ≥ $80) or genuine signal cannons at ≤ $60 with resale ≥ $300 are buys. Passenger-seat OK; functional black-powder cannons may raise shipping/legal issues (ATF antique status) – keep to pre-1899 or decor.
