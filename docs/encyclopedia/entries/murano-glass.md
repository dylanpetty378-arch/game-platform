## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-blown glass from the island of Murano (Venice), plus the huge volume of imitations. Photo tells:
- **Sommerso** (submerged): thick, heavy pieces with two or more colours layered inside clear glass with crisp boundaries (Seguso/Flavio Poli 1950s–60s, Cenedese, Mandruzzato, later V. Nason). Teardrop bowls, faceted vases, birds and ducks.
- **Bullicante / controlled bubbles**, **pulegoso** (frothy bubbles), **aventurine** (copper sparkles), **zanfirico/filigrana** (twisted white canes), **murrine/millefiori** (flower canes), **lattimo**, **calcedonio** (marbled).
- **Souvenir figurines** (1960s–today): clowns, elephants, horses, fish, birds; often amber, with white or gold fused details; paper labels "Murano Glass", "ICET Arte Murano", "Made in Italy".
- **Murano-style imports** (Taiwan/China/Czech/Mexico, 1970s–today): amethyst or dark controlled-bubble "lovers/dancers" abstract sculptures on a polished clear block base, swirl vases, "Dynasty Gallery" and "Heirloom Collection" labels (San Francisco importer); perfectly symmetric, thin or very light, colour often looks painted.
- **American studio glass** (1970s–today): Chris Heilman, Rollin Karg, Orient & Flume, Lundberg: signed and dated on the base with a pen-like engraver.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Venini** (1921–): acid-stamp "venini murano ITALIA" (three-line, 1950s–60s) or engraved "venini italia" + year; designers Carlo Scarpa, Fulvio Bianconi (Fazzoletto handkerchief vases), Paolo Venini, Tapio Wirkkala. $300–50,000.
- **Seguso Vetri d'Arte** (Flavio Poli, 1934–63): sommerso, Valva, bullicante; mostly unsigned with paper labels; Poli median ~$750 at auction.
- **Archimede Seguso**: merletto, piume; signed "Archimede Seguso Murano".
- **Barovier & Toso** (Ercole Barovier): textured "Barbarico", "Primavera"; usually unsigned, labels.
- **AVEM, Cenedese, Salviati, Fratelli Toso, Aureliano Toso, Formia, Alfredo Barbini, Gino Cenedese, Licio Zanetti, Vincenzo Nason (V. Nason & C. "Murano Glass" labels, 1970s–2000s)**.
- **ICET ("Arte Murano ICET" labels)**: 1960s–80s souvenir and decor figurines; labels; $25–60.
- **Murano-style**: Dynasty Gallery (SF importer, Asian-made), Heirloom Collection, Fifth Avenue Crystal, Chinese "Murano Style" lovers/dancers; $15–50.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight and clarity: real sommerso is very heavy, with optically clear outer glass and crisp internal colour boundaries.
- Pontil: a ground or rough pontil on the base; Asian imports usually have a flat-polished base or a cast block base.
- Imperfections: small bubbles and slight asymmetry are normal on real pieces; perfect symmetry and uniform thickness point to imports (appraisily).
- Labels: "Vetro Artistico® Murano" trademark (since 1994, Consorzio Promovetro) on modern genuine pieces; old foil labels (Seguso, Barovier, Cenedese) are strong evidence; "Made in Italy" alone is weak.
- Signatures: engraved on the base for Venini, Archimede Seguso, Salviati; acid stamps on Venini; many genuine older pieces are unsigned.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on ears, tusks, beaks, fins and rims; internal cracks (stress fractures) in thick sommerso; scratches on the base; ground-down rims to remove chips (look for an uneven, flat-polished rim). Damage cuts value 50–90%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Chinese/Taiwanese "Murano" with fake Murano labels or "Murano" written on the base.
- Fake Venini acid stamps and fake Archimede Seguso signatures (appraisily).
- Modern Murano reproductions of Fazzoletto and sommerso designs (genuine Murano, but recent) – value a fraction of period pieces.
- Lens matches to famous designers on abstract glass are usually style matches.

## Market
Genuine mid-century Murano sells well and fast (international demand; dealers buy at estate sales). Souvenir figurines and Asian "Murano-style" pieces are oversupplied; most sell under $40 if at all. Sellers widely title any blown glass as "Murano".

## What sellers usually miss
- Sommerso and Poli-type pieces listed as "heavy glass vase/bird" without the word Murano; tell = heavy, layered colour, crisp boundaries.
- Old foil labels on the base (Seguso, Barovier & Toso, Cenedese).
- Engraved "venini italia" on a "glass bowl".
- The reverse is the norm: imports titled "Vintage Murano" and priced $50–150.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy for size (sommerso 2–6 lb) and fragile; elephants/birds have protrusions. Double-box, 2–3" padding each side. $15–25 each way.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Say "Murano" only with a label, signature or unmistakable technique; otherwise "attributed" or "Murano-style". Photograph the base, pontil, label and any chips under raking light; give weight and height.
