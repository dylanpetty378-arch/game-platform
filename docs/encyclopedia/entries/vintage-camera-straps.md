## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1970s woven jacquard straps with leather ends, rivets and swivel clips or split rings, often in Mexican/Andean or geometric patterns,
sold widely in camera shops. Many "hippie" straps started as guitar straps (Photrio). Value only for OEM brand-name straps or very vivid designs.
OEM straps of the same era are different: original camera straps were usually ~1/2 in wide black leather, sometimes canvas (Photrio);
Nikon's AN-series were woven nylon with the brand woven in (e.g. AN-6Y, yellow with black "Nikon", leather-reinforced ends, supplied
with F2/F3/FM/FE-era bodies per Coastal Film Lab); Olympus OM bodies came with a rubber strap and later canvas straps with blue
"OLYMPUS" lettering (Photrio). Hasselblad and Leica straps are leather with maker-specific lug fittings.

Title words: "70s hippie camera strap", "woven guitar camera strap", "Nikon AN-1/AN-6", "genuine Leica strap", "Hasselblad strap".
Look-alikes: modern "vintage style" woven straps (new leather, crisp weave), D'Andrea "Ace" reissue guitar straps.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **OEM camera makers:** Nikon (AN-series woven nylon, earlier leather "Nippon Kogaku" era straps — general knowledge), Canon (woven
  logo straps), Olympus (rubber OM strap, canvas logo straps — check the nylon loops are included, Photrio), Pentax, Minolta, Leica,
  Hasselblad, Rollei.
- **Ace** is a guitar-strap brand (now D'Andrea, which sells "Ace vintage reissue" straps of the 1960s–70s designs) — not a camera OEM.
  Original 1960s–70s Ace guitar straps are a separate guitar-collector market.
- **OP/TECH (Optech)** is a modern neoprene strap brand (general knowledge), not a 1970s maker.
- Unbranded Japanese/US 1970s woven straps — the bulk of what is seen.
- Modern makers of new leather straps (e.g. Gordy's) are not vintage (Photrio).

### Quick ID checklist
- Brand name woven or printed on the band (Nikon, Canon, Olympus, Leica, Hasselblad) — or nothing?
- Width at the lug end: OEM leather ~1/2 in; hippie straps 1.5–2 in with tapered leather ends.
- End hardware: split rings, swivel clips, maker-specific lug clips (Hasselblad/Rollei).
- Leather: original grain and age darkening vs new-looking leather and fresh stitching.
- Weave: faded, softened cotton/acrylic vs crisp, glossy polyester (modern repro).
- Any tag or stamp on the leather tabs (maker name, "Made in Japan").
- Sold with a camera? OEM straps add more to a camera listing than they fetch alone.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Nikon AN-6Y yellow / other AN woven Nikon straps** — ~$40 retail ask (Coastal Film Lab) vs $8–20 generic — Nikon wordmark woven into a yellow or black nylon band with leather ends.
- **Leica original leather straps** — $40–150 tier (old entry, general knowledge) — Leica name embossed on the leather; check it is not the current new "vintage" Leica strap (retail $139 CAD new).
- **Hasselblad leather straps with original Hasselblad lug fittings** — $40–150 tier (old entry) vs $10 generic — wide leather strap with Hasselblad-specific clips; many new aftermarket "for Hasselblad" straps exist (eBay titles), so look for the maker's own stamp.
- **Olympus OM original rubber strap or blue-lettered canvas strap with loops** — modest premium to OM users — "OLYMPUS" lettering; nylon loops present.
- **Original 1960s–70s Ace guitar straps** (if one turns up as a "camera strap") — guitar-collector market, worth more than the camera-strap value (general knowledge) — Ace label on the leather end; compare with the D'Andrea reissue.
- Generic woven hippie straps — not golden eggs: one $20 listing "could sit there" for years (Photrio).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cotton/acrylic woven bands, real leather ends, nickel hardware. Old: faded colours, softened weave, darkened leather, patinated nickel.
New: bright polyester, stiff leather, shiny hardware, perfect machine stitching.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dry/cracked leather, fraying, stained bands. Also: torn lug holes, rusted rings, missing loops (Olympus), stretched leather. Check
the strap is strong enough to carry a camera — buyers will use it.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern reproductions sold as "vintage style" on Etsy/Amazon; D'Andrea Ace reissue guitar straps; Leica's own new "vintage" straps;
aftermarket "for Hasselblad" leather straps.

## Market
Slow, cheap. Straps mostly sell with cameras; little standalone market (Photrio). Film-camera users buy OEM straps to complete a kit.

## What sellers usually miss
OEM brand straps mixed in camera bag lots.

## Shipping / handling

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Length, width, hardware type, leather condition.

### What to tell the buyer
"Genuine __ (Nikon/Leica/Hasselblad) strap, brand woven/embossed as shown. Band __ in wide, __ in long end to end, with __ end
hardware. Leather tabs supple, no cracks; stitching intact; no fraying. Fits standard split-ring lugs / the __ lug system." For hippie
straps: "1970s woven jacquard strap, unbranded, __ in wide, original leather ends and swivel clips."
