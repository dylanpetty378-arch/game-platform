## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Slip-cast ceramic trees made in ceramic hobby classes from commercial molds (1960s–80s). Two pieces: tree and base, with a light bulb inside and plastic "pegs" (bulbs) pushed into holes. Mold maker mark is inside the bottom rim of the tree: "Atlantic Mold" (most common), "Holland Mold", "Duncan", "Arnel's", "Nowell's", "Byron", "Doc Holliday", with year. The hobbyist often signed and dated the bottom too. Green glaze with dark dry-brushed tips is the classic; colour, height, birds, star topper and multi-colour bulbs change value.

## Makers, brands and variants
Atlantic Mold (Trenton NJ), Holland Mold, Duncan, Arnel's, Nowell's, Byron; modern Chinese-made reproductions (2010s–) are lighter, with LED and battery bases.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Heavy earthenware, hand-applied glaze, drill holes for bulbs; old ones have cloth/2-prong cord and 7W bulb in base. Repros: uniform glaze, no mold mark, battery compartment.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on branch tips, missing bulbs (replacements cheap), cracks at base, missing star. Very sensitive to transit damage.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mr. Christmas and many retailers sell new "nostalgic ceramic trees" — glossy, lighter, often with music; worth $25–60.

## Market
Very seasonal: Oct–Dec strong, dead Jan–Aug. Local sale avoids shipping risk.

## What sellers usually miss
Uncommon colours and large sizes; hobby-cast trees sold as "decorations" at estate sales for $10–20.

## Shipping / handling
16–18", 8–12 lb, double-box, tree and base separately with foam. $30–45 to ship; breakage risk high.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Height, mold mark photo, bulb count, working light, chips noted.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − $35 − $35 − price ≥ max($50, price). A standard green tree at ~$110 never clears it when shipped both ways. Only buy local pickup or premium variants ≤ $40.
