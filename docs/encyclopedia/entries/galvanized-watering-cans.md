## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Galvanised (zinc-coated steel) watering cans with a top handle and/or side handle, round or oval body, and spout with or without a
sprinkler rose. Long tapered spouts suggest greenhouse use. French cans: zinc, oval, brass rose, often a maker's plaque under the handle.
Haws cans: tall body, two handles, very long spout, raised collar around the filling opening, brass rose, and a round brass medallion
with hawthorn sprigs and berries on the shoulder (House Digest, Haws). Most cans carry a **size number on the spout** — collectors sort
from small cans up to size 18 or 20 (Post Bulletin).
Material/period (Post Bulletin): painted tin (green or red) common in the 19th century, then unpainted zinc/galvanised; plastic from the 1940s.
Look-alikes: filling-station cans for engine fluids and oil, early hot-water cans (Post Bulletin) — sweep titles call watering cans "oil cans".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Haws** (England; 1886 on current medallions; widely copied from the late 1880s — rivals sold "Haws pattern" cans). Medallion dating
  (Haws): early logos have **larger hawthorn sprigs and no date**; some read **"Clapton, London"** or **"Clapton Works, Bishops Stortford"**;
  **1953–1976** (TALA era) may read **"Stourbridge, England"** round the rim; **1976–1982** may read **"Haws Elliott Limited, Walsall"**;
  current ones show **1886 + "Made in England"**. Only genuine Haws could say "registered trade mark"; "Haws pattern" = a competitor's can.
  The Haws name was once stamped on every rose. Haws still makes cans, so a Haws is not automatically old (House Digest). Copper and brass
  indoor Haws cans exist (Chairish).
- **French makers** — e.g. **Thureau, Paris** (plaque beside the opening under the handle; zinc body, copper rose, unscrewing brass cap;
  late 19th/early 20th c.; $1,090 retail incl. import, Chez Pluie). Guillouard (Nantes) and other plaque makers (general knowledge).
- **Wheeling** (Wheeling Corrugating, US) — the most plentiful American make (Post Bulletin).
- Dennis, Dover, Behrens, Rowell (US) — common; marks embossed on the body or handle (general knowledge).
- Dutch brass and 18th-century French copper cans — earlier, decorator/antique tier (Post Bulletin, Chairish).

### Quick ID checklist
- Round brass medallion on the shoulder? Read it: Haws + location (Clapton / Stourbridge / Walsall / 1886 Made in England).
- Maker plaque under the handle (French: Thureau Paris etc.) or embossed name (Wheeling, Dover, Behrens).
- Size number stamped on the spout.
- Rose present? Brass or copper? Stamped "Haws"?
- Body: oval (French/English) vs round (American); raised collar at the opening (Haws).
- Seams: hand-soldered lap seams and rivets vs machine-crimped seams; spangle pattern on old hot-dip galvanising.
- Material: zinc (dull grey, no rust), galvanised steel (spangle, rust where coating gone), copper, brass.
- Modern decor tells: thin metal, faux-aged paint wash, glued handles, Chinese label.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Haws with early "Clapton" medallion or no-date large-sprig medallion** — upper Haws tier, ~$75 to well over $150 (House Digest) vs $20–45 common — brass hawthorn medallion with Clapton wording on the shoulder.
- **Haws copper or brass indoor cans** — $100+ tier (House Digest, Chairish) — copper/brass body, long spout, medallion.
- **19th/early-20th-c. French zinc with maker plaque (e.g., Thureau, Paris)** — dealer retail to ~$1,090 (Chez Pluie) vs $20–45 — oval zinc body, brass/copper rose and cap, oval plaque by the opening.
- **Copper can with original rose** — "well over $100" (House Digest) — orange-brown copper, soldered seams, intact rose.
- **Large numbered French/English cans (size 18–20)** — premium for size (Post Bulletin, general knowledge) — big number on the spout.
- **Children's cans with printed graphics** — collected separately (Post Bulletin) — small tin can with litho print.
- "Haws pattern" lookalikes and common Wheeling/Dover — not golden eggs; $5–45 dealer asks.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Spangle pattern in zinc, hand-soldered seams on older cans; brass rose; stamped maker plates. Hot-dip galvanising shows crystalline
spangle and drips; electro-galvanised modern steel is uniform matte. Solid zinc cans (French) never rust red — they go grey-white.
Brass medallions were hand-applied and polished originally (Haws). Old: soldered seams, rivets at handle joints, heavy gauge, wear at
the base rim. New: thin gauge, spot welds, glossy clear coat, applied "rust" paint.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Leaks at seams, white rust, dents, paint remnants, missing rose. Dings and peeling paint are often treated as charm for decor (Post
Bulletin), but leaks and missing roses cut value; heavily damaged cans missing parts ~$10 (House Digest). Repairs: new solder (bright
silver lines), epoxy, replacement roses or reproduction medallions (Haws sells a 28 mm replacement medallion for £2 — a new medallion on
an old can is possible). Ask: does it hold water? is the rose original?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern decorative galvanized cans (Target, Hobby Lobby), often thin with faux aging. "Haws pattern" cans by competitors since the late
1880s. Current-production Haws (1886 medallion) sold as antique. Replacement Haws medallions (£2) can be stuck on other cans.

## Market
Décor demand spring/summer. Buyers: garden/farmhouse decorators (Etsy, local), Haws users who want a working can, and antique dealers
for French/copper pieces.

## What sellers usually miss
Haws marks on the rose/handle and the shoulder medallion's location wording; French maker plaques; size numbers on the spout; copper under grime.

## Shipping / handling
Bulky (spout length), $20–30. Remove the rose and wrap the spout; box size is driven by spout length.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Leak status, dimensions, marks. Give height, length including spout, capacity/size number, medallion or plaque text, rose material.

### What to tell the buyer
"__ watering can, __ in tall and __ in long including the spout, size __ stamped on the spout. Brass medallion reads '__'
(photographed); per Haws's own history that wording dates it to __. Original brass rose stamped __. Holds water / leaks at __.
Honest patina, no repainting."

## Buy rule
Bulky shipping makes most cans unprofitable. Buy Haws with older medallion wording (no date, Clapton, Stourbridge or Walsall), French plaque zinc, or copper/brass ≤ ~$30;
pass common galvanised. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
