## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Porcelain or bone china tableware. Identify pattern and maker from the backstamp; from photos look for:
- **Herend (Hungary)**: hand-painted fishscale (Rothschild Bird, Chinese Bouquet "Apponyi" green/rust/blue fishscale).
- **Royal Copenhagen**: blue fluted (fluted half-lace), Flora Danica botanical with serrated rims.
- **Royal Crown Derby**: Imari cobalt/rust/gold (2451, 1128).
- **Meissen**: crossed swords mark, Blue Onion, Scattered Flowers.
- **Common**: Noritake (gold-rim florals), Lenox (ivory with gold bands), Mikasa, Johann Haviland Bavaria, Homer Laughlin, Sango, Japanese "Fine China" sets.
- **English bone china teacup/saucer**: Royal Albert, Paragon, Royal Standard, Colclough, Aynsley; gold-encrusted scalloped edges and flower sprays; individual saucers $3–10.
- **Modern import tea/dessert sets (1990s–2010s)**: cobalt panels with pink cabbage roses, heavy gilding, pierced square plates, "Sorelle Fine Porcelain", "Royal Scotland", "Royal Classic", "Lefton"-style names. Made in China; decorator market only.

## Makers, brands and variants
See above. Key backstamps: Herend "Herend Hungary" crown; Royal Copenhagen three wavy lines; Meissen crossed swords; Royal Crown Derby crown with "D" and year cypher; Haviland "Haviland & Co. Limoges" or "Theodore Haviland"; Noritake "M in wreath" (1911–1940s) vs later "Noritake N".

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Bone china: translucent, thin, white; porcelain: harder, grey-white; stoneware: heavy, opaque.
- Hand-painted (brush strokes, variation) vs decal (dot patterns under magnification).
- Real gold rim wears pink-brown; rubbed gold lowers value. Not microwave safe.
- Decorative import sets are heavy-walled with thick gilding and printed "Fine Porcelain" stamps, often no country of origin.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, crazing, utensil marks, gold wear, dishwasher fade. Restored chips (paint-filled) on high-end pieces.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Meissen crossed swords and Herend lookalikes from China; "Dresden"-style generic marks. Royal Crown Derby imitations "Imari style".

## Market
Oversupplied. Boomer estates dump full sets; Replacements pays little. Only high-end patterns move quickly.

## What sellers usually miss
Herend/Royal Copenhagen in mixed lots; Royal Crown Derby Imari teacups; signed Haviland hand-painted pieces.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy and breakable: double-box, each piece wrapped. Tea set 10–15 lb ($25–40); single piece $8–12.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + pattern name + pattern number + piece or "service for N" + count + "fine china" + gold/cobalt. Junk: "dishes", "beautiful", "set of plates" alone. `Herend Rothschild Bird Dinner Plate 10.5in Hungary Hand Painted`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for high-end patterns and single pieces; Replacements buy-back as a floor; FB local for full services and import tea sets (heavy, slow).
- **Price position:** high-end pieces at the sold median, accept −15%, floor 70%; full services at the sold $50–300 range OBO, cut 25% after 60 days; orphans in lots.
- **Photos:** piece or stacked set on white; backstamp with pattern name/number; rims under raking light; gold trim wear; count laid out; any chips/crazing close-up.
- **State:** maker, pattern name and number, backstamp, piece count by type, dimensions, chips/hairlines/crazing under light, gold wear, "not microwave safe" for gold, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** carry-on, tea set 10–15 lb double-boxed with each piece wrapped $25–40; single piece $8–12; full services local pickup only.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Maker pattern], [count] pieces, all marked, no chips; Replacements lists this [piece] at [$] each and this lot is [price] for the set."

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price.
- Decorative sets ($48 median): 42 − 50 shipping − price = loss. **Pass.**
- Orphan saucers: pass.
- High-end piece median $80 at ≤ $10: 70 − 20 − 10 = $40; needs ≥ 2 pieces in the lot to clear $50.
