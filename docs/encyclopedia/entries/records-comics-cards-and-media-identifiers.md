## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
This is a reading guide, not an item type. It lists the small markings on paper-and-plastic media (LPs, comics, trading cards, video games and VHS) that separate a $5 copy from a $500 one. Use it with the type entry for the object: vinyl-records (Led Zeppelin II RL, Beatles butcher cover, Capitol and Columbia label eras, already covered there in depth), vinyl-record-lots, vintage-comic-books, underground-comix, sports-card-lots, baseball-card-wax-boxes, non-sports-trading-cards, pokemon-card-lots, big-box-pc-games, vintage-disney-collectibles.

**Where the marks sit:**
- **LP:** the label (address line, logo position, rim print, MONO/STEREO), and the **dead wax / runout**, the smooth band between the last groove and the label, where matrix numbers, stamper/lacquer suffixes, plant marks and mastering engineers' initials are etched (hand-scratched, uneven) or stamped (machine-even). Also the cover's top-right banner, spine catalogue number, promo stamps on the back, and the paper obi strip on Japanese pressings.
- **Comic:** the **cover price box** (top left), the **UPC box** (bottom left on 1976+ Marvel/DC), the **indicia** (small print at the bottom of the first interior page, or the inside front cover), the centrefold (inserts), and the grading slab label.
- **Card:** the front (rookie logo, foil serial number, nameplate), the **back** (copyright line, "Printed in Canada", bilingual text, serial number), and the edges/surface (print lines).
- **Video game:** the box front (seal of quality, "black box" art, ® vs TM after NES), the box back and flaps (hang tab, sticker seal), the cartridge label ("Not for Resale"), cartridge back (3 vs 5 screws), and the shrink-wrap seams.
- **VHS:** the spine and top-left front corner (Disney "Classics" black diamond), the clamshell and the tape label.

**How sellers word it when they have not read the mark:** "old record", "jazz LP", "Blue Note record" (no address), "vintage comic", "comic lot", "old Spider-Man", "baseball card lot", "hockey cards", "Gretzky card" (no OPC/Topps), "NES game", "old Nintendo", "sealed game" (no seam type), and the reverse error — "RARE black diamond", "1st edition", "misprint", "error card" on ordinary cards, "Not for Resale" as if it were rare.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Blue Note with "47 West 63rd · New York 23" or "767 Lexington" label, deep groove and "ear" in the runout** — $200–2,000 vs $30–100 for a Liberty repress (~5–20×). Photo tell: address line at the bottom of the label; a small ear-shaped "P" stamped in the runout.
- **35¢ Marvel 1977 price box** — Star Wars #1 CGC 9.4 $25,000; Iron Fist #14 9.4 ~20× the 30¢. Photo tell: square price box reading 35¢, with a barcode in the UPC box (Star Wars #1).
- **Canadian price variant (1982–88)** — ~2.4× the direct edition (Thor #337). Photo tell: a barcode box plus a higher price (75¢ where the US price is 60¢).
- **Mark Jewelers insert on a 1970s key** — ~2× (Werewolf by Night #32). Photo tell: a thick glossy block in the centrefold.
- **1979-80 O-Pee-Chee Gretzky** — 1.1× (low grades) to 3.4× (PSA 9) the Topps. Photo tell: French text on the back.
- **1990 Topps Frank Thomas with no nameplate** — $170,400 in PSA 10; raw copies hundreds to thousands (general knowledge, verify). Photo tell: no black name band at the bottom of the front.
- **1987 Donruss Opening Day #163 "Barry Bonds" showing Johnny Ray (dark jersey)** — ~45× the corrected card in PSA 10.
- **Sealed early NES with the correct H-seam or sticker seal, round seal, "NES TM" or hang tab** — five to six figures graded; pass unless graded, but never undersell one found in a lot.
- **Japanese LP with its obi** — ~1.9× the same record without (DSOTM).
- **Late-1960s mono first pressing** — 2–10× the stereo (see vinyl-records).

### Quick ID checklist
- **Vinyl:** photograph both labels and both runouts under raking light. Read the address line and logo position, then the matrix suffix (1A?), the engineer initials (RL, STERLING, MASTERDISK, PORKY, TML, RVG) and the plant mark. Check for a deep groove, the Blue Note ear, a MONO prefix, promo labels or stamps, cutout holes, and an obi.
- **Comic:** read the price box (30¢ in 1976, 35¢ in 1977, 75¢ CPV 1982–88), the UPC box (barcode vs art), the indicia (reprint?) and the centrefold (MJI). If slabbed, read the label colour and check the cert.
- **Card:** check the back for French text and "O-Pee-Chee"; look for a rookie logo, a serial number, the nameplate, and the specific error. Treat print lines as defects.
- **Video game:** check the seal of quality shape, ® vs TM, the box style (black box, hang tab), the cart screws, the seam type on the back of a sealed copy, and "Not for Resale".
- **VHS:** a black diamond is not a value signal; look only for sealed watermarked tapes or the few listed recalls.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Vinyl:** 1950s–60s pressings are heavy (140–180 g) with flat or raised "flat edge" rims (early Blue Note "flat edge" = pre-1958 die). Hand-etched matrices (scratchy) mean a lacquer cut; stamped text means the plant or mastering house. Original labels are glued flat with crisp type; counterfeits have fuzzy type and off-register colour (vinyl-records).
- **Comics:** Golden Age paper is coarse and tans; Silver Age covers are slightly glossy newsprint; 1980s direct editions have whiter covers. Reprint tabloids are oversized (10 × 13.5 in.). Inserts are glossier than the newsprint around them.
- **Cards:** vintage cards are on grey or cream board with a matte surface; OPC stock is whiter. Modern parallels use foil or refractor finish. Trimmed cards are slightly undersize — measure against a stock card (2.5 × 3.5 in. since 1957).
- **Games:** original NES carts have 5 screws (1985–86) or 3 (later), with a molded "Nintendo" logo; repro carts have different labels and modern boards. Original boxes are thin card stock with a matte finish; repro boxes are glossy and too crisp.
- **VHS:** Disney tapes from 1984–94 are clamshell or cardboard sleeve; sealed copies have Disney watermark wrap (genuine) vs plain shrink (reseal) (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- **Vinyl:** grade is everything — VG is about a quarter of NM for collectible pressings (general knowledge, verify). Ring wear, seam splits and writing on the cover cut value; warps and deep scratches kill it. Ask for a play test of key titles.
- **Comics:** spine stress, colour breaks, tears, missing coupons (green CGC label) and restoration (purple label). Restoration shows as colour touch-up under UV, trimmed edges (too-clean edges), re-glued staples and pressed creases.
- **Cards:** centring, corners, edges and surface. Trimming and recolouring are the common alterations; raw "gem mint" claims from sellers are not grades.
- **Games:** label tears, box crush, reseals. Sealed copies are judged by box and seal condition, so any tear in the wrap halves the value (general knowledge, verify).
- **VHS:** mould on the tape, sun-faded sleeves.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Vinyl:** counterfeit Beatles (Vee-Jay), butcher covers and bootleg white labels; 2000s–2020s 180 g reissues with copied original labels but barcodes and modern matrices; reproduction obis.
- **Comics:** altered price boxes (30¢/35¢), fake CGC slabs, reprints with original covers (Famous First Edition, Facsimile Editions), removed or added inserts.
- **Cards:** reprints (marked "reprint" on many), counterfeits of Gretzky, Jordan and 1952 Mantle, trimmed cards, fake NNOFs, fake slabs.
- **Games:** reproduction carts (Earthbound, Stadium Events), resealed games in fake H-seams, fake hang tabs, fake Wata/CGC holders.
- **VHS:** asking-price hype ("$1.28 million Beauty and the Beast") is the fraud, not counterfeits.

## Market
These media sell daily on eBay and Discogs (records), eBay/Heritage/ComicLink/MyComicShop (comics), eBay/PWCC-Fanatics/Goldin (cards) and eBay/Heritage (games). Collectors search by variant name ("35 cent variant", "CPV", "RL", "deep groove", "OPC"), so a correctly titled listing sells fast; an untitled one sells for the common price. Prices for comics and cards are grade-driven, and top variants sell mostly slabbed. Casual sellers rarely photograph the runout, price box, UPC box or card back, which is the edge; the opposite error (black diamond VHS, NFR games, "error" print lines) is as common and must be passed.

## What sellers usually miss
- The runout (RL, ear, 1A, plant), the Blue Note address line, and the Capitol logo position.
- That 35¢/30¢ is a variant and not a typo; that a 75¢ price on a 1984 Marvel is a CPV; that extra glossy pages in the centre are a Mark Jewelers insert.
- That French text on the back means O-Pee-Chee.
- The seam type and seal shape on a sealed game.
- The reverse error: "black diamond", "Not for Resale", "print line error" and "1st edition" on VHS or common cards are over-reads.

## Shipping / handling
- LPs: LP mailer with stiffeners, record out of the jacket, ~1 lb; Media Mail $5–8.
- Comics: bag and board, between two pieces of cardboard or in a comic mailer; $5–8; slabs in bubble wrap and a box, $10–15.
- Cards: penny sleeve, top-loader, team bag; plain white envelope for under $20 (eBay standard envelope), bubble mailer with tracking above that.
- Games: box in a protector, bubble wrap and box, $6–10.
- VHS: padded mailer, Media Mail ~$4–5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the exact variant and how you know: label address and logo, runout text both sides ("RL / STERLING, matrix -1A both sides"), MONO/STEREO, promo marks, obi; for comics the price box, UPC box type, indicia and any insert; for cards the set, number, OPC/Topps, serial number or error; for games the seal type, seal of quality, box revision and screws. Photograph every mark close-up. Title with the collector's words: "35 Cent Price Variant", "Canadian Price Variant CPV", "Newsstand", "Mark Jewelers Insert", "Deep Groove Ear RVG 47 West 63rd", "RL Sterling", "O-Pee-Chee", "No Name on Front", "H-seam sealed".
### What to tell the buyer
"Original [label/address] pressing: [address line], deep groove, ear and RVG in the runout, matrix [x]. Runouts pictured." / "35¢ price variant, newsstand copy, indicia pictured, no restoration seen." / "O-Pee-Chee (bilingual back pictured), not the Topps." / "Factory H-seam on the back pictured."

## Buy rule
Buy when: a sharp photo shows the valuable mark, the condition is readable, and the price is ≤ ~40% of the raw sold comp for that exact variant and grade (profit ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways). Pass when: the mark isn't photographed and the seller won't show it; a five-figure claim depends on a grade or seal no one has authenticated; the item is a black diamond VHS, an NFR pack-in or a "print line error"; the copy is a reprint, a pence copy, a 1990s dual-price book or a Liberty-label Blue Note priced like an original; any autographed card. Must fit a passenger seat (always does).
