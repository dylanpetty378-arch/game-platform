## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small (12–18") suitcases made for children, printed with licensed characters. Constructions: vinyl over cardboard with a plastic handle and metal latches (1950s–70s); printed nylon/canvas over board with vinyl piping (1980s, like the example).
- Copyright lines are printed near the art: "© 1983 Original Appalachian Artworks, Inc." identifies Cabbage Patch Kids (Coleco era 1983–89).
- Barbie cases: black or coloured vinyl with Barbie art and "© Mattel" date (1961–64 most collected).
- 1950s: "Ponytail" girl, cowboy (Hopalong Cassidy, Roy Rogers), Disney characters.
- **Two kinds of "case"**: a child's suitcase (for the child's clothes, 12–18") vs a **doll case** (sized for an 11½" Barbie, often with a clothes rail and drawers inside). Vintage Barbie doll cases are the larger collector market; open the case in the photos to tell.
- **Ponytail**: a 1950s–60s girls' brand built on a stylised ponytailed-girl graphic, used on vinyl cases, diaries, eyeglass cases, record totes and a 1959 Thermos lunch kit ("Ponytail Tid-Bid-Kit", American Thermos, pink vinyl). A c.1960 Ken doll set with carrying case is credited to **Ponytail Manufacturing** (Google Arts & Culture). The first Barbie case (1961) is marked with the manufacturer's name, Ponytail (Fashion Doll Guide).
- **Dating by materials**: thick glossy vinyl with stitched or heat-sealed seams, metal hinges and snap latches = 1950s–60s; quilted or printed nylon/canvas with plastic piping = 1980s; moulded plastic shells with wheels = 1990s on.
- **Title words**: train case, overnight case, doll case, vanity case, Barbie case, Ponytail case, character suitcase, child's luggage, 1960s vinyl case.

## Makers, brands and variants
Neevel (Kansas City, Ponytail cases), Standard Plastic Products, Mattel licensees, Coleco/OAA licensees for Cabbage Patch, American Greetings (Strawberry Shortcake, Care Bears), Hallmark/Knickerbocker (Holly Hobbie).
- Note: the Neevel / Ponytail attribution and Standard Plastic Products' role are from seller usage and not confirmed by a fetched source; museum records credit "Ponytail Manufacturing" for a c.1960 case. Treat the case's own printed mark as the authority.
- **Barbie doll cases (Mattel licence, 1961 on)**: 1961 Ponytail-marked case is the first Barbie case and, per the Fashion Doll Guide, came in several colours with small graphic differences and is "fairly common and not particularly valuable". The 1963 Barbie and Ken case came in red, blue, black and pink; Kaplan's guide calls **pink the rarest of the four**. Late-1960s to early-1970s cases often doubled as rooms or houses.
- **1950s cowboy and Disney licences**: Hopalong Cassidy, Roy Rogers & Dale Evans, Davy Crockett, Mickey Mouse — the same licences as the period's lunchboxes.
- **1980s**: Cabbage Patch Kids (© Original Appalachian Artworks), Care Bears and Strawberry Shortcake (American Greetings), Holly Hobbie, Smurfs, My Little Pony — large supply.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the copyright line: name of licensor and year (e.g. © 1983 Original Appalachian Artworks; © Mattel with year).
- Open it: doll fittings (rail, hangers, drawers) = doll case; plain lining = child's suitcase.
- Material: glossy vinyl over board with metal latches = 1950s–60s; printed fabric with piping = 1980s.
- Colour of a 1963 Barbie & Ken case: pink is the rarest (Kaplan).
- Check latches and hinges for rust, handle for splits, vinyl for cracks at corners.
- Look for stains, fading on the top, and musty interior (ask about smell).
- Contents: original dolls or clothes inside change the item entirely; photograph and research separately.
- Measure height and width; give both.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Vintage Barbie doll case with original Barbie or clothes inside** — contents can be worth many times the case (case alone $25–75 per existing ladder) — doll, outfits or tagged clothing visible when opened; research the contents separately.
- **1963 Barbie & Ken case in pink** — rarest of four colours (Kaplan, cited by Fashion Doll Guide); premium vs red/blue/black (no sold comps fetched; est., verify) — pink vinyl with 1963 Barbie and Ken art.
- **Early Barbie 1961–64 cases, near mint** — $50–150 (existing ladder) vs $10–25 for stained 1980s character cases — crisp vinyl, bright art, © Mattel 1961–64, working latches.
- **1950s cowboy (Hopalong Cassidy, Roy Rogers) and Disney vinyl cases** — $40–90 (existing ladder) vs $10–25 — named character art and 1950s licence line.
- **Ponytail-girl vinyl cases and matching accessories** — $25–75 tier (existing ladder) — stylised ponytailed girl graphic and "Ponytail" mark.
- **Clean 1980s cases with no stains** — $25–50 vs $10–25 stained (existing ladder) — bright print, no water marks, intact piping.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bright, unfaded printing; working latches and handle; clean interior lining. Fabric cases stain easily and fade.
- Vinyl gets sticky or cracked when stored hot; corners split first.
- Original paper hang tags or store labels add value and help date the piece.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Stains (example shows a dark stain on the right), faded print, split vinyl piping, broken handles, rusty latches, musty smell. No meaningful repairs.
- Vinyl may be cleaned gently; fabric cases rarely come clean. Replacement handles are noticeable and reduce value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked. Some characters were reissued (Barbie retro cases, 2000s) — reissues are newer-looking and marked with later dates.
- Hand-made miniature vinyl copies of Barbie cases exist for doll displays (Fashion Doll Guide comment); they are small and not full-size cases.

## Market
Nostalgia buyers, slow. Plenty of supply.
- Barbie doll-case buyers are a distinct, more active collector group than character-suitcase buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers price 1980s character items as if they were rare. 1950s–60s vinyl cases at yard sales are the undervalued ones.
- Contents: vintage doll clothes and accessories left inside cases are the most common hidden value.

## Shipping / handling
Bulky but light; $15–20 for a 16" case (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Copyright line and year, dimensions, interior photos, and every stain.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the character and licensor exactly as printed, with the copyright year (e.g. "© 1983 Original Appalachian Artworks", Cabbage Patch Kids, Coleco era). Give height × width × depth, the material (vinyl over board or printed fabric), whether latches and handle work, and what the inside looks like. Disclose every stain, fade and split. For Barbie cases, state the year, colour and whether it is a doll case or a child's case.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$17 to buyer − ~$17 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Only a case selling for $110+ can work at a low price. Pass on this type almost always.
