## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast iron skillets, Dutch ovens, griddles, waffle irons and bakeware. Vintage US pieces (c.1880s–1960s) have smooth, milled cooking surfaces; modern Lodge and imports are pebbly/rough. Marks are on the bottom: maker logo, size number (#3–#14; #8 ≈ 10.5" is the common family size), pattern number and sometimes a heat ring (raised ring on the bottom edge). Handle shape and logo style date the piece. Sellers write "cast iron skillet", "old frying pan", "Griswold", "Erie", "#8 skillet" (general knowledge, verify).
- The bottom photo is everything: logo, its position (centre vs 12 o'clock), the words under it ("ERIE", "ERIE, PA., U.S.A.", "SIDNEY -O-"), the size number at 12 o'clock, a pattern/catalogue number with a letter (e.g. "704 R", "1056 E"), and whether there is a heat ring (inset from the edge or at the very edge) or a smooth bottom.
- Size numbers refer to wood-stove eye sizes, not inches; a #8 skillet is roughly 10½" across the top rim (general knowledge, verify).
- Look-alikes: modern Lodge (pebbly surface, "LODGE USA" with "Made in USA" and often a logo like a skillet outline), Asian imports (rough, heavy, "Taiwan"/"China" or 3-legged "8 IN" marks), and enamelled cast iron (Le Creuset, Descoware — separate category).

## Makers, brands and variants
(general knowledge, verify, unless sourced)
- **Griswold (Erie, PA, 1865–1957)**: "ERIE" marks (earliest), then slant logo ("Griswold" slanted, with or without ERIE), large block logo (the classic, most sought), small block logo (later, lower value). Size #8 large-logo is the benchmark; #13, #20, Chef/Victor lines, wide-handle griddles and rare sizes go much higher.
  - Trademark dating (Cast Iron Collector): Selden & Griswold 1870–1890 (hollowware); Griswold's Erie diamond logo 1884–1909 (a handful of pieces); "Griswold's Erie" text 1905–1906 (short-lived); **slant logo with "ERIE"** 1906–1929, lightly incised, inset heat ring; **slant logo "ERIE, PA., U.S.A." (slant EPU)** 1906–1929, deeper incising, inset heat ring; **slant logo smooth bottom** (scarce, highly collectible, dating disputed); **large block logo (LBL)** 1920–1940, with "ERIE, PA., U.S.A.", inset heat ring or none — "perhaps the most attractive to collectors"; **small block logo (SBL)** 1939–1957, "ERIE, PA.", no heat ring except #12 and up — common; **medium block / "late large"** 1955–1957, rare; Victor economy line (text-only mark). After the Wagner takeover, Griswold-marked pieces were modified Wagner patterns with simplified marks, generally less desirable; Griswold marks ended after 1973.
- **Wagner (Sidney, OH)**: "Wagner Ware Sidney -O-" stylised logo, sidemark skillets, Magnalite (aluminium – different category). Wagner 1891 reissues are modern.
  - Trademark dating (Cast Iron Collector): block "WAGNER" 1890s; arc "WAGNER" 1891–1910; "SIDNEY, O." under the name 1895–1920 (early pieces show ghosts of Erie marks); "WAGNER WARE" lettering 1920s (brand adopted c.1914); **stylized logo** (big decorative W over "SIDNEY -O-") 1922–1959 — centred on some #4–#8 heat-ring skillets 1924–1935, otherwise at 12 o'clock; **pie logo** 1924–1934 (wedge border, "CAST IRON SKILLET" curved at 6 o'clock, often smooth bottom); after 1959 "SIDNEY -O-" dropped; after 1969 dual logo with General Housewares Corp. and markings flipped 180°. Centred-logo skillets are often "National" dual-logo pieces.
- **Birmingham Stove & Range (BSR)**: often unmarked except size and "Made in USA"; "Century" and "Red Mountain" lines.
- **Favorite Piqua Ware, Wapak (Indian-head logo), Vollrath, Martin, Lodge 3-notch (pre-1960s)**: collector interest; Wapak Indian head is notable.
- **Lodge**: early Lodge pieces have no notch in the heat ring; later one notch at 12 o'clock; then three notches at 9, 12 and 3 o'clock; size number position and a molder's mark at 6 o'clock are typical (Cast Iron Collector forum; exceptions exist).
- Unmarked American (Wagner/Lodge/BSR made unmarked pieces for retailers) – $30–60. BSR and Iron Mountain are the other common domestic unmarked skillets with unbroken inset heat rings (Cast Iron Collector forum).
- **Lodge modern / imports**: $15–30 user market only.

### Quick ID checklist
- Bottom photo: maker logo and wording (ERIE / ERIE PA USA / SIDNEY -O-).
- Griswold logo style: slant, large block, small block (size of the cross-in-circle logo vs the rim).
- Heat ring: inset, at edge, notched (count notches = Lodge), or smooth bottom.
- Size number and pattern number (with letter).
- Cooking surface: smooth/milled (vintage) vs pebbly (modern).
- Sits flat? Ask for a side-profile photo on a flat surface (spinners/rockers are warped).
- Cracks: look from the rim down, especially near the handle; ask for a photo backlit or a "ring" test.
- Pitting or sulfur "orange peel" on the bottom.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Milled smooth cooking surface, thin even walls and light weight for size = vintage American. Rough sand-cast texture, thick walls and heavy weight = modern or import. Heat ring and gate marks (long sprue mark on bottom = pre-1890s) help date (general knowledge, verify). Seasoning condition is cosmetic – rust and crud can be removed, but pitting cannot.
- Incising depth: early slant ERIE marks are lightly incised; slant EPU deeper (Cast Iron Collector).
- Finishes: bare iron; Griswold and Wagner also sold nickel- and chrome-plated pieces (plating loss lowers value) and hammered-finish lines (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Value killers: cracks (often hairline from the rim), warping (pan spins or rocks on a flat surface), deep pitting, sulfur damage (orange-peel texture from coal stoves), welded repairs. Acceptable: surface rust, old crusty seasoning, light wear. Ask: does it sit flat (no spin/wobble), photo of bottom logo, close-up of cooking surface under light, any cracks.
- Heavy grinding or sandblasting of the cooking surface or logo lowers collector value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese reproductions of Griswold/Erie pieces with grainy, rough castings and blurry logos; "Wagner 1891" modern reissues; Taiwan-made unmarked sets. Fake rare items (e.g. Griswold-marked novelty pans) appear on eBay (general knowledge, verify).
- Post-1957 "Griswold" marks on Wagner-made patterns are genuine but late and less desirable — not fakes, but not the classic Erie product (Cast Iron Collector).

## Market
Very liquid on eBay, FB Marketplace and cast-iron collector groups; buyers are cooks and collectors. Griswold/Wagner sell within days at fair prices; Q4 gift season helps (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Logo style and size number (large block vs small block Griswold, slant/ERIE marks), unmarked BSR/Wagner identity, rare sizes. Many sellers over-price modern Lodge as "vintage" (general knowledge, verify).
- Smooth-bottom slant logos and Wagner pie logos (both scarce) titled as plain "Griswold"/"Wagner".
- Lodge notch count (no-notch/1-notch earlier than 3-notch).

## Shipping / handling
Heavy: a #8 skillet ≈ 5–6 lb boxed; Dutch ovens 10–15 lb. Ground shipping $15–25. Cast iron can crack if dropped – pad corners (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, logo type, size number and pattern number, diameter, whether it sits flat (no spin/wobble), cleaned/seasoned or as-found, any cracks or pitting. Photograph bottom logo, cooking surface, side profile on a flat surface.

### What to tell the buyer
Name maker, logo type and its date range ("Griswold large block logo, used c.1920–1940, marked ERIE, PA., U.S.A., #8, pattern 704"), plus heat ring/smooth bottom. Give top-rim diameter, and state that it sits flat with no spin or wobble (or not), has no cracks, and whether there is pitting. Say whether it is as-found or stripped and re-seasoned, and with what. For Wagner, give the logo (stylized 1922–59, pie 1924–34) and whether "SIDNEY -O-" is present.

## Buy rule
Griswold block/slant logo under $50; unmarked only under $15. Pass on cracked, warped (spinner) or pitted pieces and on modern Lodge. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
Note: with the LBL #8 Terapeak median at $85, a $50 buy (85 × 0.87 − ~$18 − ~$18 − 50 ≈ −$12) does not clear the rule; only rare sizes, smooth-bottom slant logos or pie logos, or much lower prices make money.
