## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Jar lots are nearly always 1930s–70s machine-made: Akro Agate corkscrews/oxbloods, Peltier National Line Rainbos,
Christensen (CAC) swirls, Marble King, Vitro, Master, plus 1970s cat's-eyes (worthless). Handmade German (pre-1920):
visible pontil marks at both poles, latticinio cores, onionskins, sulphides (figure inside) – these are the money.
Shooters (¾"+) bring more. Sellers write "vintage marbles", "jar of marbles", "old marbles". Machine-made marbles have
seams or "cut lines" only where the machine pinched them, no pontils; cat's-eyes have a clear body with a flat
coloured vane inside.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Akro Agate** (1910s–1951): corkscrews, oxbloods, moss agates, "Popeye" corkscrews; boxed sets bring a premium.
- **Peltier Glass** (1920s–): National Line Rainbos, picture marbles with comic-character transfers (Kayo, Moon
  Mullins, Andy Gump etc.) worth $50–150 each.
- **Christensen Agate Co. (CAC)** (1925–33): flame swirls, guineas (speckled) — prized.
- **M.F. Christensen**, **Marble King**, **Vitro Agate**, **Master Marble**, **Alley Agate**, **Champion** — mostly
  common, some rare colour combos (general knowledge, verify).
- **German handmade** (c. 1860–1920): swirls (latticinio, divided core, solid core), onionskins, end-of-day, lutz
  (copper aventurine bands), micas, sulphides (clear with a figure). Size matters a lot.
- **Clay/crockery** (bennington-glazed) marbles: common, low value (general knowledge, verify).
- 1980s+ Chinese/Mexican cat's-eyes and contemporary art marbles (separate market).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Handmade: pontil marks at one or both poles, twisted internal ribbons, slight out-of-round. Machine-made: perfectly
round, seams, no pontils. Original surface (no polish) matters — collectors grade "mint", "near mint", "good"; ground
and polished marbles lose most value (look for a too-glossy surface with no pontil left). Fluorescence under UV
identifies uranium-glass (vaseline) types (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, moons (bruised impact circles), flea bites and heavy surface wear are the norm in jar lots and cut value
sharply on the good ones. Polishing/re-grinding is the main "repair" and kills collector value. Photos through a jar
can't show condition or pontils — ask for close-ups of individual marbles.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern contemporary handmade marbles and Chinese reproductions of German swirls/sulphides exist; reproduction
sulphides often have crude figures and bubbles; new Akro-colour repros exist (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Active niche collector base (marble shows, eBay, Facebook groups, Morphy auctions for top pieces). Mixed jars sell
steadily but slowly at modest prices; sorted lots of identified makers sell better. Decorators buy jars for display
(general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Any handmade (pontil) marble hiding in a jar; Peltier comic marbles (Kayo, Moon Mullins) worth $50–150 each. Photos
through a jar can't show this – pass unless a clear close-up exists.

## Shipping / handling
Quart jar ~4 lb; three jars 12 lb glass – $25–30 and breakage risk. Resell by sorted lot on eBay; ship marbles out of
the glass jars in padded flat-rate boxes.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker (Akro Agate, Peltier, CAC) + type (corkscrew, oxblood, onionskin, sulphide, lutz) + "handmade"/"German" + "shooter" + size in inches/mm + count; jars: "lot of 200 vintage machine-made marbles". Junk: "marble lot", "old marbles jar", "rare" without a type. Example: `Antique German Handmade Onionskin Marble 1 Inch Shooter Pontils Mica Vintage`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers, sorted by maker/type (identified lots sell better than jars); German sulphides/lutz to Morphy auctions; decorators buy mixed jars locally.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (mixed machine-made $30–70/quart, Akro corkscrews $5–20 each, Peltier comics $50–150, German handmade $30–300 each); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) the marble on white, good light, pontils/pattern showing; then the opposite side, pontil close-ups, any chips/moons, calipers for diameter; lots in a sorted grid with a ruler.
- **State:** maker/type, diameter, count, condition honestly (chips, moons, flakes, polished), handmade pontils present, sorted by maker, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** out of the glass jars; marbles in zip bags inside a padded flat-rate box, 2–5 lb, $10–15; single handmade in a small padded box.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A [German onionskin / Peltier Kayo comic] with [both pontils / the transfer] in photo [2], [1 inch], no chips — cat's-eyes are the $10-a-pound marbles; this type sells for [$X] each."

## Buy rule
Buy when: ≤ $15/quart of pre-1970 machine-made, or any lot with visible pontil/sulphide marbles ≤ $50. Pass when:
cat's-eyes dominate, or $30+/quart. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a
passenger seat.
