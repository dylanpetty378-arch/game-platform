## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Polyurethane foam toys. Parker Brothers era: "NERF" in rounded bubble letters, "Parker Brothers" on boxes, foam pocked texture. Example: 1983 Nerf Boomerang, blue three-arm foam with yellow tip caps, "NERF" logo embossed center, with original instruction sheet, no box.

Two collecting families:
- **Foam balls and sports toys (1970–1980s)** — the 1970 Nerf ball (sold as the "world's first official indoor ball"), Super Nerf Ball, 1972 Nerfoop indoor basketball and the Nerf football, later the boomerang (1983) and Turbo football. Sold in boxes or blister cards marked Parker Brothers with a catalogue number (the original Nerf ball box is Parker Brothers No. 0096, per a BlasterCentral reader).
- **Blasters (1989 onward)** — 1989 Blast-a-Ball (a two-player ball-blaster game set, invented by Reyn Guyer), 1990 Blast-a-Matic, 1991 Bow 'n' Arrow, 1992 Sharpshooter (the first dart blaster), Master Blaster, Missilestorm, Missile Launcher, NB-1 Missile Blaster, Slingshot (all 1992), 1993 ZapSnaps and Arrowstorm, then the mid/late-1990s Ballzooka, Crossbow, Manta Ray, Big Bad Bow and Koosh Vortex lines.

How to date from a photo: maker name and copyright line on the box or moulded into plastic parts (Parker Brothers → Kenner → Hasbro/OddzOn/Larami), colour scheme (1990s neon yellow/orange/blue), and the logo style. Look-alikes: generic foam balls and boomerangs (no NERF mark), Koosh balls (OddzOn), and Hasbro retro reissues.

## Makers, brands and variants
Parker Brothers (1969 Nerf ball, Nerfoop, Super Nerf, Turbo football, Boomerang 1983, Blast-a-Ball 1989, Arrowstorm, Bow 'n' Arrow 1990). Kenner (1991–), Larami (Super Soaker), Hasbro (2000s+).

Corrected timeline (Wikipedia, BlasterCentral, Nerf Wiki):
- **1969–70 Parker Brothers** develops Nerf from Reyn Guyer and Fred Cox's foam-ball game; the ball launches in 1970, followed by Super Nerf Ball; Nerfoop and the Nerf football in 1972.
- **Kenner** took over the line (Kenner Parker Toys); **Tonka bought Kenner in 1987**; **Hasbro bought Tonka in 1991**, bringing Nerf into Hasbro, served through the 1990s by the OddzOn and Larami subsidiaries before full Hasbro control. So 1990s blasters are Kenner/Larami/OddzOn boxed, not Parker Brothers.
- **Blast-a-Ball** 1989 (Parker Brothers box, per Nerf Wiki); **Blast-a-Matic** 1990; **Bow 'n' Arrow 1991** (not 1990); **Sharpshooter 1992** (invented by Clemens V. Hedeen Jr., sold to Kenner); **Arrowstorm 1993**.
- **Larami** bought the Super Soaker in 1989; Hasbro bought Larami in 1995.

### Quick ID checklist
- Box or card says "Parker Brothers" = 1970s–80s foam era; "Kenner" = late-1980s–1990s; "Hasbro" with a modern logo = 2000s+ or a reissue.
- Is it a blaster on the NerfCenter grail list (Arrowstorm, Ballzooka, Crossbow, Manta Ray, Big Bad Bow, Blast-a-Matic, Koosh Vortex)?
- Complete? Ballzooka and Arrowstorm lose value fast without magazines, clips or shields.
- Box state: sealed (factory tape/shrink intact) vs opened complete vs loose.
- Foam: crisp and springy, or yellowed/crumbling? Original darts and balls are usually crumbling by now.
- Plastic colour: heavy sun-yellowing means attic storage and brittle internals.
- Pump blasters (SuperMAXX line, Crossbow): ask whether it fires and holds pressure.
- Look for "Retro" or a recent copyright date on the box (reissue).

### Golden-egg tells
- Sealed grail-tier 1990s blaster (Arrowstorm 1993, Ballzooka 1994, Crossbow 1995, Koosh Vortex FireStorm 1999) — ~10×+ original MSRP (i.e. roughly $200–300 on a $20–30 MSRP) vs 1–3× loose (NerfCenter) — intact factory seal, Kenner/Larami/OddzOn box graphics.
- Complete-in-box early blaster (Blast-a-Ball 1989, Blast-a-Matic 1990, Bow 'n' Arrow 1991, Sharpshooter 1992) — 3–8× MSRP vs 1–3× loose (NerfCenter) — all accessories and inserts in the photo, box flaps intact.
- Boxed original 1970 Parker Brothers Nerf ball (No. 0096) or Super Nerf — boxed/sealed Parker Brothers tier $40–150 vs $10–35 loose foam (general knowledge, verify) — early Parker Brothers box art; ball inside not crumbled.
- Green Nerf Boomerang — sold $39 and $45 vs the $25 median for the 1983 boomerang query (Terapeak) — green foam rather than the common blue.
- Blaster with all magazines/clips/shields (Ballzooka, Arrowstorm) — complete commands the CIB band; missing parts drop it to loose (NerfCenter) — count the accessories in the photo against the box back.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original foam yellows/crumbles with age; boxes printed with Parker Brothers logo/dates.

- Polyurethane foam oxidises: original darts/balls go yellow, hard and powdery; replacement micro darts fit most classic blasters (NerfCenter), so "with darts" often means modern darts.
- Blaster plastics: 1990s neon ABS; sun exposure yellows white and pale parts and makes them brittle.
- Air-pressure blasters rely on bladders and seals that perish; a blaster that does not hold pressure is a display piece.
- Old vs new: copyright line and maker name on the box and moulded into the plastic; modern Hasbro retro packaging carries current Hasbro logos and recent dates.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foam rot (crumbling), tears, chewed edges, faded colors.

- Blasters: missing magazines, clips, shields; perished seals; cracked pump handles; brittle yellowed shells.
- Boxes: crushed corners, torn flaps, price stickers; box condition is most of the CIB premium.
- Anything priced under its original MSRP likely does not work or will not survive shipping (NerfCenter).
- Ask: does it fire, does it hold pressure, are all parts present, any smoke/pet exposure, does the foam crumble when pressed?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Hasbro retro reissues (2010s–2020s "Nerf Retro" boomerang) — check date/box. Hasbro Pulse has sold retro reissues of early blasters (e.g. a listing titled "Nerf Sharp92 Retro Blaster", a nod to the 1992 Sharpshooter); reissues carry modern Hasbro branding and recent copyright dates. Modern Hasbro Nerf sports items (e.g. Turbo Screamer footballs, 2000s) are often titled "vintage".

## Market
Nostalgia steady; loose slow. Blaster collectors (NerfCenter/BlasterCentral community) pay for complete, working, boxed 1990s blasters; Gen-X nostalgia buyers want boxed 1970s–80s Parker Brothers foam. Q4 gift demand.

## What sellers usually miss
Boxed early blasters in toy lots. Also: which blasters are grail-tier (Arrowstorm, Ballzooka, Crossbow, Manta Ray, Big Bad Bow, Koosh Vortex), the green boomerang colour, and whether accessories are complete.

## Shipping / handling
Light, bulky; $8–12. Box blasters in a rigid outer carton; boxed items need corner protection.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Year, completeness, foam condition, box.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the exact name, year and maker as printed on the box (e.g. "Nerf Bow 'n' Arrow, 1991, Kenner"), and state whether it is sealed, complete in an opened box, or loose. List every accessory present against the box back, say whether a blaster has been test-fired and holds pressure, and describe the foam honestly (original foam on toys this age is usually yellowed or crumbling; replacement darts are noted as such). Mention sun-yellowing and box wear, and state that it is an original, not a Hasbro retro reissue. Photograph the copyright line and all sides of the box.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). Loose pieces never qualify. Sealed or complete-in-box grail-tier blasters at ≤ 1/3 of expected resale only.
