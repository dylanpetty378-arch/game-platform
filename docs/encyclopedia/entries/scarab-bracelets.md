## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A link bracelet with 6–8 oval carved "scarab" stones in different colours, set in prong cups. Stones: carnelian, tiger eye, dyed green/blue chalcedony, onyx, aventurine. Mid-century costume jewelry sold by the million (Krementz, Van Dell, Winard, Curtis, Hong Kong).

Marks on the clasp or links:
- "1/20 12K GF": gold-filled, most of them
- "14K": solid gold, the money ones
- "925/sterling"
- nothing: gold-tone base metal

Sellers write "vintage scarab bracelet", "Egyptian revival". Real Victorian faience or hardstone scarabs set in 14K/18K are a different, rare thing.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Krementz, Van Dell, Winard, Curtis**: US gold-filled makers. Marked pieces with genuine stones are $30–60. Krementz is generally the best regarded of these (general knowledge, verify).
- **Hong Kong / unmarked**: gold-tone base metal, glass or plastic scarabs, $10–25.
- **Sterling (925)**: a middle option. Value is modest and driven by silver weight and stones (general knowledge, verify).
- **14K solid gold scarab bracelets**: 8–15 g, $300–900, with metal value driving the price.
- **Antique Egyptian-revival** pieces with genuine faience scarabs in 18K, signed: $1,000+.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Read the clasp tongue and the back of the links for marks.
- Gold-filled wears to brass at the edges and clasp. Solid gold shows no base-metal wear-through.
- Carved hardstones are cold, heavy and have natural variation. Glass or plastic scarabs show mould seams and bubbles and feel warm and light (general knowledge, verify).
- Total weight includes the stones, so gold weight is less than the bracelet weight.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing or replaced stones, loose prongs, a worn-through gold-filled finish and a weak clasp are common. A replaced stone in a mismatched colour or cut lowers value. Ask for photos of the clasp mark, the back of the links and every stone.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake "14K" stamps on gold-plated pieces exist. Test with acid or XRF before reselling as solid gold (general knowledge, verify). Modern Egyptian tourist-market scarab bracelets are common and low value.

## Market
Gold-filled pieces are a high-volume, slow, low-value vintage-jewelry market. 14K pieces sell quickly because of melt value and track the spot gold price. The Egyptian-revival look gets periodic fashion bumps (general knowledge, verify). Holiday gifting gives a mild Q4 lift.

## What sellers usually miss
The karat mark on the clasp tongue. A "vintage scarab bracelet $40" is only interesting if the photo shows "14K", not "1/20 12K GF".

- **State:** marks exactly, length, weight in grams, stone types and count, prong and clasp condition, missing/replaced stones by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** pocket, letter weight, padded mailer $5.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a marked [Winard 12K gold filled] scarab bracelet with [7] genuine [hardstone] scarabs, [7 in], 1960s; marked gold-filled examples sell for $[30–60], and the gold-tone Hong Kong copies are $10–25."

## Buy rule
