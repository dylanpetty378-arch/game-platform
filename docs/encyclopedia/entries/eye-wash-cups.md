## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- A small (2–3" tall) cup with an oval rim shaped to fit the eye socket, usually on a short stem and round foot. Glass (clear, cobalt, green, amber, milk, uranium), ceramic, silver, aluminium, rubber/vulcanite and later plastic.
- **Forms**: pedestal/stemmed (classic); "potbelly" or "squat" with no stem; cups that screw or cap onto a solution bottle (Wyeth, Lavoptik "bottle and cup" sets); double-ended; tulip, panelled, ribbed or barrel shapes.
- **Marks**: embossed on the base or bowl — "WYETH", "LAVOPTIK", "OPTREX", "JOHN BULL" with patent date (1917), "GLASCO PAT", "MURINE", numbers. Ceramic eye baths may carry a chemist's/retailer's name; silver carries hallmarks.
- Hand-blown cups have a rough or ground **pontil mark** under the foot, uneven walls and bubbles; moulded cups have side seams.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **John Wyeth & Brother (Philadelphia)** — cobalt cups and cobalt "Collyrium" bottles, early 1900s–1940s; very common.
- **Lavoptik Co. (Chicago)** — cobalt and white milk glass cups, sold boxed with an eye-lotion bottle (1920s–50s).
- **Optrex (UK, 1930s on)** — cobalt "OPTREX" cups, later blue and white plastic; extremely common in the UK.
- **John Bull** — US cups embossed "JOHN BULL" with a 1917 patent; emerald green and cobalt; a step above plain cobalt.
- **Glasco** — "potbelly" clear glass cups with design patent number; common.
- **Murine, Eye-Gene, Ocusol and other proprietary-remedy brands** — small cups sold with the product; boxed examples add interest.
- **Victorian/Edwardian English** — hand-blown pedestal cups in cobalt, green, amber and amethyst; ceramic transfer-printed eye baths (blue-and-white or floral); sterling silver eye baths hallmarked Birmingham/London/Chester c.1890–1930.

### Quick ID checklist
1. Material: glass / ceramic / silver / plastic.
2. Colour: clear and cobalt common; amber, uranium (glows green under UV), milk, purple scarcer.
3. Pontil mark under the foot (hand-blown = older) vs mould seams.
4. Embossed brand or patent date; hallmarks on silver.
5. With original bottle or box?
6. Chips on the eye rim (the commonest flaw).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Sterling silver eye baths (English hallmarks)** — ~10–30× a cobalt cup ($80–250, estimate) — grey-white metal with lion passant/anchor/leopard hallmarks under the foot or on the stem; often gilt-washed inside.
- **Victorian ceramic transfer-printed eye baths** — ~10–20× ($100–300, estimate) — pottery cup with blue-and-white or floral transfer pattern, sometimes a chemist's name.
- **Hand-blown pedestal cups in amber, amethyst or large sizes** — ~5–10× ($60–150, estimate; Peoria Magazine notes amber is scarce; an SCA hand-blown cup was listed at $149.99) — pontil scar, uneven bubbles, tall stem, colour other than cobalt/clear.
- **Uranium (vaseline) glass cups** — ~3–5× ($30–60, estimate) — yellow-green glass; a UV-torch photo makes it glow bright green.
- **Boxed Lavoptik/Wyeth/Murine sets with bottle, cup and paper box** — ~3–4× a loose cup ($30–70, estimate) — printed cardboard box, label on the bottle, matching cup.
- **John Bull embossed 1917 cups (emerald green)** — ~2–3× a Wyeth cobalt ($20–40, estimate) — "JOHN BULL" and a 1917 patent date embossed, bright green glass.
- **19th-century rubber/vulcanite cups** — ~8× (one listed at $160, Peoria Magazine 2013) — black or dark brown hard rubber, moulded maker name.
- No premium: clear Glasco potbelly, plain cobalt Wyeth/Optrex, plastic and aluminium cups — $1–15.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old glass: pontil marks, bubbles, slightly wavy walls, worn base. Newer moulded glass: crisp side seams, uniform thickness, sharp embossing. Silver: hallmarks, real weight, no rust. Plastic = after 1950s.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips and flea bites (the rim is the most exposed edge), cracks, stain/cloudiness from solutions ("sick glass"), ground-down rims to hide chips. Silver: dents, worn gilding, monograms removed. Ask for a close-up of the rim and the base.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern hand-made or Chinese decorative "apothecary" eye cups in cobalt, amber and green exist, sold as antique — very crisp, sometimes with fake embossing, no wear on the foot. Carnival-glass iridescent cups are mostly modern (1970s+) novelties.

## Market
Small niche: medical/pharmacy and bottle collectors, optometrists' offices, and a large UK base (London is the collecting centre). Many sellers, low prices; single cups sell slowly. eBay and bottle shows. No seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
Hallmarks on silver eye baths (often in "silver plate" lots), uranium glass (needs a UV light), pontil marks on hand-blown cups, and that boxed sets are worth more than the cup alone. Equally, dealers price ordinary cobalt cups at $15–30 each, which is retail — the "$1" craigslist titles are usually a dealer placeholder.

## Shipping / handling
4–8 oz each; a single cup ships for $6–8 in a small box, a lot of 10 for $12–15. Wrap each in bubble wrap, rims protected.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "antique eye wash cup"/"eye bath" + colour/material (cobalt / amber / uranium / sterling silver / ceramic) + maker embossing (Wyeth / Lavoptik / John Bull) + "hand blown pontil" + "hallmarked" + "boxed". Junk: "medical oddity", "apothecary decor", "rare" for cobalt. `Antique Hand Blown Amber Glass Eye Wash Cup Pontil Pedestal Victorian`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (bottle and pharmacy collectors, large UK base); singles sell slowly — list lots of 10+ together. No local premium.
- **Price position:** at the sold median ($8–25 cobalt; $20–60 amber/uranium/boxed; $80–300 silver/ceramic), accept −15%, floor 75%; OBO; cut 15% after 45 days; lots firm.
- **Photos:** cup on white; rim close up; base (pontil or mould seams); embossing/hallmarks; UV-torch shot for uranium; box and bottle if a set; lot laid out with count; next to a ruler.
- **State:** material, colour, height, maker/embossing, pontil or mould seams, rim chips/flea bites, UV glow yes/no, hallmarks transcribed, boxed set contents, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** pocket, 4–8 oz; single cup bubble-wrapped in a small box $6–8, lot of 10 $12–15, rims protected, no cup touching another.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Hand-blown amber / hallmarked sterling / Victorian ceramic] eye bath, [era], [pontil/hallmarks] as photographed; the ordinary cobalt Wyeth cups sell for [$] and [this type] brings [$]."

## Buy rule
Buy when: a lot of 15+ old cups at ≤ $2 each, a hallmarked sterling or Victorian ceramic eye bath ≤ $25, or a boxed set ≤ $10, and profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$8 to buyer − ~$8 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Pass when: single cobalt or clear cups, dealer-priced cups ($15+ each), modern carnival or decorative repro cups. Fits a passenger seat easily.
