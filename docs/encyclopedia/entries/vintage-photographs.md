## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Daguerreotype** (1839–1860): mirror-like silver plate in a hinged case; image flips negative/positive with angle.
- **Ambrotype** (1854–1865): glass negative backed with black, in a case.
- **Tintype** (1856–1920s): dark iron plate, often in paper sleeve.
- **CDV** (1860s–1880s, 2.5×4 in) and **cabinet card** (1866–1910s, 4.25×6.5 in) on studio-printed card mounts.
- **RPPC** (1903–1950s): real photographs on postcard stock; check stamp box (AZO, Velox, Cyko, EKC) to date.
- **Panoramic group photos** (c. 1905–1950s): long prints 8–10 in tall × 20–60 in wide, made with a Cirkut rotating camera; captions in white along the bottom (organization, date, photographer). Church congregations, schools, conventions, military units, company picnics.

## Makers, brands and variants
Photographer studio names matter for cabinet cards (Mathew Brady, Napoleon Sarony, regional western studios). Panorama photographers: Eugene O. Goldbeck (San Antonio, military), Fred Schutz, Panoramic Photo Co., local studios. Subject is more important than maker.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original silver gelatin has rich tones, a slight silvering at dark edges with age, and paper texture; modern inkjet copies show dot patterns, flat blacks, bright white paper. Panoramas: original photographer's caption written on the negative (white handwriting) and often a blind stamp.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foxing, water stains, tears, creases, fading, silvering, insect damage, mat burn, fly marks in frames. Panoramas often trimmed or framed with acidic backing. Glass may be cracked. Dags: tarnish halos, "wiped" images — never clean.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Inkjet/laser copies of famous images; reprinted panoramas from LOC files; "Old West" fakes and invented captions; fake Civil War tintypes. Check paper, tones, and mount.

## Market
Huge supply. Subject-led sales; church panoramas sell mainly to local church members or genealogy buyers, slowly. Military panoramas and sports sell faster.

## What sellers usually miss
Identified subjects on the back, studio stamps of famous photographers, occupational content (tools, trades), Black and Native American subjects, early street scenes with signage.

## Shipping / handling
Prints: rigid mailer, $5–8. Framed panorama 36–40 in long × 12 in: long box, glass removed or taped, $25–35 and passenger-seat borderline.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Transcribe the caption fully (church name, city, pastor, date, photographer), give dimensions, note framing and condition; tag the city and denomination for searchers.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Framed panoramas cost ~$30 each way to ship, killing all but rare subjects. Only buy subject-driven images at resale ≥ $150 for ≤ $35 (prints) or ≤ $25 (framed panoramas). No identified-celebrity/Civil War images needing authentication.
