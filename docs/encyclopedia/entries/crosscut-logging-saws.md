## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Long (4–8 ft) thick steel blades with large teeth, often with rakers (deep-gulleted teeth) between cutter groups; wooden D or straight handles at each end (two-man) or a pistol grip plus auxiliary handle (one-man).
- **Felling saws:** narrow, flexible, concave back (used under a tree). **Bucking saws:** wide, heavy, straight back.
- Tooth patterns: plain/peg tooth (oldest), lance tooth, champion, perforated lance, great American; rakers identify later better saws.
- **One-person saws** are asymmetrical, 3 to 4½ ft, with a D handle and holes at tip and butt for a supplementary handle; **two-person saws** are symmetrical (cut on the pull in either direction), vintage lengths 4–7 ft in 6" steps (WTA). Only one-person saws can pass the size rule.
- Tooth patterns in a photo (WTA): **Plain/peg** — cutters only, no rakers; **M tooth** — paired teeth forming an "M"; **Great American** — three teeth per gullet; **Champion** — two set cutters then an unset raker; **Lance** — four cutters then a raker (soft green western timber); **Perforated lance** — lance teeth with holes/bridges in the gullets (general utility).
- Handles: **loop style** (metal loop clamps the blade; the blade has a notch and teeth stop short of the end) vs **pin style** through handle holes (Climax-style, Pacific Coast) (WTA).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Simonds (Fitchburg MA), Disston (Philadelphia), E.C. Atkins (Indianapolis), Warren, Pennsylvania Saw, Diamond Saw Works, Peerless:** acid-etched logos on the blade near the handle (often rusted off).
- Handles: Disston/Simonds "Klondike" style, Atkins logos on ferrules.
- **Taper-grind trade names = the quality tell:** the best vintage grind is the **crescent taper** (thinnest at the back centre), sold as **"crescent ground" (Simonds)**, **"improved ground" (Disston)** and **"segment ground" (Atkins)** (WTA). These words in an etch mark a top-grade saw. All saws made today are flat ground (WTA).
- Atkins is also known for "Silver Steel" in its etches; Disston pre-1920 saws are the most collected (Appraisily, general).
- Other makers seen: Keen Kutter and Shapleigh (hardware house brands), Bishop (West Coast), Michigan Saw Co. (Appraisily). Penn Saw Corp (York PA) — common, no premium (field note below).
- The model names in the old summary (Simonds No. 325, Atkins Perfection) were not confirmed this run — verify before quoting.

### Quick ID checklist
- Measure the length: over ~4 ft = fails the size rule; stop.
- Back shape: concave (felling) or straight (bucking).
- Tooth pattern from a close-up: rakers present (champion, lance, perforated lance) vs plain.
- Etch near the handle end: maker, and any "crescent ground", "improved ground" or "segment ground" wording.
- Straightness: photo sighting down the blade; shiny high spots next to dull low spots indicate kinks (WTA).
- Teeth run all the way to the ends (vintage) vs stop short (modern or loop-handle notch) (WTA).
- Handles: original wood, tight on the blade; loop vs pin style.

### Golden-egg tells
- Crescent-taper saw with legible "crescent ground" / "improved ground" / "segment ground" etch — top user tier ($200–500) vs $25–80 decor — readable etch wording near the handle; blade visibly thinner toward the back.
- Straight, kink-free vintage bucking saw with full teeth and tight original handles — $80–250 vs $25–80 rusty — clean blade, even tooth line, both handles.
- Painted folk-art saw (landscape scene) — $50–200 on the decor market depending on the painting — painting on the blade; judge the art, not the saw.
- One-person crosscut (3–4½ ft) with legible maker etch — the only size-legal tier; modest ($30–80, general) — D handle plus auxiliary-handle hole.
- New premium saws (Lynx, Pax, Tuatahi, $250–600 new) — support user prices but are not vintage — no patina, modern etch.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Crisp etch, straight blade, uniform set, no kinks, good taper-ground blade (thinner at back) = user value. Pitting and kinks = decor only.
- Painted scenes (folk art) on saws: decorative market $50–200 depending on art quality.
- Vintage saws could be flexed for carrying and spring back; modern softer saws hold a bend — vintage steel is regarded as better (WTA).
- Dating (Appraisily, general): hand-filed irregular teeth and simple handles suggest pre-1900; most collectible saws are 1880–1920; production declined after the 1950s with chainsaws.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rust/pitting, kinks, broken teeth, missing handles, cracked handle wood; sharpening is a specialist repair.
- Kinks must be hammered out by an experienced filer; rust and pitch can be cleaned (WTA). Handles must not move against the blade.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- None significant; Crosscut saws are still made new (Lynx, Pax, Tuatahi) at $250–600 new, which supports user prices.

## Market
- Mostly local (Facebook, Craigslist) due to length; eBay shipping freight-like. Users (trail crews, firewood) buy good saws. Trail crews prize vintage crescent-ground bucking saws over new flat-ground ones (WTA).

## What sellers usually miss
- Lens "median $1,000" is noise (painted saws or new saws); sellers overprice rusty saws.
- The grind wording in the etch (crescent/improved/segment ground) — the real user-value tell.

## Shipping / handling
- 6–7 ft length: oversized; pipe or custom box, $40–100+; blades are dangerous to pack.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Length, tooth pattern, maker etch, straightness photo, handle condition.

### What to tell the buyer
Give length tip to tip, one- or two-person, felling (curved back) or bucking (straight back), and the tooth pattern by name (champion, lance, perforated lance, plain). Quote the etch exactly, including any grind wording ("crescent ground"). State whether the blade is straight with no kinks (show a sight-down photo), whether teeth are complete and recently sharpened, rust level, and handle type and fit. Say "pickup only" for two-person saws.

## Buy rule
- Size rule fails for two-man saws (5 ft+). Pass. One-man saws (3–4 ft) only with clear etch at ≤ $30.
