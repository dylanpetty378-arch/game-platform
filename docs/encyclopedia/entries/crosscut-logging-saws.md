## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Long (4–8 ft) thick steel blades with large teeth, often with rakers (deep-gulleted teeth) between cutter groups; wooden D or straight handles at each end (two-man) or a pistol grip plus auxiliary handle (one-man).
- **Felling saws:** narrow, flexible, concave back (used under a tree). **Bucking saws:** wide, heavy, straight back.
- Tooth patterns: plain/peg tooth (oldest), lance tooth, champion, perforated lance, great American; rakers identify later better saws.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Simonds (Fitchburg MA), Disston (Philadelphia), E.C. Atkins (Indianapolis), Warren, Pennsylvania Saw, Diamond Saw Works, Peerless:** acid-etched logos on the blade near the handle (often rusted off).
- Handles: Disston/Simonds "Klondike" style, Atkins logos on ferrules.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Crisp etch, straight blade, uniform set, no kinks, good taper-ground blade (thinner at back) = user value. Pitting and kinks = decor only.
- Painted scenes (folk art) on saws: decorative market $50–200 depending on art quality.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rust/pitting, kinks, broken teeth, missing handles, cracked handle wood; sharpening is a specialist repair.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- None significant; Crosscut saws are still made new (Lynx, Pax, Tuatahi) at $250–600 new, which supports user prices.

## Market
- Mostly local (Facebook, Craigslist) due to length; eBay shipping freight-like. Users (trail crews, firewood) buy good saws.

## What sellers usually miss
- Lens "median $1,000" is noise (painted saws or new saws); sellers overprice rusty saws.

## Shipping / handling
- 6–7 ft length: oversized; pipe or custom box, $40–100+; blades are dangerous to pack.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Length, tooth pattern, maker etch, straightness photo, handle condition.

## Buy rule
- Size rule fails for two-man saws (5 ft+). Pass. One-man saws (3–4 ft) only with clear etch at ≤ $30.
