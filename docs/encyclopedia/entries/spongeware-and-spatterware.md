## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Spongeware: utilitarian stoneware or yellowware, ~1880–1930s, decorated by dabbing cobalt (blue), brown or green colour with a sponge onto a white/cream body, then glazed. Forms: mixing bowls, pitchers, butter crocks, bean pots, wash bowl & pitcher sets, chamber pots, spittoons, plates. Blue band variants ("spongeband"). Spatterware: earlier English pearlware/soft-paste (Staffordshire, ~1810–1860) made for the US market with dense spattered borders (blue, red, green, purple, yellow) and a hand-painted centre motif (peafowl, schoolhouse, tulip, thistle, rose, fort); "stick spatter" (cut-sponge stamps) from ~1840–1900 is cheaper.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Red Wing (MN)**: Saffron Ware (yellowware with brown/blue sponge) 1930s, spongeband bowls, Gray Line; marked or with ink stamp/raised "Red Wing".
- **Western Stoneware (Monmouth IL)**, **Robinson Clay Products / Robinson Ransbottom (Roseville OH)**, **Brush/McCoy**, Ohio potteries (Uhrichsville, Crooksville, Zanesville) – mostly unmarked.
- **Robinson Ransbottom (RRPCo) 1980s–2005** made new sponge-decorated crocks/bowls – marked "RRPCo Roseville Ohio" – lower value.
- Modern makers: Pfaltzgraff/blue-sponge dinnerware (1980s–90s), Emerson Creek, Bennington Potters, "Henn" (Ohio), Polish Bolesławiec (stamped, not true spongeware) – all low value.
- **Spatterware**: English, unmarked (some impressed "Adams" for 1890s Adams reissues – those are later).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old spongeware: thick, heavy stoneware or yellowware, glaze crazing, foot ring worn and grey/brown with grit, sponging irregular and sometimes heavy, slight warping, kiln flaws, deep cobalt with softer edges.
- New: lighter, perfectly uniform sponging, bright clean white, unglazed foot that's clean/even, stamped marks, dishwasher-safe or "lead free" labels.
- Spatterware: soft-paste/pearlware (bluish glaze pooling), hand-painted centres, flakes at rims; modern spatter repros are often on heavier ironstone with transferred motifs.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips, hairlines (tap test, light test), interior staining, glaze flakes. Repairs common on spatterware (check UV). A hairline cuts spongeware value ~40–60%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Lots of reproduction "antique spongeware" since the 1970s–80s country décor boom; some artificially stained. Spatterware has British/US reproductions with fake wear. Rule: if it looks perfect, it's new.

## Market
Country/primitive décor market softened since 2010s but early spongeware still sells to collectors; slow (1–2 months). Spatterware is an auction category.

## What sellers usually miss
- Any "blue and white bowl" in a kitchen lot could be old spongeware; owners undervalue.
- Conversely, sellers often call 1980s repros "antique".
- Spatterware cups/plates sold as "old painted dish" for a few dollars.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy (bowls 2–5 lb, pitchers 3–6 lb), fragile. Double-box, $15–25 for pitchers. Factor shipping in.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "spongeware" (or "spatterware") + colour ("blue and white") + form (mixing bowl, pitcher, crock) + maker if marked (Red Wing Saffron) + size + "antique"/"stoneware". Junk: "farmhouse decor", "Pfaltzgraff" unless it is, "country blue". Example: `Antique Blue and White Spongeware Stoneware Pitcher 9 Inch Ohio Pottery`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (collectors, slow 1–2 months); FB local for heavy bowls; figural English spatterware goes to auction.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (small bowls/plates $30–70, large bowls and Saffron $60–150, pitchers/chamber sets $120–400); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 45 days.
- **Photos:** 1) front, sponge pattern full; then base (marks, wear, crazing), interior, rim all round, held to light for hairlines, any chips, ruler for diameter/height.
- **State:** form, size, maker if marked, age evidence (base wear, crazing), every chip and hairline by photo number (light test), "antique" only when confident, reproduction era stated if 1980s RRPCo.
- **Pack / haul:** bowls 2–5 lb, pitchers 3–6 lb, fragile; bubble, double-box with 2" foam, 5-lb class, $15–25.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Old [Ohio/Red Wing] spongeware, not the 1980s Pfaltzgraff or RRPCo copies — base wear and crazing in photo [2]; [pitchers] of this size sell for [$X]; no chips or hairlines under light."
