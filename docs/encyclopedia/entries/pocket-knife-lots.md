## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Case: "CASE XX" shield in the handle, tang stamp with dots (1970–2000) – jigged bone, red/blue bone bring the most; Case "American flag" shield knives are 2000s factory issues ($40–60). Buck: 110 folding hunter with brass bolsters (1964+, $30–60), 898 Impact (auto, black handle, $100–130 used). Boker: tree-brand shield, "Boker USA" stockman ($25–45). Craftsman/Camillus camp knives $15–30. Marttiini: Finnish birch-handle fixed blade ($25–50). Sellers write "vintage pocket knives", "Case knife lot", "Buck knife".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **W.R. Case & Sons**: tang stamps date the knife — "CASE XX" (1940–64), "CASE XX USA" (1965–69), dot system from 1970 (general knowledge for the pre-1970 stamp dates, verify); jigged bone, red bone and blue bone handles bring the most; pre-1940 "Case Tested XX" stag/bone top tier; 2000s flag-shield and factory issues $40–60.
- **Buck**: 110 Folding Hunter (1964+, $30–60; first/second variations collectible $80–200), 112 Ranger, 898 Impact auto (S30V, retail ~$180, used $100–130).
- **Boker**: tree-brand shield; Boker USA stockman $25–45; Solingen-made older pieces may be worth more (general knowledge, verify).
- **Schrade**: Schrade USA Old Timer (decent), "Uncle Henry" USA boxed (collectible); post-2004 Schrade is Chinese-made (Taylor Brands) and low (general knowledge, verify).
- **Camillus / Craftsman**: camp knives $15–30; Camillus TL-29 electrician's knives (military) — a Lens median of $77 asking was seen (general knowledge: genuine WWII TL-29s by several makers are collectible, verify).
- **Remington** bullet knives (pre-1940): top tier.

- **Marttiini** (Finland): birch-handle fixed blades $25–50.
- **Junk tier**: Imperial, Colonial, Frontier, Pakistan/China knives, worn Camillus.

### Golden-egg tells
- Case "TESTED XX" pre-1940 knives, mint — $500–5,000+ vs $50–200 for a standard 1970s–80s Trapper (~10–25×) — tang stamp reads "CASE TESTED XX" with no "U.S.A." and no dots; the stamp must be crisp, not buffed (https://appraisily.com/old-case-xx-pocket-knife-value/)
- Case 551 stag lockback c.1920–39 with basket-weave tooled sheath — Heritage Arms & Armor Dec 2020 (price gated, verify) vs $25–60 decent tier — 6.5" closed, nickel-silver bolsters, bark-textured stag, and a "CASE / TESTED XX" snap on the sheath (https://historical.ha.com/itm/edged-weapons/knives/vintage-and-rare-case-xx-551-stag-folding-knife-with-sheath/a/6229-40606.s)
- Case genuine stag pre-1970 (pattern number x5xx on the tang reverse) — $200–1,500 vs $50–200 jigged-bone 1970s (~3–8×) — natural antler scales with bark texture and irregular colour, not the uniform "molded stag" of later years (https://appraisily.com/old-case-xx-pocket-knife-value/)
- Case Melon Tester — $400–1,200 vs $25–60 (~10–20×) — a single long, thin blade on a slim handle about 5.5" closed; sellers call it a "long skinny knife" (https://appraisily.com/old-case-xx-pocket-knife-value/)
- Case "CASE XX" stamp with no "U.S.A." (1940–64) — $150–800 vs $50–200 dot-era (~3×) — read the tang: "CASE XX" alone = 1940–64, "CASE XX U.S.A." = 1965–69, dots under U.S.A. = 1970 on (https://appraisily.com/old-case-xx-pocket-knife-value/)
- Buck 110 early variations (2-pin scales 1964–67; 5-pin transitional, ~1,000 made; "inverted USA" stamp) — $80–200 vs $30–60 common 110 (~3×, entry text) — count the scale pins in the photo (two large pins = early; four scale pins plus rocker pin = transitional) and check the tang stamp orientation (https://www.allaboutpocketknives.com/knife_forum/viewtopic.php?p=1114796)
- Remington UMC bullet knives, original 1922–40 — $300–2,000+ vs $25–60 (~10–30×, entry text) — a bullet-shaped shield inlaid in the handle with the circle "REMINGTON UMC" tang stamp; 1982-on reissues carry the year on the tang and are the common ones (https://www.allaboutpocketknives.com/knife_forum/viewtopic.php?p=1249870)
- Case Cheetah push-button lockback — collector premium over the $25–60 common tier (band not given, verify) — a swing-guard lockback with a push-button release in the bolster; it is an automatic, so eBay-prohibited: kill it regardless of price (https://appraisily.com/old-case-xx-pocket-knife-value/ ; entry text)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Carbon steel (patina) vs stainless; jigged bone, stag, Delrin, pick bone handles; nickel silver or brass bolsters; tang stamps on the main blade. Collectors pay for: original edge (not sharpened down), crisp tang stamp, tight "walk and talk" (snappy spring, no blade play), no cracks in bone. USA-made stamps vs import stamps.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Over-sharpened blades ("sharpened down" profile), broken tips, rust pitting, cracked or replaced handle scales, weak springs, blade wobble, buffed/polished surfaces erasing tang stamps. Cleaning with abrasives lowers collector value (general knowledge, verify). Ask for tang stamp close-ups and photos of each blade open.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Case and Remington have counterfeit and "reproduction" bullet knives; Remington reissued bullet knives in the 1980s–90s as collector issues (general knowledge, verify). Chinese fakes of Buck 110 and Case exist. Re-handled knives sold as original. Check tang stamps against dating guides.

## Market
Huge, active eBay knife category; buyers are collectors (Case, Buck, Schrade, Remington), users and flippers (general knowledge, verify). Named singles sell quickly; mixed lots attract resellers. Father's Day and Christmas help (general knowledge, verify). eBay's knife policies and state laws restrict automatics.

## What sellers usually miss
Tang stamps (dots/dating) on Case; first-variation Buck 110s (no brass bolster rivets visible, 1964–67 — verify this tell against a Buck variation guide). Big 40-piece lots are mostly junk tier – value is in the 3–4 named pieces.

## Shipping / handling
Light – $8–12 per knife. Automatic knives (Buck 898) can't ship to several states; sell locally or via dealers.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, pattern/model number, tang stamp (photo) and dating, handle material, blade condition (original edge or sharpened), walk-and-talk, closed length, box/papers. Photograph each blade open, tang stamps, both handle sides, shield. Title keywords: "Vintage Case XX USA 1965-69 Jigged Bone Trapper 6254", "Buck 110 First Variation".

## Buy rule
Buy when: lot ≤ $8/knife with visible Case XX bone or Buck 110 pieces, or Case XX 1960s–70s singles ≤ $25. Pass when: seller names Case/Buck and prices at $25+/knife; auto knives (shipping rules). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (it does).
