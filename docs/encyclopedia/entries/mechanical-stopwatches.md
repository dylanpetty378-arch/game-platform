## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A pocket-watch-sized (45–60 mm) chrome, nickel, steel or silver case, crown at 12 with the start/stop/reset worked through the crown (single button) or a crown plus a side pusher (two-button), a big sweep seconds hand and a small register (15, 30 or 60 minutes). Dials are graduated in 1/5 s, 1/10 s, 1/100 s, decimal minutes (1/100 min, industrial time study) or with tachymeter/pulsometer/telemeter scales; regatta/yacht timers have coloured 5- or 10-minute countdown segments. Many Minerva and Heuer timers carry "PATENT" or "Swiss" on the dial.
A **split-seconds/rattrapante** has two stacked sweep hands (often different tips) and an extra pusher at 2 o'clock or a pusher on the crown; this is the single biggest value multiplier in photos.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Minerva** (Villeret, 1858–2006, now Montblanc) — huge production of single-button sports timers, chrome 49 mm, white dial with "Minerva" and arrow logo, 30-min register, "Patent" — the commonest Craigslist find; also split-seconds and 1/100 timers.
- **Heuer / Heuer-Leonidas** (St-Imier/Bienne) — "Heuer" shield on dial; Trackmaster, Microsplit (later quartz), single-button and split-seconds stopwatches, military and Olympic timers; Leonidas-branded timers 1964 on.
- **Hanhart** (Germany) — red pusher; "Felix" sports timers, military timers for the Bundeswehr.
- **Gallet / Clebar** (Swiss-American), **Meylan**, **Excelsior Park**, **Lemania**, **Breitling** (Sprint/sports timers), **Junghans**, **Smiths** (UK, broad-arrow military), **Longines** (13.33Z split-second stopwatches, high grade).
- **US: Elgin, Waltham, Hamilton, Galco, Jaeger-LeCoultre US contract** — WWII and Navy "BuShips"/"US Navy" timers.
- **Low end**: Camero 7-jewel Swiss (triage $20–35), Japanese Seiko/Citizen sport timers (Seiko mid), Chinese Shanghai/Russian Agat, Hanhart/Junghans pin-lever, Westclox/Ingersoll.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Split-seconds (rattrapante) by Heuer, Minerva, Longines, Excelsior Park, Gallet** — ~$400–1,500+ vs $30–80 for a common Minerva (10–20×; dealer asking higher) — two sweep hands stacked, extra pusher at 2 o'clock or a button set into the crown.
- **Heuer-signed timers (Trackmaster, single-button, 1/100 sec)** — $120–300 vs $40 (3–5×, verify) — "Heuer" shield logo on dial; Leonidas after 1964.
- **Military / government-marked** (Hanhart Bundeswehr, Smiths broad arrow, "US Navy"/"BuShips" Elgin/Waltham/Galco, Heuer military) — $150–400 (verify) — caseback engravings, broad arrow, NSN or contract numbers.
- **Silver-cased (sterling/0.800/0.935) antique chronograph stopwatches 1890–1925** — $150–500 (verify) — hallmarked silver case, enamel dial, often split-seconds or with time-of-day.
- **Regatta/yacht countdown timers** (Heuer, Minerva, Hanhart) — $150–400 (verify) — coloured 5/10-minute segments, "regatta" on dial.
- **1/100 second or 1/50 second high-beat timers** — $100–250 (verify) — finely graduated dial with 3 s or 6 s per revolution.
- **Original box and papers** — +20–50% — boxed Minerva/Heuer with paperwork.
- **Kill: "7 jewels" no-name, Camero, Japanese pin-lever, painted dials, non-running** — $10–35.

### Quick ID checklist
- Brand on dial and caseback; read it (Heuer shield, Minerva arrow, Hanhart, Longines winged hourglass).
- Count hands: two sweep hands = split-seconds.
- Count pushers and their position.
- Dial scale (1/5, 1/10, 1/100, decimal, regatta).
- Case metal: silver hallmarks vs chrome (brassing through plating).
- Caseback engravings: military, broad arrow, contract number.
- Video: start, stop, reset to zero; split hand catches up on release.
- Crystal intact; hands straight.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Jewelled Swiss lever movements (7–17 jewels, column wheel in better pieces) vs pin-lever. Chrome-plated brass cases brass through at the edges; nickel and stainless hold up; silver cases tarnish and carry hallmarks. Enamel or lacquered dials; luminous dots on military timers (radium on older ones — handle with care).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Non-running, sticky reset, hands not returning to zero, broken crown stem, missing crystal, dial stains, plating wear. A service runs $80–200 (split-seconds more) — no repairs: buy only with a working video.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Low at the common level. Heuer redials and franken parts exist on higher-value timers; some Heuer "military" timers are civilian timers with engraved backs. Split-second Heuers at $1,000+ are authentication-sensitive.

## Market
Plenty of supply of common Minervas and coach timers; they sell slowly at $30–80. Heuer and split-seconds attract watch collectors and sell in days to weeks. Motorsport, sailing and military collectors pay for specialised timers.

## What sellers usually miss
The second sweep hand (split-seconds); the military marks on the caseback; silver hallmarks inside an antique case; Heuer timers mixed into "coach stopwatch" drawer lots. Sellers overrate ordinary Minervas ("Swiss! works!").

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size; padded box $5–6, insured for higher grades. Stop the movement and protect the crystal.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, dial scale, register, pushers, case metal, jewel count if visible, function on video (start/stop/reset, split), condition of plating and crystal, not serviced unless documented.

### What to tell the buyer
"Swiss mechanical stopwatch by [brand], [single-button / split-seconds], 1/[x] second dial with [n]-minute register, [chrome/silver] case [mm]. Starts, stops and resets to zero (see video). Not recently serviced; timekeeping accuracy not certified."

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$6 to buyer − ~$6 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A common Minerva at even $80 sold nets $57.60 before price, so it cannot clear the $50 floor at any realistic price — pass (only a free/≤ $5 lot add-on). Heuer at ~$200 → buy ≤ $80. Split-seconds at ~$600 → buy ≤ $200. Require a working video. Heuer rattrapantes over ~$1,000 need watch-specialist authentication — treat as pass unless far under value and clearly original. Pocket-sized.
