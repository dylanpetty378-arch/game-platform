## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Flat die-cut shapes (solid "tracers" you draw around) or sheets with cut-outs (true stencils) in bright polystyrene or vinyl: red, yellow, blue, green primaries. 1950s–60s kids' tracers show molded interior detail lines (eyes, fur, saddles) so the child can press through; they often have a hanging hole. Drafting templates are thin transparent green/orange plastic sheets with geometric or architectural symbols, edge-printed with maker and number.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Westland Plastics / similar US injection molders (1950s–60s)**: solid primary-colour animal and nursery tracers sold in boxed sets or on cards. Usually unmarked on the piece.
- **Hong Kong / Japan imports (1960s–70s)**: thinner, slightly translucent polystyrene, "Made in Hong Kong" molded on some pieces; cheaper detail.
- **Licensed sets**: Walt Disney Productions (Mickey, Bambi, Dumbo), Peanuts (United Feature Syndicate), Hanna-Barbera, Sesame Street (1970s). Copyright line molded on piece = the value tell.
- **Colorforms / Spirograph (Kenner, Denys Fisher)** — related but separate collectible; Spirograph complete boxed sets do better ($25–60).
- **Drafting templates**: Rapidesign (Berol), Pickett, Timely, Staedtler, Alvin. Common, low value except complete sets in cases.
- **Metal lettering stencils** (brass interlocking, Reese's/Stencil Ease, Ideal) — different market, priced per set.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old polystyrene is rigid, glossy, slightly brittle, colours saturated; edges sometimes have mold flash. Newer (1980s+) pieces are softer PVC/PP, flexible, matte. Molded copyright text with a date is the only reliable dating tool. Original boxed sets with lithographed lids are worth much more than the pieces.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken legs/tails/ears on thin projections (very common), warping from heat, crayon/marker residue, sun fading. Missing pieces from a set. No meaningful repairs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not a reproduction target; modern dollar-store stencils exist but are flat sheets, not molded figural tracers.

## Market
Slow, low-dollar; maybe 50–150 eBay sales/month across all stencil/tracer listings, most under $25. Sells in 2–8 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
That the box is most of the value; that a molded © Walt Disney line moves a lot from $15 to $50+.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, light (under 1 lb), ships in a padded mailer for $5–7. Low transit risk except thin legs.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State count, list each shape, photograph molded marks, note any broken tips, mention era as "1960s style" unless marked.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship − ship − price. With medians of $15–45, profit cannot reach $50. Pass every loose lot. Only exception: boxed licensed set listed ≤ $10 where comps show ≥ $90 — rare. Not a category to hunt.
