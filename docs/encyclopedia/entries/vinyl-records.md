Summary: Ordinary 1965-85 classic-rock LPs sell $5-25 each, $13-33 as small lots. The money is a handful of specific pressings of common titles: Led Zeppelin II "RL/SS" Robert Ludwig cut $150-500 (VG-VG+) to $1,000-1,800 (NM, Presswell), Beatles "Yesterday and Today" butcher cover $2,000-20,000 (second/third state) to $125,000+ (sealed first state), original 1990s punk/hardcore/screamo LPs (Gravity, Dischord, Ebullition, Vermiform first presses) $60-300, mono first pressings of 1960s titles 2-10× the stereo.
Buy rule (short): Never buy a record lot on cover photos; buy single titles only when the seller confirms the dead-wax/label tell (RL etch, butcher image, original label/catalog number) and the price leaves ≥ max($50, price) after Media Mail both ways.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
12-inch 33⅓ rpm LP in a cardboard jacket, usually with a paper or poly inner sleeve. Sellers photograph the front covers in a grid or stack; almost none photograph the label or the dead wax (the blank band between the last groove and the label), and that is where the value lives. The identifying data on any LP: (1) label design and colour (e.g. Atlantic red/green "1841 Broadway" vs later "75 Rockefeller Plaza"; Capitol black rainbow-rim 1962-68 vs lime green 1969-71 vs orange 1972-78; Columbia "6-eye" 1955-62 vs "2-eye" 1962-70; Blue Note "47 West 63rd" deep groove); (2) catalog number on spine and label (SD 8236, ST 2553, T 2553 = mono); (3) the matrix/stamper string hand-etched or stamped in the dead wax, including mastering-engineer initials (RL = Robert Ludwig, SS = Sterling Sound, "PR" = Presswell plant, "MO" = Monarch, "CTH" = Columbia Terre Haute, "W" = Winchester, "PRC" = Richmond, "STERLING", "MASTERDISK", "Wally", "Kendun"); (4) a MONO or STEREO banner on the cover, price-code or "Printed in USA" box; (5) shrink wrap with hype stickers; (6) inserts, posters, inner sleeves. Look-alikes: 1970s-80s re-pressings use the same cover art with a later label and a different catalog prefix; 2000s-20s reissues are 180 g, have a barcode (barcodes start ~1979-80) and often "Remastered" or "RSD" stickers; a record with a barcode is never a 1960s-70s original.

## Makers, brands and variants
There is no "maker" in the usual sense; the variables are label, pressing plant, mastering engineer and sleeve state. The titles below are the ones that recur in ordinary Craigslist lots and where a specific pressing multiplies the price.

**Led Zeppelin II (Atlantic SD 8236, 1969).** The first run was cut by Robert Ludwig at Sterling Sound at very high level ("hot mix"); Atlantic recut it within weeks after cheap turntables skipped on "Whole Lotta Love". The RL cut is identified only by "RL" and "SS" (and sometimes "ST-A-691671-B" etc.) etched in the dead wax of BOTH sides. Common copies: $8-20 (We Buy Records USA). RL copies: $100-300 played, $500+ clean (same source); eBay/popsike sold: $1,500 EX/VG Presswell copy July 2022, with other Presswell NM- listings at $1,000-1,800. Presswell ("PR" in dead wax) RL copies are the most prized; Monarch ("MO") and Terre Haute ("CTH") RL copies also exist and sell for less than Presswell but far above a non-RL. A single-side RL (RL on one side only) is worth roughly a third of a double RL.

**The Beatles — Yesterday and Today (Capitol T/ST 2553, June 1966) "butcher cover".** First state = never pasted over (rare, mostly promo); second state = the "trunk" cover slick pasted over the butcher image (visible as a faint triangle of Ringo's black V-neck below the trunk's right edge and slightly raised/thicker board); third state = a peeled second state. Record sales: $125,000 (Heritage, sealed first-state stereo), $334,000 (John Lennon's own copy, RR Auction 2024-25 era). Ordinary second-state copies: stereo $5,000-15,000, mono $2,000-8,000; third-state (peeled) $1,000-4,000 depending on how well it was peeled (RR Auction lots; estimates for the lower bands). Any Yesterday and Today with the trunk cover and a 1966 rainbow-rim label is worth a $5 flashlight test: tilt the cover in raking light and look for the V-neck ghost.

**Beatles US originals generally.** Capitol rainbow-rim labels ("Capitol" around the rim in white letters on black) are 1962-68 originals; mono (T- prefix) copies of Meet the Beatles, Rubber Soul, Revolver and Sgt Pepper (MAS 2653) sell 2-5× the stereo. Vee-Jay "Introducing the Beatles" (1964) originals with the "Ad back"/"Blank back" and "VJ brackets" labels $300-3,000 but the most counterfeited Beatles LP in existence (see Fakes). UA "A Hard Day's Night" mono (UAL 3366) $20-60 worn, $100-200 clean. Apple-label 1969-75 pressings of Sgt Pepper/Hey Jude/Let It Be: $10-30 unless sealed. White Album with low serial numbers (A0000001-A0010000) $500-5,000+; A00xxxxx seven-digit numbered copies $40-150.

**1990s punk/hardcore/emo/screamo originals.** Gravity Records (San Diego, 1991-): Heroin s/t 7" was Gravity #1 (1991); the Heroin LP (1993, "skeleton on a bicycle" cover) and Antioch Arrow, Clikatat Ikatowi, Angel Hair, Mohinder originals are collected hard; originals were pressed in the low thousands and the band's whole output was reissued as the Southern Lord "Discography 1990-1993" 2LP (2023, $35 new, RSD colour variants). Estimated original-LP sold range $60-200 (no Discogs fetch this session — treat as estimate; a sealed or NM original higher). Tells: original Gravity LPs have a hand-numbered or plain black-and-white sleeve, no barcode, "GRAVITY" Golden Hill/San Diego address; the 2023 reissue is a double LP in a gatefold "tip-on" jacket with a zine. Similar money: Dischord early (Minor Threat "Out of Step" first press with "Dischord 10" and no "Flex Your Head" era barcode; Rites of Spring), Ebullition, Vermiform (Born Against), Lookout! first presses (Operation Ivy "Energy" first 1989 press with "Lookout 10" and the bright green cover), Epitaph 1988-91 originals (Bad Religion "Suffer" with the 1988 label), Social Distortion "Live at the Roxy" (Time Bomb 1998, LP only pressed once in the US; sold estimate $60-150; 2010s-20s reissues exist with barcodes/"Craft" branding).

**Other common-lot titles with a valuable version (tells in parentheses).** Pink Floyd DSOTM (Harvest SMAS 11163 with solid blue triangle, two posters and two stickers, $60-150; most copies missing the stickers $20-40). Nirvana "Nevermind" (DGC 1991 US original with "DGC-24425" and no barcode on the back? — has barcode; the tell is the Sub Pop "Bleach" first 1,000 on white vinyl, $1,500+; black "Bleach" with "Sub Pop 34" and the 1989 address $100-200). Metallica "Kill 'Em All" Megaforce 1983 (MRI 069, $150-400; Elektra 1984+ $20-40). Black Sabbath debut Warner WS 1871 green label 1970 ($100-250). David Bowie "The Man Who Sold the World" Mercury SR 61325 cartoon cover 1970 ($300-800). Rolling Stones "Sticky Fingers" with working zipper and Alan Klein credits ($40-100). Miles Davis "Kind of Blue" Columbia 6-eye (CL 1355 mono / CS 8163 stereo, $150-500). Blue Note 1500/4000 series with "47 W 63rd"/"NEW YORK USA" labels, deep groove, ear/RVG stamp in dead wax ($200-2,000; Liberty 1966+ copies $30-100). Bob Dylan "Freewheelin'" with the four withdrawn tracks ($10,000+, mono 6-eye only — check the track list on the label). Elvis RCA "Long Play" black label dog-on-top 1950s ($100-500). Velvet Underground & Nico Verve with unpeeled banana and "torso" back cover ($300-800 stereo first). Hendrix "Electric Ladyland" Reprise 2-tone "W7" steamboat label 1968 ($80-200).

**Private press / regional (e.g. JR Cadillac "This Time", Seattle 1973).** Local bar bands: $10-40 unless psych/funk/soul, where 1970s private-press LPs run $200-2,000 — the tell is a hand-made or amateur cover with no label logo, a small-town address, and a Google search of the band name returning Discogs "wants" in the hundreds.

### Quick ID checklist
1. Catalog number on the spine/label — write it down; it dates the pressing family (SD 8236 vs later "SD 19127" reissue of LZ II).
2. Label design and address (Atlantic "1841 Broadway" 1969-73; Capitol rainbow rim; Columbia 6-eye).
3. Dead-wax etchings on BOTH sides: engineer initials (RL, SS, Wally, RVG), plant codes (PR, MO, CTH), take numbers (-1A/-1B).
4. Barcode on the back = 1979 or later, never an original of a 1960s-70s title.
5. Cover details: mono/stereo banner, "trunk" cover ghost, posters/stickers/inserts present, shrink and hype stickers.
6. Vinyl weight/colour: 180 g and coloured vinyl = modern reissue unless a documented original colour (Bleach white).
7. Condition: visual grade of vinyl under light (scuffs, scratches, warps), seam splits, ring wear, writing on cover.

### Golden-egg tells
- Led Zeppelin II with "RL" + "SS" etched in both dead-waxes (Presswell "PR" best) — $150-500 VG-VG+, $1,000-1,800 NM vs $8-20 for the common cut — ask the seller to tilt the record under a lamp and read the scratched letters near the label; cover is identical.
- Beatles "Yesterday and Today" trunk cover with the V-neck ghost showing through (second state) — $2,000-15,000 vs $20-50 for a 1969+ Apple/later Capitol copy — raking-light photo of the lower-right trunk area; Capitol rainbow-rim label and "T 2553"/"ST 2553" number.
- Beatles White Album with serial number below A0100000 (first weeks of 1968) — $150-500, under A0010000 $1,000-5,000 vs $30-60 for 1970s numbered copies — the stamped number sits on the lower-right front cover; look for "A" followed by seven digits and a top-opening gatefold with four photos and poster.
- Vee-Jay "Introducing the Beatles" with brackets "VJ" label, "Ad back" or "blank back" cover — $300-3,000 vs ~$0 for the counterfeits that make up ~80% of copies — originals have a printed (not glossy stamped) label, sharp cover printing, and the "Love Me Do"/"P.S. I Love You" or "Please Please Me"/"Ask Me Why" track variants; any copy with "Stereo" in a box is suspect.
- Gravity Records / Ebullition / Vermiform / early Dischord 1990-94 first-press hardcore LPs (Heroin, Antioch Arrow, Born Against, Rites of Spring) — $60-300 vs $25-35 for the 2010s-20s reissues — original has no barcode, single LP, plain black/white printed sleeve, label address on the back; reissues are gatefold/2LP/coloured with a barcode.
- Nirvana "Bleach" Sub Pop 34 first press, white vinyl (first 1,000) — $1,500-3,000; black first press with 1989 "Sub Pop" Seattle address and no barcode $100-200 vs $20 for the 2009 Deluxe reissue — look for white vinyl, hand-numbered cover, no barcode.
- Metallica "Kill 'Em All" Megaforce MRI 069 (1983) — $150-400 vs $20-40 Elektra — red Megaforce label with the "Megaforce Records" logo and no Elektra "E" on the spine.
- Pink Floyd DSOTM Harvest SMAS 11163 with solid-blue prism triangle, both posters and both stickers — $60-150 vs $15-30 incomplete — ask the seller to photograph the inserts; the solid-triangle label (no gradient) is the 1973 first.
- Any 1960s Capitol/Columbia title with a MONO catalog prefix (T-, CL, MAS) and "MONO" banner — 2-5× the stereo for Beatles, Beach Boys, Dylan 1963-67 — mono number on the spine and label, "High Fidelity" rather than "Stereo" at top.
- Blue Note 1500/4000-series jazz with "47 W 63rd St" or "NEW YORK USA" label, deep groove around the label, "RVG" and "ear" stamp in the dead wax — $200-2,000 vs $30-100 for the 1966+ Liberty "Division of Liberty Records" label — the label text at the bottom is the tell.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Vinyl weight: 1960s pressings are often 140-160 g and feel stiffer; mid-1970s oil-crisis pressings are thin (100-120 g) and flexible, with recycled "noisy" vinyl (RCA Dynaflex is a known thin pressing). Modern reissues are 180 g, flat, with glossy surfaces. Labels: originals are printed on thin paper glued flat with sharp type; counterfeits have fuzzy type and off-register colour. Covers: 1960s US covers are pasted slicks over cardboard with the slick folded over the edges (visible at the spine), 1970s-80s are printed board; UK/EU "flipback" sleeves (the back slick folded over the front) are 1960s British. Inner sleeves with period ads or label logos help date a record. Dead wax: hand-etched matrix numbers (scratchy, uneven) indicate the lacquer cut; stamped machine numbers come from the plant; initials/signatures of engineers are hand-scribed. Quality of pressing is heard, not seen; what you can see is the run-out spacing (a tight "hot cut" uses more of the disc).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Goldmine grading: M, NM, VG+, VG, G, P. Value falls roughly: NM 100%, VG+ 50-60%, VG 25-35%, G 10% or less. Fatal: warps that affect play, deep scratches that skip, groove wear ("grey" surfaces), mould/mildew, water damage to the cover, split seams, cut-out holes/notches (a drilled hole or cut corner marks a remainder; halves the price on collectible titles), writing/stickers on the cover, ring wear (circular scuff on the cover from the disc). Acceptable: light sleeve scuffs, minor ring wear, price stickers on shrink. Repairs: taped seams (visible as yellowed tape), replaced inner sleeves, "cleaned" records — cleaning helps, nothing else does. Ask: play-graded or visual-graded; any skips; is the cover a seam-split; is shrink original.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeits exist for Vee-Jay Introducing the Beatles (the most common fake LP), the butcher cover (printed "butcher" covers sold as novelties, and faked third states with a photo pasted on), Led Zeppelin bootlegs, Nirvana Bleach white vinyl, and almost every $500+ title. Tells: glossy or blurry label printing, wrong label fonts, no dead-wax matrix or a typed-looking one, cover printing that pixelates under a loupe, missing fold-overs, light-weight flat vinyl. Reissues: everything above has been reissued (Rhino, MoFi, Analogue Productions, RSD); a barcode, "Remastered", 180 g, or coloured vinyl means reissue — worth $15-40, not the original's price. Discogs lists every variant; match catalog number + label + matrix before paying for a "first pressing" claim.

## Market
Huge and liquid: tens of thousands of LPs sell on eBay US weekly; Discogs is the collector venue with lower fees (~9% + payment) and better buyers for variants. Common LPs: slow, low margin, best sold locally or in genre lots. Variant and golden titles sell in days to a week at fair prices; trophies go to Heritage/RR/Julien's. Buyers: collectors (pressing-specific), audiophiles (RL, Blue Note, MoFi), DJs/hip-hop (funk/soul/breaks), decorators (don't care). Seasonality: Q4 and RSD (April) spikes. Trend: steady; "hot stamper"/first-press premiums rose 2019-23 and have held; punk/screamo originals rising as the 1990s audience hits peak earning years.

## What sellers usually miss
The dead wax. Owners list by cover and artist and never read the etched letters, so RL/SS Zeppelin, RVG Blue Notes and "hot" first cuts sit at $10 in lots. Also: mono vs stereo (they price by title), serial numbers on the White Album, the trunk-cover ghost, missing/present posters and stickers, Sub Pop/Gravity/Dischord address tells, promo copies ("DJ copy"/white label/timing strip), and Japanese pressings with obi strips (2-3× US). Titles say "vintage vinyl", "classic rock lot", "Beatles records" — never the catalog number.

## Shipping / handling
One LP boxed ~1 lb; Media Mail $4.50-6 per LP, 10-LP lot ~12 lb Media Mail ~$12-15. Use proper 12.5" LP mailers with corner protectors; remove the record from the jacket and ship it in the sleeve beside the jacket so the seam does not split. Fragility: low for the vinyl, medium for covers (corner dings, seam splits in transit). Heat warps in summer trucks — avoid leaving in a hot car. Shipping to Dylan: $8-15 for 1-3 LPs, so only single valuable titles pencil.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: artist – title – label + catalog number – pressing tell (RL/SS, mono, 6-eye, first press) – grade (vinyl/cover). State: Goldmine grades for vinyl and cover separately, play-graded or visual; matrix/dead-wax text for both sides transcribed; label description; inserts/posters/stickers/shrink present; any seam splits, ring wear, writing, cut-outs. Photograph: front, back, both labels, both dead waxes (raking light), inserts, spine, any flaws. Keywords: first pressing, original, RL, hot mix, Presswell, mono, rainbow rim, deep groove, RVG, OG press, no barcode.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a single title with a confirmed golden-egg tell (seller sends a dead-wax or label photo, or the tell is visible in the photos — e.g. the trunk ghost) and price ≤ 40% of the conservative sold value for its visible grade, with est. profit ≥ max($50, price) after 13% fees and Media Mail both ways (~$8 each way). Pass when: it is a lot priced per record on cover art alone, any title with a barcode or "Remastered", anything with visible warps/water/mould, trophies needing authentication (butcher covers, Vee-Jay, low-number White Album) unless the price is trivial and the risk is only "worthless common copy", and any seller who will not photograph the dead wax. Never shop by "Beatles" or "Zeppelin": shop by catalog number + matrix. Size is never an issue.
