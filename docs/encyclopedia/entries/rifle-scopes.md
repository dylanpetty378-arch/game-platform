## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Telescopic sights for rifles. Old target scopes are long (16–24") thin steel/brass tubes with external adjustment mounts — Unertl, Lyman, Fecker, Litschert. American hunting scopes of the 1940s–60s (Weaver K4 with El Paso marking, Lyman Alaskan, Redfield Bear Cub) have steel tubes and screw-on turret caps. Tasco/Bushnell/Simmons 1970s–90s: glossy black aluminium, "Japan" or "China", rubber caps — low.
- Tube diameter dates a scope: pre-war and 1940s hunting scopes are ¾" (Weaver 330) or ⅞" (Lyman Alaskan, Noske, Leupold Plainsman); post-war scopes moved to 1" as steel gave way to aluminium (Rifle magazine). Leupold's first 1" scope was the 1954 Mountaineer (Rifle magazine).
- Pre-war scopes often have no front bell (objective the same diameter as the tube) and need adjustable mounts, because adjustments were in the mount not the scope (Rifle magazine).
- Marks sit on the tube or the turret saddle: maker, city ("El Paso, Tex.", "Portland, Ore.", "Denver"), model, serial. Military scopes carry a roll-stamped model number, serial and government part number on the tube (milsurps.com).
- Title words: "vintage scope", "steel tube", "Weaver K4", "El Paso", "Lyman Alaskan", "Unertl", "target scope", "Bear Cub", "Balvar".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Unertl (Pittsburgh)**: target and USMC sniper scopes; hunting models Falcon 2.5x, Hawk 4x, Condor 6x, "Gallery" sights; the 1956 Ultra Varmint target/varmint scope (Rifle magazine).
- **Lyman**: Alaskan 2.5x (1939, ⅞"), Challenger 4x (1949), Wolverine 6x/8x/10x, All-American 2.5x–10x (1954, 1"), Perma-Center reticles from 1962; Targetspot target scopes (Rifle magazine). The Alaskan was the base of military M73/M81/M82 scopes for the M1903A4 (milsurps.com).
- **Fecker**, **Stith**: Stith "Bear Cub" (San Antonio) scopes were made by Kollmorgen Optical (Northampton MA); Kollmorgen sold under its own label from 1956 and Redfield bought Kollmorgen's scope business in 1959 (Rifle magazine). Most Bear Cub tubes are 26 mm; the 2.5x Bear Cub Deluxe is ⅞" (Rifle magazine).
- **Weaver** (El Paso TX, later Mexico): Model 330 (3x, ¾" steel tube, internal windage/elevation, 1930 price $19), 440 (4x), K-series (K2.5, K4, K6), 60-B K-series; Weaver also made "J.C. Higgins" scopes for Sears; the El Paso factory closed in 1984 (Rifle magazine), so "El Paso, Tex." marked scopes predate that. A K4 cost $45 new in 1962 (Rifle magazine).
- **Redfield** (Denver); **Leupold & Stevens** (Portland): Plainsman 2.5x (1947, ⅞" alloy tube), Pioneers 2.5x/4x/8x, Riflescope 4x (1953), Mountaineer 4x (1954, first 1" Leupold), Westerner 8x, M7 3x/4x (by 1960), Vari-X 3-9x (1961, $89.50 new in 1962); older gold-ring Vari-X (Rifle magazine).
- **Noske** (Rudolph Noske, San Carlos CA): ⅞" 2.5x and 4x by the mid-1930s; credited with the first internally adjustable hunting scope (Rifle magazine).
- **Bausch & Lomb** (Rochester NY): Baltur 2.5x, Balfor 4x, Balvar 4/8/24, Balsix, Baleight; early ones need B&L adjustable mounts; windage/elevation dials only from 1968 (Rifle magazine).
- **Bushnell** (Banner, Scopechief, made in Japan), **Tasco** (Japan, 1952–), **Simmons**.
- **European**: Zeiss (Zielvier 4x, Zeilklein 2.75x, 1-4x and 1-6x variables by the mid-1920s), Hensoldt (Wetzlar), Kahles, Pecar, Nickel. Stoeger imported Zeiss/Hensoldt (Rifle magazine).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the tube: maker, city, model. "El Paso, Tex." on a Weaver = pre-1984 US-made; "Mexico" = later.
- Tube diameter (¾", ⅞", 1", 26 mm) and whether there is a front bell: no bell + ⅞" suggests 1930s–50s.
- Length: 16–24" thin tube with external mount blocks = target scope (Unertl, Lyman, Fecker).
- Turrets: on the scope (internal adjustment) or in the mount (external, older target type).
- Any government markings: model (M73, M81, M82, M84), serial and part number roll-stamped on the tube = military (authentication needed).
- Photo through the eyepiece: clear, no haze, reticle straight and intact.
- Mounts/rings included and their maker (Redfield, Weaver, Unertl, B&L).
- Glossy black aluminium + "Japan"/"China" + rubber caps = Tasco/Simmons/Bushnell entry tier.

### Golden-egg tells
- USMC Unertl or military Lyman (M73/M81/M82) and M84 — ~10–100x the Tasco tier; $500–5,000+ — roll-stamped model, serial and GI part number on the tube; drawing-numbered turret caps on genuine military Lymans (milsurps.com). Reproductions exist (e.g. a Malcolm "8X Gen II" Unertl reproduction and an M73G4 reproduction of the Weaver 330C are advertised; milsurps.com) — pass per Dylan's rule.
- Unertl/Lyman/Fecker target scopes — ~5–20x; $150–500 — long thin tube, external mount blocks, maker engraving.
- Lyman Alaskan / Challenger, commercial — mid-collector, $100–500 with mounts — ⅞" steel tube, "Lyman Gun Sight Corp, Middlefield, Conn." marking (general knowledge, verify). A restored Alaskan nearly matched a new Swarovski Z5+ at 2.5x in Rifle magazine's test, which drives shooter demand.
- Noske 2.5x/4x and pre-war Zeiss Zielvier/Zeilklein, Hensoldt — collector tier (general knowledge, verify) — ⅞" tube, no front bell, German or San Carlos markings.
- Early Leupold (Plainsman, Pioneer, Mountaineer) and Stith Bear Cub — $60–200+ — ⅞" alloy tube or 26 mm tube, "Leupold & Stevens Portland" or "Stith / Bear Cub" engraving.
- Weaver El Paso K-series with original N-mounts/rings and box — $100–500 band per the summary — "El Paso, Tex." and model code (e.g. K4 60-B) on the tube.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Steel tubes and fine machining, maker engraving, "El Paso" Weaver, Japan-made Bushnell Scopechief are decent; Tasco 4x32 with glossy finish is entry grade.
- Collectors and shooters value internal windage/elevation with erector pivots that keep the reticle centred, nitrogen-purged sealed tubes (less fogging), post-war magnesium-fluoride lens coatings, Plex reticles, Lyman Perma-Center reticles (1962) (Rifle magazine).
- Restoration market: Vintage Gun Scopes (VGS, from 2017) offers refurbished, re-glassed and fully restored scopes; a restored scope is worth more to shooters than an unrestored hazy one (Rifle magazine).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Fogging/haze inside, loose or broken reticle, scratched lenses, frozen turrets, dents. Many are not repairable by owners; specialist restoration exists (VGS).
- Re-glassed or re-blued scopes should be disclosed; collectors of military pieces want original finish.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Military sniper scopes (Unertl USMC, M73/M84, WWII Zeiss/ZF39) are widely reproduced — professional authentication needed (Dylan's rule: pass).
- Advertised reproductions include a Malcolm 8x Unertl copy and an M73G4 copy of the Weaver 330C (milsurps.com). Commercial Lyman Alaskans may be passed off as military (milsurps.com).

## Market
Steady hunting-season demand Sept–Nov; collectors for target/military; a growing shooter market for restored vintage scopes on period rifles.

## What sellers usually miss
- Long target scopes sold as "old telescopes"; Weaver El Paso marks.
- Mounts: original Redfield/Weaver/Unertl mounts and blocks add value and are often left off the title.
- Tube diameter and no-bell pre-war construction.

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb; tube box $8–12. eBay permits scopes (not firearm parts restricted) (general knowledge, verify current policy).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Clarity photo through the lens, reticle type, markings, mount/rings included, tube diameter, length, magnification.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] [model] [power] rifle scope, marked '[exact tube marking, e.g. Weaver K4 El Paso Tex.]', [⅞"/1"] steel tube, [length] in. Optics: [clear / light haze], reticle [type] intact and centred, turrets [move freely]. Includes [rings/mounts/caps/box]. Not restored [or: restored by …]." Give the era with its evidence (tube size, city marking).
