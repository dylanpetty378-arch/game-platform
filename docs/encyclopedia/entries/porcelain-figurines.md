## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glazed or bisque (matte, unglazed) porcelain figures. Photo tells for the cheap tier:
- **Japan/Taiwan/Korea bisque (1950s–80s)**: big-eyed children ("Moppets", Lefton, Enesco, Napco, Fran Mar), pastel colours, soft modelling, octagonal or rocky base, paper label or "Japan" stamp. Usually 4–7".
- **"Occupied Japan" and 1950s Japan white-and-gold figurines**: small (3–5"), glossy white with gold dabs, crude faces.
- **Gerold Porzellan (Bavaria, West Germany, 1950s–80s)**: blue-and-white glossy children, chimney sweeps, goose girls, Hummel-like; marked "Gerold Porzellan Bavaria".
- **Hummel late marks (TMK-5 to TMK-8)**, Goebel animals, Royal Doulton 1970s–90s ladies, Lladró/Nao common glossy pieces.

Tells for the valuable tier:
- **Crisp, lively modelling of a costume or period character** (dancers, Rococo couples, Directoire dandies, Pierrots) with expressive faces, fine hand painting, pastel enamel washes and gilt base bands = German art porcelain c.1900–1935 (Rosenthal Kunstabteilung, Nymphenburg, Schwarzburger Werkstätten, Hutschenreuther Kunstabteilung, Fraureuth, Volkstedt/Ens) or Meissen/Berlin KPM.
- **Underglaze blue marks**: Meissen crossed swords, Nymphenburg impressed shield (with numbers), Rosenthal green crown "Rosenthal Selb Bavaria" with "Kunstabteilung" and an artist signature on the base edge, Hutschenreuther lion with "Kunstabteilung", Royal Copenhagen three waves, Lladró blue bell-flower stamp.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Meissen** (1710–): 18th c. Kändler figures $10,000–180,000+; 19th–20th c. $200–3,500 (estimonia). Crossed swords with lines through = second.
- **Nymphenburg** (Munich): impressed shield mark; 1900s–30s figures by Josef Wackerle and others; strong collector market.
- **Rosenthal Kunstabteilung** (Selb, 1910–1950s): figures by Holzer-Defanti, Liebermann, Caasmann, Karl Himmelstoss, Gustav Oppel; $500–1,500 at auction for dancers (Dorotheum EUR 1,024; Shapiro $1,280).
- **Hutschenreuther Kunstabteilung** (Selb, 1920s–60s): Karl Tutter dancers and figures, $150–600.
- **Schwarzburger Werkstätten, Fraureuth, Volkstedt (Ens, Karl Ens, Aelteste Volkstedter)**: Art Nouveau/Deco figures $100–800; Volkstedt "Dresden lace" ladies $50–300.
- **Hummel (Goebel)**: TMK-1 crown and TMK-2 full bee $150–6,000; TMK-5+ $10–75.
- **Lladró** (Valencia): common retired glazed $50–400; large limited editions $1,500–30,000; Nao is the cheaper sister brand $10–150.
- **Royal Doulton**: HN ladies 1970s–90s $20–100; early/rare HN $300–5,000.
- **Royal Copenhagen / Bing & Grøndahl**: animals, children $40–400.
- **Capodimonte (modern Italian)**: crown-N mark; mostly $20–150 unless signed large groups.
- **Imports**: Lefton, Enesco, Napco, Fran Mar (1970s "Moppets"), Andrea by Sadek, Homco, Gerold: $5–30.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Hard-paste continental porcelain rings when tapped and shows a bright white body at chips; Japanese bisque is chalky and softer.
- Fine work: separately applied fingers, lace, buttons; painted eyelashes; enamel colours varying in thickness; gilding with wear on high points.
- Base: German factory pieces have a flat glazed or unglazed base with an impressed model number and a painter's mark; imports have a hollow base with a felt pad or sticker.
- Age: soft gilding wear, light crazing in glaze pools, dust in crevices; reissues look crisp and bright.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken and reglued fingers, hats, feathers, swords and lace are routine. Check for colour mismatch, glue lines, matte patches (UV light shows restoration). Damage halves value or worse (estimonia). Rubbed or stained base gilding is cosmetic.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Meissen crossed swords is the most copied mark; overglaze or crudely painted swords, heavy bright figures = later copies. Dresden-style and "Capodimonte" sold as Meissen.
- Asian Hummel copies; Nao confused with Lladró.
- Ground-off marks or painted-over bases can hide seconds or other factories.
- Modern reissues of Nymphenburg/Rosenthal models exist (later marks) – value about a third of period examples.

## Market
Mass-market figurines are a falling, oversupplied market: estate sales are full of them and sell-through is low. Signed German art porcelain and early Hummels still sell steadily to collectors (Germany, Austria, US), often best through European auctions or eBay with international shipping.

## What sellers usually miss
- The base mark: "old German statue" or a misnamed subject (a Directoire "Incroyable" called "Napoleon") is how art porcelain shows up cheap.
- Artist signatures incised or painted on the edge of the base (Rosenthal, Hutschenreuther).
- Early Hummel crown/full-bee marks.
- The usual opposite: Lladró and Hummel owners price at retail.

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb; extremely fragile protrusions. Wrap protrusions individually, float in a box within a box, 2–3" padding. $12–20 each way. Lladró boxes help.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the base mark sharply, give height, model number, artist and approximate date. Disclose every repair; buyers check under UV. Name the subject correctly (e.g. "Incroyable / Directoire dandy").

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Japan/Taiwan bisque, Gerold, Moppets, late Hummel, Nao: **pass at any price over ~$5**.
- Ask seller for the base when a figure shows period German modelling (crisp costume, fine face, gilt base band). If the mark is Rosenthal/Nymphenburg/Hutschenreuther Kunstabteilung/Schwarzburger/Meissen with no repairs, buy at ≤ 25% of the sold comp (e.g. ≤ $75 for a $300 figure).
- Repaired or unmarked = pass unless ≤ $15.
- Fits passenger seat: always.
