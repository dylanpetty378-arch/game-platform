## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Soldiers' personal equipment. Tells:
- **Canteens**: US M1910 shape (kidney-shaped, nested cup); aluminium (1910–42, 1944–), stainless steel (1942–45; example 7953218341 – stainless with black plastic cap), enamelled steel (1942–43), plastic (1960s+). Maker and date stamped on the shoulder ("Vollrath 1944", "AGM 1942", "U.S. 1918").
- **Covers**: M1910 khaki/OD canvas with "lift-the-dot" snaps and wool felt lining; maker/date stamped inside the flap ("J.Q.M.D. 1942").
- **Bags**: M1936 musette (field bag) with "M-1936" and maker/date; M1928 haversack; post-war Swiss/Czech/Bulgarian/Romanian canvas shoulder bags (example 7969813347) are unmarked or with foreign stamps.
- **Compasses**: WWII US lensatic (Superior Magneto, Waltham, Jewell, Union Instrument; "Corps of Engineers U.S. Army") $50–150; Vietnam-era Lensatic (Stocker & Yale, Cammenga); Soviet "Andrianov" wrist compasses (black dial with luminous markings, 0–360° bezel, strap; example 7973842797) mass produced 1960s–80s.

## Makers, brands and variants
- US canteens: Vollrath, Aluminum Goods Mfg. (AGM), Landers Frary & Clark (LF&C), Rex, and other WWII contractors (check the shoulder stamp) – stainless 1942–45 $20–45 with cover.
- Mills Equipment Co. (web belts, cartridge belts, WWI–WWII) $30–150.
- USMC: "USMC" stamped canteen covers, P41/P44 packs, Boyt-made leather $50–300.
- Compasses: Superior Magneto, Waltham (WWII), Taylor, Wittnauer, Cammenga (current), Stocker & Yale; British Mk III prismatic (Francis Barker, T.G.Co) $80–250; German WWII Busch/Breithaupt (heavily faked).
- European surplus: Swiss salt-and-pepper bread bags, Czech M60, Bulgarian M36 bread bags $8–25.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Dated stamps, correct period snaps (lift-the-dot by "United-Carr"), canvas (cotton duck) wear consistent with age, and matching dates on canteen/cup/cover. Mint unissued gear brings premiums. Vietnam nylon and modern reproductions look too clean.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, crusty interior, missing caps/chains, torn canvas, mould, missing snaps, broken compass needles or bubbles in liquid-filled capsules, cracked lenses. Mismatched cup/canteen/cover sets.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction WWII US gear (dated stamps applied to new canvas) is common – too-bright khaki, synthetic thread. German WWII gear is the most faked area in militaria and requires expert authentication – outside Dylan's rules. Reissued Soviet compasses still made.

## Market
Large collector market (Veterans Day/Memorial Day bumps). Common surplus is abundant; dated WWII US items sell steadily at modest prices.

## What sellers usually miss
Maker/date stamps on WWII compasses and USMC markings; named items with provenance. Sellers more often over-estimate generic surplus bags and canteens.

## Shipping / handling
Canteens/bags 1–2 lb $10–15; compasses $6–10.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph all markings, maker, date, inside of covers; state original vs reproduction; disclose damage.
