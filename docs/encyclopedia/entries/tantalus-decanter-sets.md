## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A lockable frame holding 2–3 square cut-glass decanters so servants could see but not drink the spirits. A hinged/sliding bar locks over the stoppers. Typical: English, 1880–1920, oak or walnut with brass/silver-plate mounts, base often with a drawer or hidden games compartment. Miniatures (8–10 in) were made for small bottles or as novelty pieces.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **G. Betjemann & Sons (London, patent 1880s)**: "Betjemann's Patent" lock plate; most desired; $300–1,500.
- **Asprey, Mappin & Webb, Walker & Hall, Hukin & Heath**: retailer/maker-marked; $300–1,500.
- **Unmarked English oak/brass**: most common; $150–450 complete.
- **Games compendium tantalus**: base opens to cards, dominoes, cribbage; $600–2,500.
- **Modern** (Italian Mascagni, Godinger, Chinese brass and wood): $40–150.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-cut glass with polished pontil, ground stoppers numbered to bottles, brass with hand-finished screws, worn key. New: pressed glass with mold seams, Phillips screws, bright lacquered brass.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing/replaced decanters (huge value loss), chips on stoppers, stuck stoppers, missing key (keys replaceable), broken lock, cracked wood, replaced felt.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern repro tantaluses; married decanters. Check stopper numbers match bottle numbers.
