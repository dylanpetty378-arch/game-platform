## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A lockable frame holding 2–3 square cut-glass decanters so servants could see but not drink the spirits. A hinged/sliding bar locks over the stoppers. Typical: English, 1880–1920, oak or walnut with brass/silver-plate mounts, base often with a drawer or hidden games compartment. Miniatures (8–10 in) were made for small bottles or as novelty pieces.
- Two construction types (Hemswell): the common **open frame** (bottles visible, bar over the stoppers, carry handle on top or each side) and the **closed box** (a case, often with a mirrored back, that opens to reveal the bottles). Most hold three bottles; one-, two- and four-bottle versions exist.
- Where the lock sits: in the bar over the decanters or in the front rail, with a small keyhole escutcheon.
- The Betjemann mechanism: on documented examples the key is pushed in and turned, then the key barrel is pressed down to free the swing arm, which folds away so the decanters lift out (Mark Goodger; Chairish).
- Woods seen: oak (most common), mahogany, burr walnut, cherry, satinwood, coromandel, amboyna; drawers sometimes inlaid with shell or contrasting wood (Hemswell; 1stdibs titles).
- Size: a full-size 3-bottle oak Betjemann measured 38.5 × 15 × 27 cm (Mark Goodger) and 14.5 × 6 × 12 in (Chairish); a coromandel miniature was 23.5 × 9.5 × 18.5 cm (LAPADA). Both fit a passenger seat.
- Seller words: "liquor caddy", "locking decanter", "whiskey stand", "tantalus", sometimes misspelled ("tantalaus").

## Makers, brands and variants
- **G. Betjemann & Sons (London, patent 1880s)**: "Betjemann's Patent" stamp; most desired; $300–1,500. Hemswell dates the tantalus patent to 1881 and calls a Bramah lock with Betjemann markings, original key and "The Tantalus" inscription the "holy grail". Betjemann pieces were also sold through retailers such as Asprey and Mappin & Webb (Hemswell), and a retailer's plaque (e.g. "Kirby, Beard & Co Ld") can appear on a Betjemann frame (Mark Goodger).
- **Asprey, Mappin & Webb, Walker & Hall, Hukin & Heath**: retailer/maker-marked; $300–1,500. Hukin & Heath (Birmingham silversmiths) made silver-plated Art Deco tantaluses (1stdibs titles).
- **Unmarked English oak/brass**: most common; $150–450 complete.
- **Games compendium tantalus**: base opens to cards, dominoes, cribbage; $600–2,500.
- **Modern** (Italian Mascagni, Godinger, Chinese brass and wood): $40–150.

### Quick ID checklist
- Is there a stamp on the carry handle or locking bar reading "Betjemann's Patent London" plus a number, and "The Tantalus" (sometimes under a crown) on the other end? (Chairish; Mark Goodger; LAPADA.)
- Are all decanters present, the same cut pattern and height, with stoppers that fit and match?
- Is the key present? Does the seller show the bar unlocking?
- Wood: plain oak (common) vs coromandel, amboyna, burr walnut or ebony (better).
- Mounts: brass (common) vs silver plate vs sterling (hallmarks).
- Front drawer or lift-up base? Ask whether it holds games (cards, cribbage, dominoes).
- Screws: slotted, hand-finished and slightly irregular = old; Phillips heads = modern or repaired.
- Decanters: deep, crisp hand cuts and polished bases = period; mold seams and soft "cuts" = pressed or modern.

### Golden-egg tells
- Betjemann's Patent stamped handle/bar with working Bramah lock and key — ~2–3x an unmarked oak set ($300–1,500 vs $150–450) — stamped lettering on the brass handle end; keyhole on the bar.
- Coromandel, amboyna or ebony frame (often Betjemann) — top of the $500–2,500 band (general knowledge, verify) — dark striped (coromandel) or speckled burl (amboyna) wood instead of oak grain.
- Games compendium base — ~2–5x a plain frame ($600–2,500) — a drawer or lifting base; photos showing cribbage board, cards or dominoes.
- Sterling silver mounts or silver decanter collars — top tier (general knowledge, verify) — hallmarks on mounts; silver collars named for spirits (a whisky collar on the LAPADA miniature).
- Closed-box tantalus with mirrored back — above the open-frame average (general knowledge, verify) — a case with a hinged lid and mirror behind the bottles.
- Retailer-marked (Asprey, Mappin & Webb, Walker & Hall, Hukin & Heath) — $300–1,500 vs $150–450 — maker's plaque or stamped mark on the mounts or lock.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-cut glass with polished pontil, ground stoppers numbered to bottles, brass with hand-finished screws, worn key. New: pressed glass with mold seams, Phillips screws, bright lacquered brass.
- Best decanters have deep hand cutting and original stoppers (Hemswell); hobnail-cut decanters with bulbous stoppers appear on Betjemann miniatures (LAPADA).
- Period oak has a dry, darkened surface with wear on the handle and corners; bright even varnish suggests refinishing (general knowledge, verify).
- Felt-lined wells are common; new bright felt means it was replaced.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing/replaced decanters (huge value loss), chips on stoppers, stuck stoppers, missing key (keys replaceable), broken lock, cracked wood, replaced felt.
- Original keys were often lost, so an original key and working lock are highly prized (Hemswell).
- Later decanter labels (e.g. German silver labels on a Chairish example) are additions, not original.
- One decanter taller than the others (as on the Chairish example) can mean a replacement; ask.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern repro tantaluses; married decanters. Check stopper numbers match bottle numbers.
- Modern pieces: lacquered bright brass, Phillips screws, machine-perfect veneers, pressed-glass decanters with mold seams, no stamps on the handle (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Moderate; dozens sell per month. Shipping risk deters buyers; local pickup often best. Complete original Betjemann examples have made thousands of pounds at UK auction (Hemswell).

## What sellers usually miss
Betjemann patent stamp on the handle or locking bar; hidden games drawer in the base.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy and fragile (10–20 lb with glass). Pack decanters separately; $35–60. High transit-damage risk.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photo lock and key, stopper/bottle matching numbers, all chips. State key included and lock works.
- Photograph the handle stamps (both ends), the keyhole, the wood grain close up and the open drawer.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker only if stamped (quote the stamp exactly, e.g. "Betjemann's Patent London" with its number). Give the wood, the mount metal, overall dimensions, the number of decanters and whether they match and are original, whether stopper numbers match, that the key is included and the lock works (or not), and every chip. Say how it will ship: decanters packed separately from the frame.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$40 out − ~$35 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Complete $350 set: buy at ≤ $115. Empty frames: pass.
