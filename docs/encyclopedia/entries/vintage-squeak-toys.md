## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hollow rubber or vinyl figures with a squeaker in the base, 1940s–70s. Marks moulded on the base: "Sun Rubber Co. Barberton O.", "Rempel Akron", "Edward Mobley Arrow Rubber", "Sanitoy". Later Hong Kong/Taiwan vinyl and hard-plastic accordion-body "squeeze" toys (bellows torso) are common.

Background: rubber squeak toys date from about 1860 (a Goodyear rubber Jenny Lind squeak toy of c.1860 sold at a Noel Barrett auction for $95), but collectible ones are mostly 1930s and later, when rubber could be painted without flaking. Most are unmarked and shaped like cartoon characters or children (Kovel). Sizes run from 3" to about 12". The squeaker is a metal or plastic reed in a hole in the base. Look-alikes: modern dog toys (latex, no maker mark), 1970s–80s Hong Kong vinyl, and German/Eastern European rubber toys (Delacoste-type, often marked in German or with a country).

Where to look in a photo: the base or the back, for a raised moulded mark; Sun Rubber dolls and figures are also marked on the back of the head, the back, or the bottom of a foot, sometimes with a character name, a patent number and a date (Doll Reference).

## Makers, brands and variants
Sun Rubber (Barberton OH), Rempel (Akron), Arrow/Edward Mobley, Sanitoy, Oak Rubber, Irwin, Knickerbocker, Hong Kong vinyl.

- **Sun Rubber Co. (Barberton, Ohio, 1923–1974)** — squeak toys, dolls and toy vehicles; switched to vinyl around 1957 (Doll Reference). Ruth E. Newton designed the 1950s squeeze dolls (Bonnie Bear, Happy Kappy, Chunky/Rompy, Wiggy Wags) and her name appears in the mark. Licensed lines include 1937+ comic character dolls (Mickey and Minnie Mouse among them), the 1955–56 Gerber Baby advertising doll ("Gerber Baby © Gerber Products Co."), and Walt Disney Productions squeeze toys marked "Walt Disney Prod." with "Sun Rubber Co. Barberton O." (e.g. 8" Mickey Mouse, Donald Duck). Viceroy (Toronto) sold similar pieces in Canada.
- **Rempel Manufacturing (Akron, Ohio, late 1940s–1950s)** — Kovel calls Rempel the maker that brings the best prices, with rarities around $200. Best known: the Froggy the Gremlin squeak toy (1948–49, from Smilin' Ed McConnell's TV show; green frog in red jacket, white shirt, bowtie and brown shoes, marked with "J. ED. MCCONNELL"), plus farm animals (lamb, pig, duck) sold in header bags.
- **Edward Mobley / Arrow Rubber Co. (1960s)** — larger (10–12") animals with sprayed paint and eyes that close when the toy is knocked over; the elephant has fleece and satin ears; the lamb has painted eyebrows and crow's feet (Museum of Kitsch). Marked "Edward Mobley" with the Arrow name.
- **Sanitoy (Fairfax, Vt.)** — infant toys, Santa and animal squeakers; low value.
- **Oak Rubber, Irwin, Knickerbocker** — lower-value lines; Knickerbocker did licensed character vinyl.
- **Hong Kong / Taiwan vinyl and accordion "squeeze" men (1960s–80s)** — bellows torso, hard-plastic head and hands; mostly $10–25.

### Quick ID checklist
- Raised mark on the base or back: "Sun Rubber Co. Barberton O.", "Rempel", "Edward Mobley"/"Arrow", "Sanitoy" — or nothing (unmarked = few dollars).
- Licence line: "Walt Disney Prod.", a TV/comic character copyright, "Gerber Products Co." — licensed beats generic.
- Rubber (matte, may be hardened, cracked) vs vinyl (glossy, soft, post-1957 for Sun Rubber).
- Eyes that close (Mobley), fleece ears (Mobley elephant).
- Paint: original sprayed paint with even wear, or brushed-on touch-ups?
- Squeaker present and working? Ask the seller to squeeze it on video.
- Packaging: header bag or box present?
- "Hong Kong" / "Taiwan" mark or accordion bellows body = low tier.

### Golden-egg tells
- Rempel Froggy the Gremlin (1948–49) and other Rempel character pieces — Rempel rarities ~$200 (Kovel 2007) vs $6–15 generic squeakers — "Rempel" mark; frog in red jacket and bowtie; "J. Ed. McConnell" name.
- Sun Rubber Walt Disney Productions characters (Mickey, Donald, Pluto), 1930s–50s rubber — the $20–60 "licensed characters" tier vs $10–25 Sun Rubber animals (Terapeak/general knowledge) — "Walt Disney Prod." and "Sun Rubber Co. Barberton O." moulded on the base.
- Any squeak toy unused in original header bag or box (e.g. Rempel duck "unused in header bag") — top tier $60–200 vs loose (general knowledge, verify) — cardboard header stapled to a bag, or printed box.
- Sun Rubber Gerber Baby (1955–56) and Ruth E. Newton squeeze dolls (Bonnie Bear, Happy Kappy, Chunky) — character tier vs common — "Gerber Baby © Gerber Products Co." on the head; "Ruth E. Newton" in the mark.
- Edward Mobley 1960s animals with working closing eyes, especially the fleece-eared elephant — above common squeakers (est.) — eyes shut when tipped; sprayed paint; 10–12" size.
- Sun Rubber guide literature (Squeakypedia Vol. 2) — sold $50, more than most single toys (Terapeak, Aug 2026).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Early rubber hardens and cracks; vinyl stays soft. Original paint vs repainted.

- Natural rubber (1930s–1950s): matte surface, may feel stiff, develops surface cracking and goes brittle; a toy described as "hardened from age" is normal for Rempel.
- Vinyl (Sun Rubber from c.1957; most 1960s+ toys): softer, glossier, holds shape, colours more saturated.
- Original paint is sprayed or stencilled and wears on high points; repaints are brushy, cover cracks, and look too bright.
- Mould seam lines are faint on good-quality rubber; heavy flash on seams and no mark = cheap production.
- Old vs new: maker marks and town names (Barberton O., Akron) stopped with the companies (Sun Rubber closed 1974); modern reproductions are marked as such ("Repro-1955").

## Condition, damage and repairs
Hardened/cracked rubber, missing squeakers, paint loss.

- Hardened rubber can crack when squeezed — ask the seller not to squeeze a stiff piece hard, and expect splits at necks and limbs.
- Collapsed or dented hollow bodies, chewed edges (dog or child), paint flaking, sticky degraded vinyl.
- Squeakers rust or fall out; a non-squeaking toy is a modest deduction, not fatal.
- Repainting kills collector value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
"Repro-1955 Sun Rubber" items exist (Terapeak shows one at $10.59, Feb 2026). Modern pet-store latex squeakers in retro shapes are sometimes titled "vintage"; they lack moulded maker marks.

## Market
Slow, low value. Buyers are Disney and TV-character collectors, doll collectors (Sun Rubber dolls), and mid-century kitsch buyers. Christmas figures sell in Q4.

## What sellers usually miss
Licensed Disney Sun Rubber pieces. Also the Rempel mark (Rempel brings the best prices), the J. Ed. McConnell / Froggy connection, and original header bags.

## Shipping / handling
Light, $5–6. Hardened rubber cracks if crushed — box, do not bag.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker mark, squeaks or not, softness.

### What to tell the buyer
Quote the moulded mark verbatim (e.g. "Sun Rubber Co. Barberton O. / Walt Disney Prod.") and photograph it. Give the height, the material (natural rubber or vinyl), whether it still squeaks, whether the rubber is soft or has hardened, and any cracks, splits or paint loss. For a character piece, name the character and its source (e.g. Froggy the Gremlin from Smilin' Ed McConnell's 1949 TV show). Say whether it is original paint and whether it has its header bag or box. Do not squeeze hardened rubber to test it.
