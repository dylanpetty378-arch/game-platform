## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Pitching shoes:** ~7–7.5 in. long, ~2.5 lb; NHPA limits are max 2 lb 10 oz, max 7-5/8 in. long, max 7-1/4 in. wide, opening max 3-1/2 in. (used shoes 3-5/8), no part over 1 in. high (NHPA). Cast or forged, with "calks" (toe points/heel hooks) and a brand cast or forged on the face ("RINGER", "DIAMOND", "GIANT GRIP", "OHIO"); often letters "A/B" stamped to tell pairs apart. Pro shoes often carry a model name and weight.
- **Farrier shoes:** nail holes, worn, uneven; set in a crease (fullering) along the branches; toe clips and calks on draft shoes.
- **Hand-forged vs machine-made farrier shoes:** Henry Burden's 1835 machine (Troy NY) made 60 shoes a minute and supplied the Union Army, so mass-made "keg shoes" date from the mid-19th c. on; hand-forged shoes show uneven branch thickness, hammer marks and irregular nail holes, but even experts find age hard to pin down because calks and clips go back to Roman times (Wikipedia; I Forge Iron).
- Title words: "pitching", "horseshoe game", "Ringer", "drop forged", "Diamond", "Duluth", "draft", "lucky".
- Look-alikes: decorative cast "rustic" horseshoes (no wear, uniform rust), welded horseshoe art.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Diamond Calk Horseshoe Co. (Duluth MN, founded 1908 by blacksmith Otto Swanstrom)**: began with a patented insertable calk; pitching-shoe patent 1,850,816 (A.R. Swanstrom, filed 1931, issued 1932); pitching shoes in 1935 ads and 1969/1973 catalogues. Renamed **Diamond Tool and Horseshoe** in 1958; sold to Triangle Corp. 1981, later Cooper Industries (Alloy Artifacts). Models: Diamond Super Ringer, Eagle Ringer, Official (old entry; verify model dates).
- **Gordon Spin-On (John Gordon, Los Angeles)**: on the market 1931, patented 7 Nov 1933; a tournament favourite for 70+ years; Gordon was NHPA Hall of Fame 1971 (NHPA). Terapeak 365d median $69 (n=19; listings mostly "QTY 2", i.e. a pair).
- **St. Pierre (Worcester MA, since 1920s)**: family firm that made tire chains before turning to drop-forged pitching shoes and stake kits; calls itself the largest US maker of pitching horseshoes; NHPA-sanctioned; still forging in 2015 (Boston.com). "Royal" is a St. Pierre line ("St. Pierre Royal" drop-forged, Chairish listing).
- **Ohio Horseshoe Co.**, **Giant Grip (Oshkosh WI)**, **Lattore**, **Allen**, **Royal**.
- **"Ringer"** brand: several makers used "Ringer"/"Double Ringer" — Diamond is most common.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the face: maker name and model cast/forged in (DIAMOND, GORDON, OHIO, GIANT GRIP, ST. PIERRE, LATTORE, ALLEN).
- Diamond company name: "Diamond Calk Horse Shoe Co." = pre-1958; "Diamond Tool and Horseshoe" = 1958+ (Alloy Artifacts, tool-dating rule; verify on shoes).
- "Duluth" on the shoe = Diamond.
- Drop-forged (crisp edges, flash line along the sides, denser) vs cast iron (rougher, porous, may chip).
- Count: sets of 4 (two A, two B) — are they a matched set?
- Box, case, stakes and rule booklet present?
- Weight per shoe (pro shoes ~2.5 lb); length within 7-5/8 in.
- Farrier shoes: hand-forged irregularity vs machine-made keg shoe; draft-horse size (big = heavy, more decorative).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Gordon Spin-On pro shoes (1931+)** — ~2–3× a common Ringer pair (Terapeak median $69, p75 $97 vs "vintage horseshoes" median $11.60) — "GORDON" (and model name) on the face; listings often say "Professional".
- **Boxed complete pitching set with stakes/case** — $50–120 vs $25–70 loose 4-shoe sets — original wooden/cardboard box with maker graphics, stakes.
- **Early Diamond Calk (pre-1958 name) pitching shoes** — upper end of $25–70 (verify) — "Diamond Calk Horse Shoe Co., Duluth" lettering.
- **Ohio "O" / Lattore / Allen early drop-forged pro models** — $40–150 per pair vs $25–70 for a set of four common — model name cast on the face, crisp forging.
- **St. Pierre Royal / other coloured-paint drop-forged sets with original paint** — upper end of common sets (verify) — blue/red/yellow paint remnants, "ST. PIERRE" mark.
- **Large draft-horse shoes or hand-forged shoes for décor buyers** — craft lots $15–40 — oversized shoes with toe clips and calks, hammer marks.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Drop-forged steel (crisp edges, heavier) vs cast iron (cheaper, may chip). Paint remnants indicate original colour codes.
- Sets were often painted in two colours so players could tell pairs apart, in addition to A/B stamps (general knowledge, verify).
- Old surface: even dark patina and worn edges where shoes struck stakes; fresh orange flaking rust = recent outdoor storage.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rust (acceptable to rustic buyers), chipped calks, mismatched sets.
- Bent or cracked shoes are sometimes welded or ground; NHPA allows repairs that keep the original design (NHPA), so check for weld beads.
- Ask: maker name on face, number of shoes and pairs, weight per shoe, box/stakes.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Cast-iron decorative "antique" horseshoes from China for crafts.
- Some makers (Gordon, St. Pierre) remained in production for decades, so a "vintage" name does not guarantee age; check old company names, wear and paint.

## Market
- Steady but low-ticket; summer seasonal.
- Buyers: league pitchers (want sanctioned pro models by weight/model), rustic décor and craft buyers (cheap lots), a few advertising/sporting collectors (boxes).

## What sellers usually miss
- Little.
- Maker/model names on pro shoes (Gordon Spin-On) and the original box.

## Shipping / handling
4 shoes ≈ 10 lb; 8 shoes ≈ 20 lb; $20–35 to ship. Shipping kills margin.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, weight per shoe, count, rust level. Photograph the face marks, A/B stamps, and the box.

### What to tell the buyer
"Set of [4] vintage [maker/model] pitching horseshoes, [drop-forged steel / cast], marked '[face text]' with A/B pair letters; about [2 lb 8 oz] each, [7.5] in. long. [Original paint traces / surface rust], no cracks or welds. [Box/stakes included.]" For Gordon: "Gordon Spin-On design, on the market since 1931 and patented 1933." For Diamond: "Diamond Calk Horseshoe Co., Duluth, Minnesota (founded 1908)."
