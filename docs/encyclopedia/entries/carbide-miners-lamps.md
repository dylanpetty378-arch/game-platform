## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small brass/steel two-chamber lamp (water on top, carbide below) with a round reflector and a cap hook. Makers stamped on the top: Justrite (most common), Auto-Lite, Guy's Dropper, Dewar, Baldwin (early, big reflector). Larger hand/mounted carbide lamps (Dewar hand lamp, Justrite "streamlined") are a step up. Value tells: all-brass, original reflector, unusual maker, striker intact. Electric Edison/Koehler cap lamps with battery packs are a separate low-value niche. Carbide lamps were used c.1900–1950s in mines and by cavers (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Justrite (Chicago)**: most common; brass and later steel cap lamps, "streamlined" hand lamps a step up.
- **Auto-Lite (Universal Lamp Co.)**: common, brass cap lamps.
- **Guy's Dropper**: distinctive drip mechanism, collectible ($90–150).
- **Baldwin (John Simmons Co.)**: early, big reflector; pre-1915 examples top tier.
- **Dewar**: hand lamps collectible.
- Rare makers named in the entry: Shanklin, Zar. Coal-company-marked lamps (stamped with a mine/company name) command premiums.
- Electric cap lamps (Edison, Koehler) – separate, low-value niche.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
All-brass bodies with original patina beat steel or nickel-plated later ones. Original reflector (often silvered/nickel, with maker name) and working flint striker add value. Felt/cloth in the bottom chamber and the gasket may be replaced (normal). Polished-bright brass suggests a cleaning; collectors usually prefer honest patina (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, missing reflector, missing or broken striker, stripped threads between chambers, missing water-valve lever, cracked tip. Replacement reflectors are common and reduce value. Ask for photos of the top stamp, reflector front, striker and chamber threads.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction and decorative "miner's lamp" brass pieces exist; some modern carbide lamps for caving are still made (general knowledge, verify). Unusual "rare maker" stamps should be checked against collector references.

## Market
Steady niche: mining-memorabilia collectors (Appalachia, Pennsylvania, the West), cavers and decorators. Common Justrites are plentiful, so only maker/rarity moves the price. Sells on eBay; mining-collectible shows (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Maker; most sellers just say "miner's lamp". Justrite is the Zebco of the category.

## Shipping / handling
1 lb, $8–10. eBay 13%. Empty any carbide residue before shipping (it reacts with moisture).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker and model as stamped, material (brass/steel), completeness (reflector, striker, water lever, hook), whether it has been cleaned, any company marking, height. Photograph the top stamp, reflector, striker and base.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Baldwin/Guy's Dropper/Dewar ≤ $30, or a box lot with several brass lamps ≤ $10 each. Pass when: single Justrite over $25. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
