## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Valved brass (trumpet, cornet, flugelhorn, baritone, mellophone, French horn) and slide brass (trombone). Most Craigslist horns are school-band student models in a fitted case. Read the engraving on the bell (maker, model, city) and the serial number on the 2nd valve casing (Olds/Conn/King/Holton) or 3rd valve (Bach). City tells era: "Olds Los Angeles" = earlier (to mid-1950s), "Olds Fullerton Calif" = 1950s–79 (verify switch date); "Conn Elkhart Ind" (pre-1970) beats "Conn Abilene/Eastlake" later student lines; "King Cleveland Ohio" = H.N. White era; "Holton Elkhorn Wis".
Visual tells for better horns: nickel-silver (silver-colored) trim on valve casings and slides on a lacquered brass body, engraved bell with model name in script ("Super", "Recording", "Special", "Studio", "Stradivarius", "Committee", "Connstellation"), first-valve slide thumb saddle plus third-valve adjustable ring, Monel valves, two-piece bell bracing. Student tells: plain bell stamping, plastic or simple cases, "Bundy", "Director", "Cleveland", "Collegiate", "Ambassador", "TR300", "Prelude", "Vito", "Jupiter".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **F.E. Olds & Son** (Los Angeles, later Fullerton; 1910s–1979, then Norlin): Ambassador (student, 1940s–79) — common; Special (intermediate, popular with players); Super (pro, nickel trim, often tuning slide in first valve); Studio; Recording (pro, offset bell ferrule, high value); Mendez (Rafael Mendez signature, pro); Opera; Olds trombones (Super, Opera, Special). Collectors want Olds pro models, especially Los Angeles-stamped.
- **C.G. Conn** (Elkhart, IN): Director (student), Victor, 22B/80A cornet, Constellation 38B/36B trumpet & cornet (pro, 1956+), Connstellation, 88H trombone (pro), 6H/8H. Pre-1970 Elkhart pieces most desirable.
- **H.N. White / King** (Cleveland, OH): Cleveland (student), Tempo, Liberty and Silver Flair (sterling-silver bell, pro), King 2B/3B trombones (pro, jazz).
- **Frank Holton** (Elkhorn, WI): Collegiate (student), Revelation, Stratodyne, Galaxy; Holton French horns (Farkas H179 pro).
- **Vincent Bach** (Mt. Vernon NY then Elkhart): Stradivarius 37/43/72 bells (pro, $700–1,500); Mt. Vernon-era (serials < ~ 1965 production) $1,500–3,000+; TR300/Aristocrat (student).
- **Martin** (Elkhart): Committee trumpets (Miles Davis association) pre-1960s $1,000–3,000; Handcraft, Imperial.
- **Getzen** (Elkhorn): 300 series (student), Eterna (pro). **Reynolds** (Cleveland): Contempora. **Selmer/Bundy**: Bundy (student, extremely common), Selmer K-Modified/Paris pro.
- **Yamaha**: YTR-2320 etc. (student, reliable, sell steadily $150–300); Xeno pro.
- Low-value imports: Jupiter, Blessing student, Chinese "Mendini/Cecilio" — $20–80.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Yellow-brass body, clear lacquer (wears to brown/green patina), silver plate (higher original cost, looks gray-silver; pro horns), gold-brass/rose-brass bells, sterling-silver bells (King Silver Flair, stamped "Sterling Silver Bell"). Nickel-silver tubing on valve slides = better construction. Old vs new: older US horns use thin lacquer that has gone amber, felts and corks in valves; "Made in USA" city stamps; modern imports have bright epoxy lacquer and "China/Taiwan" stamps.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents (bell rim, leadpipe, tuning slide crooks), stuck/seized valves (dried oil and corrosion), stuck slides, red rot (dezincification pink spots on the leadpipe), broken braces/solder joints, worn valves (leaky compression), missing mouthpiece, cracked trombone hand slides, worn lacquer. Professional dent work and chem-clean runs $75–250, so per Dylan's no-repair rule only horns with free-moving valves and slides qualify. Relacquered horns lose 30–50% for pro collectors.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not much faking; the issue is mis-labelled model names (sellers call an Ambassador "Olds Super") and Asian "stencil" horns engraved to look like Bach. Chinese horns engraved "Bach Stradivarius" exist — tell is wrong serial location/format, rough bell engraving, cheap case. Reissues: Olds name was revived in 2000s on Chinese horns.

## Market
Steady, deep market; hundreds of student horns sold monthly on eBay; school-year start (Aug–Sep) lifts student-horn prices. Pro horns sell to players who want a play-test; returns are a risk. Reverb.com is often better than eBay for pro horns.

## What sellers usually miss
- The model name on the bell ("Super", "Recording", "Special", "Committee", "Stradivarius") — they list it as "old trumpet".
- Silver plating and sterling bells.
- Mouthpieces: vintage Bach Mt. Vernon / Olds / Giardinelli mouthpieces in the case sell $30–150 alone.
- Los Angeles-stamped Olds and Mt. Vernon Bach serials.

## Shipping / handling
Trumpet/cornet in a hard case fits a passenger seat and ships ~$20–35 (boxed 24×12×10, ~8–10 lb). Trombones are long (case ~45") — still fits a seat but ship cost $35–60. Baritones, euphoniums, tubas, sousaphones are too big — pass. Pack with the case in a box; protect the bell and leadpipe.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model, serial and city stamp; valve/slide action ("all valves move freely, slides move"), dents (photograph each), lacquer %, mouthpiece included; say it is untested by a professional player and unserviced; recommend a chem-clean. Don't claim "professional" for a student model.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$30 ship to buyer − ~$20 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Student horns (median ~$130) leave room only at ≤ $25. Pro-line Olds/Bach/Conn/King/Martin at ≤ 25% of sold, valves and slides free (ask for a short video), no red rot. Pass on large brass (baritone and up), anything stuck or with significant dents, Chinese stencil horns. No professional authentication needed except for high-dollar Mt. Vernon Bach serials — verify the serial.
