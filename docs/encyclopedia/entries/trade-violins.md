## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- A paper label inside the f-hole reading "Antonius Stradivarius Cremonensis 1713", "Nicolaus Amatus" or "Jacobus Stainer" marks a Markneukirchen/Schönbach factory copy, 1880–1930. Millions were made.
- Tells of a better instrument: a one-piece flamed maple back, a hand-cut scroll with crisp turns, inlaid (not painted) purfling, and a shop label "Made in Germany/Czechoslovakia" (E.R. Pfretzschner, Roth, Heberlein, Juzek, Wurlitzer).
- Modern Chinese and Romanian student outfits (Cremona, Stentor, Euro-Standard) cost $80–200 new.
- Seller words: "old violin", "antique fiddle", "Stradivarius violin" (it never is).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Ernst Heinrich Roth** (Markneukirchen, then Bubenreuth): branded inside the back; 1920s–50s are best.
- **John Juzek** (Prague; "Master Art" is the better grade).
- **E.R. Pfretzschner**, **Heberlein**.
- **Wurlitzer** (US retailer labels).
- **French Mirecourt**: JTL (Jérôme Thibouville-Lamy), Laberte, Couesnon.
- **Bows**: Pfretzschner, Nürnberger and Bausch stamps on pernambuco sticks with silver-wound frogs.
- **Modern student**: Cremona, Stentor, Euro-Standard, Yamaha, Eastman (Eastman is better quality).
- **Sizes**: 4/4 full, 3/4, 1/2, 1/4. Fractional sizes are worth less except for good makers (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Spruce top, maple back, ribs and scroll, ebony fingerboard.
- Cheap tells: painted purfling, stained and lumpy varnish, machine-cut scrolls, a beech fingerboard painted black, a two-piece plain back.
- Quality tells: deep flame, clean purfling corners, an even varnish with patina, a stamped brand on the back button or inside.
- Bows: an octagonal pernambuco stick, silver or gold winding, a mother-of-pearl slide, and a maker stamp on the stick near the frog.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Top cracks (especially the soundpost crack, which is serious), open seams, a loose or broken neck, a warped fingerboard, missing bridge, soundpost or strings, and worm damage.
- Setup costs $150+ at a luthier, which wipes out the margin on junk-tier fiddles.
- Ask for photos of the back, the scroll, the label through the f-hole, and any cracks.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Every "Stradivarius" label is a copy, by design, not fraud. Fake labels of good German makers also exist; branded stamps are more reliable than paper labels (general knowledge, verify).
- Bows: fake stamps on cheap Brazilwood sticks.

## Market
Student and fiddle players buy on Reverb and eBay; better instruments go through Tarisio, Skinner and Bromptons. Back-to-school (Aug–Sep) lifts student outfits. Supply of trade fiddles is huge (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- The BOW is often worth more than the fiddle: a silver-wound frog, an octagonal pernambuco stick, a stamp.
- Shop labels (Roth/Juzek) on the inside rib.
- Sellers' "Stradivarius" labels mean nothing.

## Shipping / handling
- 6–8 lb in the case, $30–40 each way. Loosen the strings and pack the case in a box. Sell on Reverb or eBay.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Size, label text (photo), brand stamps, back length (356 mm for 4/4), one- or two-piece back, cracks and repairs, setup state, bow details (stick material, stamp, weight), case.
- Photograph the front, back, scroll, label, bow stamp and frog.
- Title keywords: "Ernst Heinrich Roth Violin 4/4 1928", "Juzek Master Art", "German Trade Violin Stradivarius Copy", "Pernambuco Violin Bow Silver".

## Buy rule
Buy when: Roth/Juzek/Pfretzschner/French labelled and playable at ≤ $150; or a stamped silver-mounted bow at ≤ $50.
Pass when: "needs repair"; no strings or bridge; unlabelled trade copy; anything that requires an appraiser.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
