## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mixed lots of rods, reels, lures, hooks. Value tells from photo: wooden bodies with glass eyes and cup/screw-eye hardware (pre-1950), tack eyes, see-through hook hangers, original cardboard boxes with lure pictured; reels with jeweled caps, German silver, side plates engraved. Example 7963064736: a saltwater/Great Lakes style lot — chrome casting spoons and diamond jigs, large glow/white tube and plug lures with skirts, a large wooden-looking carved plug (possibly homemade), rubber worms in a cup, packets of hooks, and a cork-handled baitcasting rod with an old level-wind reel (likely a 1940s–60s Pflueger/Shakespeare/Penn-type conventional reel).
- **Wood vs plastic from a photo:** wood shows paint crazing ("age lines"), chipped paint revealing grain at the nose and hook hangers, and slightly irregular shapes; plastic has crisp mould seams, a glossy uniform finish and moulded or painted eyes.
- **Side-hook minnows** (three or five trebles along the sides, some with six) and see-through hook mounts are early (c. 1900–1920s); the Shakespeare Wooden Minnow used clip/plate side hooks for about ten years from 1906 (MDC).
- **Boxes:** early two-piece cardboard boxes with an end label naming model and colour; a correct box multiplies value.
- **Reels:** maker's name and model engraved on the side plate or foot; ABU Ambassadeurs carry a serial and a name plate.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Lures: **Heddon** (Dowagiac MI; L-rig, 2-piece flap hardware), **Creek Chub** (Garrett IN; Pikie, Injured Minnow), **Pflueger** (Akron; Neverfail hardware), **Shakespeare**, **South Bend** (Bass-Oreno), **Paw Paw**, **Bomber**, **Arbogast** (Fred Arbogast; Jitterbug, Hula Popper).
- Reels: **Pflueger** Supreme/Akron/Medalist (fly), **Shakespeare** Wondereel, **Meek / Talbot / Milam** (Kentucky reels, high value), **ABU Ambassadeur** 5000 red (1950s–70s), **Penn** saltwater (common).

**Maker dates and key models (MDC, US Lures):**
- **James Heddon & Son** (Dowagiac, MI): founded 1900; first commercial plugs 1901, first patent 1902. Dowagiac Minnow No. 150 (c. 1904–1953, glass eyes), Surface Minnow No. 300 (1905–1941, renamed Musky Minnow 1925), Dummy Double (1913–16), Game Fisher (1923–33). Heddon's hook hardware changed from the 2-piece "flap" surface rig to a 1-piece surface rig in the late 1940s (Lure Lore), then to surface-mounted cups.
- **Creek Chub Bait Co.** (Garrett, IN): idea from 1906, officially founded 1916 by Henry Dills, Carl Heinzerling and George Schulthess; first product the Wiggler (1916); Pikie Minnow (1920–1978); Plunker (1926–1978); 1919 patent for scale finish sprayed through mesh. Kept making wooden lures into the 1950s; sold to Lazy Ike Corp in 1978.
- **Pflueger** (Enterprise Manufacturing Co., Akron, OH): early wooden lures, "Neverfail" hardware; Pflueger Globe made 1910–1966 before the company was sold to Shakespeare (MDC).
- **Shakespeare** (Kalamazoo, MI): William Shakespeare Jr. started 1897, first lure patent 1901.
- **South Bend**: Bass-Oreno (c. 1915), Surf-Oreno (1916), Pike-Oreno (1922), Woodpecker two-hook (1914–23).
- **Worden** (South Bend, IN, bucktails from c. 1895), **Pontiac Manufacturing** (rare side-hook minnows), **Immell Chippewa** (1910–17, all sizes rare), Springfield, MO "barber pole" baits.
- **Kentucky and Missouri reels**: Meek, Milam, Talbot (William Talbot, Nevada MO, reels from the early 1890s; models Star, Comet, Meteor, Mars, Jupiter, Niangua) – the high end of reel collecting.
- **ABU Ambassadeur** (Sweden): name plates carry a year code (e.g. "L9" ≈ 1961, "M9" ≈ 1962; not foolproof); the 5000C changed from four-screw to three-screw frames around 1967–68; four-digit-serial 5000 De Luxe reels with black star drags are early (Real's Reels).

**Era guide for wooden lures (Appraisily, general):** 1890s–1915 small-batch wood, cup-rig hangers, glass eyes; 1916–1939 factory expansion, screw-eye hardware standard; 1940s–50s plastics and painted/decal eyes replace glass. Glass eyes + cup hardware = earliest; tack or painted eyes and plastic = later.

### Quick ID checklist
- Any wooden lures with glass eyes? Count them; each named one can be worth more than the whole lot.
- Hook hardware: cup-and-screw-eye, 2-piece flap, 1-piece surface rig, or modern split rings.
- Maker stamps on the metal lip, belly plate or propellers (Heddon, CCBC, Pflueger, South Bend).
- Original boxes with end labels (model, colour) – photograph them.
- Reels: maker and model on the side plate or foot; jewelled caps, German silver, red ABU.
- Paint: original crazing and hook-rash arcs vs fresh even gloss (repaint).
- Screws: slotted (period) vs Phillips (modern work).
- Exclude rods from shipping; judge them only for local resale.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old lures: wood with glass eyes, cup-and-screw-eye or flap hardware, crackled paint ("age lines"). Modern: plastic body, molded eyes, split rings. Reels: German silver and jeweled oil caps (older, better); stamped steel (cheap).
- Repaint/alteration tells (Appraisily): Phillips-head screws (period screws are slotted); new-looking hardware against aged paint; extra or misaligned holes under hangers; modern clear coat that glows under UV; no hook-swing wear arcs on an otherwise worn lure; glass eyes set proud or crooked; lip not matching the maker; fresh solvent smell or uniform gloss.
- Painting: Heddon No. 300 has three long hand-painted red gill marks; Creek Chub scale finishes were sprayed through mesh (from 1919).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint loss, hook rust (normal; collectors prefer original hooks), repainted lures (big value hit), reel internals frozen, broken rod tips, rotten cork. Also: cracked or split wooden bodies, missing propellers or lips, swapped hooks, replaced reel name plates (check the whole reel, Real's Reels).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Heddon/Creek Chub reissues in wood (1990s–2000s "Collector" series) and repainted "folk art" lures sold as old. Modern lures copying old shapes (Pikie, Plunker, Game Fisher-style plugs are still made) are not fakes but are often mislisted as vintage (MDC). Boxes can be "married" to the wrong lure; the end label must match model and colour.

## Market
Very liquid for named vintage lures; mixed modern lots move slowly at low prices. Spring is peak. Buyers: lure collectors (by maker, model and colour), reel collectors, and anglers who fish with vintage gear.

## What sellers usually miss
A single wooden glass-eye Heddon or Creek Chub hiding in a coffee-can lot; original boxes thrown in loose; old level-wind reels with makers' names. Also the colour name on box end labels (rare colours sell high) and reel serials/name-plate codes.

## Shipping / handling
Lures/reels: small box $8–15. Rods are not passenger-seat friendly as shipped items (long boxes, $25+) — exclude rods. Cover hook points (cork or tubing) so they don't snag each other or the packing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Sort and list named lures individually; photograph hardware, eyes, belly marks; reels with side plate stamping.

### What to tell the buyer
For each lure: maker, model and number, colour name, material (wood or plastic), eye type (glass, tack, painted), hook hardware type, length, and whether the hooks are original. State "original paint" only when there are no repaint tells; describe paint loss, crazing and hook rash honestly. Mention a box only if it matches (model and colour on the end label). For reels: maker, model, serial or name-plate code, whether the handle turns freely and the drag/click works, and any missing parts.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$12 − ~$12 − price ≥ max($50, price). Only lots where named wooden lures or collectible reels are visible. Exclude rods. Ask seller for close-ups.
