Buy rule (short): Buy only lots showing wooden glass-eye lures, lure boxes, or named vintage reels (ABU red, Pflueger Supreme, early Shakespeare) at ≤ $40; pass on spoons/jigs/saltwater bucktails.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mixed lots of rods, reels, lures, hooks. Value tells from photo: wooden bodies with glass eyes and cup/screw-eye hardware (pre-1950), tack eyes, see-through hook hangers, original cardboard boxes with lure pictured; reels with jeweled caps, German silver, side plates engraved. Example 7963064736: a saltwater/Great Lakes style lot — chrome casting spoons and diamond jigs, large glow/white tube and plug lures with skirts, a large wooden-looking carved plug (possibly homemade), rubber worms in a cup, packets of hooks, and a cork-handled baitcasting rod with an old level-wind reel (likely a 1940s–60s Pflueger/Shakespeare/Penn-type conventional reel).

## Makers, brands and variants
- Lures: **Heddon** (Dowagiac MI; L-rig, 2-piece flap hardware), **Creek Chub** (Garrett IN; Pikie, Injured Minnow), **Pflueger** (Akron; Neverfail hardware), **Shakespeare**, **South Bend** (Bass-Oreno), **Paw Paw**, **Bomber**, **Arbogast** (Jitterbug, Hula Popper), **Fred Arbogast**.
- Reels: **Pflueger** Supreme/Akron/Medalist (fly), **Shakespeare** Wondereel, **Meek / Talbot / Milam** (Kentucky reels, high value), **ABU Ambassadeur** 5000 red (1950s–70s), **Penn** saltwater (common).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old lures: wood with glass eyes, cup-and-screw-eye or flap hardware, crackled paint ("age lines"). Modern: plastic body, molded eyes, split rings. Reels: German silver and jeweled oil caps (older, better); stamped steel (cheap).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint loss, hook rust (normal; collectors prefer original hooks), repainted lures (big value hit), reel internals frozen, broken rod tips, rotten cork.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Heddon/Creek Chub reissues in wood (1990s–2000s "Collector" series) and repainted "folk art" lures sold as old.

## Market
Very liquid for named vintage lures; mixed modern lots move slowly at low prices. Spring is peak.

## What sellers usually miss
A single wooden glass-eye Heddon or Creek Chub hiding in a coffee-can lot; original boxes thrown in loose; old level-wind reels with makers' names.

## Shipping / handling
Lures/reels: small box $8–15. Rods are not passenger-seat friendly as shipped items (long boxes, $25+) — exclude rods.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Sort and list named lures individually; photograph hardware, eyes, belly marks; reels with side plate stamping.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$12 − ~$12 − price ≥ max($50, price). Only lots where named wooden lures or collectible reels are visible. Exclude rods. Ask seller for close-ups.
