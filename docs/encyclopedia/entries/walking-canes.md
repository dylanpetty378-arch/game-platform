Summary: Plain wood crook canes $10–30; hickory/malacca with silver collar $30–90; sterling or gold-filled knob/handle canes $75–400; carved folk-art (snakes, figures) $100–1,000+; gadget/system canes (sword, flask, gun) $300–5,000 (general knowledge, verify)
Buy rule (short): Pass on plain crooks; buy only metal-handled (sterling/gold-filled marked), carved figural, or gadget canes ≤ $40. Note full-length canes do fit diagonally in a car but cost $25–40 to ship.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Example 7970919702: two plain bent-wood crook canes — one dark stained/ebonized with rubber ferrule, one natural blond (ash/chestnut or hickory) with a white rubber ferrule and a cap on the crook end. Mass-produced 20th-century hospital/everyday canes. The Lens "median $400" is noise from gadget/silver canes.

## Makers, brands and variants
Brigg / Swaine Adeney Brigg (London; collar marked), Fayet (France), Cooper, Irish blackthorn shillelaghs, American Civil War/veteran-carved canes, Black Forest carved, Appalachian folk-art snake canes, Tiffany/Gorham sterling handles.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Collars: hallmarked sterling or gold-filled; ferrules: brass/horn (older) vs rubber (later). Shafts: malacca (mottled cane), snakewood (expensive), ebony. Plain steam-bent crooks with rubber ferrules = low.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks at crook bend, loose handles, dented collars, replaced ferrules.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian-made sword canes and "antique" brass-handle canes (dog head, duck head) sold in the $20 range flood the market.

## Market
Small niche; high-end canes sell at specialist auctions.

## What sellers usually miss
Small hallmarks on collars; folk-carved canes from veterans; malacca shafts.

## Shipping / handling
Long (34–37"); tube or long box $25–40. Shipping is the main margin killer.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Length, materials, hallmarks, ferrule, condition of crook.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$30 − ~$30 − price ≥ max($50, price) → need ≥ $175 sale on a $20 buy. Only marked silver/gold handled or folk-art carved canes qualify.
