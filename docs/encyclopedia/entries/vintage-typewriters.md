## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Portables in cases are the market: Royal Quiet De Luxe (1939–59, $100–200; 1950s colour versions $250–400),
Smith-Corona Silent/Sterling/Clipper ($80–150), Olympia SM3/SM4/SM9 ($150–300), Hermes 3000 seafoam ($300–500), Hermes
Baby/Rocket ($150–250), Olivetti Lettera 22/32 ($120–200), Royal Model P/O 1930s ($100–200, colour $300+). Desktop
standards (Underwood 5, Royal 10/KMM, Remington 16) are 30 lb, $40–100 unless 1920s glass-sided Royal 10 ($150–250).
Painted-over or spray-bombed machines are worth $20. Sellers write "antique typewriter", "vintage typewriter", "works".
The serial number (under the carriage or on the frame) dates the machine via typewriterdatabase.com.
- **Portable vs standard**: a portable sits low (about 4–6 in. tall), comes with a fitted case or lid and weighs 10–20 lb; a standard (desktop) is tall, open-framed or boxy, 25–35 lb and has no case.
- **Reading a photo**: the model name is on the paper table, the front panel or the top cover; the maker's decal is on the front or the paper table; the serial is on a frame rail under the carriage (move the carriage to one side), on the right or left of the frame, or under the machine.
- **Typeface**: shown only by a type sample. Pica (larger, 10 per inch) and elite (12 per inch) are standard; script or cursive is the premium.
- Look-alikes: 1970s–80s plastic-bodied portables and electrics sharing the same brand names (Smith-Corona, Royal, Brother); decorative "typewriter-look" objects.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Royal**: Quiet De Luxe (1939–59; colours 1950s), Model P/O (1930s), Arrow; desktop Royal 10 (glass sides 1920s),
  KMM.
- **Smith-Corona / L.C. Smith / Corona**: Silent, Sterling, Clipper, Skyriter; Corona 3 folding (1912–), top.
- **Olympia**: SM3/SM4/SM9 portables; SG1/SG3 desktops.
- **Hermes** (Paillard, Switzerland): Baby/Rocket, 3000 (seafoam is the prized colour). The Hermes 3000 was introduced in 1958 as a refinement of the Hermes 2000 and made into the early 1970s; segment shift, curved cover on early machines; parts are scarce, so servicing is harder (Finding Dulcinea).
- **Olivetti**: Lettera 22/32, Valentine (red, design icon) (general knowledge, verify Valentine values).
- **Underwood**: No. 5 standard, Universal/Champion portables.
- **Remington**: No. 12/16 standards, Remington Portable; early Remington No. 2 (1880s) top
  (general knowledge, verify).
- **Early/rare**: Blickensderfer, Oliver (1890s), index typewriters.
- Script/cursive typefaces add 50–100%. ShopGoodwill observations (Aldrich, undated): script machines usually sell for $100–150 more than the same machine without script; an Olympia SM3 with an unadvertised script face sold for $334, while non-script SM3s were selling around $137–150 (two went to $202.03 in a bidding war); an Olympia portable usually $75–120 sold for $232 with a cursive face listed.

### Quick ID checklist
- Maker and model name visible (decal or paper-table badge), not painted over.
- Serial number photo (under the carriage or on the frame) to date it on typewriterdatabase.com.
- Type sample on paper showing every key: typeface (pica, elite, script/cursive) and any missing or broken letters.
- Original factory colour (Royal 1950s colours, Hermes seafoam) vs repaint (paint over decals, overspray on rubber or chrome).
- Keytops: glass with chrome rings (older) vs plastic (later); all present.
- Carriage moves along its full travel and advances on each keystroke; bell rings; margin set works.
- Platen and feed rollers soft, not hard or cracked.
- Case present, latches and handle intact, no mouse damage inside.
- Portable (fits a seat easily) vs standard (heavy; local only).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Steel frames with enamel/crinkle paint, glass keytops with chrome rings (older) or plastic keys (later). Original
decals intact; colour paint must be factory (Royal colours, Hermes seafoam). Repainting erases decals — "no brand seen"
usually means a decal lost to repainting. Smooth carriage travel and crisp type are quality tells.
- Old vs new: pre-1950s machines are mostly black enamel with glass keys and gold decals; 1950s–60s portables bring colour, crinkle finishes and plastic keys; from the 1970s, plastic bodies and electrics take over, and value drops.
- Swiss and West German machines (Hermes, Olympia) are prized for build quality and action.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Sticky/frozen segments and keys (often just dried oil), worn platen (hard, cracked), dead ribbon, broken drawband,
missing feet, cracked case, rust, mouse damage in case. Missing keys or broken type slugs are hard to fix. Ask for a type
sample and a photo of the serial.
- Hermes 3000: parts scarcity makes repairs harder (Finding Dulcinea).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes; repainted machines sold as "colour" and Frankenstein machines (parts swapped) are the risk. Modern
decorative "typewriter-look" items are not typewriters (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Writers, decorators and a strong typewriter-collector community ("typosphere"); portables sell steadily on eBay and
Etsy, standards mainly locally. Serviced/cleaned machines sell for 1.5–2x untouched ones (general knowledge, verify). ShopGoodwill auctions are an active venue where bidding wars can overshoot (Aldrich).

## What sellers usually miss
Model and colour (colour = 2–3× value); "no brand seen" usually means a decal lost to repainting. Script/cursive typeface
machines bring 50–100% more – ask for a type sample.

## Shipping / handling
Portable in case 15–20 lb, $30–40, lock the carriage and wrap the case. Standards 30+ lb – local only.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model, year (from serial), typeface (pica/elite/script, with a type sample photo), colour, working status
(all keys, carriage advance, bell, ribbon), case condition. Title: maker, model, year, colour, "manual portable
typewriter", "working", typeface.

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker, model and year dated from the serial number (state the serial). Show a full type sample and name the typeface (pica, elite or script). Say what was tested (every key types, carriage advances, bell, shift, ribbon reverse) and whether it has been cleaned or serviced. State that the colour and decals are original, or disclose a repaint. Describe the platen, feet and case condition, and note anything missing.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Olympia SM/Hermes/colour Royal ≤ $60, Royal QDL/Smith-Corona ≤ $35, with a type sample. Pass when: standards
(freight), repainted, seller quoting "worth $100 online". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping
both ways. Must fit a passenger seat.
