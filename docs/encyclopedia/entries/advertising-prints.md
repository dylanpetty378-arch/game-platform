Buy rule (short): Only buy complete, uncut early (pre-1935) advertising calendars and Parrish/Rockwell/Elvgren originals; pass on 1950s–60s mail-in premium prints.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Prints given away or mailed in exchange for proofs of purchase, or printed as calendars. Northern Paper Mills (Green Bay, WI; Northern Tissue and Charmin) offered sets of soft pastel children's portraits in the 1950s–60s, chiefly by Frances Hook, printed about 8×10 to 11×14 on heavy stock with the artist's name and sometimes "Northern Tissue" in the margin. Telltales: pastel-chalk look, big-eyed children, white borders, often uniform paper toning.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Northern Paper Mills/Northern Tissue (Frances Hook, 1950s–60s; also Charlotte Becker baby prints).
- Gerber and Carnation baby premiums.
- Cream of Wheat ads (E.V. Brewster, N.C. Wyeth 1900s–1920s).
- Edison-Mazda calendars by Maxfield Parrish (1918–1934), the top tier.
- Brown & Bigelow calendars (Rockwell Boy Scouts, Elvgren pin-ups).
- Coca-Cola calendars (pre-1940 valuable; many reproductions).
- Fisk Tire "Time to Re-tire" boy.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original lithographs show a rosette/halftone dot pattern under a loupe (modern giclées show a stochastic inkjet spray). Early chromolithographs (pre-1910) have no dot pattern, just rich layered colour. Look for printer credits in the bottom margin, period paper toning, and calendar pads.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foxing, mat burn, water stains, trimmed margins (these kill value on calendars), tears, and missing calendar pads. Fading from sunlight.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Heavy reproduction of Parrish, Coca-Cola and Cream of Wheat. Northern prints were reissued in the 1960s–70s and are cheap anyway.

## Market
Low tier: high supply, slow. Parrish/Rockwell calendars: steady collector demand. Days to sell: 30–90 for low tier.

## What sellers usually miss
Full-size Parrish calendars sold as "old print"; complete calendar pads add 2–5× value; an artist's signature in the plate identifies the series.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, between rigid boards, in a sleeve: $6–12. Framed pieces with glass are risky: remove the glass or double box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** brand/series (Edison-Mazda, Cream of Wheat, Northern Tissue), artist (Maxfield Parrish, Frances Hook, Elvgren), "calendar"/"print"/"lithograph", year, title of the image, size, "complete pad". Junk: "wall art", "retro poster", "antique" on 1950s premiums. `1927 Maxfield Parrish Edison Mazda Calendar Complete Pad Reveries 18x38`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for Parrish/Rockwell/Elvgren (median $200 for Edison-Mazda); Etsy for framed decor-grade prints; FB local for Northern/Gerber pairs.
- **Price position:** premium pairs at the sold median ($30 asking, $5–35 sold), OBO; Parrish full calendars at p75 ($400) with intact pad and margins, firm 3 weeks, then −10%.
- **Photos:** first: full image straight-on, no glare; then loupe close-up of the dot pattern, printer credit margin, calendar pad, each corner/margin, foxing/stains, back of the sheet, ruler.
- **State:** artist, series, year, printer credit, sheet and image size, margins intact or trimmed, pad complete, foxing/toning/tears/mat burn by photo number, framed or loose.
- **Pack / haul:** flat between rigid boards in a sleeve, $6–12; framed with glass — remove the glass or double box.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Original [1927] [Edison-Mazda] lithograph with the [complete pad] and untrimmed margins — a rosette dot pattern, not a giclée; the pad alone puts it 2–5× over a cut print."

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$8 out − ~$8 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Premium mail-in prints never qualify. Buy only pre-1935 advertising calendars or famous-illustrator originals, with confirmed litho dots and intact margins, at ≤ ~35% of the median.
