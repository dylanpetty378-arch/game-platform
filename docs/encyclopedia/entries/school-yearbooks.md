## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Bound annual of a school's students, staff, sports and clubs. Commercial yearbooks (from ~1920s) have printed hard covers with embossed school name/year and a publisher's mark inside the back cover (Walsworth, Jostens/"American Yearbook Co.", Taylor Publishing, Herff Jones, Delmar). Pre-1950 small schools often produced their own: spiral/comb-bound, mimeographed or hectographed pages, hand-drawn stencil covers (exactly the 1946 "Napawinah '46" example: plastic-comb binding, stenciled lettering, hand-drawn tiger). College annuals are thicker, larger, often leather-grained. The year on the cover and the school/town name are the whole identification.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Jostens (Minneapolis, 1897; yearbooks from 1950s via American Yearbook Co. acquisition) – mark on rear endpaper/colophon.
- Walsworth (Marceline MO, 1937), Taylor Publishing (Dallas, 1939), Herff Jones (1920), Delmar (Charlotte). Their presence dates a book to the commercial era.
- Local print-shop annuals 1900–1930: letterpress, thin card covers, tipped-in photos.
- Mimeograph/stencil student-produced annuals (1930s–1950s, rural): spiral or ring bound, typed pages, few copies printed – the scarcest per-school.
- College/military academy annuals (West Point "Howitzer", Annapolis "Lucky Bag"), and WWII-era books with servicemen honor rolls – stronger demand.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Commercial books: padded or embossed cloth/leatherette covers, glossy coated paper, halftone photos. Homemade: mimeo ink (purple or black), typewritten, sometimes original photo prints pasted in (adds value). Pre-1930: letterpress with real photographs or high-quality halftones. Signatures and inscriptions are normal and generally neutral; a signed page by a later-famous person is the jackpot.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Water damage/mold (smell) kills value; cut-out photos (students clipped portraits) reduce it sharply; loose comb bindings and missing pages common in homemade annuals. Writing is expected. Do not repair; tape repairs reduce value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reprints exist mostly for famous-alumni books (reunion reprints, "commemorative edition" stamped). Celebrity yearbooks are faked by swapping pages or claiming the wrong person — verification needs the person's name and photo on a page. Normal school yearbooks are not faked.

## Market
Steady but thin: eBay sells thousands of yearbooks a month across all schools, but any one school/year may have 0–2 buyers. Typical days-to-sell 30–180. Title with school, town, state, year, and every notable name inside (searchable). Etsy and local Facebook history groups also work.

## What sellers usually miss
Pre-fame celebrities (search notable alumni lists for the school); WWII service rosters; original photographs pasted in; that a homemade mimeographed annual from a tiny town may be one of a handful left and is wanted by the local historical society.

## Shipping / handling
Books ship USPS Media Mail ($4–8) or Ground Advantage; padded mailer or box with cardboard. 1–4 lb each.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State school, town, state, year, binding type, page count, condition of binding, whether photos are cut out, signatures present. Photograph cover, title page, index of seniors, any sports/club pages.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$6 ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A single $40 book cannot clear this; only lots of pre-1950 books bought at ≤ $5–10 each, or a celebrity-alumni book, qualify. Size fits easily. No authentication issues except celebrity claims (verify the name on the page yourself).
