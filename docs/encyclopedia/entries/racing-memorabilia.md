## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Paper (posters, calendars, programs, tickets, pit passes), diecast cars (1:24, 1:64), apparel, flags, and race-used items.
- 1990s licensing produced enormous quantities. The market collapsed after the mid-2000s, and Earnhardt items spiked briefly after 2001 then fell.
- **Programs**: about 8.5 × 11 in souvenir books. The first Daytona 500 (February 20–22, 1959) program is the landmark NASCAR paper (EBTH lot description). Indy 500 programs run back to 1911, and the speedway sells official reprints ("Program Vault"), so reprints exist for many years.
- **Diecast**: the box end names the maker, scale and edition size. Premium Action/RCCA cars have opening hoods and detailed engines, and many are coin banks ("bank" with a slot in the rear window).
- **Race-used**: sheetmetal cut-outs, tires, lug nuts and firesuits, with a team or track certificate. Card companies (Press Pass "Legends Artifacts", Wheels) embedded race-used swatches in numbered cards.
- **Title words**: "race used", "team issued", "Goodwrench", "Winston Cup", "1:24 Action", "RCCA", "bank", "program", "pit pass", "Simpson".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Diecast**:
  - Action/RCCA (premium; RCCA was the club line with low production numbers).
  - Racing Champions, Revell, Winner's Circle (mass).
  - Lionel Racing (2010s+, the successor licence holder).
- **Paper**: Competitive Motorsports Products (Mooresville NC) calendars, Goodwrench/Winston promo posters, track programs, Press Pass cards.
- **Race-used**: sheetmetal with a COA from the team, tires, firesuits. Serious collectors only.
- **Apparel**: Simpson, Jeff Hamilton and Chase Authentics jackets. Jeff Hamilton leather and twill team jackets are the premium apparel line. See also the field note on Simpson Rusty Wallace jackets.
- **Indy / F1**: official programs, Borg-Warner trophy items, Indy pace-car promotional items, F1 team-issued apparel.

### Quick ID checklist
- Date first: copyright line on posters, year on programs, and the car's paint scheme year.
- Paper: is it pre-1985? Daytona/Darlington/Indy programs from the 1950s–70s are the paper that matters.
- Diecast: Action/RCCA 1:24 with the edition size on the box vs Winner's Circle or Racing Champions blister cards.
- Race-used: is there a team or track letter with a matching serial, or is the piece embedded in a numbered card (e.g. "#/50")?
- Signatures: treat as unauthenticated unless there is photo-proof of signing or a major third-party authenticator.
- Condition: flat or rolled (not folded), no pinholes, no tape, no sun fade.
- Box: diecast value depends on the original box and the clear outer sleeve being present.

### Golden-egg tells
- **1959 first-annual Daytona 500 program**: the top NASCAR paper, worth multiples of any 1990s item (paper keys $50–500 per this entry; no sold price cited). Photo tell: "First Annual Daytona 500, February 1959" on the cover; check for a detached cover (the EBTH copy had one).
- **1950s–60s Indy 500 and Daytona/Darlington programs**: about 5–25× a 1990s poster ($50–500 vs $3–20). Photo tell: the year on the cover; check that it is not stamped or printed as an IMS reprint.
- **Documented race-used sheetmetal, firesuits and tires** (team COA, matching photo-match): $100–1,000+, about 10–50×. Photo tell: real paint layers and decal edges, rivet holes and tire rubber, plus a team-letterhead COA naming the race and date.
- **Numbered race-used memorabilia cards** (Press Pass Legends Artifacts firesuit/sheetmetal, low print runs like "/25" or "/50" of Earnhardt, Richmond or Allison): a multiple of base cards (no $ cited; verify on sold). Photo tell: a swatch window and serial numbering on the card front.
- **Action/RCCA 1:24 Dale Earnhardt Sr. banks and special paint schemes** (e.g. the "Wrangler" and "Goodwrench" eras): $20–80, about 3–5× mass diecast (existing entry). Photo tell: the Action/RCCA logo and edition size on the box, an opening hood, the bank slot.
- **1960s–70s STP / Richard Petty #43 and Petty Enterprises items**: a premium over the 1990s (existing entry; verify). Photo tell: STP red and Petty blue, period typography, pre-1980 dates.
- **Jeff Hamilton leather team jackets**: a premium over twill Simpson/Chase jackets (no $ cited; verify). Photo tell: the Jeff Hamilton label and all-leather sponsor-patch panels.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Paper**: value requires flatness and no pinholes. Folded posters (from magazines or calendars) sell lower. Early (1950s–70s) programs from Daytona/Darlington/Indy are the paper that matters.
- **Original vs reprint**: originals have period printing (letterpress/offset with uneven registration). Reprints have modern glossy stock, sharp digital dots and often a reprint or vault line.
- **Race-used sheetmetal**: shows multiple paint layers, tire rubber, scuffs and bent edges. Fakes are clean single-layer pieces cut from show cars or new panels.
- **Diecast**: Action 1:24 cars are heavy and have opening hoods. Winner's Circle and Racing Champions are lighter, with fewer separate parts.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Fold lines, pinholes, tape, sun fading and water stains.
- Programs: detached covers, missing pages, filled-in scoring pages (usually acceptable, sometimes a plus).
- Diecast: missing antennas and mirrors, box crush, yellowed sleeves.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Forged autographs are endemic (Earnhardt especially). Fake "race-used" sheetmetal. Reprinted posters.
- COAs are unregulated. Embossing, holograms and elaborate certificates prove nothing. The gold standard is photo proof of the signing with the date and place, or a COA naming the item specifically, plus provenance; judge the item, not the paper (Motor Sport 2025).
- Indy programs: official IMS reprints are sold today, so check for reprint markings.

## Market
Saturated with 1990s supply; buyers are mostly regional (Southeast). Slow sales for common items. Older programs sell to a small, steady group of collectors, with a February (Daytona) and May (Indy) bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Early programs and tickets mixed in with 1990s junk; 1960s–70s STP/Petty items; race-used card inserts among ordinary trading cards.

## Shipping / handling
Posters in tubes $8–15; diecast $8–15. Programs ship flat in a rigid mailer.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the year, publisher, size, and any folds or pinholes.
### What to tell the buyer
Give the exact year and event ("1959 First Annual Daytona 500 official program"), the publisher or maker and copyright line, the dimensions and page count, and whether it is complete. State originality plainly ("original, not an IMS reprint"). For diecast, give the maker, scale, driver, sponsor and year, the edition size from the box, and whether it is a bank. For race-used items, describe exactly what the documentation is (issuer, serial, race named) and do not claim more than it says. Disclose folds, pinholes, tape, detached covers and box wear.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$10 out − ~$10 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs $70+ resale at ≤ $10: almost never for 1990s posters. Pass unless pre-1985 paper.
