## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Paper (posters, calendars, programs, tickets), diecast cars (1:24, 1:64), apparel, flags, and race-used items. 1990s licensing produced enormous quantities; the market collapsed after the mid-2000s and Earnhardt items spiked briefly after 2001 then fell.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Diecast**: Action/RCCA (premium), Racing Champions, Revell, Winner's Circle (mass), Lionel Racing (2010s+).
- **Paper**: Competitive Motorsports Products (Mooresville NC) calendars, Goodwrench/Winston promo posters, track programs, Press Pass cards.
- **Race-used**: sheetmetal with COA from team, tires, firesuits; serious collectors only.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Paper value requires flatness, no pinholes; folded posters (from magazines/calendars) sell lower. Early (1950s–70s) programs from Daytona/Darlington/Indy are the paper that matters.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fold lines, pinholes, tape, sun fading, water stains.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Forged autographs are endemic (Earnhardt especially). Fake "race-used" sheetmetal. Reprinted posters.

## Market
Saturated with 1990s supply; buyers mostly regional (Southeast). Slow sales for common items.

## What sellers usually miss
Early programs and tickets mixed in with 1990s junk; 1960s–70s STP/Petty items.

## Shipping / handling
Posters in tubes $8–15; diecast $8–15.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State year, publisher, size, folds/pinholes.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$10 out − ~$10 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs $70+ resale at ≤ $10: almost never for 1990s posters. Pass unless pre-1985 paper.
