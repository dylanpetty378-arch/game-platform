## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Collections of 12" LPs or 7" 45s sold together. Value sits in a handful of records; the rest is filler. Read the spines and labels:
- **Jazz**: Blue Note (address on label: 47 West 63rd/767 Lexington = early; "Liberty" or "A Division of United Artists" = later), Prestige (NJ/Bergenfield yellow), Riverside, Impulse! (orange/black), Contemporary, Pacific Jazz. "Deep groove" ring on label, "RVG" or "Van Gelder" stamped in deadwax.
- **Rock**: original first pressings of Beatles (Vee-Jay, Capitol rainbow), Led Zeppelin (Atlantic plum/red), Pink Floyd (Harvest), Velvet Underground (Verve), Nirvana Bleach (Sub Pop), Bowie, Stones (London), psych/prog on Vertigo swirl, private press.
- **Soul/funk**: Motown/Tamla/Stax/Atlantic originals, Northern Soul 45s, Blaxploitation soundtracks.
- **Low value**: classical box sets, show tunes, easy listening (Ray Conniff, 101 Strings), Christmas compilations, Herb Alpert "Whipped Cream", 1980s mainstream pop (Michael Jackson Thriller), Reader's Digest sets.

## Makers, brands and variants
Key value rules by label variant (general knowledge, verify):
- Blue Note original NY/Lexington pressings $100–2,000+; later Liberty $20–60; reissues $15–40.
- Columbia "6-eye" (1955–62) jazz originals (Miles Davis Kind of Blue 6-eye mono $150–600).
- Capitol Beatles first pressings and Butcher Cover (paste-over $1,000+).
- Promo copies (white label, "Promotional – not for sale" stamp), mono pressings (1960s), and sealed originals add value.
- 1970s rock like Heart "Dreamboat Annie" (Mushroom Records, 1976) is common: $5–15.
- George Benson "Breezin'" (Warner Bros. 1976) and "Blue Benson" (Polydor 1976) are common: $5–12; genuine autographs add $50–150 but need provenance.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight (180g vs. thin 1980s "Dynaflex"), original inner sleeves, shrink wrap with hype stickers, deadwax matrix numbers (identify first pressings: e.g. "A1/B1", RL stamped "Robert Ludwig").
- Reissues: barcodes on 1970s titles, "Digitally remastered", modern 180g reissue stickers.
- Cut-outs (drilled hole, cut corner, saw cut) reduce value 20–40%.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Grading (Goldmine): M, NM, VG+, VG, G. Most estate lots are VG or worse: scratches, scuffs, warps, ring wear, seam splits, water damage, mold. Value falls 50% per grade below VG+. Storage in hot garages warps records.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit pressings of rare titles (Beatles, Elvis Sun, Velvet Underground, punk rarities), bootlegs, and unofficial European reissues. Check matrix numbers and label typography against Discogs.

## Market
Liquid market (eBay, Discogs). Common titles move slowly at low prices; rare titles sell fast. Lots sell to dealers at $1–2/record.

## What sellers usually miss
Jazz and psych buried in a box of easy listening; mono or promo copies; first pressings of common titles that are worth 5–10× reissues; 45s in sleeves with picture sleeves.

## Shipping / handling
Media Mail eligible. LP: ~1 lb each; mailers $5–8 for 1–3 LPs. Lots of 50–100 are 50–100 lb (box, $40–80 via Media Mail). Remove discs from jackets to avoid seam splits. Heat/warp risk in summer.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Catalog number, label variant, matrix/runout, Goldmine grade for vinyl and jacket separately, play-tested or visually graded, inner sleeve and inserts.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$6 ship per LP (Media Mail) − ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Only buy lots where the photographed titles prove value (jazz originals, psych, punk, soul) at ≤ $2/record. A box of 1970s mainstream rock at $1/record is break-even after time. Records fit in a passenger seat. No authentication except autographs (avoid unless provenance).
