## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Offset lithograph reproductions (1940s–1990s):** the bulk of "vintage prints". Under magnification they show a regular rosette dot pattern; signatures are printed. Often on textured board (fake brushstrokes) in ornate gold or gesso frames (Turner Mfg., Windsor Art, Donald Art Co., Marshall Field's labels on the back).
- **Chromolithographs (c.1860–1920):** printed from many stones; rich, layered colour with a grainy, non-rosette stipple; often on board; frequently advertising or calendar art (DuPont, Remington, Winchester for sporting dogs).
- **Hand-coloured photographs (Wallace Nutting, David Davidson, Sawyer, 1900–1940):** platinum/gelatin prints coloured by hand, titled and signed in pencil on the mat.
- **Foil art/Dufex (1960s–80s):** metallic foil prints (geishas, flowers, deer), often by Jane Lahive "Manifestations" or F.J. Warren Dufex. Low value.
- **Limited editions:** pencil signature plus edition number in the margin (e.g., 125/950). Value depends on the artist.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Robert William Wood (1889–1979):** prolific landscape painter whose works were reproduced in huge numbers 1950s–70s (October Gold, October Morn, Sea and Sand). Small prints $15–25; large (35"–48") versions in original ornate or relief frames with brass title plates sell $85–320.
- **Grandma Moses (Anna Mary Robertson Moses):** offset reproductions from the 1940s–60s; $20–75 framed, median $40.
- **Huldah (Cherry Jeffe Huldah):** 1950s–60s fashion/children portraits ("Suzanne", "Annette", "Yvonne") in ornate frames; median $20; large ones $55.
- **Monteague "Monte" Flagg:** 1950s Native American children prints ("Billy Big Pine", "Little Many Goats"); $5–35, median ~$13.
- **Edmund Henry Osthaus (1858–1928):** sporting dog painter; DuPont Powders calendar prints (c.1899–1920) and chromolithographs are collected by sporting/gun collectors (Parker Gun forum). Modern photo reprints sell $12; original period chromolithographs in good condition are more (sold data not found – estimate $75–300 depending on title/size/condition).
- **Wallace Nutting:** hand-coloured photos $30–170, median $40.
- **Maxfield Parrish:** period (1920s) art prints in original frames sell $100–300 at best; modern poster-stock repros $9–13 dominate.
- **Old masters reproductions (Constable, etc.):** $10–15.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Magnify:** a loupe photo of the image shows dots (offset) vs continuous tone/stipple (chromolitho, photo) vs brushstrokes with raised paint (painting).
- **Back of the frame:** publisher labels, dated copyright lines, framer labels, paper dust covers with old stamps help date. Staples and MDF backing = later.
- **Paper:** acidic toning and foxing suggest age but are not proof.
- **Frames:** sometimes the frame is worth more than the print (carved gilt wood, tiger maple, Arts & Crafts oak). Compo/plaster ornate frames from the 1950s–70s add moderate value to big Robert Wood pieces.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Fading, water stains, foxing, mat burn, tears, prints stuck to glass, broken frame corners, flaking gesso.
- Textured board prints crack and lift. Foil prints oxidize.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Nearly everything in this category is a reproduction by design. The fraud risk is sellers calling prints "paintings" (look for a printed signature, uniform surface, dot pattern) or calling modern reprints "antique".
- Osthaus, Parrish, Nutting and Currier & Ives have been reprinted repeatedly; period pieces need dating evidence (publisher line, paper, backing).

## Market
- Low-value mass reproductions flood eBay and sell slowly; shipping large framed glass is expensive and risky. Large Robert Wood pieces in good frames have a real decorator following (fall decor demand, Sept–Nov).
- Sporting-dog and advertising prints sell to gun/hunting collectors, best in the fall.

## What sellers usually miss
- Big Robert Wood pieces with original ornate frames and brass title plates sell for $200–320, not the $25–50 owners ask.
- Period advertising chromolithographs (DuPont, Peters, Winchester, Remington dog prints) are mistaken for ordinary prints.
- The frame: carved gilt, tiger maple or Arts & Crafts frames can be worth $50–200 on their own.
- Conversely: "signed" prints with printed signatures (Osthaus, Huldah, Moses) are routinely over-priced at $75–100.

## Shipping / handling
- Framed with glass: remove glass or tape it in a grid, corner protectors, double box; 24"x30" ships $25–45; anything over ~36" goes oversized ($50–90) and is hard to fit in a car seat.
- Unframed prints: rolled in a tube or flat between boards, $8–12.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- State clearly "lithograph print" vs "original painting", give image and frame sizes, publisher info from the back, and photograph the signature close-up, the back and the frame corners.
- Note fall/holiday decor fit for autumn landscapes.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; must fit a passenger seat (roughly ≤ 36" on the long side). Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$30–45 framed) − shipping to Dylan (~$30–45 or $0 local) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Shipped both ways, framed prints essentially never work. Locally, only large Robert Wood–class pieces (median $200+) at ≤ ~$40, or period chromolithographs with sold medians ≥ $150.
- Never pay over $10 for foil art, Huldah, Flagg, Constable/old-master repros or small Grandma Moses.
