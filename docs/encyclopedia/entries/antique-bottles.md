Buy rule (short): Pass on anything machine-made after ~1915 unless it's a known rarity; buy only pre-1910 hand-finished bottles in strong colour (cobalt, emerald, amber poisons, bitters) or rare-town Coca-Cola at ≤ 30% of sold median.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Age tells visible in photos:**
  - **Mould seams:** a seam that stops below the lip = mouth-blown, finished by hand (pre-c.1915). A seam running through the top of the lip = machine-made (after c.1905–1920).
  - **Lip ("finish"):** applied/tooled lips, uneven drips = old; screw threads and perfect crown tops = 20th century.
  - **Glass:** bubbles ("seeds"), whittle marks, slightly crooked bodies = old. Uniform glass and Owens-Illinois "Ⓘ" marks = 1929+.
  - **Base:** pontil scar = pre-c.1860; smooth base with embossed maker codes and date codes = 20th century.
- **Coca-Cola "hobbleskirt" (contour) bottles:** patent dates on the side (Nov 16 1915; "Bottle Pat'd Dec 25 1923" = 1923–37; "Pat. D-105529" = 1937–51), city name on the base. 6–6.5 oz green. Only rare towns, early straight-sided bottles, and errors sell for real money.
- **Clorox:** amber glass with embossed "CLOROX" and textured shoulders (1929–1960s); cork-top 1929–1931 earliest. Common.
- **Poison bottles:** cobalt or amber, ribbed, hobnailed or coffin/skull shapes = desirable; a clear screw-top bottle with a paper skull label is 1940s–60s household chemicals.
- **Apothecary/druggist:** aqua or clear with tooled lips (pre-1915) or ground-glass stoppers; often embossed with a pharmacy name and town.
- **Decanters:** Jim Beam (1955–1990s), Ezra Brooks, Avon – ceramic or glass novelty bottles. Collected in the 1970s; market collapsed.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Coca-Cola** (bottled by hundreds of local bottlers): straight-sided "Hutchinson" and amber Coke bottles (pre-1916) are the money; hobbleskirts are $3–10 except rare towns (an Atlanta GA example sold $127.85).
- **Clorox** (Oakland, CA): cork-top 1929–31, then screw-top quart/half-gallon/gallon. $8–40.
- **Jim Beam:** "175 Months" (14.5-year bourbon) book-box decanters dated 1963–1973; sold $9–30.
- **Owl Drug Co., Lattimore, Triloids (poisons):** cobalt and amber figural/ribbed poisons $60–550.
- **Bitters (Drake's Plantation, Hostetter's, etc.):** pre-1880 embossed, $50–1,000+.
- **Modern decor reproductions:** "Absolutely Pure Milk" bottles with embossed cow, Made in Italy (1960s–present, also sold by Amici Home), $10–25.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Colour sells: cobalt, emerald, puce, deep amber, milk glass, and "sun-colored amethyst" (clear glass turned purple by manganese and sunlight, c.1880–1915) beat clear and aqua.
- Embossing (names, towns, figures) adds value; paper labels add a lot if intact (labelled poison and medicine bottles sell 2–5× unlabelled).
- Dug bottles often have "sick" haze (stain) that can only be removed by professional tumbling; cleaned bottles are disclosed.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips on lips and bases, cracks (hold to light), heavy stain/haze, label loss. Bottles are heavily condition-sensitive: a chipped lip halves value.
- Tumbling/polishing removes haze but also removes some value for purists; buyers want disclosure.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduced: bitters, historical flasks, Coca-Cola amber and straight-sided bottles, poison bottles, apothecary jars, milk bottles. Many are marked on the base (e.g., "Wheaton", "Made in Italy", "Taiwan").
- Coca-Cola commemorative and NASCAR bottles (1990s) are full-retail collectibles worth ~$7–35 a six-pack.
- New apothecary jars with "bubble" lids (Belgium, India) look old in photos.

## Market
- Serious bottle collecting is club-driven (FOHBC, regional shows) and pays well for rarities; the general eBay market for common bottles is saturated and slow.
- Big mixed lots rarely sell well shipped (weight and breakage); they sell locally to decorators at $1–5 a bottle.

## What sellers usually miss
- Colour and age tells (applied lips, pontils, whittle marks) – a cobalt or amber poison or bitters mixed into a "box of old bottles" is the find.
- Paper labels: intact 19th-century labels multiply value.
- Rare towns on Coke bottle bases and early straight-sided Cokes.
- Conversely: owners overprice hobbleskirt Cokes, Beam decanters, Clorox jugs and Italian milk bottles because Lens shows asking prices.

## Shipping / handling
- Each bottle in 2" of bubble, double-boxed. Single bottle $8–14; lots get heavy fast (a 70-bottle lot is 80–120 lb – not shippable profitably).
- Never ship decanters that still contain liquor (illegal on eBay and USPS).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** type + colour + embossing + era: "cobalt poison bottle Owl Drug", "amber straight sided Coca-Cola [town]", "aqua embossed druggist bottle [city]", "labeled laudanum"; Junk: "old bottle lot", "antique glass", "rare" on hobbleskirts and Beam decanters. Example: `Antique Cobalt Blue Owl Drug Co Poison Bottle 8" Embossed Ribbed 1900s`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for singles (coloured poisons, bitters, early sodas, rare-town Cokes); mixed lots local to decorators at $1–5 a bottle (shipping kills them). Rarities to FOHBC-connected auctions.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (hobbleskirt $8; Clorox $12; Beam $11; aqua apothecary $18; labeled laudanum $101; cobalt Owl Drug $510), accept −15%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo the bottle against a white background in daylight; then lip, base (marks, pontil), seams, embossing/label in raking light, held to light for cracks and haze, ruler for height.
- **State:** height, colour, embossing text exactly, estimated date range with reasons (lip type, seam height), chips/cracks/haze by photo number, tumbling or cleaning disclosed, repro check (Wheaton, Italy, Taiwan base marks).
- **Pack / haul:** each bottle in 2" bubble, double-boxed, $8–14 single; never ship decanters with liquor in them; lots not shippable profitably.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Owl Drug] cobalt poison, [8"], tooled lip and seam stopping below it — c.[1900–1915] original, not a Wheaton repro; no chips, no haze; the cobalt figurals bring [$200–600]."

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; passenger-seat size (single bottles fine; mega-lots usually not). Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$10) − shipping to Dylan (~$10–20) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Practically: only pre-1910 coloured poisons, bitters, early sodas, labelled medicines or rare-town Cokes with a sold median of $100+ bought at ≤ ~$25–30.
- Mixed lots: only local pickup, and only if the photo shows at least one high-value bottle by the tells above.
