## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass fused in a kiln from layers of colored frit/enamel on clear glass, then slumped into a mold. Peggy Karr Glass (New Jersey, 1978–2010s): textured frosted underside, vivid enamel designs (fish, florals, Christmas), Peggy Karr gold foil label or etched "PK" signature. The example is a Peggy Karr "Rainbow Trout" large oval platter with fishing-fly border, with label.
- Peggy Karr dates: the Schiffer book on the company describes its fused-glass production as beginning in 1987 in Randolph, NJ (the 1978 date above is unverified and may be when the studio itself began). Process: decorated glass is set on a clay-dusted ceramic mold and fired to about 1600 °F, leaving a pebble texture on the back; each finished piece is inscribed "PKG…" (Glass Messages). Products: plates, bowls, trays, platters, clocks, ornaments, roundels — florals, animals, fish, still life, seasonal and special-order designs.
Higgins (Chicago, 1948–present): enameled "glass on glass" designs with bubbles and lines; "higgins" gold signature on face (1950s–60s Dearborn era with "Dearborn" mark or "Rondelay" etc.) — top value.
- Correction on Higgins marks: the gold screened "higgins" signature on the face is itself the Dearborn Glass (Bedford Park, IL) mark, 1957–1964 (Higgins Glass Studio FAQ); there is no separate "Dearborn" stamp — unsigned Dearborn pieces carried paper labels. Rondelay (interlocking fused squares/discs) is a product form, not a mark.

Signature and era guide (Lancaster Farming, Higgins FAQ):
- **Chicago studio, c. 1948–57**: lowercase "higgins" signature; a stylised dancing-man figure sometimes cast in. Most important to museums and serious collectors.
- **Dearborn Glass, 1957–64**: gold screened "higgins" on the face; sometimes a colour signature on the reverse. Mass-produced; the most common signed Higgins.
- **Haeger Potteries, c. 1965–66**: gold signature continues; Rondelays produced.
- **Riverside studio (later)**: "Frances Stewart Higgins" on Frances's work; "Michael Higgins" with date and title on his sculptures.

## Makers, brands and variants
Higgins, Peggy Karr, Annieglass (gold rims), Bullseye studio artists, Fiesta-era studio, Mexican Bullseye-like imports.
- **Higgins lines**: "Barbarac Jewels" (spelling as given by Lancaster Farming; made from discarded grey television glass at Dearborn), Rogue ashtrays and clocks (highly collectible), Gemspread and Filigree colour patterns, Rondelays, mobiles, torn-paper and woven-copper-wire techniques. Pattern and size drive value (Higgins FAQ).
- **Peggy Karr**: retired patterns and holiday/fish designs; the Schiffer book has a rarity scale and pattern index — use it to price rare patterns.
- **Annieglass** (California studio; general knowledge): slumped glass with gold or platinum rims (e.g. Roman Antique line); Terapeak platter median $55.

### Quick ID checklist
- Higgins: small gold lowercase "higgins" on the face (Dearborn 1957–64) or etched/coloured signature on the back; look for bubbles, enamel lines and layered colour.
- Peggy Karr: pebble-textured frosted underside, "PKG"/Peggy Karr inscription or foil label, bright enamel imagery (fish, birds, florals, holiday).
- Annieglass: gold or platinum rim band, signature on the underside.
- Shape and size: measure; large platters, clocks and mobiles beat small dishes.
- Pattern name: Higgins patterns and Peggy Karr retired designs move value.
- Condition: angle light across the face for scratches; check rim for chips.
- Unsigned "Higgins-style" pieces: assume not Higgins without a signature or label.

### Golden-egg tells
- Early Chicago-studio Higgins (c. 1948–57) — multiples of a Dearborn dish ($100–800+ tier vs Terapeak $63 bowl median) — lowercase signature, studio techniques, sometimes the cast dancing-man figure.
- Higgins Rogue ashtray or clock (Dearborn) — collectible line above common dishes (Lancaster Farming) — named line, gold signature, clock movement.
- Higgins "Barbarac Jewels" pattern — distinctive grey TV-glass ground — gold signature on grey glass with jewel-like inclusions.
- Large Higgins platters, mobiles and screens — several times a small dish (verify sold) — size plus gold signature.
- Peggy Karr large retired platter (fish, holiday) — $50–150 vs $20–50 for small plates (entry tiers) — pebble back, PKG inscription, large size, retired pattern per Schiffer index.
- Generic craft fused plates — the common version, $10–30 — no signature, simple frit patterns.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Signatures and labels; even enamel color; textured underside; no inclusions of dust.
- Higgins: crisp enamel lines, controlled bubbles, gold signature not rubbed off.
- Peggy Karr: uniform pebble texture on the back from the clay-dusted mold; sealed surface (colour fused between layers, not painted on top).
- Cheap production: painted-on decoration that scratches off, rough unfinished edges, no signature.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on edges, scratches on face, devitrification haze. Fused glass breaks in shipping if poorly packed. Worn gold signatures lower Higgins value; ground-down rims indicate chip repair.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Higgins knockoffs unsigned; Peggy Karr not widely faked. Higgins Glass Studio continues to make Rondelays and other designs — newer studio pieces are not Dearborn-era; check the signature style.

## Market
Moderate; Peggy Karr sells best in fishing/lodge and holiday seasons; Higgins has strong mid-century buyers. Many small Higgins items are available under $50 (Lancaster Farming), so only the better lines and sizes clear the profit rule.

## What sellers usually miss
Higgins signatures (small gold "higgins") on "retro glass ashtray".

## Shipping / handling
Large platter ~17×12", 4–6 lb, fragile: $20–28 double boxed.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Signature/label, dimensions, chip check under light, design name.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker and quote the signature or inscription exactly (e.g. "gold 'higgins' signature on the face — Dearborn Glass era, 1957–64"; "PKG inscription, pebble-textured back"). Give the pattern or design name, length, width and depth. State that the piece was checked under raking light for chips and scratches, and photograph the rim, the signature and the back.
