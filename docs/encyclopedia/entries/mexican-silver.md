## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Silver made in Mexico from the 1930s revival onward, centred on Taxco (Guerrero) after William Spratling opened Taller de las Delicias in 1931, plus Mexico City silversmiths and retailers (Sanborns, Tane, Maciel, Ortega). Forms: jewelry (cuffs, link bracelets, brooches, earrings, necklaces), hollowware (bowls, pitchers, candlesticks, trays, tea services), flatware, figural items (cocktail picks, salts), and souvenir pieces.
Photo tells: heavy-gauge silver with dark oxidation in the recesses; pre-Columbian, Aztec or Deco-geometric motifs; inlays of amethyst, turquoise, malachite, obsidian, abalone, rosewood or tortoise; "married metals" (silver/copper/brass inlaid together, a Los Castillo hallmark); hand-hammered surfaces on hollowware. The money is on the back: a stamp cluster such as "STERLING / MEXICO / 925" plus an eagle with a number, or a maker name, or (post-1980) an alphanumeric code like "TS-24".
Not silver: "ALPACA", "MEXICO" alone, "German silver", "Mexico Silver Plate", "EP". Alpaca (nickel silver) is the most common false lead: same designs, yellowish-grey tone, often brass showing at wear points.

## Makers, brands and variants
**Marking system (silvercollection.it):**
- **Before 1948:** "silver"/"sterling", often with "Mexico" or a town name and a fineness (925, 950, 980). The 980 and 990 marks are typical of 1930s–40s Spratling-era and early Taxco work.
- **1948 to c.1980: the eagle assay mark** — a spread eagle with a number assigned to a maker (e.g. Pedro Castillo eagles 1, 2, 3 and 120; Oscar Bach 17; Codan 7). Numbers were sometimes loaned between workshops, so an eagle number supports but does not prove an attribution.
- **c.1980 to present: letter-letter-number codes.** First letter = city (T Taxco, M Mexico City, G Guadalajara, C Cuernavaca), second letter = maker's initial, number = registration. So "TS-24" is a Taxco maker with an S name — modern production, valued mainly by weight unless the maker is a recognised name.
**Makers and lines (marks and dates, partly from sterling-silver-jewelry entry):**
- **William Spratling (1931–67):** "WS" monogram in a circle, "SPRATLING SILVER", "SPRATLING MADE IN MEXICO", often 980. Post-1967 "Spratling" pieces made at his ranch workshop from original moulds are a separate, lower tier (general knowledge, verify).
- **Hector Aguilar / Taller Borda (1939–c.1962–66):** conjoined "HA" monogram, "940", "TAXCO"; heavy rope-twist and panel links. Partnered with Coro 1943–50 (silvercollection.it).
- **Los Castillo (from 1939):** "LOS CASTILLO TAXCO" with design number; married metals, feather-mosaic and jaguar forms.
- **Antonio Pineda:** "ANTONIO" inside a crown, "970"; sculptural modernist with stones.
- **Margot de Taxco (Margot van Voorhies Carr, 1940s–70s):** "MARGOT DE TAXCO" + design number; champlevé enamel.
- **Matl / Matilde Poulat (Mexico City, 1934–60) and Matl Salas (her nephew Ricardo Salas, after 1960):** mark "MATL" or "Matl" (often with "Mexico" and 980/925); figural religious and floral pieces with turquoise, amethyst and coral cabochons, often doves, crosses and baskets. "Matl-style" in a title means it is **not** Matl (triage note).
- **Fred Davis, Enrique Ledesma, Felipe Martínez (Piedra y Plata), Salvador Terán, Sigi Pineda, Frederick Davis:** collector names, typically $150–600 for good pieces (general knowledge, verify).
- **Hollowware houses (Mexico City):** Sanborns ("SANBORN MEXICO STERLING 0.925 HECHO EN MEXICO" — Heritage lot of a 7-piece service weighing 357 troy oz), Tane ("TANE" in an oval), Maciel, Ortega, Alfredo Sciarrotta. Value is weight-led with a design premium.
- **ATI Mexico 925:** modern mass Taxco maker; worth about melt plus a little, not a premium mark (triage note).
- **Modern TS/TM-coded pieces:** chain bracelets, hoops, tourist rings; resale ~$1.3–2.5/g.
**Related items that ride in "Mexican silver" lots:** silver 8 Reales coins ("REPUBLICA MEXICANA", eagle on cactus, 1824–1897, ~27 g, .903 silver ≈ $48 melt) — ask for the reverse (date/mint/assayer) and weight; cast copies are light or off-tone (triage note; see world-coins). Mexican copper, Alpaca serving pieces and Saltillo serapes are not silver.

### Golden-egg tells
- Spratling "WS" circle monogram or "SPRATLING SILVER" (pre-1967) — ~10–45× an unsigned heavy cuff ($984–3,998 at Skinner vs ~$80–90 melt) — round monogram stamp, 980 fineness, pre-Columbian motifs, rosewood or amethyst inlay.
- Hector Aguilar conjoined "HA" with 940 — ~10–40× ($861 for three bracelets; $3,825 a pair of cuffs, Bonhams 2021) — two letters joined into one glyph, "TAXCO", heavy rope or panel construction.
- Antonio Pineda crown mark with 970 — ~10–20× ($923–1,722) — the word "Antonio" under a crown; tension-set stones, sculptural forms.
- Los Castillo married metals — ~6–30× ($492–2,583) — copper and brass bands inlaid flush into silver; "LOS CASTILLO TAXCO" plus design number.
- Margot de Taxco enamel — ~10× an import pendant ($250–1,000) — opaque champlevé enamel in Deco or Asian patterns; any chip halves it.
- Matl / Matilde Poulat — ~3–15× melt ($200–1,500, general knowledge, verify) — "MATL" stamp (not "Matl-style"), figural doves/crosses/flower baskets with turquoise and coral.
- Pre-1948 980/990 fineness with "TAXCO" and no eagle — ~2–5× an eagle-era generic piece (general knowledge, verify) — very high fineness number, soft hand-forged look, early Deco designs.
- Eagle mark with a low or documented number + maker name — ~1.5–3× weight value (general knowledge, verify) — spread eagle in a small cartouche with a 1–3 digit number; cross-check the number on silvercollection.it before paying a premium.
- Heavy signed hollowware (Sanborns, Tane, Maciel) — priced from weight up; a 300–600 g bowl ≈ $400–800 melt alone — hand-hammered surface, "0.925 HECHO EN MEXICO" with a house name; ask for a scale photo.

### Quick ID checklist
- Ask for a photo of the stamp cluster on the back and a kitchen-scale photo in grams.
- Any of "ALPACA", "SILVER PLATE", "EP", "German silver", or "MEXICO" with no fineness → not silver; walk away from silver pricing.
- Eagle with number → 1948–c.1980; letter-letter-number code (TS-, TM-, TL-) → post-1980; neither, with 980/990 and "TAXCO" → likely pre-1948.
- Look for a designer name or monogram (WS, HA, ANTONIO in crown, LOS CASTILLO, MARGOT, MATL).
- "-style" or "attributed" in the title means the seller is borrowing a name; no mark = melt.
- Inlay check: married metals and enamel are value multipliers; glued resin "turquoise" chips are tourist work.
- Bare "925" stamp with no Mexico/TAXCO/code = generic import (triage note).
- ATI Mexico 925 = modern mass production, melt plus a little.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Fineness 925 standard; 940, 950, 970, 980 and 990 appear on better and earlier pieces. Sterling is non-magnetic.
- Quality: thick gauge, crisp edges, hand-applied details, stones set in bezels (not glued), hand-hammered hollowware with even planishing marks. Hinges on cuffs and box clasps hand-fitted.
- Cheap production: thin stamped sheet, hollow beads, glued chips, cast pieces with pitting, bright uniform machine polish, "925" stamped crookedly.
- Old vs new: old pieces show softened high points, deep dark patina in recesses and hand-cut stones; new pieces are bright and uniform, with alphanumeric codes.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing or replaced stones, lifted or chipped enamel (Margot), cracked inlay, broken hinges or clasps, solder repairs (lumpy grey seams), polishing that softens marks, dents and splits in hollowware, weighted candlesticks with cracked filler. Over-polishing removes the oxidation that buyers expect; never strip patina before selling.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Fake Taxco and designer marks on Alpaca and on modern import silver (sterling-silver-jewelry entry).
- Spratling and Aguilar designs are reproduced; post-1967 Spratling ranch production is legitimate but a lower tier.
- "Matl-style", "Margot-style" and "Los Castillo-style" listings are borrowed names, not attributions.
- Eagle numbers were loaned or misused, so an eagle number alone cannot carry a top-designer price (silvercollection.it).

## Market
Deep, fast collector market for named designers (days to a few weeks); unsigned and modern pieces sell steadily at weight-based prices. Silver at ~$60/oz (Oct 2026) puts a high floor under heavy pieces and makes melt the dominant value for anything unsigned. Hollowware sells slower (4–10 weeks) but weight protects the downside.

## What sellers usually miss
- The tiny eagle-with-number stamp or a designer monogram; they list it as "Mexico silver".
- Weight: a "pretty bracelet" can be 80 g of 950 silver (~$140 melt).
- High-fineness 980 pieces from the 1930s–40s.
- Dealer tell (triage 2026-10-13): a Phoenix CL seller writing all-caps "AVAILABLE FOR PURCHASE IS A ..." with exact dimensions is a volume reseller (Greenleaf gouache, Cynthia December etching, Bruce Freund vase, Tom Knapp bronze, Peru charger, Mexican copper, Navajo pot) — dealer-priced, skip.

## Shipping / handling
Jewelry: padded envelope or small box, $5–6. Hollowware: double-box with foam, $12–25 depending on weight; insure anything over $100. Pack stones and enamel so nothing rubs.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Taxco", "Sterling" / "925", the maker as stamped (Spratling, Los Castillo, Margot de Taxco, Sanborns, Matl, Pineda), "Eagle [number]" for 1948–80 assay marks, form, stone/enamel, weight in grams, "mid-century". Junk: "Mexican silver" alone (matches imports), "Alpaca" (not silver). Example: `Los Castillo Taxco Sterling Silver Married Metals Brooch Eagle 25 Mid-Century 32g`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for signed and eagle-marked pieces (collectors worldwide); unsigned TS/TM-coded modern Taxco near melt, coin shop or lot it. Hollowware: eBay only if light; heavy trays local or auction.
- **Price position:** signed/eagle-marked list at the sold median, accept −15%, floor 1.5× melt; modern coded pieces melt +10–30% firm.
- **Photos:** first photo whole piece on black velvet. Then macro of every mark (eagle number, maker, "STERLING MEXICO 925", TS/TM code), the back, hinges/clasps, enamel or stones close, on a gram scale.
- **State:** every mark exactly as stamped, weight in grams, fineness, maker and era with the eagle number or code, dimensions, stone loss, repairs, patina left as found; "attributed to" unless the mark is unambiguous.
- **Pack / haul:** jewelry $5–6 padded mailer or small box; hollowware double-boxed with foam $12–25; insure over $100; wrap enamel and stones so nothing rubs.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Sterling, marked [STERLING / MEXICO / 925 / eagle 25 / maker], [##] g; the eagle assay mark dates it to 1948–c.1980; measurements and every mark are pictured."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$6 jewelry / ~$15 hollowware) − ship to Dylan (same) − price ≥ max($50, price). Unsigned, ATI and TS-coded pieces: pay ≤ 80% of melt (≈ ≤ $1.10/g) and only in quantity that makes $50 profit. Signed eagle-era pieces: ≤ 40% of the maker's sold median. Designer pieces (Spratling, Aguilar, Pineda, Los Castillo, Margot, Matl): buy only from a clear mark photo; top-tier pieces may need specialist authentication for high-value resale — price conservatively. Fits a passenger seat in all forms.
