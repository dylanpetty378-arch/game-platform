## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Bone-china figures from Royal Doulton (Burslem) with an HN number (after Harry Nixon, 1913 to present). Base mark: the lion-on-crown backstamp, "Royal Doulton", "Made in England", the title, HN number and "© Royal Doulton" with a year. Thick, glossy, hand-painted glaze. Characters: crowd-pleasing street vendors (Mask Seller HN2103, 1953–1995), Balloon Sellers, the "Pretty Ladies" series.
- The HN series began in 1913 with 20 figures; HN1 was Charles Vyse's boy in a nightshirt, named "Darling" by Queen Mary (ATG). Darling is still made, so only the very early issues are valuable.
- Genuine figures are hollow with a small, discreet firing hole in the base; a very large hole is not Doulton practice (Rinker).
- Typical height 5–10 in. Each model has a fixed height; a different height from the catalogue is a warning sign (Rinker).
- Title words: "Royal Doulton", "HN", the figure name, "bone china", "England", "retired".

## Makers, brands and variants
- Early HN (1913–1938, HN1–~HN2000): Art Deco ladies, hand-painted by artists; signed numbers, high value. *(Correction on deepening: HN numbers in the 1900s ran to about 1940 — e.g. Lavinia HN1955 is a 1940 introduction — and HN2103 Mask Seller is 1953, so HN2000 falls in the late 1940s; pre-war figures are roughly HN1–HN1900s.)*
- Mid (1938–1975): character figures by Peggy Davies, Leslie Harradine, Mary Nicoll.
- Later (1975–2000s): huge volume of "Pretty Ladies", seasonal, Classics; low value.
- Production moved to Indonesia in 2004–05: "Made in Indonesia" (lower value). Antique HQ notes the post-2000 backstamp names the country (Indonesia or Bangladesh).
- Limited editions with certificates; colour variations (same model, different HN number).

**Modellers and what they mean (ATG):** Charles Noke (chief modeller from 1889, launched the HN range and the first character jugs in 1934); Charles Vyse and Phoebe Stabler (early sculptors); Leslie Harradine (Art Deco bathing beauties and ladies of the 1920s–30s, and long-running ladies such as Lavinia); Peggy Davies (post-war ladies and characters); Mary Nicoll (characters). The most desirable figures are mostly from the first half of the 20th century, especially those withdrawn in the mid-1930s, in 1938, or during WWII restrictions.

**Rarity drivers:** short production runs, trial colourways, commercial failures, early withdrawal and small editions. Example: Bluebeard HN75 (1917, withdrawn before 1936) sold for £4,000 at Louis Taylor in Sept 2012 against a £1,200–1,600 estimate (ATG).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the HN number, title and any date code from the base; look it up for introduction/withdrawal years.
- Backstamp style: "Made in England" under the lion and crown (c. 1930 on); "Fine China" (1973 on); country of manufacture named on post-2000 marks.
- Small numerals beside the crown (1928–1954): add 1927 to get the year made.
- Is there a painted-in "Potted by Doulton & Co." or artist signature (early)?
- Check hands, fingers, hat brims, masks and other protrusions for chips and restored areas.
- Scratched-through or drilled backstamp = a second.
- Height matches the catalogue height.
- Is the base painted over? That can hide a replaced or fake mark.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
English bone china with a heavy glaze. Older examples have finer face painting and more muted colours. Gold or printed backstamp; pre-1950s may have an artist's signature. "Potted by Doulton & Co" on very early pieces.
- **Dating the base mark (Antique HQ):** lion, crown and roundel (1901–22); a crownless variant (1922–27, uncommon); lion-crown-roundel with a production number (1928–36); "Made in England" below the crown from c. 1930; "Bone China" on bone china; "Fine China" from 1973; post-2000 lion logo with "Fine China", country and copyright date.
- **Copyright date ≠ year made.** A "© 1937" figure could have been produced until 2004; production usually began about a year after the copyright date (Antique HQ).
- **Date code numerals** printed to the right of the crown, 1928–1954: add 1927 (Roman numerals until 1930, Arabic after).
- Prototype marks such as "Survey Figure", "Factory Proof", "Design Sample" or "Not Produced for Sale" denote pre-production pieces (Lion & Unicorn); treat such claims with suspicion without provenance.
- Old vs new: earlier pieces have softer, more muted colour and finer faces; later "Pretty Ladies" are brighter and more uniform.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on hands, fingers, hat brims and masks; restored fingers (check under UV); crazing on early ones; worn paint. Restorations are common on character figures with protruding parts. Also check for hairline cracks at the base. Restored areas often look slightly matte, a different white, or thicker than the surrounding glaze. For the rarest models collectors will accept restoration if no perfect example exists (ATG); for common ones a restoration kills the sale.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Some Chinese copies and fake backstamps exist, but they are uncommon. Watch for "seconds": a scratched-through backstamp (factory reject) cuts value 30–50%. (Rinker notes that claims of "prototype" or "factory second" status should be treated with suspicion, so verify any such claim.) Rinker's example of a fake Lavinia had a base mark without a proper HN number and an odd "WW" mark; check that the mark, height, workmanship and colours match catalogue photos.

## Market
Very large supply from estates; prices are down 60–80% from their 1990s peak. eBay: hundreds sold per month. Days to sell: 20–60. ATG: most HN figures and character jugs are worth under £100 and most sell for under £30 without reserve at auction; prices have softened since the 1980s–90s as major collectors left and supply rose, while rarities still make high three and four figures.

## What sellers usually miss
A low HN number (under ~1500) or an artist-signed base; Art Deco 1920s–30s ladies; colourway variants with different HN numbers. Also the 1928–54 date-code numerals and early withdrawal dates.

## Shipping / handling
A 9" figure needs a double box with its extremities padded; $16–22.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the base mark showing the HN number and copyright year. Disclose chips and restoration (UV check). Give the height.

### What to tell the buyer
State the figure name, HN number, modeller and production years (introduced/withdrawn), and the backstamp as it reads, including any date-code numeral and the year it indicates. Explain that the © year on the base is the design copyright, not the production date. Give the height. State condition from a close inspection and a UV check ("no chips, cracks or restoration found under UV"), or disclose what was found. Mention a second (scratched mark) or Made in Indonesia if present.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$18 out − ~$18 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Common characters at $30+ never qualify. Buy only low-HN/Deco/limited/rare colourway pieces, with no restoration, at ≤ ~30% of the sold median.
