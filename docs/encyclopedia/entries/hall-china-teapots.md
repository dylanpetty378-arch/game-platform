## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Heavy vitrified pottery, glossy solid-colour glaze (often with gold decoration), "HALL" in a circle or "Hall's Superior Quality Kitchenware" on the base. Shapes are the key: common Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Windshield, Hollywood vs. novelty Aladdin, Airflow, Automobile, Football, Basketball, Donut, Rhythm, Nautilus, Birdcage. Seller words: "Hall teapot", "vintage teapot gold trim", "Jewel Tea Autumn Leaf".

Photo checklist by shape (dates from the Museum of Ceramics, East Liverpool, unless noted):
- **Boston** (1916 onward; knob-lid in 7 sizes, sunken-lid in 4): plain round body, short spout. Still made into the 2000s, so a Boston can be any age. The 7-cup green and brown Bostons were the first Hall teapots Jewel Tea carried (1924).
- **Philadelphia** (1920s; 7 sizes, 1½–10 cups): tall, stepped-shoulder body; one of the first shapes with gold trim.
- **Los Angeles** (1926; 3 sizes): tall, flared, often cobalt with gold band and lace; also celery green. Made into the 1960s.
- **Hollywood** (1927; 4, 5, 6 and 8 cup): oval with stepped lid; found in Gold Decorated Group 8 and in matte Orchid.
- **Albany** (1929): ribbed with a pointed finial, often black with gold. Easily confused with the **Ceylonator** (1930s), which has an infuser.
- **Streamline** (early 1930s; gold-decorated from 1937): standard gold on top, edge and spout.
- **Globe** (late 1930s–40s): round with a squared spout; by 1942 offered in about 18 colours, usually with gold. The **No Drip** (early 1950s) is the second Globe shape, with an inverted spout tip.
- **Novelty series, 1938**: **Automobile** (car body, spout as the hood), **Donut** (ring with a hole through the body), **Basket** (6-cup), **Birdcage** (embossed birds in a cage), **Airflow** (8-cup 1938, 6-cup added 1939; teardrop streamline). **Nautilus** (1939, shell spiral). Football, Basketball and Rhythm belong with this premium group (shape names per the existing entry; dates unverified).
- **Aladdin** (oval 1939–1963, by J. Palin Thorley; round 1948 in 6 and 7 cup): genie-lamp shape with long spout; oval or round lid opening, with or without an infuser. The Autumn Leaf Aladdin is the most common Aladdin seen.
- **Hook Cover** (1940, 6-cup only), **Windshield** (1941, flattened oval "windshield" front), **Parade** (early 1940s; gold from the mid-1950s, incl. the Gold Label squiggle), **Sani-Grid** (1940s, 3 and 6 cup), **Victorian series** (1946–49, six shapes), **T-Ball** (1948, round and square, side tea-bag cavities, for Bacharach), **E-Style** (1950s–70s, Thorley; Cameo Rose version for Jewel Tea), **Flat-sides** (1961, 1½-cup restaurant pot, Bob Evans; Golden Glo full-gold glaze version), **Pear** (1970, Don Schreckengost).
- **Promotional/contract shapes**: **Bru-O-Lator** for McCormick (1933–1996, often green-and-white or platinum trim), **Dodecagon** (12-sided, for Forman Brothers), **Twinspout** (patent 1938, for Twinspout Pottery Co., NY), **Cube** (1926 patents, 2-cup, spout and handle inside the cube outline), **Tea for Two / Tea for Four** (1930–1996).
- **Late commemoratives**: Ronald Reagan caricature (1982), Anne Hathaway Cottage / Ye Olde McCormick Tea House (1984 and 1985, year above the door), 1993 Autumn Leaf Automobile (60th anniversary of the 1933 decal).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hall China Co.** (East Liverpool, Ohio – general knowledge, verify): teapots from the 1920s on; shapes listed above. The Donut shape was introduced in 1938 and was one of Hall's most popular (kovels). Hall made more than 100 different marks, including ones for railroads, airlines, restaurants and stores such as Montgomery Ward, Sears and Jewel Tea (Post-Bulletin). Hall is now part of HLC Inc. together with Homer Laughlin (Post-Bulletin).
- **Gold Decorated line**: Hall's first decorated teapot line and very popular (Museum of Ceramics). Named gold treatments: standard gold trim, "Gold Label" (gold squiggle on Parade and others), Gold Decorated Group 8 (Hollywood), Golden Glo (all-over gold glaze), platinum trim (Bru-O-Lator), and Chrysler decoration (Dodecagon). Circa-1960 Thorley shapes were sometimes gilded with grape designs set with rhinestones (Chipstone).
- **Jewel Tea Autumn Leaf**: Hall-made premium line for Jewel Tea of Chicago, given through door-to-door salesmen; first pieces were teapots, the decal dates from 1933, Hall discontinued the line in 1978 and leftover stock got a 1978 date added to the backstamp; from about 1990 Hall made new shapes for the pattern, often from old moulds with original decals (Post-Bulletin; Museum of Ceramics for 1933). Aladdin teapot, utility jugs, etc. National Autumn Leaf Collectors Club is the reference body.
- **Hallcraft / Eva Zeisel** designs (e.g. Big Ear cookie jar, Tomorrow's Classic – general knowledge, verify) – mid-century collectors.
- **Colours**: cobalt, Chinese red, emerald multiply value; green, yellow, brown are common. Colours recorded on museum examples include cobalt with gold, Chinese red (Donut), Marine (Airflow), chartreuse (No Drip), lime green with gold, baby blue, Old Pink, canary (Parade), matte Orchid (Hollywood), black with gold (Albany), maroon, rose, ivory.
- **Restaurant-ware**: common.

### Quick ID checklist
- Base mark: "HALL" in a circle, "Hall's Superior Quality Kitchenware", or a gold "Hall … 6 cup Made in U.S.A." stamp with a numeric decoration code (photograph it).
- Name the shape from the outline (car = Automobile, ring = Donut, shell spiral = Nautilus, cage = Birdcage, genie lamp = Aladdin); shape is most of the value.
- Colour: cobalt, Chinese red or emerald are premium; green, yellow and brown are common.
- Gold: check spout tip, lid rim and handle for wear; a named gold line (Gold Label, Golden Glo) helps.
- Lid fits and matches the body colour and shape; Aladdin lids differ for oval vs round openings.
- Birdcage finial: open ring = early; closed finial = 1990s reissue.
- Autumn Leaf backstamp with "1978" = leftover/late stock; unfamiliar Autumn Leaf shapes may be post-1990 production.
- Weight: genuine Hall is heavy and glassy; a light, unmarked donut is the Chinese lookalike.
- Look for chips at spout tip, lid flange and finial; Hall glaze shows chips as white body.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hall's single-fire vitrified china is heavy, non-crazing and very glossy. Gold decoration (gold label line) wears with washing. Base stamps changed over time; the circle "HALL" and "Superior Quality Kitchenware" are the common ones (general knowledge, verify). Chinese-made lookalike donut teapots are lighter and unmarked.
- Body: dense vitrified whiteware; tap rings clear. Crazing in the glaze is unusual on Hall and should make you look again at the maker (general knowledge, verify).
- Gold: applied over the glaze and fired; worn gold appears as a faint brown/purple shadow line. Rhinestone-set gilding exists on c. 1960 Thorley shapes (Chipstone).
- Marks: a Hall 6-cup teapot of c. 1960 carried "hall" in a circle plus "made in u.s.a. 1528" in blue and "hall, 6 cup made in u.s.a. 0917" in gold, i.e. a body stamp and a separate decoration stamp (Chipstone). The earliest Hall backstamp on the Molded teapot was used 1903–1913 (Museum of Ceramics).
- Old vs new: a 1978-dated Autumn Leaf mark marks late stock; Birdcage open-ring vs closed finial; commemoratives often carry a year or event in the mark or decoration (Anne Hathaway Cottage year over the door; Nautilus mould reused in 1999 for an East Liverpool Alumni auction).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on spouts, lids and finials; lid mismatches (colour or shape); gold wear; hairlines; staining inside. Any chip on a novelty shape cuts value sharply (general knowledge, verify).
- Ask for photos of the spout tip head-on, the lid flange (underside rim) and inside the pot under light; tea staining hides hairlines.
- Infuser models (Aladdin, Ceylonator) are often missing the infuser; state whether present.
- Restored spouts and finials show as a slightly different gloss or colour under raking light (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese-made lookalike donut teapots sell $8–15. Hall itself reissued some shapes and colours later (general knowledge, verify) – check base marks.
- Hall reintroduced several old dinnerware and teapot lines in 1985 (Museum of Ceramics).
- Birdcage was reissued in the 1990s; early pieces have an open ring finial, later a closed finial (Museum of Ceramics).
- The Musical teapot was continued for China Specialties (a 1990s reissue client) (Museum of Ceramics).
- 1993 Autumn Leaf Automobile is a commemorative, not a 1930s piece (Museum of Ceramics).
- Autumn Leaf: post-1990 Hall production uses old moulds and decals, so shape and decal alone do not prove age (Post-Bulletin).
- Nautilus shape reused in 1999 for an East Liverpool Alumni auction (Museum of Ceramics).

## Market
Collector market is older and shrinking for common shapes; novelty shapes and rare colours still sell to Hall collectors. Autumn Leaf has a dedicated club following (general knowledge, verify) — the National Autumn Leaf Collectors Club (Post-Bulletin). "The unusual" sells best in Autumn Leaf (Post-Bulletin).

## What sellers usually miss
Shape names. A seller calling it "Hall teapot set" with an Automobile or Football shape is the win; colour (cobalt, Chinese red, emerald) multiplies. Also missed: named gold lines (Gold Label, Golden Glo), contract shapes (Bru-O-Lator, Twinspout, T-Ball, Dodecagon) and whether an infuser is present.

## Shipping / handling
Pottery, 2–4 lb per piece, double box; ~$15–20 in. eBay 13%. Wrap lid separately and tape it to stop rattle; pad spout and finial.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, shape name, colour, decoration, cup capacity, base mark, lid matches, chips/hairlines/gold wear. Photograph base mark, spout, lid edge and finial. Title: "Hall China [shape] teapot, [colour], [cups]-cup, [decoration]".

### What to tell the buyer
Hall China Company of East Liverpool, Ohio made its teapots in a heavy vitrified china with a hard, glossy glaze. Name the shape and its introduction year from the Hall shape list: Automobile, Donut, Basket and Birdcage date from 1938, Airflow from 1938–39, Nautilus from 1939, and the oval Aladdin from 1939–1963. Quote the backstamp exactly as it reads, and give the cup size and colour. Say whether the gold is crisp or worn, whether the lid is original and matching, and whether an infuser is included. If the piece is a known reissue or commemorative, say so (for example the 1993 Autumn Leaf Automobile, a closed-finial Birdcage, or Autumn Leaf with a 1978 or post-1990 mark), because collectors check for these.

## Buy rule
Buy when: novelty shape (Automobile/Football/Basketball/Donut/Nautilus/Airflow/Rhythm) ≤ $60, no chips. Pass when: Boston/New York/Philadelphia shapes at any price over $15. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
