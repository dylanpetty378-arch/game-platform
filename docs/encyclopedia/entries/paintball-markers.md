## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Air-powered paintball guns. Value tells: milled aluminium bodies with brand engraving (Eclipse, Dye, Shocker), electronic grip frames, "Sheridan", "Nelson", "PMI Piranha", 12-gram stock-class guns. Cheap: plastic Brass Eagle bodies, rental Tippmanns, 12-oz/20-oz CO2 tanks with expired hydro-test date (5-year rule).
Families by look:
- **Vintage pumps and pistols (1980s)**: Nel-Spot 007 (Nelson Paint Co., Iron Mountain MI; the marker used in the first paintball game, chronicled by Sports Illustrated in 1981), Splatmaster (spring-powered .50 cal plastic pistol by JT; NSG Splatmaster pump), Sheridan PGP/P68 pistols and pumps, PMI Piranha pumps, 12-gram "stock class" guns that feed from a horizontal tube on top.
- **Classic semi-autos and pumps (late 1980s–90s)**: Tippmann SMG-60 / SMG-68 (full-auto capable, stripper-clip fed), Line SI Bushmaster, AGD Automag, WGP Autococker (a rod and pneumatic ram along the top), Sheridan / Kingman, Bob Long, Shocker (Smart Parts), Angel (WDP).
- **Entry and rental (1995–now)**: Tippmann 98 Custom and A-5 (cast aluminium clamshell), Brass Eagle Talon/Stingray/Raptor (plastic body), Spyder (Kingman; aluminium tube body with a screw-on side plate).
- **High-end electro (2000s–now)**: Planet Eclipse Ego/Geo/LV/CS, Dye DM/DSR/M2, Smart Parts Shocker/Ion, Bob Long Intimidator, Angel; short barrels, electronic trigger frames with a battery, HPA tank, and an electronic hopper on top.
The model name is usually laser-engraved or milled on the body side; Tippmann and Brass Eagle have raised moulded names; serial numbers are on the body or under the grip frame.

## Makers, brands and variants
Tippmann (98, A-5, Cronus), Brass Eagle (Talon, Stingray, Raptor), Spyder/Kingman, Planet Eclipse, Dye, Smart Parts/Shocker, WGP Autococker, Sheridan/Nelson (vintage). Also:
- **Nelson Paint Co. (Nel-Spot 007)**: bolt-action CO2 pistol, originally for marking trees and cattle; the founding marker of the sport.
- **Tippmann SMG-60 and SMG-68**: Tippmann's late-1980s full-auto markers (SMG-60 .62 cal fixed barrel; SMG-68 .68 cal with removable barrel; some converted at the factory to the "68 Special").
- **Line SI**: Bushmaster series, made in quantity but collected (vintagerex).
- **Airgun Designs (AGD) Automag**; **WDP Angel** (UK, early electro); **Smart Parts Shocker/Impulse**; **Bob Long**.
- **PMI (Pursuit Marketing Inc.)**: Piranha series and pumps; **Sheridan**: PGP pistol, P68, VM-68, Equalizer.
- **Splatmaster (JT, NSG)**: spring pistols; JT Splatmaster Z100/Z200.
- Named collector variants: Grey Ghost and Desert Duck ("aura of collectability", vintagerex).
- Modern markers branded Umarex/T4E are training pistols and rifles (.43/.50/.68); they sell new by volume and are not collectible.

### Quick ID checklist
- Name on the body: plastic moulded Brass Eagle/Spyder vs engraved Eclipse/Dye/Shocker/Angel/Autococker vs vintage Nelson/Sheridan/PMI/Tippmann SMG.
- Feed: top-mounted horizontal 12-gram tube or stick feed (vintage), elbow feed with hopper (entry), short vertical feed neck (electro).
- Trigger frame: electronic frame with a battery door or LED (high-end) vs mechanical.
- Air: 12-gram CO2 cartridges (vintage stock class) vs bulk CO2 tank vs HPA (compressed air, round tank with a regulator).
- Tank neck stamp: DOT 3AL / TC, and the last hydro date (5-year cycle).
- Condition: anodizing wear, broken parts, missing bolt or barrel; ask whether it holds air.
- Box, manuals and spare parts kit present.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Anodized aluminium vs plastic; braided macro-line; brand on body. More: high-end bodies are CNC-milled aluminium with crisp machining and deep, even anodizing (often two-tone "splash" or fade), with stainless or anodized hardware and a branded regulator. Entry guns are injection-moulded plastic or cast aluminium clamshells held by screws. Steel-braided remote lines and aftermarket barrels (Smart Parts, J&J, Freak) add little. Vintage guns: blued or painted steel (Nelson, Sheridan), brass parts, and wood or plastic grips; patina and holster wear are normal.

## Condition, damage and repairs
O-ring leaks, broken bolts, corrosion from CO2, expired tanks. Also: dried-out seals in guns stored for years (normal; a $10 o-ring kit fixes most), dead solenoids and boards in electros, corroded battery contacts, cracked plastic bodies, missing bolts or detents, scratched anodizing. Ask: does it hold air, does it cycle, which batteries/tank do you have, are all parts original?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Clones of Autococker/Spyder bodies; no significant fake market. Note: brands reissued "retro" editions (e.g. Autococker, Shocker revivals), which sell as new guns, not vintage.

## Market
Steady for top brands, saturated for entry gear. Players buy high-end electros on MCBazaar (MCB) and eBay; vintage collectors (vintagerex community) pay for condition and rarity, and several sellers dumping the same marker at once pushes prices down (vintagerex: the last units of a hoard sell for $25–50 less than the first).

## What sellers usually miss
Vintage pump guns from 1980s–90s. Also the Tippmann SMG series and Nel-Spot pistols buried in "paintball stuff" lots.

## Shipping / handling
Marker ships fine (~$15); pressurised or unpressurised tanks are restricted — ground only, valve removed, or local only. Remove batteries from electros; degas fully.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model, holds air, last-hydro date on tanks, upgrades. Photograph the engraved or moulded model name, the serial, both sides, the bolt and the feed neck; list included parts (barrels, manual, box); say whether it was tested on air.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] [model], [year/era]. [Tested: holds air and cycles on CO2/HPA / Untested, sold as is]. Original [body/bolt/frame]; upgrades: [list]. Includes [barrel, manual, box]. Cosmetic: [anodizing wear, scratches]. Shipped degassed, with no tank (tanks are local pickup only)."
