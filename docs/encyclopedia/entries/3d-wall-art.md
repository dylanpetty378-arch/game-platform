Buy rule (short): Most are decorator art that does not fit the car or ship economically; pass unless small (≤ 24×28 framed), signed by a recognised artist, and ≤ 1/4 of realistic sold value.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Canvases or boards with raised modelled figures (plaster, gesso, resin, clay or paper-mache) projecting from a painted background, often with metallic paint and dramatic lighting. A recognisable subgenre (1980s–2000s, sold in galleries, art auctions on cruise ships, and furniture stores) shows a female figure in a sculpted robe standing in a painted "spotlight" beam, on a gradient dark background, with sculpted waves or plants at her feet; usually framed in a light-wood moulding with linen liner. The listing example is exactly this.
Tells for higher value: a known artist's signature and a COA from a gallery/Park West, edition number on a sculptured serigraph or "sculptural relief" (e.g., Guillaume Azoulay "sculptural etching", Gonzalo Gonzales), original (unique) work with gallery label on back.
Tells for low value: factory/workshop decor pieces with "signature" in paint that is just a scribble, staple-backed canvas, no labels; furniture-store frames.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Rafael Martin**: name Lens associates with spotlight relief women; little documented market presence — treat as decor-grade (general knowledge, verify).
- **Guillaume Azoulay**: hand-embellished "sculptural" etchings/serigraphs with raised surfaces, sold via Park West; secondary prices $100–600.
- **Yaacov Agam**: agamographs (lenticular, not relief) $300–2,000+.
- **Itzchak Tarkay, Martiros Manoukian, Marcus Glenn**: Park West mixed-media/serigraphs with raised impasto; secondary $100–800.
- **Gonzalo Gonzales/various Latin American "esculto-pintura"**: relief canvases; low hundreds.
- **Decorator workshops** (Mexico, Italy, China, Thailand): unsigned or pseudonymous, $20–100.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Look for: crisp, intentional modelling of anatomy; consistent metallic leaf rather than craft paint; artist labels, COAs, inventory stickers, gallery stamps on the back; hand-signed and numbered edition marks. Cheap pieces have blobby relief, flaking acrylic, and generic brush signatures.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Raised parts chip and crack (especially fingers, robe edges); frames dent; linen liners stain. Damaged relief is hard to repair invisibly.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Park West-type editions are produced in large runs; "original" claims are frequently overstated. Many decorator pieces carry invented signatures. Authentication for listed artists usually means provenance/COA, which Dylan cannot verify cheaply.

## Market
Slow, buyer-poor on eBay; best sold locally (Facebook Marketplace, consignment) because of size. Days to sell often 60+.

## What sellers usually miss
Owners usually overvalue these based on the gallery/cruise price they paid. The miss in the other direction is a real listed artist piece (with label and COA) sold in a house clear-out.

## Shipping / handling
Framed 30×34" piece: oversize box, 15–25 lb, $50–90 shipped; mirror-box packaging with corner protectors. Usually uneconomic.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give exact dimensions framed and unframed, the signature close-up, back of the frame, any labels, and state "attributed" unless documented.
