## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Canvases or boards with raised modelled figures (plaster, gesso, resin, clay or paper-mache) projecting from a painted background, often with metallic paint and dramatic lighting. A recognisable subgenre (1980s–2000s, sold in galleries, art auctions on cruise ships, and furniture stores) shows a female figure in a sculpted robe standing in a painted "spotlight" beam, on a gradient dark background, with sculpted waves or plants at her feet; usually framed in a light-wood moulding with linen liner. The listing example is exactly this.
Tells for higher value: a known artist's signature and a COA from a gallery/Park West, edition number on a sculptured serigraph or "sculptural relief" (e.g., Gonzalo Gonzales), original (unique) work with gallery label on back.
Tells for low value: factory/workshop decor pieces with "signature" in paint that is just a scribble, staple-backed canvas, no labels; furniture-store frames.

Related types that sellers lump in as "3D art":
- **Tourist relief paintings from Spain/Latin America (1970s–80s)**: mixed media on masonite with raised figures (women with baskets or umbrellas, street scenes), often signed and dated on the back with a place (e.g. Marbella 1985). Small sizes (about 11×15") exist and ship easily.
- **Lenticular / kinetic prints (Agam agamographs)**: flat, but the image changes as the viewer moves; not relief.
- **Embossed etchings**: the plate mark and inked lines sit slightly raised or recessed; not sculpture.

### Quick ID checklist
- Measure: is it under about 24×28" framed (passenger seat and parcel-shippable)? If not, it is local-only.
- Signature: a legible name you can search, vs a brush scribble.
- Back of the frame: gallery label, COA, inventory sticker, artist stamp, pencil inscription with date/place.
- Edition marks: "x/99", "A.P.", or hand-signed in pencil or marker in the margin (prints), vs no numbering (decor).
- Relief quality: crisp anatomy and folds vs lumpy, blobby modelling and craft-paint metallics.
- Agam: image changes with viewing angle, hand signature and number in the margin, Agam name on labels.
- Damage: chips on raised fingers, robe edges and wave crests; cracks around the relief base.
- Frame: furniture-store moulding with linen liner (decor) vs conservation framing (gallery).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Rafael Martin**: name Lens associates with spotlight relief women; little documented market presence — treat as decor-grade (general knowledge, verify).
- **Guillaume Azoulay** (born Casablanca, Morocco, 1949; sold via Park West and galleries): known mainly for hand-signed, numbered **etchings** (intaglio) and **gold-leaf serigraphs**, sometimes with hand-drawn remarques; not true sculptural relief. (Corrected: the earlier entry called his works "sculptural etchings/serigraphs with raised surfaces"; the sources describe etchings and serigraphs.) Secondary prices $100–600 (general knowledge, verify); gallery asking prices run to $2,000 for an etching, which is retail, not resale.
- **Yaacov Agam**: agamographs (lenticular lithography, not relief), hand-signed and numbered in marker, typical edition 99 (e.g. "Meridia", 1985, 15×14" image) $300–2,000+.
- **Itzchak Tarkay, Martiros Manoukian, Marcus Glenn**: Park West mixed-media/serigraphs with raised impasto; secondary $100–800.
- **Gonzalo Gonzales/various Latin American "esculto-pintura"**: relief canvases; low hundreds (general knowledge, verify).
- **Spanish/Mediterranean tourist relief art**: signed regional artists on masonite, 1970s–80s; a small example is asked at $399 on Chairish (asking, not sold).
- **Decorator workshops** (Mexico, Italy, China, Thailand): unsigned or pseudonymous, $20–100.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Look for: crisp, intentional modelling of anatomy; consistent metallic leaf rather than craft paint; artist labels, COAs, inventory stickers, gallery stamps on the back; hand-signed and numbered edition marks. Cheap pieces have blobby relief, flaking acrylic, and generic brush signatures.
- Supports: masonite/hardboard (older, rigid, common on 1970s–80s tourist pieces), stretched canvas (decor), resin cast panels (mass production; identical copies exist).
- Old vs new: yellowed backing paper, period gallery labels with phone numbers lacking area codes, rusty staples or nails and tape on older works; new decor has bright staples, plastic hangers, and barcode stickers.
- Agamographs: the lenticular screen should be clean and unscratched; a print without the moving effect is a poster, not an agamograph.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Raised parts chip and crack (especially fingers, robe edges); frames dent; linen liners stain. Damaged relief is hard to repair invisibly. Repainted touch-ups show as a slightly different sheen; ask for raking-light photos. Lenticular screens scratch and can delaminate.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Park West-type editions are produced in large runs; "original" claims are frequently overstated. Many decorator pieces carry invented signatures. Authentication for listed artists usually means provenance/COA, which Dylan cannot verify cheaply. Agam posters and unsigned lenticular reproductions are sold as "Agam"; the hand signature and edition number are what count.

## Market
Slow, buyer-poor on eBay; best sold locally (Facebook Marketplace, consignment) because of size. Days to sell often 60+.

## What sellers usually miss
Owners usually overvalue these based on the gallery/cruise price they paid. The miss in the other direction is a real listed artist piece (with label and COA) sold in a house clear-out.

## Shipping / handling
Framed 30×34" piece: oversize box, 15–25 lb, $50–90 shipped; mirror-box packaging with corner protectors. Usually uneconomic.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give exact dimensions framed and unframed, the signature close-up, back of the frame, any labels, and state "attributed" unless documented.

### What to tell the buyer
"Mixed-media relief painting with hand-modelled raised figure(s) on [canvas / masonite], signed [as read] lower [right], [label / inscription on back reads …]. Measures [framed] / [image]. Raised areas are [intact / small chips at …]. Sold as decorative art; attribution is based on the signature only and no certificate is included [unless one is]." For agamographs: "Hand-signed and numbered [x/99] in marker; lenticular image shifts as you move."
