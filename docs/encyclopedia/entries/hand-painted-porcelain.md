## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
White porcelain "blanks" were imported from Limoges (T&V, J.P.L.), Bavaria (Rosenthal, Hutschenreuther, Thomas, PT Tirschenreuth, ZS&Co), Austria (MZ Austria, Victoria Carlsbad) and Japan (Nippon) and painted by tens of thousands of American hobbyists (mostly women) and by professional studios c.1880–1930. Photo tells:
- **Soft airbrushed or washed backgrounds** (purple/lavender, green, yellow) blending into fruit or flowers – blackberries, cherries, grapes, violets, roses, poppies (example 7981033239). Typical amateur work.
- **Artist signature** in paint near the rim and often a date or initials on the back; "Bavaria" or "Limoges" factory mark in green/blue underglaze.
- **Raised gold paste, heavy gold rims and etched gold borders** = professional studio (Pickard, Stouffer).
- **Transfer (decal) roses with hand-applied gold** – common on 1900–1920 German chocolate pots and dresser sets (example 7961361633). Decal tells: identical repeated motifs, tiny dot screens under magnification, no brush strokes.
- **Moriage (raised slip dots and beading) and heavy enamel jewelling** = Nippon (1891–1921).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Pickard China** (Chicago, 1893–; hand-painted on blanks until 1938; marks "Pickard" with lion, "Hand Painted China Pickard", artist signatures like Challinor, Marker, Yeschek) – $80–1,000+.
- **Stouffer** (Chicago, 1906–1946) $60–300; **Donath** (Chicago) $60–250; **Osborne Art Studio**, **Atlas China** etc. $40–150.
- **Blanks**: T&V Limoges, J.P.L. France, "Bavaria" (many factories), Rosenthal, Hutschenreuther, Thomas Bavaria, Z.S.&Co Bavaria, PT Bavaria, MZ Austria, Victoria Austria, O&EG Royal Austria, Haviland – the blank adds little; the painting quality sets value.
- **R.S. Prussia / R.S. Germany / R.S. Suhl / R.S. Tillowitz** (Schlegelmilch) – transfer-decorated with hand finishing; R.S. Prussia molded red-wreath mark pieces $50–500, portrait/animal scenes up to $2,000+; heavily faked.
- **Nippon** (Japan export 1891–1921, "Hand Painted Nippon" with maple leaf, M-in-wreath, rising sun) – $15–60 common; moriage, coralene and portrait pieces $100–600+.
- **Chocolate pots** (tall, slim, lidded, sold as sets with 4–6 cups): Germany/Austria transfer roses $25–60; Nippon $30–90; Pickard $150–500; R.S. Prussia $100–400.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Translucent hard-paste porcelain rings when tapped; a thick opaque body is earthenware or semi-porcelain.
- Good painting: shaded leaves with veins, layered petals, controlled washes, fine gold; amateur: flat colour, blotchy wash, uneven gold rims.
- Gold: worn gold on rims and handles is normal; burnished raised gold paste adds value.
- Wall plates usually show hanger marks on the rims (example 7981033239 has wire spring hangers) – check the rims under the hanger claws for chips.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips and flea bites, hairline cracks (hold up to light; tap-test for a dull thud), gold wear, painted-over restorations (matte patches, under UV fluoresce differently), chipped lid flanges and finials on chocolate pots, scratched centres from cutlery, hanger rust stains.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- R.S. Prussia: the red wreath and star mark is faked as a decal since the 1960s on Asian-made pieces; fake marks are often too bright, slightly crooked, glazed over, and the molds are cruder.
- Nippon: fake "Nippon" backstamps (e.g., an "hourglass" or "China E-OH" variant, and maple leaf fakes) on 1980s–2000s Chinese porcelain.
- Pickard: few fakes; the post-1938 Pickard (decal/dinnerware) is worth far less than studio hand-painted.
- Modern "hand painted" Chinese porcelain plates with faux-antique backstamps.

## Market
Very large supply from estates; demand has collapsed since 2000 except for top studio work and R.S. Prussia/Nippon specialists. Sell-through low for amateur plates. No seasonality (slight Mother's Day / tea-party bump).

## What sellers usually miss
- Pickard marks on the back of pieces described as "hand-painted Limoges plate".
- Stouffer/Donath studio signatures.
- Sellers usually OVER-estimate: "antique hand painted" amateur plates are almost always $10–20.

## Shipping / handling
Plates 1–2 lb, double-boxed $12–18; chocolate pots and sets $15–25, high breakage risk (lids and spouts). Sets of 4 plates ~$18–22.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Show the back mark and any artist signature/date; describe the motif, diameter, and gold wear; disclose chips, hairlines, and hanger marks; say "artist-painted on a [Bavaria/Limoges] blank" rather than "Limoges" when the decoration is American.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$15) − ship to Dylan (~$15) − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs a ~$95+ sold piece. Buy only Pickard (pre-1938 hand-painted), Stouffer, Donath, genuine R.S. Prussia molded pieces, or Nippon moriage/portrait at ≤ $40, after seeing the back mark. Pass amateur fruit/flower plates, relish dishes, single cups and decal chocolate pots at any price above ~$5 each. No repairs or cracked pieces.
