## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Paper money from outside the US. Most of what families keep from travel or war souvenirs is common and worth little. Value = issue rarity × grade. Identify by country, denomination, date and series → Pick number (Standard Catalog of World Paper Money).
- Grade from the photo: folds, creases, soft corners, tears, stains, pinholes, tape → circulated (VG–VF). Crisp, flat, sharp corners, bright paper → UNC.
- Overprints, signatures and serial prefixes change Pick numbers and value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Austro-Hungarian Bank (Oesterreichisch-ungarische Bank)**: 1902–1918 Kronen notes. 10,000 Kronen dated 2 Nov 1918 (Pick 25) with Art Nouveau woman's portrait; with red vertical "DEUTSCHÖSTERREICH" overprint (1919, Austria Pick 65) — very common, $3–15 circulated, $20–40 UNC (general knowledge, verify). Other successor-state overprints/stamps (Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Romania stamps) can be worth more.
- **Germany**: 1922–23 hyperinflation Reichsbanknoten (millions, billions) $1–10; Notgeld city emergency money $1–5; 1924 Reichsmark 1930s notes $2–20; Third Reich era notes common.
- **Brazil**: Cruzeiros 1970s–90s (1000 Cruzeiros Barão do Rio Branco, Pick 197 etc.) — $0.25–3; overprinted cruzeiros novos; high grade still low.
- **Japan invasion money (JIM)** WWII Philippines, Burma, Oceania, Netherlands Indies: $1–5 each.
- **US Military Payment Certificates (MPC)** 1946–73: $5–100+ depending on series and grade; replacement notes more.
- **China**: pre-1949 Central Bank, Bank of Communications, Bank of China — common circulated $2–20; scarce issues and UNC high value; heavy fakes.
- **Zimbabwe** 100 trillion dollars (2008): $5–30 depending on market swings.
- **Mexico** revolutionary notes, **Philippines** guerrilla notes, **Iraq** Saddam dinars, **Russia** imperial 1898–1912: most $1–15.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Rag paper with watermark, security thread and intaglio printing (raised ink felt by touch) on genuine notes.
- UNC notes have no folds and original sheen; "AU" has one light fold. Pressed/washed notes look limp and flat with faded colours — lowers grade.
- Star or replacement prefixes (country specific), low serial numbers, error printing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tears, missing pieces (the Brazil note in our sweep is in rags), tape, pinholes, writing, foxing, glue from albums, framing damage (sun fade). Repaired/pressed notes graded "net" lower.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Chinese counterfeit pre-1949 notes, Confederate-style copy notes, fake overprints and stamps on Austro-Hungarian notes, "souvenir" replica sets (Zimbabwe trillion copies, "COPY" printed), and modern Chinese fantasy notes.
- PMG/PCGS slabbed notes can be fake holders. High-value notes need professional grading — outside Dylan's rules.

## Market
Liquid for lots and UNC popular notes; circulated singles barely sell. Collectors buy by Pick number and grade; eBay and Heritage dominate. Lots of 50–100 mixed world notes sell for $20–60.

## What sellers usually miss
- Successor-state stamps/overprints on Austro-Hungarian notes (Czechoslovakia stamp, Yugoslavia SHS stamp) can multiply value — ask for close-ups.
- MPC and replacement/star notes mixed into travel lots.
- Reverse: sellers think any 100-year-old or high-denomination note is valuable; nearly all inflation notes are cheap.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size. Toploader or currency sleeve between cardboard, plain envelope with stamps $1–2 or $5 first-class with tracking. Framed notes ship $15+ — remove from frame.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Country, denomination, date, Pick number, signature/overprint variety, honest grade with front/back photos, serial number. Don't call a note "uncirculated" if it has a fold.

## Buy rule
A $10 note must sell for ~$80 to clear $50 profit. Only uncirculated scarce notes or graded notes do that. Pass on circulated foreign currency and inflation notes. Anything whose value depends on grade over ~$100 needs PMG/PCGS grading — outside Dylan's lane unless already slabbed by PMG/PCGS (verify the cert online).
