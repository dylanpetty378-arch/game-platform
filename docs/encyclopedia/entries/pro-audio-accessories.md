## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small accessories for studio and PA work. Tells:
- **Cables**: XLR (3-pin, Neutrik with black boots and chuck-type strain relief; Switchcraft A3F/A3M nickel barrels with set-screw; Cannon XLR-3 ITT older), 1/4" TS/TRS (Switchcraft 280/297 nickel plugs), RCA, breakout/Y-cables (example 7928965992 – Switchcraft-style nickel XLR and 1/4" plugs on grey and black cable, one with spade lugs and red heat-shrink).
- **Cable brand**: printed on the jacket (Mogami 2534/W2549, Canare L-4E6S, Belden 8412/1192A, Gotham GAC-3). Branded quad cable has value; generic/unprinted does not.
- **Horn throat adapters**: cast aluminium or iron spacers with a flange at each end, bolt holes matching a driver pattern (e.g., Altec 3-bolt 1.4", JBL 2"/4-bolt) – examples 7959622827 and 7959623697 are cast, black-painted 1.4–1.5" to 2" adapters with an ear/lug for a mounting bracket, maker unknown.
- **Shock mounts**: Neumann EA 87 / EA 170 (silver/grey, spider rings), AKG H85, Rycote.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Connectors**: Neutrik (Liechtenstein, 1975–), Switchcraft (Chicago, 1946–), Cannon/ITT Cannon (original XLR inventor), Amphenol, Western Electric (vintage 310 patch plugs, 1/4" "telephone" connectors for vintage consoles).
- **Cable**: Mogami, Canare, Belden, Gotham, Van Damme, Monster (low resale).
- **Patch bays**: ADC (TT/bantam), Switchcraft TT/TRS, Bittree; vintage ADC long-frame bays from old consoles $100–500.
- **DIs**: Radial JDI/J48 ($100–200), Countryman Type 85 ($100–150), Jensen-transformer boxes, Whirlwind Director ($60–100).
- **Mic mounts**: Neumann EA 87 ($100–200), EA 170 ($150–250), AKG H85 ($30–60), Shure A53M ($15–30).
- **Horn parts**: Altec 30210 throat adapters, Altec 511/811 horns, JBL 2327/2328 adapters ($40–120 each), JBL 2345/2397 lenses, Western Electric throats (high value to vintage theatre audio collectors).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Nickel or gold contacts, solder (not crimp) terminations, printed cable jacket, heavy cast metal on horn parts, factory part numbers cast in. Home-made/cheap moulded cables are worthless in bulk.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Intermittent cables (cold solder joints), cracked strain reliefs, missing set screws, broken latches on Neutrik female XLRs, corroded pins, dried-out rubber suspension on shock mounts, chipped horn flanges, stripped threads.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit Neutrik connectors (lighter, weak latch, no "Neutrik" logo moulded into the insert) and counterfeit Mogami cable are common on cheap Asian marketplace cables. Generic shock mounts sold as "Neumann-style".

## Market
Steady for branded studio gear; slow for generic cables and unknown adapters. Horn parts sell to a small vintage-PA/theatre audio niche – identified parts sell, unidentified ones linger.

## What sellers usually miss
Neumann/AKG shock mounts and Western Electric/ADC patch gear in "audio junk" boxes. Sellers usually over-estimate generic cables and unknown adapters.

## Shipping / handling
Cable lots 2–4 lb $10–15; cast horn adapters 2–5 lb each $12–18; patch bays $15–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
List cable types, lengths, connector brands, and state "tested for continuity" only if tested with a cable tester; give horn adapter throat diameters, bolt pattern and bolt-circle diameter.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$12) − ship to Dylan (~$12) − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs ~$85+ median. Buy only identified branded mounts, DIs, patch bays and Altec/JBL/Western Electric parts at ≤ 1/3 median. Pass generic cable lots and unknown adapters. Active electronics (DIs with batteries/phantom) count as untested electronics – buy only if seller demonstrates them working or price assumes parts value.
