## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Croquet sets with 4-8 mallets, matching striped balls, wickets and stakes, usually on a wooden or wire cart. 1950s-90s American sets have multi-colour banded shafts and solid wood heads with rubber/plastic end caps (as in the example). Early (1880-1930) sets have hand-painted rings, mallets with long cylindrical heads and come in pine boxes with paper labels.

More detail by type:
- **English "association" croquet sets (Jaques and rivals), 1860s–1950s** — four (or eight) heavy mallets with square-ended or cylindrical boxwood or lignum heads, often brass-bound at the ends, ash shafts about 36–37" long; solid composition or wood balls in blue, red, black, yellow; narrow iron hoops; a winning post; coloured clips; in a pine box with a stencilled lid ("Jaques Croquet, London") and a paper label inside the lid.
- **Victorian garden/parlour sets, 1860s–1890s** — eight mallets and balls, ten hoops, start and finish posts, scoring abacus, gilt-brass clips (Bellmans lot 696).
- **American backyard sets, 1880s–1990s** — painted maple mallets with coloured rings, banded shafts, wire wickets, two striped stakes, on a wooden stand or wire cart; mid-century sets by Forster, Sportcraft, South Bend, Spalding.
- **Lawn bowls** — lignum vitae (very dense, dark tropical hardwood) biased bowls about 5" diameter, sold in sets of four, with inset discs on both ends carrying the maker, bias number, owner's initials and association test stamps. Later bowls are composition (Henselite, etc.).
- **Quoits, horseshoes, ring toss, badminton** — iron quoits and early wooden ring-toss sets are small and shippable.

Look-alikes: decorator "antique-style" painted mallets (new wood, artificial distress), bocce sets (unbiased balls, no discs), skittles/ninepins.

## Makers, brands and variants
Forster Mfg (Maine), Sportcraft, South Bend Toy, Spalding, Brookline, Jaques of London (premium), Garcia.

- **John Jaques & Son, London** (Jaques of London; 1795–, credited with popularising croquet in England in the 1860s) — mallets stamped "JAQUES LONDON" or "Jaques London, made in England"; clips embossed "JAQUES PATENT"; boxes with a metal label "Jaques & Son, London" or a stencilled lid "JAQUES'S PRIZE MEDAL CROQUET" (Victorian) or "Jaques Croquet, London" with a set number (c.1920s). Jaques also made lawn bowls (example c.1928, Australian Sports Museum).
- **Thomas Taylor (Glasgow)** — the leading lignum vitae lawn-bowl maker; discs stamped "Thomas Taylor Glasgow", bias number (e.g. "No.3"), owner's initials, and association test/expiry stamps; bowls were periodically reconditioned and re-stamped (e.g. "Reconditioned 1955", I.B.B. expiry 1955).
- **Forster Mfg (Maine)** — the commonest US mid-century croquet maker (model numbers such as "377"); Terapeak median $75 for "Forster croquet set".
- **Sportcraft, South Bend Toy, Spalding, Brookline, Garcia** — US sets, 1940s–90s.

### Quick ID checklist
- Mallet stamp: "JAQUES LONDON" or another maker's name on the head?
- Box: pine box with stencilled lid, metal or paper label inside? Original box adds most of the value of an English set.
- Mallet heads: brass bands at the ends = English quality set; plain painted maple with plastic end caps = mid-century US.
- Count: 4 or 8 mallets, matching balls, all hoops/wickets, posts, clips — complete with original parts is the premium.
- Lawn bowls: dark, very heavy wood (lignum vitae) with inset discs on both ends; read the discs (maker, bias number, initials, test stamps).
- Ball material: wood or composition (early) vs plastic (modern).
- Size: a full cart set exceeds the size rule; individual mallets, balls, bowls and ring sets do not.

### Golden-egg tells
- Complete Victorian Jaques "Prize Medal" croquet set in original pine box with patent clips and abacus — £1,000–1,500 estimate (unsold, Bellmans 2025) vs $40–120 mid-century US sets — "JAQUES'S PRIZE MEDAL CROQUET" stencil on box front; "JAQUES PATENT" clips; all original mallets and balls.
- c.1920s Jaques set with brass-bound boxwood mallets in stencilled box — Jaques complete tier $250–500 (general knowledge, verify) vs $40–120 — "Jaques London, made in England" stamped on heads; brass end bands; paper label inside lid.
- Matched set of four Thomas Taylor lignum vitae bowls with legible discs — dealer-level sets (Eron Johnson, Sport Antiques) vs $30–100 per pair generic (general knowledge, verify) — "Thomas Taylor Glasgow" on the discs; bias number; matching numbers 1–4.
- Early (1880–1930) American boxed set with paper label and hand-painted rings — mid tier $150–300 vs $40–120 cart sets (general knowledge, verify) — wooden box with printed paper label; long cylindrical mallet heads; no plastic.
- Single early painted mallets/balls with original paint — $10–40 each as decor, shippable, vs unsellable shipped full cart sets — crazed original paint, square-cut wood, no plastic caps.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Early: hardwood (maple, lignum vitae), paint loss consistent with age, brass bands. Mid-century: lacquered banded hardwood. Modern: plastic balls/caps.

- English quality: boxwood or lignum heads, brass binding, ash shafts, iron hoops, pine box.
- American: maple heads and shafts, painted rings, lacquer; mid-century sets have plastic or rubber end caps.
- Lawn bowls: lignum vitae is extremely dense and oily; discs are inset (ivory, bone or composition — check before listing, see Selling) and stamped.
- Old vs new: wear concentrated on striking faces and ball contact points; age cracks along the grain; new decor mallets show uniform artificial distress and machine-sharp edges.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Split heads, missing balls/wickets, loose caps. Decor buyers forgiving.

- Lignum vitae bowls check (crack) if dried out; reconditioning stamps are normal and not damage.
- Woodworm in pine boxes (dealers note "treated for worm").
- Replaced posts or hoops reduce value (the Sport Antiques Jaques set had a new winning post — noted in the listing).
- Ask: count of mallets/balls/hoops, maker stamps, box label photos, any splits.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Distressed "antique" decor mallets. Jaques still makes croquet sets; modern Jaques sets carry current branding and are not antiques.

## Market
Spring/summer local; shipped sales rare. English sets and lignum bowls sell through UK sporting-antique dealers and decorators; lignum bowls are bought as decor (bowls of three to five in a dish).

## What sellers usually miss
Early boxed sets in barns. Also the "JAQUES LONDON" stamp on mallet heads, and Thomas Taylor discs on lawn bowls (often titled "wooden balls").

## Shipping / handling
30-40" long, 20-30 lb with cart: $45-70. Lawn bowls are ~3 lb each; a set of four ships at ~14 lb in a small box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Completeness, maker, age.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker as stamped (e.g. "mallets stamped JAQUES LONDON; box stencilled Jaques Croquet, London") and give a date range, then list every component with counts (mallets, balls, hoops, posts, clips, abacus) and say which are original and which are replacements. Give mallet length and head material (boxwood, brass-bound, maple). For lawn bowls, quote the disc stamps (maker, bias number, owner initials, test or reconditioning dates) and the diameter. If disc inlays may be ivory, say so and check selling-platform rules (eBay prohibits ivory) and state law before listing; describe them as "bone or ivory, untested" only if allowed. Note splits, worm, and paint loss. Large cart sets: local pickup only.

## Buy rule
Fails size/shipping economics. Pass full cart sets. Consider marked Jaques mallets/boxed English sets ≤ $50 for local resale, and Thomas Taylor lignum bowls ≤ $15 each.
