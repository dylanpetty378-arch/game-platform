## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass or metal fuel fonts (oil reservoirs) with a threaded collar, a wick burner and a glass chimney. Types: finger lamps (small, with handle), stand lamps (pedestal, 10–20"), hand lamps, banquet lamps (tall, with shades), Gone-with-the-Wind parlor lamps (two painted globes), and Aladdin mantle lamps (centre-draft, very bright).
Photo tells:
- **Antique EAPG (1860s–1910)**: thick pressed glass with a named pattern (e.g., Peanut, Banner, Bull's Eye, Prince Edward, Lincoln Drape), a collar of brass or plaster-set, a slight ripple or bubble in the glass, wear on the base rim.
- **Modern (1960s–2020s)**: Lamplight Farms (USA), Chinese and Hong Kong imports. Mould-perfect, thin glass, often with a stamped "Lamplight" or "Made in China/Hong Kong" burner, and plastic or aluminum collars; bright coloured lamp oil (red, blue) inside clear fonts.
- **Burners**: "P&A Mfg Co Eagle" burners (1890s–1950s) are common and not proof of age. "Queen Anne" burners (Scovill Mfg., Waterbury CT) are 1890s–1920s. "Aladdin" and "Model B/NU-Type" = Mantle Lamp Co. (1908–), B-series 1932–1955.
- **Electrified**: a cord exiting the base or a socket in the burner means a converted lamp, which collectors value less.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **EAPG makers**: Adams & Co. (Apollo), Atterbury, Westmoreland, U.S. Glass, Bryce, Hobbs-Brockunier, Central Glass, Riverside (Eagle-type lamps). Values depend on pattern and colour.
- **Aladdin / Mantle Lamp Co. of America** (Chicago, 1908–): Model 1–12 (brass), then glass B-series (Washington Drape, Lincoln Drape, Corinthian, Majestic, Cathedral, Simplicity, Venetian). Washington Drape clear: median $68 (49 sales). Coloured, Moonstone, Alacite and Lincoln Drape ruby: $150–400.
- **Rayo** (Standard Oil), **Miller** (Edward Miller & Co., Meriden), **Bradley & Hubbard**, **Plume & Atwood (P&A)**, **Scovill**: brass and nickel burners and banquet lamps.
- **Lamplight Farms** (Wisconsin, 1970s–): modern reproduction stand and hurricane lamps. About $10–30.
- **Sail Boat brand**: Yung Tack Cheong Hardware Manufactory, Hong Kong (founded 1935, incorporated 1969) made Sailboat-brand kerosene lamps, cycle lamps and lanterns (industrialhistoryhk.org). Mid-century export lamps: $10–30.
- **Hurricane lamps (1930s–50s)**: pressed diamond-point or hobnail glass, often electrified (Fenton hobnail, L.E. Smith, Westmoreland). About $25–80 each.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old EAPG: soapy feel, slight greyish or straw tint in clear glass, flowing mould seams that fade out, plaster-set collars.
- Modern: crisp seams to the top, perfectly clear or bright colours, threaded plastic collars, lighter weight.
- Colour in the glass vs coloured oil: check the empty lamp. Our sweep's "red" lamp has red oil inside a clear font.
- Vaseline/uranium glass glows under UV and adds value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips on font edges and base rims, cracks in the font (which leak), cloudy "sick" glass inside fonts, cracked or broken chimneys (replaceable, $10–20), broken burner gears, glued joins between font and base (common on two-part lamps), drilled bases for electrification.
- Never ship with fuel inside. A lamp with any kerosene in it can't be shipped by USPS.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Lamplight Farms and Asian copies of EAPG patterns.
- Reproduction Gone-with-the-Wind lamps (1950s–1990s) with decal flowers instead of hand-painted ones, and electric-only construction.
- Married lamps: an antique base with a modern font, or wrong burners and chimneys.
- Aladdin reproductions are rare; some Aladdin shades and chimneys are reproduced (check burner markings).

## Market
Steady and slow. Supply is large (every farmhouse had several). Demand comes from décor buyers, preppers and power-outage buyers (spikes after storms and in winter) and Aladdin collectors (Aladdin Knights). Clear common lamps sell in 30–90 days at $25–50.

## What sellers usually miss
- Aladdin lamps listed simply as "oil lamp". Look for the Aladdin centre-draft burner with a mantle gallery and "Aladdin" on the wick knob.
- Coloured EAPG fonts (amber, cobalt, green, vaseline) priced like clear.
- Conversely, sellers think any lamp with an Eagle burner is "antique 1800s": Eagle burners were made into the 1950s and fitted to modern lamps.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile and tall. Ship the chimney separately wrapped, burner removed, font empty and clean, double-boxed with 2–3" of padding. Expect $18–30 per lamp. Breakage risk is real; pack to survive a drop.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the pattern name (if known), glass colour, height with and without chimney, burner maker and size (No. 1/2/3), whether it's electrified, chips or cracks (check under raking light), and that it's shipped empty.
- For Aladdins, give the model, burner type (B, NU-Type) and parts included (chimney, mantle, shade).

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price. Pass unless profit ≥ max($50, price). Tier A: profit ≥ 2× price.
- With about $20–30 shipping each way, a lamp needs a sold median of about $120+ to clear $50 even at $0 cost. Only coloured Aladdins, GWTW/banquet lamps, Westmoreland and rare-colour EAPG qualify, priced ≤ $25–40.
- Never clear common stand lamps, Hong Kong imports or electrified hurricanes.
- Fits a passenger seat. No authentication needed.
