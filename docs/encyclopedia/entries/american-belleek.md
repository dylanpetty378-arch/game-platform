## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thin, ivory-coloured, translucent porcelain made in Trenton, NJ, 1882–1930, hand-painted by in-house or amateur china painters, heavy gold gilding, often raised-paste gold. Shapes: three-handled loving cups, tankards with grape/berry painting, ewers, tall vases, shell-form dishes, demitasse sets. Marks on the base (red, green, brown or lavender):
- **Ott & Brewer** 1883–1892: crescent with "BELLEEK" and "O&B" – rarest, highest.
- **Willets Manufacturing** 1880–1909: snake coiled into a "W" over "BELLEEK".
- **Ceramic Art Company (CAC)** 1889–1906: **artist's palette with brushes, "CAC" monogram, "BELLEEK" below** – this is early Lenox. Red/lavender palette = decorated at the factory; green = sold as blank for home decoration.
- **Lenox** 1906+: wreath with "L", later "Lenox" – post-1930 wreath pieces are ordinary Lenox, low value.
- Also Knowles Taylor & Knowles "Lotus Ware", Columbian Art Pottery, Cook Pottery, Morgan Belleek, Perlee.
Not to be confused with **Irish Belleek** (Fermanagh; harp/hound/tower mark; different market, mostly $20–150).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Ott & Brewer** (1883–1892, crescent mark) – top; lotus jugs, swan dishes, tea sets.
- **Willets** (1880–1909, snake "W") – factory-decorated pieces collectible, amateur-decorated decent.
- **Ceramic Art Company / CAC** (1889–1906, palette mark) – early Lenox; factory-decorated (red/lavender) beats green-mark blanks; silver-overlay CAC is a premium variant.
- **Lenox** (1906+, wreath) – post-1930 is ordinary.
- **Knowles Taylor & Knowles Lotus Ware** – top tier.
- **Columbian Art Pottery, Cook Pottery, Morgan Belleek, Perlee** – smaller makers.
- Known artists: W. H. Morley, Nosek, Marsh – signatures raise value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Very thin, translucent ivory body (hold to light). Factory decoration: skilled, even painting, consistent raised-paste gold, artist signature on the side. Amateur decoration (on green-mark blanks): uneven, heavier, sometimes signed and dated by a home painter (general knowledge, verify). Silver overlay on CAC is a quality tell. Gold wear on rims and handles is the normal age tell.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Gold wear and hairlines matter – look at rims, handles and bases. Chips and hairlines drop pieces a tier; restorations show as matte or colour-mismatched patches under raking light or UV (general knowledge, verify). Ask for a backlit photo to spot hairlines.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No widespread fakes known (general knowledge, verify). The real risk is confusing Lenox wreath-mark or Irish Belleek pieces with the early American makers, and amateur-decorated blanks with factory work.

## Market
Niche collector market (American ceramics collectors, Lenox collectors); sells on eBay and at regional auction houses. Mid-tier pieces can be slow; top Ott & Brewer pieces do best at specialist auction (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Estate sellers call it "Belleek" generically (thinking Irish) or "hand painted vase" and never photograph the mark. A palette or snake mark at a $20–60 "porcelain vase" price is the find. Gold wear and hairlines matter – look at rims.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile; double-box, 2–4 lb packed, $15–25 each way. Sell on eBay (13%) or send Top-tier pieces to a regional auction house.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker and mark (with mark colour), approximate date range from the mark, factory vs amateur decoration, artist signature, height, and condition (gold wear, hairlines). Photograph the mark, signature, each side, rim and handles, and a backlit shot. Keywords: "American Belleek", maker (CAC/Willets/Ott & Brewer), form (tankard, loving cup, vase), "hand painted", "Trenton".

## Buy rule
Buy when: palette (CAC), snake (Willets) or crescent (O&B) mark visible and price ≤ $60 for decorated pieces, ≤ $150 for O&B or artist-signed. Pass when: Lenox wreath mark, Irish Belleek, chips/hairlines, or dealer-tagged at $150 (needs a $450 sale to clear Dylan's rule). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat (all do).
