## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Single-action reel with a spool on a frame, foot to mount on the rod, handle on the spool face (general knowledge, verify). Maker name and model are engraved or stamped on the face plate or back plate; Hardy reels say "Hardy Bros Alnwick, Made in England". Size is given as a number (e.g. Marquis #6, System 2 78) matching line weight; older Hardy reels are sized by diameter in inches (2½", 2⅞", 3⅛", 3⅜", 3¾"...) (general knowledge, verify).

Photo routine: (1) back plate — maker, model, size, country; (2) face — handle shape and material (ivorine/bone, black ebonite, amber plastic), line guard type; (3) foot — material and stamping; (4) the edge — perforations, rim-control lip (exposed rim on later reels); (5) inside if possible — pawls, springs, drag mechanism.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hardy** (Alnwick, England): Perfect, Marquis – $150–400.
  - **Perfect**: introduced in the 1891 catalogue (13 sizes by year end). Early ones brass with iron line guards; aluminium spools adopted by 1895; brass largely phased out by 1910. Trout "contracted" Perfects from 1895, made until 1995. A Silent Check version was made only 1908–1910. Ball-bearing spindle, click check; later versions anodized. Smallest and largest sizes are the most valuable (Wikipedia).
  - **Marquis**: 1970s-on trout/salmon reel line, numbered sizes (#6, #7, #8/9, #10 etc.) (general knowledge, verify).
  - Other Hardy lines collectors check: St George, St John, Princess, Featherweight, Lightweight, Uniqua, Bougle, Silex (general knowledge, verify).
- **Scientific Anglers System 2** (made by Hardy): $100–180. Hardy-made SA "System" reels are marked as made by Hardy in England and their spools and frames are interchangeable with the Hardy Marquis series (spinozarods; Mike's Reel Repair). SA reintroduced a **System 2 large-arbor line in 2003** (678 and 890 models with caliper disc drag, perforated frames) (Saltwater Sportsman) — a different, later design; do not price it as the Hardy-made System 2.
- **Pflueger Medalist** (USA, common): $20–50. Model numbers 1492 (smallest), 1494, 1495, 1495½, 1496½, 1498 (largest). Pre-1937 1494s and larger had only a spring-and-pawl check; adjustable drag arrived in 1937; the 1492 always had only spring-and-pawl. Early features collectors look for: metal (not plastic) latch plate, round "Diamolite" line guard, sculpted pillars, amber handle, aluminium spool-latch button; Akron-marked reels are USA-made; later reels changed hands of ownership and origin (Washington Fly Fishing forum).
- Orvis (CFO, Battenkill), Ross, Abel, Bogdan, Vom Hofe likely matter but are not researched yet (general knowledge, verify; Vom Hofe and Bogdan are top-tier vintage).

### Quick ID checklist
- Back plate legend: "HARDY BROS LTD ALNWICK ENGLAND" (or "Made in England") vs Korea/China.
- Model name and size engraved (PERFECT, MARQUIS, ST GEORGE; SA "SYSTEM 2" with number; Pflueger model 1492–1498).
- Brass anywhere on a Hardy Perfect (face, foot, whole body) — then check whether it is a modern replica (see Fakes).
- SA System reel: does it say made by Hardy / England? Does a Marquis spool fit (seller may have one)?
- Medalist: round line guard, metal latch, sculpted pillars, amber handle = early; plastic latch and later guards = later.
- Handle: ivorine/bone or ebonite on old Hardys; check for cracks.
- Foot: original, not bent or filed; spare spools and leather/Hardy case present?
- Spin/drag video: smooth, clicks crisply, no grinding or wobble.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Early brass / brass-faced Hardy Perfect (1891–c.1910)** — up to 100×+ a normal Perfect (record £44,000 for a 2¼" all-brass Perfect in 2014, Wikipedia) — brass body or face, iron line guard, early "Hardy's Alnwick" engraving; rule out the modern brass-face replica first.
- **Hardy Perfect Silent Check (1908–1910)** — scarce variant, multiple of a common Perfect (exact multiple unknown; verify at auction) — engraved model wording and absence of the click when spun.
- **Smallest and largest Perfect sizes** — premium over mid sizes (Wikipedia: "generally the most valuable") — measure diameter: the smallest (2¼"–2½") and largest salmon sizes.
- **Vintage Hardy Perfect, any good one** — ~2× a Marquis (Terapeak median $325 vs $162.50) — "PERFECT" on the back plate, ball-bearing spindle, ventilated spool.
- **Hardy-made SA System 2** — ~3–5× a Pflueger Medalist ($100–180 vs $20–50) — "Made in England by Hardy" wording; spools interchange with the Marquis.
- **Pre-1937 Pflueger Medalist (click-only 1494 and up) with round Diamolite guard, metal latch, sculpted pillars, amber handle** — premium over a common Medalist (collector-favoured; magnitude unverified) — no drag-adjust knob on a 1494+ and the early hardware above.
- **Vom Hofe / Bogdan** — top-tier vintage, multiples of anything above (unpriced here; verify) — maker name on the plate.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hardy/System 2: machined or cast aluminium with quality click-pawl or disc drag, crisp engraving. Pflueger Medalist: stamped/cast aluminium, common. Check for maker engraving, smooth spin and drag (general knowledge, verify). Hardy Perfect: early brass, then aluminium (spools by 1895, bodies by about 1910), later anodized finish; ball-bearing spindle and click check are the defining features (Wikipedia). Old vs new: hand-engraved or stamped legends with wear vs laser-crisp modern marking; ebonite/ivorine handles vs plastic; original finish loss on the rim from use.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dropped/bent frames or spools, worn pawls/springs, corrosion (saltwater), missing line guards, replaced handles. Ask for a spin video and photos of both plates (general knowledge, verify). Also: refinished/repainted bodies (collectors want original finish), Medalists with broken latches (forum reports of broken 1492s), aftermarket parts (e.g. One Pfoot counterbalanced spools, metal parts, left-hand clutch conversions on Medalists) — these help anglers but count as non-original for collectors.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Hardy has re-issued Perfect models; check vintage details (general knowledge, verify). Current Hardy-made replicas include the **1921 Wide Spool Perfect** (sold new by Pure Fishing/Hardy and fly shops) and **brass-faced Perfect replicas** (sport antiques dealer lists a "Hardy Perfect Replica 3" Brass Face"). Modern replicas are made in Alnwick using many original methods and machinery (Wikipedia), so the box, modern packaging, current anodizing and replica wording are the tells; ask the seller for any box or paperwork.

## Market
Steady eBay demand from fly anglers and collectors of vintage British reels; spring season peak (general knowledge, verify). Medalists are bought mostly by users who fish them, so working condition and parts matter more than collector details except for early examples.

## What sellers usually miss
unknown – likely that a System 2 is Hardy-made, and the model/size engraving (general knowledge, verify). Also: early brass on a Perfect, the Silent Check variant, and early Medalist features (round guard, metal latch, sculpted pillars, amber handle).

## Shipping / handling
< 1 lb, small box, ~$6–10 (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, model, size, weight, left/right retrieve, drag type, condition, spare spools and pouch/box. Photograph both plates, the foot and engravings (general knowledge, verify).

### What to tell the buyer
Give the exact legend on the back plate (e.g. "Hardy Bros Ltd, Alnwick, England — The Perfect, 3⅛ in"), diameter and width, weight in ounces, retrieve direction and whether it converts, check type (click-pawl or adjustable drag), line guard and handle material, and whether the foot is original and straight. For SA System reels, say "made by Hardy in England" only if the plate says so, and note Marquis spool compatibility. For Medalists, give the model number and the early features present. State that a spin video is available and list included spools, case or box. Say "replica" or "reissue" if it is one.

## Buy rule
System 2 under $45; Hardy under $90. Pass Pflueger Medalist unless nearly free. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
