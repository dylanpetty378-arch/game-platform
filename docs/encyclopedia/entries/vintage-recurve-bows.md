## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
One-piece laminated wood bow, 48–70 in, with curved tips (recurve) or straight limbs (longbow); glass-backed limbs show
a coloured stripe along the edge. Maker and model are silk-screened on the belly side of the riser near the grip, usually
with draw weight ("45#", "52# @ 28") and a serial. **Bear Archery** (Grayling, Mich.) risers carry a coin/medallion in
the riser: copper coin = 1959–61, aluminium = 1962–63, pewter = 1963–65, brass = 1965–69, nickel-silver = 1970–72,
raised medallion = 1972+.

Titles: "recurve bow", "vintage bow", "long bow", "hunting bow" – often with no brand because the seller never read the
riser. Modern takedowns (PSE, Samick, Martin) have metal or modern risers with limb bolts — a separate, lower market.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bear Archery**: Kodiak (1959–63 coin era, "Kodiak" script) top; Kodiak Hunter, Kodiak Magnum, Super Kodiak
  (1967–69 brass coin top) collectible; Takedown A/B/C risers top; Tamerlane top; Grizzly (1950s top, later
  decent); Polar, Cub, Black Bear decent; Little Bear (kids) junk. Fred Bear takedowns.
- **Damon Howatt** (Yakima, WA): Hunter, Mamba, Ventura, Monterey, Super Diablo, El Dorado – thin elegant risers,
  often phenolic tip overlays. Original-finish Howatts $200–300.
- **Wing**: Red Wing Hunter, Chaparral, Presentation II (top).
- **Browning**: Nomad, Wasp, Explorer.
- **Ben Pearson**: Palomino (collectible), Hunter, Colt.
- **Shakespeare**: Kaibab, Necedah, Condor (collectible).
- **Indian Archery** (Cochise, Seminole, "Scout" kids), **Hoyt**, **Root**, **Herter's** (general knowledge, verify
  values for these).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hardwood risers (bubinga, zebrawood, rosewood, maple laminates; later phenolic/"Bear Bond" colours), fibreglass-backed
and -faced limbs. Original factory finish and crisp silk-screen add value; "Bow Doc" or amateur refinishes are
accepted on rare models but lower value on common ones. Check that the silk-screen model/weight is legible.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Check limb tips for cracks, sight limbs for twist (a twisted limb is $0), and look for delamination (glass lifting
from wood at the edges) and stress cracks in the glass. Dry-firing damage is common. A bow that has been strung for
decades may have lost draw weight. Re-glued tips and refinished risers show as uneven gloss or mismatched colour. Ask for
a photo strung and of each limb edge.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Bear has reissued classic models (e.g. "Kodiak", "Super Kodiak", "Grizzly" in later production) — check the coin or
medallion type and serial to date it (general knowledge, verify). Coins can be swapped in refinished risers.

## Market
Traditional-archery shooters and collectors (Bear collectors especially). Sells steadily on eBay and on Archery Talk /
Leatherwall classifieds (no fee). Late summer/early fall (pre-hunting season) is the peak (general knowledge, verify).
40–55 lb right-hand bows sell fastest; left-hand and heavy draws sell slower.

## What sellers usually miss
The riser silk-screen. "Old wooden bow" at $20–40 with a Bear coin or a Howatt script visible in the photo is the play.
Check: limb tips for cracks, limbs sighted for twist, and draw weight (40–55 lb is the sweet spot). Left-hand bows take
longer to sell (knock 20%).

## Shipping / handling
Long (54–70 in) but light (1–2 lb): needs a bow tube or 72-in box; $25–40 each way – this is the killer. Sell on eBay
(13%) or Archery Talk / Leatherwall classifieds (no fee). A 60–70 in bow may not fit a passenger seat lengthwise but
fits diagonally across a car interior.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + model + length + draw weight + hand: "Bear Kodiak Hunter 60" 45# RH", "Damon Howatt Hunter", "Wing Red Wing Hunter" + "vintage recurve bow" + coin/era; Junk: "old wooden bow", "archery", "hunting bow", "decor". Example: `Bear Kodiak Hunter Recurve Bow 60" 45# RH 1969 Brass Coin Original Finish`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (Bear collectors, traditional shooters) or Archery Talk / Leatherwall classifieds (no fee); late summer–early fall peak. Classifieds beat eBay net because of fees and the $25–40 shipping.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (1970s+ Polar/Grizzly/Pearson $75–175; Kodiak Hunter/Howatt/Wing/Condor $175–350; coin-era Kodiaks/takedowns $350–1,000), asks sell at 70–85%, so list at median and accept −15%, floor p25; knock 20% for LH or 60 lb+; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo the bow strung, side-on, full length; then riser silk-screen (model, length, weight, serial), coin/medallion, both limb tips, limbs sighted from the tip for twist, glass edges (delamination), finish close-up, string groove.
- **State:** maker, model, length, draw weight @ 28", RH/LH, serial and coin type (era), original or refinished, limbs: no twist, no cracks, no delamination, shot recently or not, string included, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 54–70" but 1–2 lb; bow tube or 72" box, tips padded, unstrung; $25–40. Fits diagonally in a car, not a passenger seat lengthwise.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Bear [Kodiak Hunter], [1969 brass coin], [45#] at 28" right-hand with the original finish and straight limbs — the sweet-spot weight shooters want; a new Bear Grizzly is [$X] and this is the Grayling-era bow."

## Buy rule
Buy when: Bear coin-riser Kodiak/Super Kodiak/Grizzly under $90; Howatt, Wing, Shakespeare Condor, Pearson Palomino
under $70; any recurve with a Top-tier name under $150 if limbs look straight. Pass when: no brand visible, draw over
60 lb, LH, or seller names the model and prices over $100. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping
both ways (~$60 round trip). Must fit a passenger seat (bows fit diagonally; long length drives shipping cost).
