## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wooden (hickory) shafts, leather wrap grips, forged iron heads with a "cleekmark" (maker's symbol) stamped on the back of the head, often with "Hand Forged in Scotland": Nicoll (hand), Gibson (star), Stewart (pipe), Spalding, MacGregor, Wright & Ditson, Forgan, Anderson. Putters, mashies, niblicks, cleeks, jiggers. Smooth-faced (pre-1905) and rut irons are the early valuable ones; 1920s–30s dot/line-faced irons are common. Woods: persimmon long-nose (pre-1890s) or bulger heads by Tom Stewart, Auchterlonie, Forgan – look for a maker stamp on the crown. Reproduction "hickory" clubs (St Andrews Golf Co, modern) have perfect finishes and no pitting. Seller words: "antique golf club", "wood shaft putter", "hickory", "old wooden golf club".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Tom Stewart** (St Andrews; clay-pipe cleekmark) – the best-known iron maker.
- **Gibson of Kinghorn** (star) and **George Nicoll of Leven** (hand) – common but well liked; Nicoll model names "Gem", "Whippet", "Zenith" add interest.
- **Spalding, MacGregor, Wright & Ditson, Burke, Kro-Flite** – American; Kro-Flite and Burke are the common end.
- **Forgan, Auchterlonie, Morris, McEwan** – wood makers; long-nose woods by Morris/McEwan are top tier.
- Valuable variants: smooth-face irons, rut niblicks (tiny round head), jiggers, cleeks, patented specialty clubs (aluminium-head putters, Schenectady-type putters), matched sets, player provenance.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-forged heads show slight asymmetry, hammer marks and deep cleekmark stamping; face scoring (dots, lines) dates them (smooth = earliest). Original sheepskin/leather grips with underlisting are a plus; replaced grips reduce collector value. Shafts should be straight hickory with original whipping at the hosel. Old vs new: pitting and patina vs modern clear-coated perfect finish. (general knowledge, verify)

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common: warped or cracked shafts, missing whipping, replaced grips, heavy rust/pitting, cleaned/buffed heads (lower collector value), split woods heads, replaced sole plates. Acceptable for the playable market: light rust, re-whipped hosel. Ask for photos of the cleekmark, face, shaft straightness (sighting down the shaft) and grip.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern reproduction hickories (St Andrews Golf Co and others) for the hickory-play market; fake cleekmarks on generic heads; married heads on later shafts. Repros look too perfect. (general knowledge, verify)

## Market
Two buyer groups: collectors (rare makers/forms, auction houses like Green Jacket) and hickory players (want playable, straight-shafted named irons and putters). eBay steady volume; most common clubs sell slowly at $20–60. Spring/summer slightly stronger (playing season). (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
The cleekmark and maker, and the model name ("Whippet", "Gem", "Zenith"). Most list "old wooden golf club $20". "Old golf club wood shaft" listings with a visible pipe/star/hand mark are the ones to open.

## Shipping / handling
~1 lb, ~40" long – tube or long box, ~$20–25 each way. eBay (13%).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, cleekmark, model name, club type (mashie, niblick, putter), face scoring, length, grip (original/replaced), shaft straightness, playable or display. Photograph the cleekmark, face, back, sole, grip and the shaft sighted end-on. Title keywords: "hickory shaft", "cleekmark", "Tom Stewart pipe", "Nicoll Leven", "hand forged Scotland", "smooth face".

## Buy rule
Buy when: named Scottish maker iron/putter (recognised cleekmark visible, original grip) at ≤ $30, or a lot of 5+ hickories under $60. Pass when: single named putter at $75–80+ (seller knows) – resale ceiling for a single common-maker club is ~$100. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat.
