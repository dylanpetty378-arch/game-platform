## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Boxed 1990s–2000s Mattel collector lines in pink-and-white or black boxes: Happy Holidays (1988–98; only the 1988 first issue and 1989 are worth real money), Hollywood Legends (Marilyn, Scarlett, Dorothy – $40–80), Timeless Treasures I Love Lucy episodes ($25–50), Bob Mackie ($80–250), Silkstone Barbie Fashion Model Collection (2000+, black box – $150–500), Dolls of the World ($15–30). Vintage 1959–66 ponytails/bubblecuts in stripe swimsuit: $100–2,000+ (loose). Sellers write "Barbie collectors", "NRFB", "in box", "Holiday Barbie".
- The year on a boxed collector doll is printed on the box front or side with the stock number. The year moulded on the doll's back/buttocks is the copyright year of the body or head mould, **not** the year the doll was made (e.g. many 1990s dolls are marked 1966 or 1976).
- Vintage dolls (1959–66) have a ponytail or bubblecut hairstyle, side-glancing eyes, heavy eyeliner on the earliest, and a solid (1959–60) or hollow (1961+) body.

## Makers, brands and variants
All Mattel. Lines and relative value:
- **Happy Holidays** 1988 (first, top of the line) and 1989; 1990–98 common.
- **Hollywood Legends** (Marilyn, Scarlett, Dorothy) mid.
- **I Love Lucy / Timeless Treasures** (e.g. "Job Switching" #21268) low-mid.
- **Bob Mackie** 1990–94 originals; Platinum and Neptune Fantasy are top. The series starts with **Gold** (1990, #5405), then **Platinum** and **Starlight Splendor** (1991), **Empress Bride** and **Neptune Fantasy** (1992), **Masquerade Ball** (1993), **Queen of Hearts** (1994); later 1995–2000s Mackies (Goddess of the Sun, Moon Goddess, Madame du Barbie, Fantasy Goddess series etc.) are generally lower (list after Gold/Platinum is general knowledge, verify). Mackie dolls come in a box with a sketch of the gown, a shipper box, a Mackie-signed design print and certificate; missing paperwork lowers value.
- **Silkstone / Barbie Fashion Model Collection** (2000+, hard vinyl "silkstone" body, black box). Per My Vintage Barbies: the line launched in 2000 with **Lingerie #1 (blonde) and Lingerie #2 (brunette)** – the brunette is the harder of the two to find – plus Delphine; the sculpt is based on the 1959 #1 ponytail; the final doll was The Gala's Best (2020). The 2009 Paris Convention 50th Anniversary Silkstone (brunette only) was limited to 500.
- **Pink/Silver/Gold/Platinum Label** – Gold Label higher.
- **Vintage 1959–66**: #1 ponytail (holes with copper tubes in feet – general knowledge, verify), bubblecut, American Girl, Color Magic.
- Junk: Dolls of the World, Avon exclusives, Millennium, played-with 1990s dolls ($2–5 a doll in lots).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the year and stock number from the box (not the doll body).
- NRFB? Look for intact tape/cellophane, the doll still tied to the insert, and the shipper (brown outer box) if it came with one.
- Black box + hard, heavy, porcelain-like doll = Silkstone. Pink box = mainstream collector line.
- Bob Mackie: Mackie name and sketch on the box; check for certificate and design print.
- Holiday: 1988 (first) or 1989 only are the better years; 1990–98 are common.
- Vintage loose doll: photograph the buttocks mark, feet (holes?), eye colour and eyeliner, and the ears (green ear).
- Box faults: crushing, sun fading, split corners, yellowed window, price stickers, smoke smell.
- Original swimsuit, shoes, earrings and booklet present (vintage).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Collector value is in the box: NRFB (never removed from box) with crisp box, intact cellophane and shipper beats opened. Silkstone has a heavy, hard, matte body (not soft vinyl). Vintage dolls: marks on buttocks ("Barbie T.M." / "Barbie® ©1958 Mattel Inc." – see the ponytail table above), face paint, hair condition, green ear (from earrings) lowers value.
- #3 ponytails' vinyl fades from tan to ivory; this is age, not a sign of a fake.
- Rooted hair in original set (ponytail with original rubber band, bubblecut un-combed) is worth much more than redone hair.
- Old vs new: vintage dolls have side-glancing eyes, non-twist waists (until 1967) and the Pats. Pend. marks; modern dolls have forward-looking eyes, twist or articulated bodies and later copyright years.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Box crushing, sun fading, torn cellophane, yellowed boxes, smoke odour; for loose vintage dolls – green ear, cut hair, faded face paint, sticky/tacky vinyl, missing original swimsuit/shoes. Ask for photos of all box sides and the box window.
- Repainted faces and re-rooted hair are common on vintage dolls and are worth disclosing; compare with reference photos.
- Split necks and leg seams on early dolls; holes in the feet of a #1 can be split.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mattel's own reproductions of vintage dolls (e.g. 35th-anniversary #1 ponytail reissues, 1990s–2000s) are often mistaken for originals (general knowledge, verify). Fake/clone dolls and outfits from Asia; re-boxed dolls in wrong boxes.
- The 35th Anniversary reproduction (1994) copies the #1 face (white irises) and came with a reproduction box, wrist tag and a **clear** stand; an original #1 used a black disc stand with two prongs in a box dated 1959 (Fashion Doll Guide). The repro box is the strongest tell, so loose dolls need the body mark and foot checks.
- Many later "vintage reproduction" outfits and Silkstone dolls copy 1959–60 looks; check the box and body.

## Market
Very oversupplied for 1990s boxed collector lines; strong and steady for vintage, Silkstone and Mackie. Buyers: adult doll collectors, nostalgia buyers. Q4 bump for Holiday Barbies (general knowledge, verify). eBay is the main venue.

## What sellers usually miss
Silkstone (black box, hard-body) vs ordinary collector lines; 1988 vs later Holiday. "Big lot of Barbies" from the 1990s is $2–5 a doll. Vintage wardrobe cases occasionally hide 1960s dolls/outfits. Also: foot holes on a loose ponytail (#1), the brunette Lingerie #2 Silkstone, and Mackie paperwork.

## Shipping / handling
Boxed doll 2 lb, $12–15; keep boxes crisp – ship in an oversized box with padding. Resell on eBay; fees 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Line, doll name, year, stock number, NRFB/opened, box condition (all sides), shipper box included, smoke-free home. Photograph all box sides, window, stock number, and for loose dolls the buttocks mark, face, feet and hair.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the line, doll name, box year and stock number (e.g. "1990 Bob Mackie Gold #5405"), and state NRFB or opened. Describe each box side, the window and the shipper, and list paperwork (certificate, Mackie print, booklet). For loose vintage dolls quote the body mark exactly, the foot holes (yes/no), eye and eyeliner colour, hair (original set or redone), green ear, and which clothes and accessories are original. Mention smoke-free/pet-free storage only if true. Never call a 35th-anniversary or other reproduction "vintage".

## Buy rule
Buy when: Silkstone BFMC ≤ $50, Bob Mackie ≤ $40, 1988 Holiday NRFB ≤ $60, any 1959–66 vintage ≤ $100. Pass when: 1990s Holiday, Hollywood Legends over $25, lots of played-with 1990s dolls. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
