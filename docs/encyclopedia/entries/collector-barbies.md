## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Boxed 1990s–2000s Mattel collector lines in pink-and-white or black boxes: Happy Holidays (1988–98; only the 1988 first issue and 1989 are worth real money), Hollywood Legends (Marilyn, Scarlett, Dorothy – $40–80), Timeless Treasures I Love Lucy episodes ($25–50), Bob Mackie ($80–250), Silkstone Barbie Fashion Model Collection (2000+, black box – $150–500), Dolls of the World ($15–30). Vintage 1959–66 ponytails/bubblecuts in stripe swimsuit: $100–2,000+ (loose). Sellers write "Barbie collectors", "NRFB", "in box", "Holiday Barbie".

## Makers, brands and variants
All Mattel. Lines and relative value:
- **Happy Holidays** 1988 (first, top of the line) and 1989; 1990–98 common.
- **Hollywood Legends** (Marilyn, Scarlett, Dorothy) mid.
- **I Love Lucy / Timeless Treasures** (e.g. "Job Switching" #21268) low-mid.
- **Bob Mackie** 1990–94 originals; Platinum and Neptune Fantasy are top.
- **Silkstone / Barbie Fashion Model Collection** (2000+, hard vinyl "silkstone" body, black box).
- **Pink/Silver/Gold/Platinum Label** – Gold Label higher.
- **Vintage 1959–66**: #1 ponytail (holes with copper tubes in feet – general knowledge, verify), bubblecut, American Girl, Color Magic.
- Junk: Dolls of the World, Avon exclusives, Millennium, played-with 1990s dolls ($2–5 a doll in lots).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Collector value is in the box: NRFB (never removed from box) with crisp box, intact cellophane and shipper beats opened. Silkstone has a heavy, hard, matte body (not soft vinyl). Vintage dolls: marks on buttocks ("Barbe T.M." / "Barbie® ©1958 Mattel Inc." – general knowledge, verify), face paint, hair condition, green ear (from earrings) lowers value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Box crushing, sun fading, torn cellophane, yellowed boxes, smoke odour; for loose vintage dolls – green ear, cut hair, faded face paint, sticky/tacky vinyl, missing original swimsuit/shoes. Ask for photos of all box sides and the box window.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mattel's own reproductions of vintage dolls (e.g. 35th-anniversary #1 ponytail reissues, 1990s–2000s) are often mistaken for originals (general knowledge, verify). Fake/clone dolls and outfits from Asia; re-boxed dolls in wrong boxes.

## Market
Very oversupplied for 1990s boxed collector lines; strong and steady for vintage, Silkstone and Mackie. Buyers: adult doll collectors, nostalgia buyers. Q4 bump for Holiday Barbies (general knowledge, verify). eBay is the main venue.

## What sellers usually miss
Silkstone (black box, hard-body) vs ordinary collector lines; 1988 vs later Holiday. "Big lot of Barbies" from the 1990s is $2–5 a doll. Vintage wardrobe cases occasionally hide 1960s dolls/outfits.

## Shipping / handling
Boxed doll 2 lb, $12–15; keep boxes crisp – ship in an oversized box with padding. Resell on eBay; fees 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Line, doll name, year, stock number, NRFB/opened, box condition (all sides), shipper box included, smoke-free home. Photograph all box sides, window, stock number, and for loose dolls the buttocks mark, face, feet and hair.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Silkstone BFMC ≤ $50, Bob Mackie ≤ $40, 1988 Holiday NRFB ≤ $60, any 1959–66 vintage ≤ $100. Pass when: 1990s Holiday, Hollywood Legends over $25, lots of played-with 1990s dolls. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
