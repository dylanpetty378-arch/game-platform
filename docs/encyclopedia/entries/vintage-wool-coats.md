## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Women's wool, cashmere or wool-blend coats from the 1940s–80s, often with fur collar/cuffs. Era tells: button closures rather than zippers, big buttons, bracelet (¾) sleeves, swing shapes (1950s–60s), union labels.

**ILGWU union label dating (corrected in this revision):** the earlier entry put the red-white-blue label at 1964–74; the Kheel Center timeline gives:
- Label reading "AFL" without "CIO": before 1955.
- "AFL-CIO": 1955 or later (1958: small blue imprinted outerwear label).
- 1959–1963: ILGWU union label with code letters and serial numbers.
- 1963–1974: redesigned label emphasising the words "union made".
- **1974–1995: red, white and blue label with "Made in the U.S.A."**
- 1995 on: UNITE label.

Silhouettes by decade (general knowledge, verify): 1940s strong shoulders, nipped waist, sculptural seaming (Lilli Ann); 1950s swing/trapeze and princess coats, big collars, bracelet sleeves; 1960s A-line, boxy and mod shapes, oversized fur collars; 1970s longer maxi coats, belted wraps, wider lapels; 1980s big shoulders, oversized cuts.

### Quick ID checklist
- Label photo (neck or inside front facing): maker name, plus city ("San Francisco", "Paris" on post-war Lilli Ann).
- Union label: AFL / AFL-CIO / "union made" / red-white-blue — dates the coat (see list above).
- Fibre tag: 100% wool, cashmere, camel hair, or "Forstmann" fabric label; pre-1960 coats often have no fibre-content tag (the US Wool Products Labeling Act dates from 1939; content tags became routine later — general knowledge, verify).
- Fur: part the fur at the collar to see leather skin (real) vs knit backing (faux).
- Construction: bound buttonholes, hand-picked edges, silk or rayon lining = better make.
- Moth check: under the collar, armpits, behind lapels and along hem folds.
- Measurements: bust, shoulder, sleeve, length (vintage sizes run small).
- Leather trim and brass turnlock closures on a boxy coat = look for a Bonnie Cashin / Sills label.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Lilli Ann (SF, 1933–c.2000)**: founded by Adolph Schuman in San Francisco in 1933, named for his wife Lillian. Sculptural 1940s–50s suits/coats, elaborate seaming, buttons and fur trims; $300–1,500. After WWII Schuman bought fabric from small French mills, and labels added "Paris" (a post-war dating clue); many labels also name the French mill. 1960s lines included Knits and a mod "London" line; 1970s were career-oriented. Schuman died in 1985; heirs ran the firm until it was sold in the 1990s.
- **Bonnie Cashin / Sills**: Bonnie Cashin Designs ran 1953–77, with outerwear made by Sills and Co.; leather-trimmed, rectangular, minimally darted coats designed to layer; brass turnlock (Coach-style) closures are a hallmark on many pieces (1stDibs listings). $200–800.
- **Forstmann wool (fabric label)**, **Mr. Blackwell, Hattie Carnegie, Pauline Trigère**: $150–500. Forstmann was a Passaic, NJ mill; its label signals fine wool, not a designer.
- **Department store/unlabeled**: $25–100.
- **Marvin Richards**: modern (1990s–2010s) wool-cashmere with fur trim, $40–100 (but see Terapeak: median $124.99 for fur-collar lambswool coats).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bound buttonholes, silk/rayon lining, hand-picked seams = quality. Fur: real has leather backing (part the fur); dyed fox/raccoon common on 1960s collars.
- Better makers: matched plaids at seams, interfacing that holds the collar roll, weighted hems, covered snaps, generous seam allowances, fabric-covered or carved buttons.
- Cheaper production: fused (glued) interfacing that bubbles, polyester linings, machine-blind hems that pucker, plastic buttons.
- Old vs new: metal zippers (when present) in older coats, union labels, hand-sewn labels; modern "vintage style" coats have care labels with symbols and country-of-origin tags (Asia).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Moth holes (check under collar and arms), lining shredding, fur shedding/drying (brittle skins crack), odor, missing buttons. Also: shattered silk linings, sun-faded shoulders, worn cuffs and pocket edges, re-dyed fur, replaced linings (acceptable if done well; disclose).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern "vintage style"; check labels/union tags. Label swaps (a designer label sewn into a plain coat) occur; check that the label stitching matches the other interior stitching.

## Market
Seasonal (Sep–Jan). Fit-dependent; sizes small for modern buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Label value; Lilli Ann and Cashin at estate sales priced as "old coat". Also the union label that dates the coat, and the French mill name on Lilli Ann labels.

## Shipping / handling
Folds into a box; 4–6 lb; $15–25. Fur trim: some states' and international restrictions on certain furs.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Measurements (bust, shoulders, sleeve, length), label, fiber, flaws.
