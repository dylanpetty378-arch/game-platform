Buy rule (short): Buy a legible maker stamp (Bazin, Morizot, Cuniot-Hury, Nürnberger, Pfretzschner, Knopf), straight stick, ≤ $150; lead on good frogs without a stamp photo; pass unstamped/brazilwood/plastic/warped.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Value sits in three things: the stick wood, the maker's stamp, and the mounts. Pernambuco sticks are dense, red-brown,
often octagonal at the handle and round at the tip; brazilwood is lighter/orangey and cheaper; carbon/fibreglass is
modern. Stamp: look just above the frog on the player's side ("BAZIN", "OUCHARD", "TOURTE" (generic German trade stamp,
not a real Tourte), "GERMANY", "NÜRNBERGER", "PFRETZSCHNER", "GLASSER" (student)). Mounts: silver or gold winding and
ferrule = better grade; nickel = trade grade; plastic/whalebone-imitation winding = student. Frog: ebony with Parisian eye
(pearl dot in metal ring) is a French tell; plain pearl dot is generic; tortoiseshell/ivory frogs are top-tier and have
shipping/legal issues. Tip plate: ivory/bone (old) vs plastic. Sellers' words: "fiddle bow", "antique bow", "old violin
bow". Viola and cello bows are heavier/thicker with differently shaped frogs (general knowledge, verify).

Note on the "TOURTE" stamp: this entry calls it a generic German trade stamp, yet the 2007 comp below is a C.N. Bazin
(Mirecourt) bow stamped "Tourte" — so French workshops used it too. A "TOURTE" stamp means "trade bow, maker unknown",
not "German" for certain.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Top (French masters)**: Tourte (real), Peccatte, Voirin, Sartory, Ouchard — need an expert certificate (Raffin).
- **Mirecourt workshops**: Bazin (C.N. and family), Morizot (Frères), Cuniot-Hury, Husson — stamped bows $600–3,000.
- **German**: Nürnberger, Pfretzschner, Knopf; silver-mounted examples in the collectible tier; nickel-mounted unstamped
  Markneukirchen trade bows decent tier.
- **Student**: Glasser fibreglass, brazilwood, Chinese unbranded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Pernambuco vs brazilwood (colour, density, weight ~60 g for violin); silver/gold vs nickel mounts; silver hallmarks or
"silver" stamps on the ferrule/winding; ebony vs plastic frogs; Parisian eye; ivory/bone tip plate vs plastic. Hand-cut
octagonal handles with crisp facets point to better work (general knowledge, verify). Ivory/tortoiseshell parts have
CITES/state-law shipping restrictions.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing hair is normal and cheap to fix (~$60–80 rehair) but a warped or cracked stick is terminal. Check: straightness
(sight down the stick), camber retained, cracks at the head/tip (often repaired with a spline — big value hit), worn or
replaced winding/thumb grip, damaged frog, stripped screw/eyelet, missing button. Ask for a close-up of the stick above
the frog, the head from the side, and the underside of the frog slide.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
False stamps of famous makers (Tourte, Vuillaume, Sartory) on trade bows are very common; only certificates from
recognised experts count for top-tier bows (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Players upgrading, violin shops, dealers and auction buyers (Tarisio, Brompton's, Bonhams, Skinner). Stamped workshop
bows sell best at specialist auctions; unstamped trade bows sell on eBay steadily. Year-round; school-term starts lift
student demand (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The stamp (they never photograph above the frog), pernambuco vs brazilwood, silver vs nickel. A bow that "came with
grandpa's fiddle" is usually a $30 trade bow but 1 in 20 is a $500+ workshop bow. Ask the seller for a close-up of the
stick above the frog and the underside of the frog slide.

## Shipping / handling
Under 1 lb; ship in a rigid tube; $10–15. Resell on eBay or via Tarisio/Brompton's for stamped makers. Cannot
authenticate from photo alone – Dylan's rule says pass unless the stamp is legible in the photo.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State stamp (with photo), stick wood, mount metal, weight in grams, length, frog material and eye, tip plate material,
straightness/camber, hair condition, any repairs. Photograph the stamp, both sides of the frog, the head in profile and
the whole stick sighted. Title: stamp/maker, "pernambuco", "silver mounted", country/era, "violin bow", weight.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a legible maker stamp (Bazin, Morizot, Cuniot-Hury, Nürnberger, Pfretzschner, Knopf) is visible, stick
straight, price ≤ $150. Pass when: no stamp visible, brazilwood colour, plastic winding, warped stick. Lead when: good
frog (Parisian eye, metal winding) but no stamp photographed – ask for one photo before deciding. Profit must be ≥
max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat.
