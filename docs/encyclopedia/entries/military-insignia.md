## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Metal badges and cloth patches worn on uniforms: officer and CPO cap devices (USN officer: silver eagle over a gold shield on gold crossed fouled anchors), USMC eagle-globe-and-anchor (EGA), collar and rank insignia, qualification badges and wings, distinctive unit insignia (DUI crests, enamelled), sweetheart pins, and shoulder sleeve insignia (patches).
Photo tells: WWII officer cap devices are multi-piece (eagle, shield and anchors made separately, soldered or riveted), with the eagle in sterling and the anchors gilt or gold-filled, and **screw-post or prong attachments**; post-war devices tend to be one-piece die-struck gilt with clutch pins, or anodized aluminium (Vietnam-era and later). The back carries "STERLING", often "1/20 10K GF" on gold-filled parts, and a maker's mark.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Vanguard (NY):** "VANGUARD" with "STERLING" and often "1/20 10K GF" or "14K GF" (Flying Tiger lists a Vanguard WWII USN officer full-size badge in sterling and 14K gold fill). Common, good quality.
- **Hilborn-Hamburger (H&H, NY):** "H&H" in a shield or "H-H"; USN, USCG officer devices; USMC 1937-pattern officer EGA (IMA listing).
- **Amcraft (Attleboro):** "AMCRAFT STERLING"; wings and devices.
- **N.S. Meyer (NY):** "N.S. MEYER INC NEW YORK" with a shield/"M"; long-running maker, also post-war (date by attachment and metal).
- **Blackinton (Attleboro):** police badge maker; USN officer devices marked Blackinton are described as scarce.
- **Balfour (Attleboro):** fraternity jeweller; WWII officer overseas-cap badges.
- **Coro:** costume jeweller; sterling WWII US Maritime Service CPO cap badge described as rare.
- **Luxenberg, Robbins, Josten's, Gemsco, Wolf-Brown, Sweet Orr:** wings and qualification badges (general knowledge, verify).
- **Theater-made:** Australian (K.G. Luke, Melbourne — "extremely rare" per Flying Tiger), British, Indian and Philippine-made versions; often heavier, cruder, with local hallmarks.
- **Service and era variants:** USN officer vs CPO (CPO = gold fouled anchor with "USN" silver letters), USCG (shield with CG emblem), US Maritime Service / Merchant Marine (different eagle and device), overseas-cap miniature vs full-size visor device.

### Golden-egg tells
- WWII sterling USN/USCG officer cap device by a named maker (Vanguard, H&H, Amcraft) — ~3–5× a post-war gilt device ($30–100 vs $8–20, general knowledge, verify) — multi-piece construction, "STERLING" plus maker on the back, screw posts or long prongs.
- Scarce maker marks (Blackinton, Balfour, Coro) — ~2× a common maker (general knowledge, verify) — read the maker name on the back; Flying Tiger lists these as scarce/rare.
- Theater-made (Australian K.G. Luke, Indian, British) — ~3–10× a US-made device (general knowledge, verify) — unusual proportions, hand-cut details, foreign hallmarks or maker names.
- US Maritime Service / Merchant Marine officer and CPO devices — ~2× USN equivalents (general knowledge, verify) — different eagle and shield design; "USMS" or Maritime emblems.
- Sterling WWII pilot/aviator wings by Luxenberg or Robbins, and rarer ratings (glider, observer, bombardier, flight surgeon, WASP) — $60–400+ vs $15–30 post-war wings (general knowledge, verify) — "STERLING" on the back, 3-inch full-size, pin with safety catch; rare rating devices in the centre.
- WWI and earlier hand-embroidered bullion or multi-piece devices — $150–1,000+ (general knowledge, verify) — gold/silver bullion thread on wool, or screwback devices with early hallmarks; ask for full back and provenance.
- USMC 1937-pattern officer EGA (H&H) — ~2–3× enlisted EGAs (general knowledge, verify) — pierced globe with separate anchor, sterling, screw posts.

### Quick ID checklist
- Always get the back: "STERLING", maker name, "GF" fractions, attachment (screw posts, prongs, pin-and-catch = older; clutch-back and one-piece = post-war; anodized aluminium = 1960s+).
- Multi-piece construction with visible solder or rivets → WWII or earlier.
- Patina: sterling tarnishes grey-black; gilt brass wears to yellow brass.
- Mixed lots: count the sterling-marked pieces; they carry the value.
- Patches: cut-edge (no merrowed border) and cotton twill → WWII; merrowed rolled edge → post-war; glow under UV → modern synthetic thread.
- Any swastika, SS runes or hate symbols → policy kill.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling silver (eagle parts, wings), gold-filled ("1/20 10K GF") or gilt brass (anchors, shields), enamel (DUIs), bullion thread on wool (officer embroidered devices), anodized aluminium (post-1960s). Quality: crisp die-striking, separate pieces neatly soldered, sharp feather detail; cheap modern copies are cast with soft details and pitting.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing screw backs or bent prongs, separated components, worn gilt, enamel chips on DUIs, moth damage on bullion, re-soldered posts. Original screw nuts add value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Cast copies of WWII wings and devices with fake "STERLING" marks are common; reproduction theater-made and WWI pieces exist. Many modern Chinese replicas of US and foreign insignia are sold as "vintage". German WWII (Nazi) insignia is heavily faked and is a policy kill regardless. Weight, die-strike quality, attachment hardware and patina are the tells; high-value wings may need specialist opinion — price only what a common example would carry.

## Market
Large, active militaria market with many knowledgeable buyers; marked WWII sterling pieces sell in 1–3 weeks, common post-war pieces slowly. Patriotic holidays (Memorial Day, Veterans Day) add some demand.

## What sellers usually miss
- That the eagle on an old USN cap device is sterling and the back is marked.
- Theater-made foreign versions in family lots.
- Sterling wings mixed with costume jewelry or sweetheart pins.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket-size: padded envelope or small box, $5–6. Bag each piece separately so posts don't scratch others.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Front and back photos, maker mark, metal marks, attachment type, dimensions, era attribution with the reason, condition (missing nuts, separations).
### What to tell the buyer
"WWII-era US Navy officer cap device, marked STERLING and [VANGUARD / H&H], multi-piece construction with screw posts [nuts present/absent]. Eagle is sterling; anchors are gold-filled/gilt. Shown front and back; dimensions in photos."
