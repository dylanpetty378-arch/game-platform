## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Coleman lanterns/stoves**: green (stoves) or red/green/silver lanterns; Coleman stamps a month/year date code on the base (e.g. "5 72" = May 1972) from 1920s on. Single-mantle 200A (red, 1950s–), 228, 220E/F, 242C. Pre-war nickel or brass founts.
- **Backpacking stoves**: Svea 123 (Sweden, brass, 1955–), Optimus 8R/111, Primus (brass, pumped kerosene). Brass with tins.
- **Down gear**: Eddie Bauer (Seattle) 1950s–70s with "Eddie Bauer Expedition Outfitter" labels; Holubar (Boulder), Gerry (Denver), Sierra Designs (60/40 cloth), The North Face early brown label (1968–80s), Frostline kits.
- **Modern big-box**: Greatland (Walmart/Target private label, 2010s, "Born to Roam" tagline with stylised "G" mountain triangle), Ozark Trail (Walmart), Coleman synthetic rectangular bags, Wenzel – polyester fill, printed labels, Chinese manufacture. pid 7971219129 is one of these.

## Makers, brands and variants
Coleman (Wichita KS, 1900–), Primus (Sweden 1892–), Optimus (Sweden), Svea, Sigg (Switzerland), Kelty (1952–; early external-frame packs with welded aluminium), Gerry, Holubar, Eddie Bauer, Sierra Designs, The North Face, Woods (Canada, "Arctic" down bags), BSA (Boy Scout mess kits, axes, canteens), US military (USGI) mess kits/canteens, Greatland, Ozark Trail, Wenzel, Texsport.

### Golden-egg tells
- Coleman 200A mint, never fired, in box — $200–325 vs $40–150 for a used dated 200A — unburned mantles, no scratches on the bottom lip of the fount, box and papers (https://forum.expeditionportal.com/goto/post?id=2227418)
- Coleman 200A "Burgundy" (c.1962–63) — collector-preferred variant vs the ordinary red 200A — a visibly deeper red fount than the standard 1950s–60s red; band colour (Black Band 1951–53, Silver Band 1953) is also date-visible on the collar (https://forum.expeditionportal.com/goto/post?id=2227418)
- Coleman 200B in red (1995 and 1997 only) — scarce-colour premium over green 200Bs — red fount on a 200B-stamped lantern (https://forum.expeditionportal.com/goto/post?id=2227418)
- Coleman 242C 1946 painted fount — $45 paid by a collector for a non-nickel variant — painted (not nickel) fount and a globe with a match hole (https://forum.expeditionportal.com/goto/post?id=2227418)
- Coleman Quick-Lite L327 (1920–24), L427 (1925–27), L227 (1927–32) — Terapeak Quick-Lite median $65, p75 $99, complete examples in the $150–600 rare tier vs $25–60 for a 1970s–90s two-burner — nickel fount, mica globe, "Quick-Lite" on the fount; L327 has no date stamp; L227 has the wide ventilator (https://terry-marsh.com/coleman-us-lanterns-1920-1930/ ; entry comp)
- Coleman H416 Arc Lantern (1922–25) — only "hundreds" made, rare tier vs $65 Quick-Lite median — larger mantles, big round air tubes, 800 candlepower (https://terry-marsh.com/coleman-us-lanterns-1920-1930/)
- Coleman 2LQ half-pump Quick-Lite (shipped Jul–Dec 1924) and BLQ wall-bracket lantern (1924–25) — "several hundred" shipped each, rare tier — 2LQ shows a check valve between the pump and filler cap with the pump only partly in the fount; BLQ is a bracket model with a 7 in fount and unmarked ventilator (https://terry-marsh.com/coleman-us-lanterns-1920-1930/)
- Green 200A in box, unused (1968) — $135 appraised vs $20 for a generic 1940 lantern — green fount, original box (https://www.heraldnet.com/?p=177599)
- Eddie Bauer "Expedition Outfitter" / Holubar / Gerry down bags — $80–300 vs $5–25 synthetic big-box bag (~10×) — goose-down label, feather tips poking through, compresses small (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Quality: down fill (feather tips poking out, compresses to small size), nylon taffeta/60/40, YKK or Talon zippers, brass and nickel-plated steel stoves with stamped makers, Coleman date codes. Cheap: polyester batting ("Coleman ThermoLock", "Hollow fibre"), rectangular cotton-flannel-lined bags, plastic zips, printed "Made in China".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Sleeping bags: odour, mildew, flattened fill, stuck zippers, rodent nests – almost unsellable used. Stoves/lanterns: rusty founts, leaking pumps (leather pump cups dry), missing globes/generators – refurbishing = repair (excluded unless tested working by seller).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Coleman reissued "vintage" anniversary lanterns (e.g. 2000s 100th anniversary). Reproduction decals. Chinese Svea 123 copies exist. Not a big fake problem otherwise.

## Market
Coleman lanterns and brass stoves: deep, steady collector market (seasonal spring/summer). Modern sleeping bags: no shipped resale market – shipping costs exceed value.

## What sellers usually miss
Date-coded 1950s Coleman lanterns in garage boxes priced $10–20; brass Svea/Optimus stoves in tins; Eddie Bauer down bags priced as generic sleeping bags (look for the Eddie Bauer goose-down label and compressibility).

## Shipping / handling
Sleeping bags bulky, 4–6 lb, $15–25 to ship; lanterns 3–4 lb with glass globe – box well, $15–20; brass stoves 1–2 lb $10.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** Coleman + model (200A/242C/Quick-Lite) + date code + "lantern"/"stove" + colour; or maker + "down sleeping bag"/"pack" + era. Junk: "camping lot", "outdoor", "retro". `Coleman 200A Lantern 6/62 Red Single Mantle Tested Burns`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for dated Coleman, brass Svea/Optimus, heritage down (collector market, spring/summer); FB local only for tents, synthetic bags and bulky sets.
- **Price position:** dated Coleman/brass stoves at the sold median, accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days or after August. Modern gear priced to clear locally.
- **Photos:** lantern/stove upright on plain background; date stamp on fount base; globe (match hole, cracks); lit and burning (video); pump and valve; for bags, the fill label, zipper and unrolled full length.
- **State:** model and date code, tested (burns/pumps) or untested, original parts vs replacements, rust, fuel drained; bags: fill type, dimensions, temp rating, zipper works, odour/smoke-free; flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** lanterns 3–4 lb double-boxed around the globe, $15–20; brass stoves 1–2 lb, $10; bags 4–6 lb, $15–25; drain all fuel, tape caps, immobilize the globe.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Coleman [model] dated [month/year], fired and burns bright, all original parts; a new 2-mantle Coleman runs [$] and these early ones hold value because the [variant/colour] is collected."

## Buy rule
Untested stoves/lanterns count as needing repair – only buy tested-by-seller or clearly complete date-coded pre-1975 Coleman at ≤ $20. Modern synthetic sleeping bags: resale $10–25, shipping $15+ – automatic pass. Heritage down gear only if photographed label and clean, at ≤ $30. Most bags/stoves fit a passenger seat; tents usually do too but rarely profit.
