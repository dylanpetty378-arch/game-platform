## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Coleman lanterns/stoves**: green (stoves) or red/green/silver lanterns; Coleman stamps a month/year date code on the base (e.g. "5 72" = May 1972) from 1920s on. Single-mantle 200A (red, 1950s–), 228, 220E/F, 242C. Pre-war nickel or brass founts.
- **Backpacking stoves**: Svea 123 (Sweden, brass, 1955–), Optimus 8R/111, Primus (brass, pumped kerosene). Brass with tins.
- **Down gear**: Eddie Bauer (Seattle) 1950s–70s with "Eddie Bauer Expedition Outfitter" labels; Holubar (Boulder), Gerry (Denver), Sierra Designs (60/40 cloth), The North Face early brown label (1968–80s), Frostline kits.
- **Modern big-box**: Greatland (Walmart/Target private label, 2010s, "Born to Roam" tagline with stylised "G" mountain triangle), Ozark Trail (Walmart), Coleman synthetic rectangular bags, Wenzel – polyester fill, printed labels, Chinese manufacture. pid 7971219129 is one of these.

## Makers, brands and variants
Coleman (Wichita KS, 1900–), Primus (Sweden 1892–), Optimus (Sweden), Svea, Sigg (Switzerland), Kelty (1952–; early external-frame packs with welded aluminium), Gerry, Holubar, Eddie Bauer, Sierra Designs, The North Face, Woods (Canada, "Arctic" down bags), BSA (Boy Scout mess kits, axes, canteens), US military (USGI) mess kits/canteens, Greatland, Ozark Trail, Wenzel, Texsport.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Quality: down fill (feather tips poking out, compresses to small size), nylon taffeta/60/40, YKK or Talon zippers, brass and nickel-plated steel stoves with stamped makers, Coleman date codes. Cheap: polyester batting ("Coleman ThermoLock", "Hollow fibre"), rectangular cotton-flannel-lined bags, plastic zips, printed "Made in China".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Sleeping bags: odour, mildew, flattened fill, stuck zippers, rodent nests – almost unsellable used. Stoves/lanterns: rusty founts, leaking pumps (leather pump cups dry), missing globes/generators – refurbishing = repair (excluded unless tested working by seller).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Coleman reissued "vintage" anniversary lanterns (e.g. 2000s 100th anniversary). Reproduction decals. Chinese Svea 123 copies exist. Not a big fake problem otherwise.

## Market
Coleman lanterns and brass stoves: deep, steady collector market (seasonal spring/summer). Modern sleeping bags: no shipped resale market – shipping costs exceed value.

## What sellers usually miss
Date-coded 1950s Coleman lanterns in garage boxes priced $10–20; brass Svea/Optimus stoves in tins; Eddie Bauer down bags priced as generic sleeping bags (look for the Eddie Bauer goose-down label and compressibility).

## Shipping / handling
Sleeping bags bulky, 4–6 lb, $15–25 to ship; lanterns 3–4 lb with glass globe – box well, $15–20; brass stoves 1–2 lb $10.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
For fuel-burning items: state whether tested, date code, original parts, any rust; ship drained of fuel. For bags: fill type, dimensions, temperature rating, odour/smoke-free, zipper function.

## Buy rule
Untested stoves/lanterns count as needing repair – only buy tested-by-seller or clearly complete date-coded pre-1975 Coleman at ≤ $20. Modern synthetic sleeping bags: resale $10–25, shipping $15+ – automatic pass. Heritage down gear only if photographed label and clean, at ≤ $30. Most bags/stoves fit a passenger seat; tents usually do too but rarely profit.
