## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Decorative dinner/service plates with central fish or game-bird scenes, made in Bohemia (Austria-Hungary, now Czech Republic), Bavaria and Limoges, mostly 1890–1930, sold as sets with a matching large platter and sauce boat.
- **MZ Austria (Moritz Zdekauer, Altrohlau near Carlsbad, 1884–1909; then C.M. Hutschenreuther)**: "MZ Austria" with crown/shield mark in green; scalloped gilt-rim plates, transfer printed fish or birds often over-painted, coloured borders (green, pink, cream).
- **Victoria Carlsbad Austria**, **Carlsbad Austria**, **Imperial Crown China Austria**: similar transfer-decorated sets.
- **Limoges** (France): hand-painted fish in water with seaweed, often signed by decorators (e.g. "Dubois", "Max", "Luc", "R.K."); Elite, T&V, J.P.L. blanks; heavy gilt rococo rims; sets of 12 + platter.
- **Haviland & Co.**: fish sets designed by Félix Bracquemond (Japonisme), etc. — collectible.
- **English**: Royal Doulton (artist-signed fish, Charles Hart, J. Birbeck), Minton, Royal Worcester (Harry Davis): hand-painted and expensive.

## Makers, brands and variants
- MZ Austria (Altrohlau): mark dates c.1884–1909, stamped in green; later "MZ Altrohlau CMR Czechoslovakia" after 1918.
- Carlsbad/Victoria (Schmidt & Co.), Pirkenhammer (Fischer & Mieg), Epiag.
- Bavaria: Z.S. & Co. (Zeh, Scherzer), Hutschenreuther, Thomas, Rosenthal.
- Limoges: Haviland, T&V (Tressemann & Vogt), Elite (Bawo & Dotter), J.P.L. (Pouyat), Coiffe, Délinières (D&Co); artist signatures on face.
- American decorating studios on Limoges blanks: Pickard (Chicago), Stouffer, Rosenthal-blank amateur painting.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Transfer vs hand-painting: transfers have flat, uniform edges and dot texture under zoom; hand-painted show brushstrokes, overlaps, raised enamels and signatures on the face.
- Gilt: rubbed gilt rims (common) cut value; tooled heavy gold indicates higher grade.
- Platter and sauce boat presence; matching sets of 6–12.
- Holes or wire hangers indicate wall use; small drilled holes reduce value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips under hanger clips, hairlines, gilt wear, knife scratches, crazing on overglaze, discolouration from hanging; wire hangers scratch rims.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese "Limoges" marks on decals; reproductions of Royal Doulton/Minton fish painting rare. "Hand painted" stamped on backs of transfer-decorated plates (overpainted transfers) is common and misleading.

## Market
Slow; fish sets are out of fashion except with coastal/fishing decorators and a few collectors. Signed hand-painted sets still sell.

## What sellers usually miss
- Artist signatures on Limoges hand-painted plates hung as decor.
- English hand-painted signed plates (Doulton, Minton) mixed with Austrian transfers.
- Usually sellers overprice transfer plates as "antique hand painted".

## Shipping / handling
8.5–9.5" plates, ~1 lb each; dish-pack with foam dividers; set of 5–6 ~$18–25 each way.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State transfer vs hand-painted, mark and dates, number of plates, diameters, any hanger holes, chips or gilt wear; photograph backstamps.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 to buyer − ~$20 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Transfer-printed MZ/Bavaria sets of ≤ 6 without platter: pass.
- Hand-painted signed Limoges/English with platter: buy at ≤ $80 if resale ≥ $300 (backstamp + signature photos first).
