## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Ray-Ban was a Bausch & Lomb (Rochester NY) brand from 1937 until Luxottica bought it in 1999. B&L pairs were US-made with mineral glass lenses (G-15 grey-green, B-15 brown, RB-3 "Tru Green" early, Ambermatic photochromic, Chromax, Diamond Hard, mirror). The whole value is "is it B&L, which model, which lens, what condition".
- **B&L tells in a photo:** a tiny laser-etched "BL" in a corner of one lens (from ~1982; earlier B&L lenses have no etch — then the frame marks date it); the other lens may carry an etched or painted "Ray-Ban" script (1990s); the temple inside reads "B&L Ray-Ban U.S.A." (sometimes "Ray-Ban B&L" earlier, or "B&L Ray-Ban Made in U.S.A."), often with a model code (e.g. "L2823", "W0365", "5022"); metal aviators are stamped on the bridge ("B&L 1/10 12K GF" 1940s–50s, "B&L 1/10 10K GF" earlier/also, later "B&L Ray-Ban U.S.A." plated); 5- or 7-barrel hinges on acetate; Wayfarer 5022 has elongated chrome inserts on the front and temples (not the raised metal "Ray-Ban" logo of later stems) and "B&L 5022" on the inside of the front above the right lens, "WAYFARER" on a temple.
- **Luxottica tells:** "RB" etched on the lens, "Ray-Ban" script printed on the lens, model number like "RB2140 50□22 150" on the temple, "Made in Italy", CE mark, modern packaging/QR.
- Models by silhouette: Aviator (teardrop wire, "L0205" 58mm / "L2823" 62mm), Outdoorsman (aviator with a plastic "sweat bar"/brow bar and cable or plastic temple tips), Shooter (aviator with a round "cigarette holder" ring at the bridge), General (aviator with a flat engraved brow bar; 1987 50th-anniversary, "50" hollowed in the lens), Caravan (square wire), Olympian/Olympian II (1965–; wrap-style wire with wide temples, "Easy Rider" style), Wayfarer 5022 (1952–; acetate trapezoid), Wayfarer II (larger, straighter), Wayfarer Max/Street Neat/Nomad (1980s–90s variants, W05xx codes), Clubmaster W0365 (1986–, browline), Cats 5000/Cats 3000 (oversized plastic 1980s), Wings (one-piece shield, 1980s), Signet, Dekko (1980s), Stateside, Traditionals (acetate, 1980s), 1990s W-series (W2xxx: Predator, Orbs, Sidestreet, Rituals, Sport, Cutters — metal and plastic wraps with "BL" etch and a printed "Ray-Ban" on the lens).
- Look-alikes: Randolph Engineering, American Optical (AO Original Pilot — also valuable, separate), Serengeti, Foster Grant, countless 1980s–2010s fakes. Our lot photos show 1990s-style metal wraps and a square-lens aviator — the W-series/early-Luxottica era, with "Ray-Ban" printed on the lens corner on at least one pair.
- Title words sellers use: "vintage Ray Ban", "Bausch Lomb", "B&L", "USA", "aviator", "Wayfarer", "sample", "optician", "rep samples", "sunglasses lot".

## Makers, brands and variants
**Bausch & Lomb (1937–1999).** Anti-Glare aviator 1937, Outdoorsman 1939, Shooter 1938, Wayfarer 1952, Olympian 1965, Ambermatic 1978, Wings early 1980s, Clubmaster 1986, General 1987, Chromax early 1990s, W-series 1990s. Gold-filled frames (1/10 12K GF, 1/10 10K GF, 1/30 10K GO = gold overlay on later) through the 1960s; plated "Arista" gold after.
**Luxottica (1999–).** Same model names, "RB" codes, Italian-made; used $40–100 — not our target.
**Lot companions worth separating out:** Vuarnet (Pouilloux, France; nylon frames, Skilynx/Nautilux/Unilynx/PX-2000/3000/4000/5000 mineral lenses; models 002, 003, 027, 085, 4002, 006, Cat-Eye; "PX Pouilloux" / "Pouilloux on a ski" on the outside of the temples, "France" and the model number inside — dealer asks $325 for a 002 Skilynx; eBay sold est $100–250 clean, $60–120 scratched); Persol (Ratti-era Italy, "Meflecto" temples, arrow hinge; 649/714 $150–400); Carrera (Austria 1980s, 5512 Top Gun-style $100–250); Serengeti (Corning Drivers 1980s–90s $60–150); Maui Jim (modern, $60–150 used); Armani/Giorgio Armani 1980s–90s metal $60–150; Oakley 1990s (Frogskins, M Frames, Romeo/Juliet $100–600 — a separate entry).

### Quick ID checklist
1. Lens corner: "BL" etch = B&L (post-1982). "RB" = Luxottica. Nothing = pre-1982 B&L, a fake, or replaced lenses — go to the frame marks.
2. Temple inside: "B&L Ray-Ban U.S.A." + model code; or Luxottica "RBxxxx … Made in Italy".
3. Bridge (metal): "B&L 1/10 12K GF" (1940s–60s) / "1/10 10K GF" / "Ray-Ban" + "B&L" plated (1970s–90s).
4. Name the model from the silhouette and the extras: sweat bar (Outdoorsman), ring at bridge (Shooter), engraved flat brow bar + "50" (General), chrome inserts (Wayfarer 5022), browline (Clubmaster), shield (Wings).
5. Lens: glass (heavy, "clink", no distortion, gradient or mirror even), colour (G-15 grey-green, B-15 brown, Ambermatic amber-to-brown photochromic, Chromax orange-tinted driving, Diamond Hard, Changeables).
6. Size: lens width in mm on the temple or measure (58 vs 62 aviator; 50 vs 54 Wayfarer).
7. Condition: lens scratches (hold up to light), frame straight, hinges tight, plating worn through, nose pads present, temple tips intact, case/box.

- **Chromax (early 1990s driving lens, orange-amber "Chromax" lenses, W-series frames)** — $300–600 (avg ~$450) vs $125–250 (~2–3×) — distinctly orange-tinted lens with a mirror flash; "Chromax" on the temple or case.

- **1940s–60s gold-filled aviators ("B&L 1/10 12K GF" or "10K GF" on the bridge), 52–58mm** — $200–385 (dealer $385) vs $100–150 plated 1980s (~2–3×) — stamp on the bridge underside; warmer, heavier gold; often with RB-3 "Tru Green" lenses (lighter green than G-15).

- **NOS/boxed with original case, cloth, paperwork** — +50–100% at every tier — B&L brown or black leather-grain case with gold embossing, "Bausch & Lomb" paperwork.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- B&L: mineral glass lenses (heavy, cool to the touch, "clink"), US-made frames — acetate (5022) with 7-barrel-style hinges and chrome inserts; gold-filled or plated metal with cleanly stamped marks; nose pads on metal pairs are silicone/plastic on 1980s+, earlier are plastic "Bakelite"-style. Smooth, tight hinges.
- Fake/cheap: printed (not etched) "BL"/"Ray-Ban", plastic lenses on a "vintage" aviator, misspellings, rough hinge action, peeling plating, lightweight, mismatched font on the temple.
- Age: 1940s–60s = gold-filled stamps, no lens etch; 1970s = "Ray-Ban" + "B&L" plated, no etch; 1982–99 = "BL" etch; 1990s = "BL" etch plus printed "Ray-Ban" script on W-series; 1999+ = "RB".
- Replaced lenses (aftermarket) kill the premium — look for the etch and for matching gradient/tint on both lenses.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Common: lens scratches (the main value killer — hold to a light and photograph against a dark background), chipped lens edges, worn plating at the nose/temples, bent temples/bridge, loose or stripped hinge screws, missing/yellowed nose pads, cracked acetate at the hinge, brittle temple tips, "verdigris" on gold frames.
- Kills value: deep scratches in the viewing zone, cracked lens, replaced non-B&L lenses, re-plated frames, cracked frame, bent beyond true.
- Acceptable: light plating wear, hairlines outside the viewing zone, missing case.
- Ask: close-up of each lens held to light, close-up of the lens corner etch, inside of both temples, bridge stamp, "any scratches, chips, bends, replaced lenses or screws?"

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Fake Ray-Bans are everywhere (1980s–now), including fake "B&L" marks on cheap aviators and Wayfarers. Authenticate every pair: etch precision, hinge quality, glass lenses, correct model code on the temple, correct typography, weight. Modern fakes imitate Luxottica codes and even QR tags.
- Luxottica reissues: Wayfarer RB2140 (2000s "Original Wayfarer"), Aviator RB3025, Outdoorsman RB3030, Shooter RB3138, Clubmaster RB3016, "Ray-Ban Legends" and "Reloaded" lines — all legitimate, all $40–120 used; the names do not make them vintage.
- "Vintage style" no-brand aviators sold as Ray-Ban.

## Market
- Deep, fast market: Ray-Ban is one of the most-searched vintage eyewear brands; eBay US moves thousands of Ray-Ban pairs a month, of which B&L-marked vintage is a few hundred. Clean B&L pairs sell in 1–2 weeks at a fair price; premium models in days.
- Buyers: wearers (want size, lens colour, no scratches), collectors (want model, era, box/case, NOS), stylists/costume. eBay is best (13.6% + $0.40); Poshmark 20%; Etsy for styled "vintage" listings; Grailed for men's fashion.
- Seasonality: peak April–August, secondary December; slow Jan–Feb and Oct–Nov (Underpriced).
- Trend: B&L premium steady to rising as supply dries; 1990s W-series rising with Y2K fashion.

## What sellers usually miss
- The "BL" etch and "B&L Ray-Ban U.S.A." — they list "vintage Ray-Ban" and price like a used Luxottica pair ($30–60).
- The model name (Outdoorsman vs Aviator; Wayfarer vs Wayfarer II; Orbs vs generic "wrap") and the lens type (Ambermatic, Chromax, RB-3, mirror, changeables).
- Gold-filled bridge stamps on 1940s–60s aviators.
- Optician/rep sample lots: samples are often mint, unworn, with demo lenses or genuine lenses; a lot of "assorted brands" hides the few B&L and Pouilloux pairs that carry all the value — ask for temple photos of every pair.
- Original cases, which add 20–40%.

## Shipping / handling
- 4–8 oz per pair; hard case or a small box with bubble; ~$5–6 Ground Advantage, $9 Priority for $200+ pairs with tracking/insurance. Lenses scratch against each other — never ship two pairs loose in one bag.
- Fragility 2/5; transit risk low if cased.

## Buy rule
Buy when: the pair is B&L-marked (etch and/or temple/bridge stamp photographed), in a premium model (Outdoorsman, Shooter, General, GF aviator, coloured 5022, Wayfarer II/Max, Wings, Dekko, Ambermatic, Chromax, Olympic) or a clean common B&L with case, lenses scratch-free, frame straight, and price ≤ 30% of est. sold — e.g. premium models at ≤ $60, common B&L at ≤ $35, Vuarnet Pouilloux 002/027/085 at ≤ $40. In mixed lots, buy only the identified B&L/Pouilloux pairs individually or the lot at ≤ $25/pair with ≥ 3 confirmed B&L premium pairs.
Pass when: Luxottica (RB etch/Made in Italy), no marks visible and seller will not photograph them, scratched or replaced lenses, bent/re-plated frames, missing nose pads, cracked acetate, generic "designer" lots (Armani, Maui Jim, Oakley basics) at more than $15/pair. No authentication service exists for sunglasses, so the marks ARE the authentication — if they are not clear, pass. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (always).
