## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Polyethylene food-storage containers sold through Tupperware home parties from 1948 on. The patent "burping" seal lid is the defining feature.
- **Servalier (1970s–80s):** round canisters with a **sunburst (starburst) lid** and a push-button lid seal. Colours: harvest gold, orange, avocado green, brown. The example listing is a four-piece orange Servalier canister set.
- **Wonderlier (1950s on):** bowls with flat or "bell" lids, often in pastels (pink, yellow, aqua, mint).
- **Millionaire line (late 1950s–60s):** frosted pastel ribbed pieces with a sleek, mid-century look. Most collected.
- Other recognisable pieces: Ice Tups popsicle moulds, Jel-Ring moulds, Fix-N-Mix bowl, ribbed pitchers, the "Pick-a-Deli" pickle keeper.

## Makers, brands and variants
Only Tupperware itself (Earl Tupper, 1946; Tupperware Home Parties 1951). Embossed "TUPPERWARE" and a mould number under the base. Lookalikes: Rubbermaid, Eagle, Tucker, Stanhome, which sell for less.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Soft polyethylene. Frosted/translucent pastels = 1950s–60s; opaque harvest gold, orange, avocado and brown = 1970s.
- Check that **lids match** the bases in colour family and the "TUPPERWARE" mark is on both.
- Original sheen (not chalky) is the condition tell.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Staining (tomato sauce), odour, scratches, faded colour, warping from the dishwasher, and cracked or missing lids. A missing lid kills a canister's value. No repairs exist.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Tupperware reissued retro colours and Servalier-style canisters in the 2000s–2010s. Newer pieces have different mould numbers and brighter colours. Not a fake risk, but "vintage" is used loosely.

## Market
High volume, low prices. Retro-kitchen buyers like the 1970s colours, but supply from estate sales is huge. Sets take 2–6 weeks to sell.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers overestimate value. "Grandma's Tupperware" listings ask $50–100 for 1970s sets that sell for $25–45. The undervalued case is frosted pastel Millionaire pieces sold in a mixed kitchen lot.

## Shipping / handling
Light but bulky. A 4-piece nesting set ships in a 12×12×14 box, about $15–20 (general knowledge, verify). Not fragile.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the line (Servalier, Wonderlier), colour, number of pieces, and mould numbers. Show that all lids are present and seal. Note stains and odours.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$18 to buyer − ~$18 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Even a $55 set nets about $12 before the price. This type never meets the rule unless a rare Millionaire set selling for $120+ is found for under $20. Treat as pass.
