## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small decorative castings: rickshaws, pagodas, lanterns, carts, animals, used as candle holders, incense burners, matchsafes, trinket boxes, paperweights, bookends. Example 7948712989 is a two-wheel rickshaw with a pagoda-style roof with cut-outs (votive/incense chimney), floral spoked wheels, overall red rust — typical Japanese (Nambu-style) or later Chinese export souvenir/votive.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Japanese Nambu tekki / Iwachu** (Morioka, 1950s–now): good crisp casting, black lacquer finish, sometimes marked with cast kanji or "JAPAN". Incense burners, lanterns, rickshaw and cart figures.
- **US foundries pre-1940**: Hubley (Lancaster PA), Judd (Wallingford CT), Wilton Products, Bradley & Hubbard (B&H triangle), Albany Foundry, National Foundry — usually door-stops/bookends with casting numbers and paint.
- **Modern Taiwan/China repros (1970s–now)**: rust-washed "antique" finish, grainy surface, sold in gift shops and on Amazon for $10–25.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old quality: smooth fine-grained surface, tight seams, filed edges, flat-headed slotted screws or rivets joining halves, layered old paint with wear on high points. New: sandy/pitted texture, rough flashing at seams, Phillips screws, even orange "instant rust" or flat paint, very light weight. Japanese pieces show crisp detail and black lacquer.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Overall rust (reduces value unless original paint), cracks, broken handles/shafts (rickshaw poles are a weak point), welded or epoxied repairs, repainting.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
The whole doorstop/bank/figural field is saturated with reproductions; assume unmarked rusty pieces are post-1970 unless proven otherwise. Reproduction finish rust is uniform and orange; real age shows black patina and worn paint.

## Market
Abundant supply, slow sell-through for generic figurals; decorator buyers on Etsy pay slightly more than eBay.

## What sellers usually miss
Little — owners usually overprice rust-finish pieces as "antique." The miss runs the other way: true signed Hubley/B&H pieces priced as yard-sale iron.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy for size (1–4 lb), robust; small box, $10–14.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker (Hubley/Judd/Bradley & Hubbard/Iwachu), form (doorstop/bookends/incense burner/rickshaw), subject, "cast iron", "original paint", casting number, height. Junk: "antique" on rust-wash repros, "farmhouse", "rustic". `Hubley Cast Iron Scottie Dog Doorstop Original Paint #xxx 1930s`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for signed US pieces (Judd median $60, Hubley $36); Etsy for Japanese Nambu/decor pieces (pays slightly more); FB local for unmarked repros.
- **Price position:** signed US at the sold median, OBO, accept −15%, floor p25 ($30–40); Japanese iron $20–60 firm 3 weeks; repros $10–30 local. Cut 10% at 45 days.
- **Photos:** first: piece front in raking light; then bottom for marks/casting number, seam and screw heads (slotted vs Phillips), paint layers close-up, each crack/repair, back, ruler and weight.
- **State:** maker/mark or "unmarked", dimensions, weight, paint original/repaint/rust finish, "vintage" unless age is proven, cracks/welds/broken shafts by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 1–4 lb, robust; small box with padding on projections, $10–14.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Signed [Hubley] #[xxx] with layered original paint and slotted screws — the rust-wash imports are $15 gift-shop iron; the pre-1940 marked pieces are what sells at $50–200."
