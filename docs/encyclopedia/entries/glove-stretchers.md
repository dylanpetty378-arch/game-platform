## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Scissor- or tong-shaped tool (c. 1870–1930) with long tapered blades inserted into glove fingers and spread by squeezing the handles. Wooden versions are two shaped slats with a coil spring between them (the example: two tapered fruitwood/maple slats, coil spring, ~10–11 in) — spring tong type. Better types: scissor pivot with sterling repoussé handles (part of dresser sets), bone or ivory, ebony.

## Makers, brands and variants
Mostly unmarked; English and American dresser-set makers (Unger Bros., Gorham, Webster for sterling handles). Dresser sets matched with buttonhooks, shoehorns.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling handles: "STERLING" stamp, repoussé floral. Bone: fine dark pores, yellowing. Ivory: Schreger lines (no buy). Wood: hand-shaped, worn patina vs modern.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks in wood near spring/pivot, dents in thin sterling, missing springs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little reproduction activity; some resin "ivory".
