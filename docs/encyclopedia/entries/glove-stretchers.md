## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Scissor- or tong-shaped tool (c. 1870–1930) with long tapered blades inserted into glove fingers and spread by squeezing the handles.
Used so kid-leather gloves (from young goats, with tapered fingertips) could be pulled on without damage, and to reshape fingers after
washing; "every good household" had one in the Victorian era (Wikipedia). Wooden versions are two shaped slats with a coil spring between
them (the example: two tapered fruitwood/maple slats, coil spring, ~10–11 in) — spring tong type. Better types: scissor pivot with sterling
repoussé handles (part of dresser sets), bone or ivory, ebony, celluloid ("French ivory"). Typical length 8¾–11 in (Whiting examples 8¾–9 in).
Marks: on sterling, on the inside of a handle or along a blade — maker's trademark, "STERLING", pattern number, sometimes a patent date.
Look-alikes: sugar tongs, curling tongs, asparagus tongs (shorter and broader tips), and bread/glove "openers".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Whiting Mfg. Co.** (US) — repoussé sterling stretchers marked "Whiting / Sterling / 3324 / Patent June 29.75" (1870s, 8¾ in, ~93 g;
  1stDibs) and a hammered-surface version with pearl-motif decoration attributed to Charles Osborne, marked Whiting trademark and
  "STERLING / 1250", c. 1880s, 9 in, 4.55 troy oz, $1,400 asking (Spencer Marks).
- **Gorham, Unger Bros., Webster** and other US sterling vanity makers; English hallmarked silver from Birmingham/Chester vanity-set makers
  (general knowledge, verify) — usually as part of dresser sets with buttonhooks and shoehorns.
- **Celluloid / early plastics** — "ivory-coloured" stretchers (MoDiP), often in celluloid vanity sets of the 1910s–30s.
- **Wood** — mostly unmarked; spring-loaded two-slat tong type (Wikipedia: "two pieces hinged in the middle and spring-loaded").

### Quick ID checklist
- Material: wood, bone, celluloid, sterling (full or handles only), ivory (pass).
- Marks on the inner handle: "STERLING", maker trademark (Whiting lion/W, Gorham lion-anchor-G, Unger Bros. UB), pattern number, patent date.
- Decoration: repoussé florals, hammered surface, Art Nouveau faces = sterling better tier.
- Mechanism: scissor pivot vs coil-spring tong; spring present and working.
- Length 8–11 in; tips rounded and smooth.
- Monogram (common, slightly lowers value for some buyers, fine for most).
- Condition of thin sterling: dents, splits along repoussé, bent handle.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Whiting hammered sterling, Charles Osborne design (c. 1880s)** — $1,400 dealer ask (Spencer Marks) vs $10–25 wood — hammered surface with applied pearl/nautical motifs; "STERLING 1250" with Whiting mark.
- **Whiting repoussé sterling with "Patent June 29.75" mark** — dealer-tier (1stDibs, price on request) vs $35 Terapeak sterling median — dense floral repoussé handles; patent line stamped inside the handle.
- **Named-maker Art Nouveau sterling (Unger Bros., Gorham)** — upper end of the $50–150 band or above (general knowledge) — maker mark, Art Nouveau maiden/flower handles.
- **Full matched sterling dresser set including the glove stretcher** — set premium over the single piece (general knowledge) — same repoussé pattern across buttonhook, shoehorn, stretcher.
- **Turned ebony or carved bone** — $25–60 vs $10–25 plain wood (old entry) — dense black wood or fine-pored bone with turned finials.
- Plain wooden spring tongs and celluloid — not golden eggs.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling handles: "STERLING" stamp, repoussé floral. Bone: fine dark pores, yellowing. Ivory: Schreger lines (no buy). Wood: hand-shaped,
worn patina vs modern. Celluloid: creamy with regular parallel "grain" lines and seams, light weight, may smell of camphor when warm.
Full sterling is heavy for its size (Whiting ~93–140 g); sterling-handled pieces have steel or silver-plated blades. Quality: crisp chasing,
smooth pivot, symmetrical tips. Old vs new: wear on high points of repoussé, oxidation in recesses, hand-finished pivots.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks in wood near spring/pivot, dents in thin sterling, missing springs. Also: bent handles, splits along repoussé, polished-flat
high points, solder repairs at the pivot, monograms removed (thin spots). The 1stDibs Whiting example carries a slight bend, high-point
wear and a monogram and still sells at dealer level — light wear is acceptable on sterling.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little reproduction activity; some resin "ivory". Modern silver-plate reproductions of vanity tools exist (general knowledge) — look for
"STERLING" plus a maker mark, not "silver plate" or nothing.

## Market
Niche, slow. 30–90 days. Buyers: sterling/vanity collectors, glove and costume collectors, curiosity buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling handles under tarnish; maker marks (Whiting, Gorham, Unger) inside the handles; patent dates.

## Shipping / handling
Small flat box, $6–9.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Length, material, marks, mechanism function. For sterling, weight in grams/troy oz.

### What to tell the buyer
"Victorian glove stretcher, __ in long, used to open the fingers of kid-leather gloves before wearing. __ (sterling repoussé /
turned wood) handles, marked '__' inside the handle (photographed). Pivot/spring works smoothly; __ (no dents / minor high-point
wear); weight __ g."

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): wooden ones fail; sterling at ≤ $25 marginal unless a named maker (Whiting/Gorham/Unger). Fits pocket/shoebox.
