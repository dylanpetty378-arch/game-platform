## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Spittoons (cuspidors) were standard in depots, offices, cars and hotels into the 1910s–20s. Railroadiana Online and the Union Pacific Railroad Museum agree:
- **Authentic railroad spittoons** are **low-profile and heavy** (hard to tip over), with a wide mouth or funnel top, sometimes enamelled (the UP museum shows short green, silver and brass-coloured examples). Railroad marks are **simple initials**, cast in or incorporated into the finish. Supplier catalogues (Handlan / Handlan-Buck, St Louis, 1893 and 1930s–40s) show the same low form. Railroad-marked examples are fairly rare.
- **Reproductions** are **tall, vase-shaped brass imports** of the last few decades, embossed with company names — Union Pacific Railroad, Wells Fargo & Company Express (San Francisco Division), Pullman Silver Palace Car Company, Chattanooga Choo Choo, Hannibal & St. Joseph — often with an embossed locomotive. One was found still bagged with an $18.98 novelty-store sticker. Heavy weathering does not prove age; it reflects cheap brass (Railroadiana).
- Companion field note (railroadiana entry, 2026-10-14): thin spun-copper bodies with a brass flare and an embossed 4-4-0 locomotive over "UNION PACIFIC RR" / "NORTHERN PACIFIC R.R." are 1970s–80s decorative souvenirs; real railroad spittoons are heavy cast/weighted brass with property stamping.
- Triage (2026-10-13): brass/copper spittoons embossed "Union Pacific" with a locomotive are reproductions; UP never issued one. Kill on sight.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Genuine railroad:** Handlan-Buck (St Louis railway-supply house), other railway-supply catalogues; marks are initials (e.g. "C.B.&Q.", "N.Y.C.", "P.R.R.") cast or stamped, sometimes on the base (general knowledge for the specific initials, verify). The Grand Trunk System used an ornate china spittoon with a crest instead of initials (Railroadiana).
- **Non-railroad genuine cuspidors:** weighted spun-brass hotel/bank/office cuspidors (often with a lead or cast-iron weighted base), enamelware (graniteware) cuspidors, nickel-plated Pullman-type car cuspidors without lettering, Victorian decorated porcelain, and American stoneware/pottery — spongeware, Rockingham-glazed yellowware, redware (Crocker Farm sold an Eberly, Strasburg VA, redware cuspidor), Red Wing and other Midwest stoneware (general knowledge, verify).
- **Reproductions:** tall vase forms marked UP, Wells Fargo, Pullman, Chattanooga Choo Choo, Hannibal & St. Joseph, Santa Fe, Northern Pacific, "Saloon", "Bank"; often with a locomotive or stagecoach.

### Golden-egg tells
- Genuine railroad-marked low-profile spittoon — ~10× a reproduction ($150–500+ vs $15–40, general knowledge, verify) — squat, wide, heavy; simple initials cast in, no pictorial locomotive; matches Handlan catalogue form.
- Grand Trunk System crested china spittoon — rare; several hundred dollars (general knowledge, verify) — china (not brass) body with an ornate crest and backstamp.
- Decorated American stoneware/redware cuspidors (spongeware, Rockingham, attributed redware) — $80–400 vs $30 for plain (general knowledge, verify) — sponged blue or mottled brown glaze, low drum or squat form, hand-thrown.
- Hotel/bank/government-marked heavy brass cuspidors — ~2–4× unmarked ($80–250, general knowledge, verify) — stamped hotel or agency name on the base or flange, weighted base, wide funnel top.
- Enamelled car or depot cuspidors in original green/grey paint with railroad stencil — $150–400 (general knowledge, verify) — painted sheet-iron or cast form, original paint wear.

### Quick ID checklist
- Silhouette: tall, narrow-waisted vase = reproduction; short and wide, wider than tall = candidate.
- Lettering: embossed railroad/express name or locomotive in relief = reproduction; small initials cast in or stamped = candidate.
- Weight: ask "how heavy?" — real ones are heavy for their size; spun-copper repros are light.
- Base photo: weighted base, maker or supplier stamp, property marks.
- Story: "found in an old depot" with a tall vase form is a sales story, not provenance.
- Wear: heavy corrosion is not age (Railroadiana).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Genuine: thick spun or cast brass with weighted bases, enamelled sheet iron, nickel-plated brass (car fixtures), stoneware/redware, porcelain. Repros: thin brass or copper, stamped seams, soft embossing, artificial patina.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, split seams, missing liners or inner funnels, re-plating, lost enamel, chips and cracks on pottery. Polishing original patina off railroad pieces lowers value with collectors.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
The whole tall, embossed "railroad" spittoon category is fake per railroad historians (Railroadiana) and the UP museum; they keep reappearing online with invented 1800s histories. Also reproduced: "Wells Fargo" and "Pullman" brass items generally, and decorated stoneware (fake sponge decoration). Lens matches repros to eBay "vintage UP spittoon" asks (brass-decor note) — asking prices on repros are not evidence.

## Market
Railroadiana collectors are knowledgeable and wary; genuine pieces sell to them in 2–6 weeks. Repros sell slowly to decorators. Stoneware cuspidors sell to Americana pottery buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers overrate the repros (the embossed name is what convinces them) and underrate plain heavy cuspidors and stoneware ones with no lettering.

## Shipping / handling
Shoebox-to-carry-on size, 3–12 lb; double-box pottery; $15–25. Fits a passenger seat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Height and base diameter, weight, every mark, base photo, enamel/pottery condition.
### What to tell the buyer
"Low-profile weighted brass cuspidor marked [initials] on [location]; [height] in tall, [diameter] in base, [weight] lb. Form matches period railway-supply catalogue examples. Not one of the tall embossed decorative reproductions." Never describe a tall embossed piece as railroad-used.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $20 − $20 − price ≥ max($50, price). Reproductions: kill (also a misrepresentation risk). Genuine low-profile railroad-marked: buy ≤ $40–60 when photos show initials and form clearly (resale $150+). Unmarked weighted brass and plain stoneware: pass unless ≤ $15. Decorated stoneware: ≤ $40. Anything needing re-plating or crack repair: pass.
