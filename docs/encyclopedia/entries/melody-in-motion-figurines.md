## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-painted porcelain figurines (roughly 6–12 in tall) with a built-in Japanese music movement (usually Sankyo) and a mechanical motion: a figure sways, an arm turns a crank, a hat lifts, a carousel turns. Look for: a glossy, softly-colored porcelain figure on a round or oval porcelain/wood-look base; a wind-up key or on/off lever under the base; a paper or foil label "Melody in Motion" / "Willitts Designs" and often "Seiji Nakane" (sculptor) and a song title. Subjects: hobo clowns ("Willie" series – Willie the Fisherman, Organ Grinder, Trumpeter, etc.), tradesmen (lamplighter, shoemaker, blacksmith), sailors, Santas (very popular), carousels, ballerinas, musicians. Mostly 1980s–2000s (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Willitts Designs** (Petaluma, CA importer founded by Bill Willitts, early 1980s) – the brand on box and label. Made in Japan in the earlier years; later production moved offshore (Taiwan/China) – check the bottom label (general knowledge, verify).
- **Seiji Nakane** – sculptor; his name appears on labels/certificates and makes the line collectible.
- Variants: standard musical figures; **Santa series** (annual Christmas pieces, best sellers); **carousels and multi-figure "group" pieces** (larger, scarcer); limited editions with numbered certificates; some later pieces have lights/battery (general knowledge, verify).
- Later licensed/continued production existed after Willitts; details unknown.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real pieces: heavy bisque/glazed porcelain, fine painted faces, crisp detail, smooth action, clear tune. Earlier Japan-made pieces tend to be better painted. Original box with styrofoam insert and certificate adds ~20–40% for collectors. Cheap look-alikes (generic resin "musical clown" from gift shops) are light, resin, blurry paint, no Willitts label.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Movement failure is the #1 problem: overwound/broken spring, motion gears stripped, music plays but figure doesn't move. Always ask for a video.
- Chips on fingers, hat brims, fishing poles, instruments; glued breaks (look for glue lines at wrists/necks).
- Missing accessories (umbrellas, balloons, poles, lanterns).
- Battery pieces: corrosion in the compartment.
Repairs are not worth it for Dylan's rules.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No meaningful fakes – value is too low. Risk is confusing generic resin musical figures (Enesco, San Francisco Music Box Co., unbranded) for Melody in Motion. Check the label.

## Market
Mature, soft collector market. Many listed, moderate sales; buyers are older collectors and gift buyers. Strong Nov–Dec for Santa pieces. Typical days to sell 30–60 at fair price (estimate).

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers usually overprice ("Retail was $150"). Underpricing happens in estate lots ("clown music box") where the Willitts label isn't mentioned; Santa and carousel pieces are the ones worth picking.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile porcelain with protruding parts. Double-box, wrap protruding arms, immobilize the motion (tape the wind-up so it doesn't run in transit). 3–5 lb packed; $15–22 ground.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State title, sculptor (Seiji Nakane), Willitts Designs, tune name, movement working (include a short video), any chips, box/COA included. Photograph the base label.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$18) − ship to Dylan (~$18) − price ≥ max($50, price). With commons at ~$40 median, the math never works for single common pieces. Only possible: a Santa/carousel/limited piece with a realistic $100+ sold median, working on video, at ≤ $25. Default: pass. Local pickup lots of several working pieces for ≤ $30 could work. No repairs.
