## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Factory-made decorative vases, bowls and planters from American potteries ca. 1880–1970. Photo tells:
- **Arts & Crafts high end**: thick matte glazes (green "Grueby green", Teco green, Van Briggle "Persian Rose"/"Mulberry"/"Ming Blue"), hand-incised or carved floral decoration (Newcomb: bayou moss, oaks, moon, in blues/greens), Rookwood "Standard Glaze" (brown with yellow/orange underglaze slip floral) or "Vellum" (misty landscapes). Look for an impressed/incised bottom mark — buyers will ask for a bottom photo.
- **Roseville**: molded floral patterns in named lines (Pinecone, Futura, Blackberry, Sunflower, Baneda, Morning Glory, Ferrella, Wisteria, Jonquil = high; Freesia, Zephyr Lily, Snowberry, Magnolia, Bittersweet, Apple Blossom, Clematis, Columbine, Peony, Gardenia = mid-low). Raised "Roseville U.S.A." mark with shape number from 1940s; earlier pieces unmarked with foil labels.
- **Weller**: Louwelsa (brown standard glaze), Hudson (artist-painted matte), Coppertone (frogs), Woodcraft. Ink stamp or impressed "Weller".
- **Mid-century commercial**: Gonder (glossy mottled two-tone glazes like "Shell Pink", "Mother of Pearl", "Gunmetal", "Chinese crackle"; deep "Gonder Original" or "Gonder U.S.A." marks plus H-, J-, K-, E- shape numbers), McCoy (marked "McCoy" or "NM" Nelson McCoy), Hull (Bow-Knot, Woodland, Parchment & Pine; "Hull Art USA"), Shawnee, Haeger (red/gold Haeger paper labels, "Royal Haeger"), Red Wing (numbered, "Red Wing U.S.A."), Frankoma (Ada clay = honey/light; Sapulpa clay = red, after 1953), Bauer, Pacific (California).
- **Folk/hand-painted imported** (Mexico, Peru, India, Eastern Europe): red earthenware with painted enamel or lacquer-like folk florals — not American art pottery, $10–40.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Rookwood** (Cincinnati, 1880–1967, revived): reversed "RP" flame mark with one flame per year after 1886; Roman numeral year after 1900. Artist-signed Standard Glaze $200–800; Vellum landscapes $800–5,000; post-1930 production molded pieces $50–200.
- **Roseville** (Zanesville, OH, 1890–1954): see patterns above. Common later floral vases $60–250; Futura, Della Robbia, Baneda, Blackberry, Pinecone (esp. blue) $300–2,000+.
- **Weller** (Zanesville, 1872–1948): production $40–250; Sicard, Hudson artist-signed, Coppertone $300–2,000.
- **Van Briggle** (Colorado Springs, 1899–2012): dated 1900–1920 pieces $500–5,000; later "Colo Spgs" and post-1960 $40–200.
- **Grueby** (Boston, 1894–1920), **Teco** (Gates, Illinois), **Marblehead** (MA), **Newcomb College** (New Orleans, 1895–1940, NC cypher + artist and date), **Fulper** (Flemington NJ, vertical "Fulper" ink rectangle, flambé glazes, $150–2,000), **Cowan** (Ohio, 1912–31, $50–400), **Pewabic** (Detroit).
- **Gonder** (Zanesville, 1941–1957, Lawton Gonder): glossy art-ware glazes, mark impressed "Gonder" or "Gonder Original" with shape number (e.g. H-67 triple leaf, H-14, J-25). Most sell $15–50; Chinese-crackle and large Asian-style forms (e.g. ewers, panther, dragon) $60–200.
- **McCoy** (Roseville OH, 1910–1990): planters $10–50; rare figural planters and early stoneware higher.
- **Hull** (Crooksville OH, 1905–1986): pre-1950 matte florals (Bow-Knot, Magnolia matte, Woodland matte) $40–250; later glossy $10–40.
- **Haeger** (Dundee IL, 1871–2016): 1940s–70s Royal Haeger $10–80; Earth Wrap and some MCM forms higher.
- **Red Wing** (MN): art ware $20–80; stoneware crocks separately.
- **Frankoma** (OK, 1933–2011): Ada clay/Norman pieces $50–300; later $10–40.
- **NC folk**: Jugtown, North State, Pisgah Forest (cameo) $50–500.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight and clay: heavy, dense, crisp molding and well-defined detail = early factory; soft mold detail and thin glaze = later or reproduction.
- Glaze: matte organic glazes with "curdling" = Arts & Crafts. Glossy two-tone drip/mottle = 1940s–50s commercial.
- Bottom: dry-footed unglazed ring with impressed mark, shape number, artist cypher. Stilt marks on fully glazed bottoms = commercial ware.
- Size matters: a 14" Roseville is often 3–5× a 6" in the same pattern.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, hairline cracks, crazing (normal on Roseville glossy and Rookwood standard glaze, tolerated), glaze flakes on high relief petals, restored rims (spray-paint/epoxy — check with a blacklight or ask seller to tap for a dull sound). Repaired pieces drop 50–80%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Chinese Roseville fakes** (1990s–today) are everywhere: blurry molds, garish wrong colors, raised "Roseville U.S.A." mark on the wrong patterns, often too light. Also fake Weller and fake Rookwood marks.
- Van Briggle made the same Art Nouveau shapes (Lorelei, Despondency) for decades; dated pieces are what bring money.
- Haeger, McCoy and Hull molds were reused by later companies; McCoy repros from Mexico/China and the "Nelson McCoy" reissues.

## Market
Roseville/Rookwood market is soft compared with 2000 peaks; common pieces move slowly (weeks to months). High-end Arts & Crafts remains strong at auction. MCM pottery (Gonder, Haeger) sells to decorators in the $20–60 band.

## What sellers usually miss
Unmarked early Roseville (foil labels lost), Newcomb/Grueby in a plain "green pottery vase" listing, Van Briggle with a date on the base, Fulper's ink mark mistaken for a store stamp, Rookwood flame marks with a year cypher.

## Shipping / handling
Box in box, 2" foam/bubble on all sides; stuff the interior. Typical 3–6 lb shipped $15–25. High breakage risk on handles and leaves.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give maker, pattern name, shape number and size, photos of the base mark, note crazing and any chips/repairs specifically (blacklight-checked).

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 ship to buyer − ~$20 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). In practice: Roseville rare patterns, Rookwood, Fulper, Van Briggle dated, Grueby/Newcomb/Teco at ≤ 30% of median. Pass every Gonder/McCoy/Hull/Haeger single. Passenger-seat size is never an issue. No professional authentication needed, but require a clear photo of the base mark.
