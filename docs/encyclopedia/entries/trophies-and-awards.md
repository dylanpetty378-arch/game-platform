## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Telly Award statuette: stylized winged/leaping silver figure on a black cylindrical base (the example); given in large numbers each year for TV/video/commercial production. Clio: elongated abstract figure; Addy: silver/gold "A" form. Emmy (winged woman with atom), Oscar (knight on reel) — Oscars cannot legally be resold (post-1950 agreement) and Emmy has similar restrictions. Sterling loving cups: two- or three-handled, hallmarked "STERLING" + maker (Gorham, Tiffany, Reed & Barton). Figural Art Deco trophies: spelter/bronze sportsmen.
- Telly Awards: founded 1979 for video and television; awarded in Gold, Silver and Bronze tiers; over 12,000 entries a year, and each credited team member can receive a statue, so supply is large (marjennings.com).
- Restricted awards (cllct; Variety): Oscars awarded after 1951 must first be offered back to the Academy (for $1 per cllct, $10 per Variety) — a rule binding heirs, upheld in a 2015 Los Angeles ruling. Emmys may not be sold at all and are to be returned to the Television Academy. Grammys are technically owned by the Recording Academy and cannot be sold. Tony winners must first offer the trophy to Tony Award Productions for $10. Pre-1951 Oscars can sell: "Gone With the Wind" Best Picture $1.54M (1999), "Citizen Kane" screenplay $645,000 (2023).
- Sterling presentation cups: often 6–10" tall, 2–3 handles (sometimes antler/horn handles), engraved with event, date and names; marks under the foot or on the side near the base.
- Look-alikes: silver-plated cups (EPNS, "Quadruple Plate", "Silver Soldered"), pewter, and novelty replica statuettes.

## Makers, brands and variants
Telly Awards (est. 1979, statuettes by RS Owens/Society Awards-style makers); Society Awards and R.S. Owens make Emmy/Clio/Grammy-type statuettes (makers are agent knowledge, verify). Sports trophies: plastic columns (low). Sterling: Gorham, Tiffany, Reed & Barton.
- **Gorham** sterling loving cups: lion-anchor-G hallmarks plus "STERLING" and a pattern/model code; a c.1900 Gorham three-handled antler cup (7.75") engraved as a church presentation was appraised at $700–1,000 fair market value (Mearto, estimate not sale). Marks of the 1863–1890s style help date Gorham.
- **Tiffany & Co.**: "TIFFANY & CO. MAKERS STERLING SILVER" with pattern and order numbers; Tiffany trophies (yachting, racing, golf) carry a premium (general knowledge, verify).
- **Reed & Barton, Wm. B. Durgin, Shreve, Black Starr & Frost, J.E. Caldwell**: American sterling makers/retailers seen on trophies (general knowledge, verify).
- **English sterling**: lion passant hallmark + town mark + date letter + maker's initials; date letter dates the cup to the year.
- **Art Deco figural trophies**: spelter (zinc) or bronze athletes on marble/onyx or wood bases, 1920s–30s; bronze beats spelter.
- **Industry awards**: Telly (Gold/Silver/Bronze), Addy, Clio, regional Emmys (also restricted), Cannes Lions, Webby; value comes from the name plate (notable production or person) more than the object.

### Quick ID checklist
- Mark under the foot or near the base: "STERLING" / "925" / lion passant (silver) vs "EPNS", "Quadruple Plate", "Silver on Copper", "Silver Soldered" (plated, low).
- Maker mark: Gorham (lion-anchor-G), Tiffany & Co., Reed & Barton; English town mark + date letter.
- Weighted base? Many sterling trophies have a filled/weighted base or a separate wood/Bakelite plinth — the silver weight is less than the scale weight.
- Engraving: event, year, names; famous names, early sporting events (golf, yachting, horse racing, early motoring, aviation) add value.
- Statuette type: Telly (winged figure on black cylinder) vs Emmy/Oscar (resale restricted — avoid).
- Name plate present and legible; notable production/person named.
- Figural trophies: test metal (spelter shows grey-silver at chips and is light; bronze is heavy and brown at wear).
- Dents, split handles, worn plating, missing finial or lid.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Industry statuettes: cast zinc alloy/pewter with silver/gold plating, weighted bases, engraved plates naming winner and project (the name plate can raise interest if a notable production). Sterling = hallmark; plated cups "EPNS" low. Quality sterling cups are heavy-gauge with crisp chased or applied decoration and cast handles; thin, light cups with seamed bodies and stamped decoration are lower quality. Old vs new: hand-engraved script (varied line depth) on older cups vs laser/machine engraving on modern ones; period hallmarks vs modern "925 China". Polishing wear softens engraving and hallmarks — note readability.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Plating wear, bent wings, missing name plate, base chips. Sterling cups: dents, split or resoldered handles, holes from removed plaques, heavy polishing wear; value assumes no dents, repairs or losses (Mearto). Ask for: hallmark close-up, weight on a kitchen scale (and whether the base is weighted), all-round photos, engraving close-up.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Replica statuettes (Emmy/Oscar novelty) common and worth $10–40. Fake hallmarks on plated cups exist; English "pseudo-hallmarks" on American plate (look for "EP" or company names in imitation-hallmark boxes) (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Thin, novelty-driven; Telly statuettes appear regularly. 30–120 days. Sterling cups sell to silver buyers (weight floor) and sporting-memorabilia collectors (subject premium).

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling under tarnish; notable winner names on plates; Deco figural trophies. Also early-sport subjects (golf, yachting, motoring, aviation) and Tiffany/Gorham marks on cups listed as "silver trophy".

## Shipping / handling
1–4 lb, box with foam; $12–18.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Award, year, winner/plate text, material, height.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] sterling silver [two/three]-handled loving cup, [height] in, marked '[exact marks]'. Engraved '[exact inscription]'. [Weight] oz troy [base not weighted / weighted base]. Condition: [no dents or repairs / list]." For statuettes: "Genuine [Telly Award, Gold/Silver/Bronze, year] statuette, silver-tone cast metal on black base, [height] in; name plate reads '[text]'." Never offer an Emmy, Grammy or post-1951 Oscar; say "replica" for novelty statuettes.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). Telly at $30 needs ~$110+ resale — unlikely. Don't buy Oscars/Emmys (resale restrictions).
