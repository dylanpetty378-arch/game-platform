## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lithographed tinplate robots made in Japan 1950s–70s (and Hong Kong/Taiwan later), wind-up or battery-operated, with chest pistons, sparking eyes, lights. Maker marks are tiny logos on the back/feet: TN (Nomura), SH (Horikawa), KO (Yoshiya), Y (Yonezawa), MT/Modern Toys (Masudaya), Alps. Reproductions: China-made with "Made in China" stamping, bright new lithography, often Schylling or Ha Ha Toy labels.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Horikawa (SH)**: Gear Robot, Attacking Martian, Mighty Robot; $150–600.
- **Masudaya (MT) "Gang of Five"**: Machine Man, Target Robot, Radicon, Train, Sonic; $5,000–50,000+.
- **Nomura (TN)**: Robby the Robot (1956), Mechanized Robot; $1,000–5,000.
- **Yonezawa**: Directional Robot, Mr. Atomic (Cragstan) $1,000–8,000.
- **Linemar/Marx**: various; $200–2,000.
- **Hong Kong/Taiwan 1970s plastic**: $30–120.
- **Repros**: $10–40.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: soft lithography, metal tabs, pitted tin, original battery-box covers; box art matters (box can be 30–50% of value). New: crisp bright colors, uniform plastic, "Made in China".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Battery corrosion, missing antennae/arms, broken wind-up springs, scratched litho, repaints, replacement parts sold aftermarket.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Extensive; reproduction boxes too. Japanese 1990s reissues (e.g. Osaka Tin Toy Institute) are legitimate collectibles but lower.
