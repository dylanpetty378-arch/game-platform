## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lithographed tinplate robots made in Japan 1950s–70s (and Hong Kong/Taiwan later), wind-up or battery-operated, with chest pistons, sparking eyes, lights. Maker marks are tiny logos on the back/feet: TN (Nomura), SH (Horikawa), KO (Yoshiya), Y (Yonezawa), MT/Modern Toys (Masudaya), Alps. Reproductions: China-made with "Made in China" stamping, bright new lithography, often Schylling or Ha Ha Toy labels.
- Construction: printed (lithographed) sheet tin folded and held with **tabs** through slots, not screws or glue; battery robots have a battery door in the back or feet and a remote or on/off switch; wind-ups have a key in the side or back.
- Sizes: wind-ups are typically 5–9"; the big battery robots of 1957–64 run to ~15" (Masudaya Gang of Five).
- Title words sellers use: "space robot", "tin toy", "battery op", "Made in Japan", "with box", "works", "Horikawa", "SH".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Horikawa (SH)**: Gear Robot, Attacking Martian, Mighty Robot; $150–600. Attacking Martian (1960s, battery, chest doors open to fire guns) exists in several head/face variants including a "human face" version (Science Museum Group; UnderpricedAI).
- **Masudaya (MT) "Gang of Five"**: about 15" battery robots — **Radicon Robot (1957**, first radio-controlled toy robot), **Non-Stop "Lavender" Robot (1959)**, **Giant Machine Man** (red variation of Non-Stop, made only in 1960, special order for an American importer, probably fewer than a dozen exist), **Giant Sonic Robot (1962)** and **Shooting Giant / Target Robot (1964**, with dart pistol) (ATCA; Morphy). The old entry listed "Train" as one of the five — that is wrong; the fifth is the Non-Stop (Lavender) Robot. $5,000–50,000+, Machine Man far more.
- **Nomura (TN)**: Robby the Robot (1956), Mechanized Robot; $1,000–5,000. Note: Nomura's 1956 Robby toy *is* the "Mechanized Robot" (same toy sold under that name), not a separate model.
- **Yonezawa**: Directional Robot, Mr. Atomic (Cragstan) $1,000–8,000.
- **Linemar/Marx**: various; $200–2,000.
- **Yoshiya (KO)**: Robot Lilliput-era wind-ups and novelties such as the robot bulldozer (c. 1955).
- **KT (fan logo)**: Robot Lilliput (one of the earliest tin robots) — heavily reproduced (see Fakes).
- **Ideal "Robert the Robot"** (1950s) is **plastic**, not tin; listed among classics above but it belongs in the plastic tier, usually far below the Japanese tin classics (general knowledge, verify).
- **Hong Kong/Taiwan 1970s plastic**: $30–120.
- **Repros**: $10–40.

### Quick ID checklist
- Find the maker logo (back, feet or box end): TN, SH, KO, Y, MT/Modern Toys, Alps, KT.
- Find "Made in Japan" or "Japan" printed in the litho — originals have it; Chinese repros say "Made in China" or nothing.
- Look at the tabs: bent tin tabs through slots, with litho wear at tab edges, = period. Phillips screws = later/repro.
- Check the battery door from inside: corrosion, missing door, replaced wires.
- Antennas, arms, guns, chest doors, plastic domes — count every part against a reference photo.
- Box: original box with correct maker logo, insert and end flaps can be 30–50% of value; check for a reproduction box (too bright, crisp, wrong card).
- Height: measure; repros of some models run larger than originals (e.g. Lilliput).

### Golden-egg tells
- Masudaya Giant Machine Man (red, 1960) — unboxed sold $45,600 (2012) and $86,100 (2019); boxed near-mint estimated $60,000–90,000 (Morphy 2020) vs a few hundred for an ordinary SH robot — 15" robot in red litho, MT/Modern Toys logo; same body as the lavender Non-Stop.
- Other Gang of Five (Radicon, Non-Stop Lavender, Sonic, Target) — $5,000–50,000+ — large 15" Masudaya bodies, MT logo; Target comes with a dart gun; Radicon with a radio transmitter.
- Nomura Robby / Mechanized Robot (1956) — $1,000–5,000 vs $100–500 for 1960s SH — Robby's dome head with moving pistons, TN logo.
- Yonezawa Mr. Atomic and Directional Robot — $1,000–8,000 — Mr. Atomic's domed head and Cragstan box; Y logo.
- Horikawa Attacking Martian, working and boxed — sold median $295 over 15 sales, up to $640 boxed and working vs $139 non-working (UnderpricedAI) — SH logo, chest doors with guns, box present.
- Original box present on any 1950s–60s Japanese robot — +30–50% — photo shows a printed box with matching maker logo and insert.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: soft lithography, metal tabs, pitted tin, original battery-box covers; box art matters (box can be 30–50% of value). New: crisp bright colors, uniform plastic, "Made in China".
- Litho on originals often shows fine dot screen, slight registration shift and wear on high points; repros are glossy and uniformly sharp.
- 1970s Japanese and Hong Kong robots increasingly use plastic bodies with tin chests; all-tin bodies = earlier.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Battery corrosion, missing antennae/arms, broken wind-up springs, scratched litho, repaints, replacement parts sold aftermarket.
- Non-working battery robots sell at about half of working boxed ones (Attacking Martian: $139 non-working vs $315–640 working boxed).
- Ask for a video of it walking/firing; ask for a photo inside the battery compartment.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Extensive; reproduction boxes too. Japanese 1990s reissues (e.g. Osaka Tin Toy Institute) are legitimate collectibles but lower.
- **Robot Lilliput:** original has a fan-shaped "KT" logo and "Made in Japan"/"Japan"; the reproduction carries a "Tin Tom Toy" logo, lacks the Japan mark, is taller and wider, and is assembled differently (Collectors Weekly).
- Reproduction boxes are sold on their own and paired with real robots — check box card stock and printing.

## Market
Strong collector market; auction houses (Morphy, Hake's) for top. eBay moves mid-tier in weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
Maker logo on back; original boxes stored separately.

## Shipping / handling
Shoebox; $12–25; pad protrusions.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photo maker mark, all sides, battery box, function video.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker by its logo ("SH Horikawa", "TN Nomura", "MT Masudaya") and the model, and quote the origin printed on the toy ("Made in Japan"). State height, wind-up or battery, and exactly what works (walks, lights, sparks, guns fire), with a video. List missing or replaced parts, battery-compartment condition and any corrosion. Say whether the box is original and include photos of all box panels and the insert.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$15 out − ~$15 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Battery robots untested = pass unless seller demonstrates. Posters always pass.
