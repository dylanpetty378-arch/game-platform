## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Analog meters with dial faces; tube testers in suitcase-style cases with roll charts; scopes with CRTs. Bakelite cases and printed scale faces with maker and city ("Marion Elec. Inst. Co., Manchester N.H.") date them to the 1930s–60s.
- **VOM / multimeter**: hand-held box with one large meter, a big central range-selector knob and banana jacks. Simpson 260: tall brown/black Bakelite case, meter on top half, knob and jacks below. Triplett 630: wider case, large rotary knob in the centre of the lower half.
- **Panel meter**: round or square meter with a flange and studs on the back, made to mount in a panel; single scale (volts, amps or milliamps).
- **Tube tester**: wooden or leatherette suitcase; inside, a meter, rows of tube sockets, lettered lever switches or punched-card slot (Hickok Cardmatic), and a scrolling paper **roll chart** of tube settings behind a window.
- **Oscilloscope / signal generator / lab gear**: heavy steel cabinets with front-panel knobs; Tektronix (blue-grey cabinets, round CRT), HP (grey, "hp" logo), General Radio (black crackle).
The model number is on the meter face, the front panel, or a plate on the back or inside the lid.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hickok (Cleveland)**: tube testers — top tier for musicians/audio people ($200–800 serviced).
- **Simpson 260 (Elgin IL)**, **Triplett 630 (Bluffton OH)**: workhorse VOMs ($30–100).
- **Weston (Newark NJ)**: early meters; model 1 etc. $20–100.
- **Marion Electrical Instrument Co. (Manchester NH, 1920s–1960s)**: panel meters and Multi-Ranger VOM ($20–60).
- **Heathkit/Eico kits**: $20–80.
- **Tektronix/HP/General Radio**: lab gear, heavy.

**Hickok tube tester models (Tone Lizard, Radiomuseum).** All Hickoks are sold as "dynamic mutual conductance" testers.
- **539A/B/C** (c. 1956–1970s; the 539C is the last): the most sought-after Hickok, which "commands incredible prices" on eBay. Many were made, so plenty come up.
- **6000 / 6000A** (6000 c. 1957–62; 6000A in a 1964 ad): popular with guitar/audio buyers. The 6000A dropped 4-, 5- and 6-pin tube testing but covers Nuvistors and Compactrons.
- **600A** (c. 1950), **532/533/534** (mid-1940s–1950s), **540** (1942), **580**, **800** (advertised as "Lowest Priced Hickok" in the early 1960s), **799 "Mustang"** (1965, transistorised; rare but less collected).
- **Military TV-7** family (Hickok circuit, US military 1960–1982) and **533A**: good testers, plentiful from surplus.
- **Cardmatic 121/123** (punched-card setup, c. 1959).
- **510X** (to c. 1935): early, doubles as a VOM, no roll chart; less coveted.
Other tube tester makers: Sencore (Mighty Mite), B&K, Precision, Eico (e.g. 666, 667), Superior, Jackson; most are emission-type testers and worth less than Hickok.

**VOM variants (general knowledge, verify).** Simpson made the 260 from the late 1930s in numbered "series" (260-1 through 260-8, later 260-8P etc.); the series number is on the meter face. Triplett 630 variants include 630, 630-A, 630-NA (1964 example on antiqueradio.org) and 630-PL; the NA and PL versions are the higher-spec versions. Simpson 260 and Triplett 630 remain in demand as working tools for radio repairers, who like a needle for alignment peaks.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the maker and model from the meter face or panel (e.g. "Hickok 539C", "Simpson 260 Series 6", "Triplett 630-NA").
- Tube testers: is the roll chart present and readable? Are the manual, adapters and lid intact?
- Meter needle resting on zero with the unit off; a needle that is stuck, bent or pinned is a problem.
- Case: cracks or chips in Bakelite, burnt or missing knobs, broken glass.
- Battery compartment (VOMs): look for corrosion from old leaking cells.
- Calibration sticker or service tag (adds confidence).
- Test leads present (VOMs), with original probes.
- Is there a photo of it switched on and reading something?

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bakelite cases crack; check needle zero, mirror scales, roll charts present on tube testers.
- A mirrored scale (a strip of mirror under the needle) marks a better-grade meter.
- Glass meter faces and D'Arsonval movements look good but don't change accuracy (Tone Lizard).
- Tube testers used in service vans often show heavy case wear; a clean case and lid usually mean bench use.
- Old vs new: Bakelite and leatherette cases, slotted screws and paper roll charts mean mid-century; plastic cases and digital displays mean modern.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Open meter movements (dead), corroded batteries, cracked cases, missing leads.
- Tube testers: drifting calibration, worn switches, dried filter capacitors, missing roll charts, missing or dead rectifier tubes (keep spare 83 tubes and #49 and #81 bulbs, per Tone Lizard). Manuals for many models include calibration procedures.
- VOMs: burnt shunt resistors from overloads, dirty range switches (cleanable), dried paper capacitors (the 630-NA on antiqueradio.org needed one capacitor and switch cleaning).
- A professional calibration or service note adds value; ask for the date.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None significant; decor "steampunk" meters. Reproduction roll charts and manuals are sold separately and are fine if disclosed.

## Market
Steady among hams/repair techs; tube testers move fast. Tube testers sell to guitar amp, hi-fi and radio people who want to test or match tubes; VOMs sell to radio restorers and collectors; panel meters sell to decor/steampunk buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Hickok model numbers; accessory adapters for tube testers. Also the letter suffix (539C vs 539A, 630-NA vs 630), calibration stickers and original manuals.

## Shipping / handling
VOM $12–20; tube testers $25–45, meter movements sensitive to shock. Pack the meter so the face is cushioned and the unit cannot shift; set VOMs to OFF (or the highest range) and remove batteries.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Test results, leads included, photos of scale/back.

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker, exact model and suffix, and year range. State exactly what was tested and how (e.g. "tested a known-good 12AX7, reads in the good range" or "powers on, untested"), whether it has been calibrated and when, and list everything included (roll chart, manual, adapters, leads). Describe case condition, glass and needle. For Hickok, note it is a "dynamic mutual conductance" tester; avoid claiming laboratory accuracy.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$15 out − ~$15 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Untested VOMs: pass.
