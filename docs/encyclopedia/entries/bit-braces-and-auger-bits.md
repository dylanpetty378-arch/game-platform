## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A bit brace is a U-cranked hand drill: round head (wood or with ball bearing) on top, wooden sweep handle in the middle, a chuck (two-jaw "alligator" or patent) at the bottom, usually with a ratchet cam ring. Auger bits are spiral wood bits with a square tapered tang (to fit brace jaws) and a threaded lead screw.
- Older braces (pre-1900) have rosewood/ebony heads with ivory or brass rings, plain (non-ratchet) chucks, patent dates on the chuck shell, and nickel plating worn to iron.
- 20th-century braces have a stamped steel frame, ratchet ring, painted or varnished beech handles, sweep size stamped (8", 10", 12", 14").
- Bit sets: Russell Jennings pattern (double twist), Irwin (solid center), Stanley/Ridgely, often in a canvas roll or wooden/metal index box numbered 4–16 (sixteenths of an inch).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Stanley** (No. 923, 945, 966, 813; Bed Rock-style "Stanley Victor") 1910s–1980s. Stanley "Workmaster" (1970s–80s homeowner line; yellow warning labels) = low.
- **Millers Falls** (No. 732, 734, 1532 etc.) and **Goodell-Pratt** — good users, $15–40.
- **North Bros. Yankee** No. 2100 series ratchet braces — $40–100.
- **Spofford** (split-chuck patent 1850s+) $30–80; **Fray** (patent spinner heads) $30–100; **Barber** (Amidon patent) $30–80; **Peck, Stow & Wilcox (PS&W)** $20–60.
- **Sheffield framed braces** (beech with brass plates, 1800s; "Ultimatum" by William Marples) $200–1,500.
- **Bits**: Russell Jennings (Stanley owned from 1944), Irwin (Wilmington, Ohio), Ridgely, Greenlee (Rockford, IL), Stanley "Power Bore", Clark's expansive bits. Spade bits = later and for power drills (low).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Wood: rosewood/ebony/lignum heads with brass ferrules = older and better than painted beech or plastic.
- Ball-bearing head, enclosed ratchet, "cam ring" ratchet and patent stamping = quality.
- Bits: crisp spurs and lips, intact lead screw threads, full length (not sharpened to stubs), maker stamp on shank and size stamp on tang.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Surface rust is normal; pitting lowers value. Split handles, missing head bushings, broken ratchet pawls and missing chuck jaws are the main faults. Bits often have bent tips or broken lead screws. Users want working ratchets; collectors want original finish (don't over-clean).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Essentially none on the low end. Some modern reproductions of Sheffield framed braces exist; newer imported braces (India/Pakistan) with bright plating are common.

## Market
Steady trickle; buyers are woodworkers and decorators. Lots of 3–5 braces sell in 1–3 weeks at $40–100. Loose bits sit.

## What sellers usually miss
Sheffield framed braces (wood body with brass plates) listed as "old drill"; Fray/Spofford patent chucks; complete 13-bit sets in original wood box. They often over-price ordinary Stanley braces as "antique".

## Shipping / handling
Braces ship fine wrapped (3–4 lb each); a lot of four is ~12–15 lb ($20–30). Wrap bit tips — they cut through boxes.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name maker and model, sweep size, whether ratchet works in both directions, jaw condition, head spins freely; for bits, list sizes present and whether lead screws/spurs are intact.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 ship out − ~$20 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Realistically only Sheffield framed braces, rare patent braces or big complete boxed bit sets meet that. Pass ordinary lots. Fits a passenger seat. No authentication issue.
