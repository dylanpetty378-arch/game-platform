## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Triangular wool or rayon felt pennants with screen-printed scenes, flocked letters and a contrasting header strip with ties.
- Age tells: 1920s–40s wool felt with sewn-on felt letters or tassels; 1950s–60s screen-printed colourful scenes (roadside attractions, turnpikes, parks); 1970s+ thinner synthetic felt, simpler prints, plastic sticks.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Chicago Pennant Co., Trench Mfg (Buffalo — sports pennants, "Trench" tag), Crown Pennant, Mohawk, Wincraft (modern sports), unmarked souvenir makers.
- High-value subjects: World's Fairs (1933 Chicago, 1939 NY), early MLB/NFL team pennants (1940s–60s), Route 66, defunct attractions, bands (Beatles), college pre-1940 with sewn letters.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Bright colours, sharp graphics, no moth holes, intact ties/streamers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Moth holes (very common), fading, staining, creases, tape residue, torn ties.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Modern reproductions of sports pennants (Mitchell & Ness, Wincraft throwbacks) exist; usually labelled. Some reproduction World's Fair pennants.

## Market
- Steady decorator and collector demand; lots sell well. Single common pennants are slow.

## What sellers usually miss
- Sellers sell "assorted pennants" in bulk without checking for sports/World's Fair pieces mixed in.

## Shipping / handling
- Flat, light: poly mailer or flat box, $6–15 for lots.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Length, subject, condition (moth holes), era; group into lots by theme.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$8) − ship to Dylan (~$10) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- A lot reselling at $100 allows max buy ≈ $30. Single common pennants never.
