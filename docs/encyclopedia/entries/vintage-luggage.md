## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Suitcases, train/vanity cases, overnight cases and hat boxes from the 1920s–1980s. Value comes from maker and material. Most Craigslist "vintage luggage" is 1950s–70s vinyl-covered plywood or hardboard (Samsonite, Lady Baltimore, Skyway, Amelia Earhart, American Tourister) worth $25–80 as décor.
Photo tells:
- **Vinyl over board**: textured basket-weave or "cross-hatch" vinyl, piping on the edges, script metal nameplates (Lady Baltimore, Amelia Earhart), satin or pleated linings.
- **Samsonite (Shwayder Bros, Denver)**: tan, brown or two-tone, with "Samsonite" logo plates and pleated rayon linings. A wardrobe hanger bar in the lid = 1940s–50s Streamlite-era.
- **Hartmann**: tweed with leather trim, or **belting leather** (thick saddle-tan leather, heavy brass, white-stitched corners).
- **Genuine alligator/crocodile**: real scales vary in size and shape across the hide; you can see pores or follicle dots in belly scales (crocodiles), and natural creasing; panels are cut and seamed. "Hornback" (bony raised back tiles) is the most valuable look.
- **Embossed cowhide or leatherette**: the pattern repeats perfectly, the "scales" are shallow and identical, and it continues uninterrupted across seams and corners. Edges show fibrous leather or paper.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Samsonite / Shwayder Trunk Mfg.** Jesse Shwayder founded it in Denver in 1910. The "Samsonite" suitcase came in 1939; Ultralite (magnesium and injection-molded) in 1956; the company was renamed Samsonite Corporation in 1965 (fundinguniverse). Look for "Shwayder Bros" labels (pre-1965), Streamlite tapered shells, and Silhouette 1960s shells. Most sell for $30–90; a few mod colours and train cases do better.
- **Hartmann** (Racine, WI, 1877–). Belting leather and walnut tweed are sought after. Hartmann belting-leather pieces sell at a $114 median (58 sales; middle half $49–219, underpricedai, Oct 2026).
- **Lady Baltimore** (Baltimore Luggage Co., Baltimore, MD; trademarks on Justia). Its 1950s–60s textured vinyl cases come in red, blue, turquoise, white and pink, with a gold script badge. Mostly décor value, $40–90.
- **American Tourister** (1933–), **Skyway** (Seattle), **Amelia Earhart** (1940s–80s), **Royal Traveller**, **Starline**, **Platt**, **Herculyte**, **Ventura**: mid-century vinyl, $25–80.
- **Leather makers**: Crouch & Fitzgerald, Mark Cross, T. Anthony, Abercrombie & Fitch, Wheary, Belber, Oshkosh (trunks), English makers (Finnigans, Drew & Sons, Army & Navy CSL). Medallions are often round stamped badges by the handle.
- **Luxury**: Louis Vuitton, Goyard, Moynat, Hermès. These need authentication: outside Dylan's lane.
- **Alligator/crocodile**: 1920s–50s American and Cuban-made cases, often with no maker name.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Thick, smooth, firm leather with saddle stitching and solid brass hardware = quality. Thin split leather with painted edges = cheap.
- Combination locks on the handle plate, and keyed latches signed by the maker (Hartmann, Samsonite, Excelsior, Eagle Lock).
- Linings: original satin or leather in good shape adds value. Torn rayon is common and acceptable on vinyl cases.
- Hardshell vs board: true 1960s ABS/moulded shells (Samsonite Silhouette) are lighter. Vinyl-over-plywood is heavier.
- Monograms (owner initials) reduce value slightly for users and not at all for décor buyers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Corner scuffs and splits, water stains, mildew smell (a hard sell), red rot on old leather, broken or missing keys, latches that don't lock, torn linings, missing handles.
- Leather: dried and cracked surfaces, peeling grain, torn stitching at the corners.
- Alligator: lifting scales, cracks across scales, dry rot.
- Repainted or decoupaged cases are altered and appeal only to craft/décor buyers.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Embossed "faux alligator" cowhide and leatherette is far more common than genuine alligator (underpricedai: faux-gator train cases median $41).
- Louis Vuitton and Goyard fakes are everywhere: a reason to stay out.
- Modern "vintage-style" suitcases (World Market, Target, TJ Maxx décor stacks) are made of cardboard with printed maps or postcards.
- Decoupaged or crafted cases (maps, pearls, fabric) are modern alterations of mid-century cases.

## Market
Large supply of mid-century vinyl cases. They sell as décor (stacked side tables, photo props, wedding card boxes), mostly locally, because shipping a 24–26" case costs $30–60. Sell-through online is low for full-size cases. Small train cases ship cheaply and sell better. Leather by known makers sells steadily to users and collectors. Genuine alligator sells, but eBay and some states restrict exotic-skin sales (California has historically restricted alligator/crocodile product sales). Check rules before listing.

## What sellers usually miss
- They don't check the maker label inside the lid or the medallion by the handle. A plain brown leather case can be Hartmann, Crouch & Fitzgerald or Mark Cross.
- They call embossed leather "alligator", and occasionally a genuine alligator case "faux". Real scales vary and show pores or follicle dots; embossing repeats.
- They price full-size cases at Lens ask prices ($150–400) that don't sell once shipping is added.

## Shipping / handling
This is the category's killer. A 26" suitcase needs a 30×20×12" box: about 15–20 lb dimensional weight, $35–70 via UPS/FedEx Ground. A 21–24" case costs $25–45. Train cases (14–16") cost $15–25. Stuff the interior with paper, wrap the corners, and use a box with 2" of clearance. Luggage fits a passenger seat but inbound shipping from a nationwide seller is the same high cost.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the maker, model/line, measurements, material (say "embossed leather" or "vinyl" honestly), key or combination status, lining condition and smell (smoke or mildew).
- Show the interior label and hardware stamps.
- For alligator: show the scale pattern up close, and note any CITES or state restrictions. Ship only to states where it is legal.
- For décor pieces, sell locally or on FB Marketplace to avoid shipping.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price. Pass unless profit ≥ max($50, price). Tier A: profit ≥ 2× price.
- With $30–60 each way in shipping for full-size cases, even a $114-median Hartmann barely clears at $0. Full-size cases are buys only when the sold median is ≥ $200 (genuine alligator, rare leather) and the price is ≤ $40.
- Train cases and small leather cases (≤ 16") qualify only when the sold median is ≥ $120 and the price ≤ $25.
- No luxury-brand cases (LV, Goyard, Hermès): they need professional authentication.
- Passenger-seat size is OK for anything up to a 29" suitcase. Steamer trunks are too big.
