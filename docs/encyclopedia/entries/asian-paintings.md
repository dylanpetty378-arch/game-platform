## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ink and colour paintings on paper or silk in Chinese, Japanese or Korean style, sold mounted as hanging scrolls, framed under glass, or painted in enamel on a porcelain slab ("porcelain painting"). Photo tells:
- **Tourist/export hanging scroll (1970s–today)**: bright white paper or brocade mounting, plastic or lacquered-wood roller knobs, a wire hanger, a short title in calligraphy (e.g. 寒梅 "winter plum") plus a cyclical date and one or two red seals. Plum blossoms, bamboo, tigers, horses, landscapes, ladies. Thousands identical.
- **Framed silk landscape**: misty mountains, pines, pavilions; the silk has a regular woven grid; signed with two characters and one seal; brocade mat. 1960s–90s Taiwan/Hong Kong/PRC export.
- **Decorator brush painting (1960s–80s)**: flowers and birds on paper or board, signed in English script as well as Chinese ("Lo Vinc", "Lee", etc.) in a thin metal frame, sometimes with a US distributor label ("Arts International 1971"). Made for American department stores.
- **Porcelain plaque painting**: a flat glazed porcelain slab painted in overglaze famille-rose enamels, framed like a picture. Modern Jingdezhen workshops make large square/rectangular plaques (lady in a lotus boat with a Li Bai "Picking Lotus Song" 采莲曲 poem is a stock subject). Small Republic-period (1912–49) plaques with fine enamels are the valuable kind.
- **Gongbi (fine-line) figure paintings**: precise outlines and flat colour; widely printed as giclée with added red seals.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Anonymous export workshops** (PRC Jingdezhen and Suzhou, Taiwan, Hong Kong): the bulk of the market.
- **Listed 20th c. painters** with real markets: Republic period Shanghai School (Wu Changshuo followers), Lingnan School, Taiwan's Huang Junbi followers; Hong Kong 1950s–70s painters who sold to Western visitors. Values $300–3,000 at auction when genuine.
- **Masters** (Qi Baishi, Zhang Daqian, Xu Beihong, Pu Ru, Wu Guanzhong): six- and seven-figure records mean thousands of fakes; anything with these names from a private seller is presumed fake.
- **Porcelain plaques**: Republic-period "Zhushan Bayou" school plaques (Wang Qi, Deng Bishan, Wang Dafan) are high-value and also heavily faked; modern Jingdezhen plaques are decor.
- **Japanese**: hanging scrolls (kakejiku) with box (tomobako) and inscribed lid; woodblock prints (ukiyo-e/shin-hanga) are a separate market.

- Scroll with a full inscription — artist name, cyclical year (e.g. 戊子) and dedication — and two or more seals — Terapeak "Chinese scroll painting signed" median $50 vs $25 for rice-paper scrolls (~2×), and $300–3,000 if the name is a listed Republic or Hong Kong painter — several columns of varied-size calligraphy rather than a two-character title; old brocade mounting with toning and roller wear (entry comp)
- Printed gongbi "painting" with added red seals — ~0.5× (below the $15–60 souvenir band) — flat uniform colour, dot/grid pattern under magnification, seal red identical in texture to the print; a seal with no ink bleed is the giveaway (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Painted vs printed**: hand-painted ink sits in the fibres with varied density, pooling and dry-brush streaks; printed works show a uniform dot or grid pattern under magnification, and the "seal" red is flat and identical to the print. A seal that is printed (no ink bleed, no uneven pressure) is a giclée tell.
- **Age**: old paper/silk is evenly toned, may have foxing, wormholes and creases from rolling; traditional wet mounting; old silk frays along the grid. New scrolls have bright white mounts and machine brocade.
- **Calligraphy quality**: confident, varied brushwork with an inscription that dates the work (cyclical year like 戊子) and names the artist; souvenir pieces have short formulaic titles.
- **Porcelain plaques**: hand enamelling is slightly raised, with brush marks; transfer-printed plaques have uniform outlines; old plaques show wear, enamel flaking and old wooden frames; the back is unglazed biscuit with a hand-written mark only on some.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Water stains, mould spots, tears and flaking pigment on scrolls; fading from light; cracked or chipped porcelain plaques (fatal); broken glass in frames. Remounting is normal and doesn't kill value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Printed paintings with added hand-applied seals or a few added brush strokes ("over-painted prints").
- Famous-name forgeries, including old forgeries sold in the 1980s.
- Modern porcelain plaques signed with Republic-period master names.
- Google Lens "matches" to listed artists on MutualArt usually reflect style similarity, not authorship (our sweep's Lens hits to Xiang Weiren and Yu Lunxin were style matches only).

## Market
Souvenir and decorator paintings: oversupplied, slow (months), Etsy decor buyers. Republic-period and listed works: strong demand from Chinese buyers at US regional auctions (Leland Little, Vogt, Gianguan, Bonhams), which is where real ones go – not eBay. Authentication risk dominates.

## What sellers usually miss
- A small, old porcelain plaque with fine enamels in a worn wooden frame listed as "Chinese tile".
- A scroll in an old box with an inscribed lid (Japanese tomobako) or an old collector's label.
- The reverse is more common: sellers price souvenirs at $60–150 because Lens shows a famous artist.

## Shipping / handling
Scrolls roll and ship in a tube for $10–15. Framed pieces under glass: remove glass or use plexi, $20–35. Large porcelain plaques in frames are heavy (10–25 lb) and break easily: $40–70, high risk.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Say "hand painted" only if verified with close-ups; translate the title/inscription; give dimensions of image and mount; describe as "signed and sealed, artist unidentified" rather than naming a master. For porcelain paintings, state "modern" or "Republic period" honestly and show the enamel close-up and back.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Souvenir scrolls, framed export silk, decorator brush paintings: **pass at any price over ~$10**.
- Any attribution to a famous master: requires professional authentication → **pass** (Dylan's rule).
- Large framed porcelain plaque paintings: pass (shipping and breakage both ways wipe out a $100–150 sale).
- Possible buy: small (under 12") hand-enamelled Republic-period plaque in an old frame at ≤ $40, after close-ups of the enamel and back.
- Fits passenger seat: framed pieces to ~30×30"; scrolls always.
