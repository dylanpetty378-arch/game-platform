## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Earthenware/ironstone decorated by transfer printing: an engraved copper plate is printed onto tissue and transferred to the ware before glazing. Look for tiny dot/line engraving texture, repeat seams where borders meet.
- **Early historical blue (c. 1815–1840):** deep cobalt, scenes of American cities, ships, landmarks; irregular backs, stilt marks (three tiny marks on the base from kiln stilts), impressed makers' marks (WOOD, CLEWS, ADAMS, RIDGWAY), no "England" (added after 1891).
- **Romantic period (1830–1860):** lighter blues, pinks, browns, purples, greens; fanciful scenes.
- **Modern (1900–present):** crisp printing, uniform white bodies, printed back-stamps with "England", "Made in England", "Dishwasher Safe", "Detergent proof".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Spode / Copeland Spode:** Blue Italian (since 1816; still made); Tower; Camilla; Willow. Vases, ginger jars and unusual shapes sell; dinner plates $10–20.
- **Enoch Wood, James & Ralph Clews, Ridgway, Stevenson, Rogers, Stubbs:** early American historical blue — highest value.
- **Adams:** Calyx Ware, English Scenic; modern Adams pieces modest.
- **Johnson Bros (1883–2003):** Old Britain Castles, Friendly Village, Indies, Historic America (modern, $5–25 per piece).
- **Enoch Wedgwood (Tunstall), Myott, Churchill, Royal Staffordshire (Meakin), Masons:** 20th-c. patterns, low value.
- **Salem China "Olde Staffordshire" / English Village (Ohio, 1960s–80s):** American-made imitation; low value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old: pearlware (bluish glaze pooling in foot), soft earthenware, crazing, stilt marks, slightly irregular potting.
- New: whiter bodies, perfect glaze, crisp foot ring, printed backstamp with registration or "Dishwasher safe".
- "Flow blue" = blurred blue (deliberate); separate entry if present.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rim chips, hairlines (hold to light / tap for a dull note), crazing with brown staining, utensil marks, restored rims (painted, slightly matte — check with blacklight).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Spode Blue Italian has been in continuous production; most vases and jars are late 20th-century; Spode/Portmeirion still sell new. Back-stamp variants date them (Copeland Spode, Spode England, Spode Portmeirion).
- Chinese reproductions of historical blue (soft, blurred printing, odd marks) exist; also 1950s–70s "Staffordshire" souvenir plates (Rowland & Marsellus, Wedgwood commemoratives) look early but are worth $10–40.

## Market
- Modern patterns: high supply, low sell-through. Spode Blue Italian decorative shapes: steady decorator demand. Historical blue: strong but specialist.

## What sellers usually miss
- Unmarked or impressed-only plates with deep blue American scenes and stilt marks are the jackpot; owners call them "old blue plates".
- Conversely, owners overvalue modern Spode and anything with "Staffordshire".

## Shipping / handling
- Plates: box-in-box, 2–3 lb, $12–15. Vases: 3–5 lb, $15–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give back-stamp (photo), dimensions, era, any crazing/chips; name pattern and maker.
