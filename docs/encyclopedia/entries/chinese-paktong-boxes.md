## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small round, square or oval lidded boxes of paktong (baitong, a copper-nickel-zinc alloy, silvery with a slightly yellow cast) or brass, used for ink paste (墨盒) or seal paste. Republic-era (1912–1930s) types often show the crossed **Five-Colour flag** (horizontal stripes, ROC 1912–28) and the **Iron Blood 18-Star flag**, sometimes yin-yang/bagua, and English words like "SHANGHAI BEST" or "CHINA". Example 7940283372: oval lid, die-stamped/cast relief on a stippled ground, crossed five-stripe and star flags, yin-yang roundel, two other roundels, text "SANHGAI" (misspelling of SHANGHAI) above and "BEST" below.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Liulichang (Beijing) ink-box workshops** 1900–1940: Tongguzhai, Chen Yinting, Yao Meibo-style engraved scholar boxes with calligraphy, landscapes; seal marks on base. The collectible tier.
- **Republic commemorative boxes**: crossed flags, "Republic of China", sometimes portraits (Yuan Shikai, Sun Yat-sen). Many originals but heavily reproduced.
- **Export/souvenir and modern repros** (1980s–now, Hebei/Zhejiang): stamped relief, artificially aged, misspellings ("SANHGAI"), shallow uniform stippling.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real paktong: whitish-silver, does not tarnish to brown like brass, faint ring; often with a copper (bronze-coloured) inner liner or base. Quality: hand-chased/engraved fine line work with variable line depth, calligraphy with artist seal, crisp lid fit. Cheap/new: cast or die-stamped raised relief with soft edges, grainy stippled ground, poorly spelled English, "aged" grime in recesses, loose lid, lightweight.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, worn engraving, missing cotton ink pad (normal), solder repairs at hinge (hinged types), heavy polishing wiping out engraving.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Crossed-flag/"Shanghai Best"/"Shanghai Fan" boxes are among the most common Chinese "antique" reproductions sold at flea markets and on eBay/AliExpress. Misspelled English (SANHGAI) is a reproduction red flag. Fake Liulichang seals also exist; judge the engraving quality, not the mark.

## Market
Chinese-buyer demand drives the high end at auction (Bonhams, Christie's, Hong Kong); eBay generic tier is slow and saturated.

## What sellers usually miss
Plain-looking, finely hand-engraved scholar boxes (calligraphy only, no flags) are the undervalued ones; owners pick the flashy flag boxes as "valuable".

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size, 3–8 oz; padded mailer ~$5–6.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Describe the alloy as "white metal / paktong-type", state dimensions/weight, photograph base and interior; do not claim Republic period for crossed-flag stamped boxes.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $6 − $6 − price ≥ max($50, price). Stamped crossed-flag boxes (≤ $35 sold) never qualify. Buy only hand-engraved calligraphy/landscape boxes with copper liner at ≤ $40 when a credible $150+ resale exists. Repro risk is high; no professional authentication needed at that tier but be conservative.
