## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
The name comes from the Royal Factory on the Capodimonte hill in Naples (1743–59); the Bourbon king moved it to Spain (Buen Retiro) and the Real Fabbrica Ferdinandea reopened in Naples (1771–1806). Ginori (Doccia, Florence) bought moulds and used the crowned N from 1896, and from the 1920s dozens of Naples/Veneto/Florence workshops made "Capodimonte" as a style: high-relief, brightly enamelled putti/cherubs and classical scenes moulded around urns, boxes and plaques; delicate hand-built porcelain flowers (roses, daisies, morning glories) in bouquets and baskets; peasant, tavern, beggar and fisherman figurines. In US estates the term covers:
- **Lamps (1950s–70s)**: urn-shaped porcelain bodies with relief putti scenes, sometimes reticulated (pierced) panels, gilt scroll handles, floral cartouches, mounted on brass/bronze-tone pedestal bases with filigree, 28–36 in tall, often with a period silk bell shade. Importers: Stellacraft, Marbro, Frederick Cooper, Underwriters' labelled. Examples 7930033333, 7942534317, 7982452092, 7981349160.
- **Flowers**: porcelain bouquet in a woven-porcelain basket or vase, pastel enamels (example 7969521555). Italian (marked crowned N "Capodimonte Made in Italy") or English bone-china (Coalport, Royal Doulton, Crown Staffordshire – Lens matched Coalport) or Japanese/Chinese copies.
- **Figurines**: clowns, fishermen, hobos, drunks, Napoleonic soldiers, ladies; signed Italian artists sell; unsigned 1970s–80s gift pieces don't.
- **Boxes, plaques, tankards, ewers** with relief putti – often German (Volkstedt, Ernst Bohne) or Italian 1900–1950.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Quality: crisp relief putti with individually modelled fingers and faces, layered translucent enamels, soft skin tones, gilding fired in (doesn't rub off), thin delicate petals with veins. Cheap: thick glossy enamel, blurry faces, heavy uniform gold, mould seams, petals thick and rounded.
- Marks: hand-drawn blue underglaze crowned N with irregular lines on old; printed/overglaze crowned N in blue, gold or black with "Capodimonte" or "Made in Italy" = 20th-century decorative; foil labels = retail 1960s–90s; impressed model numbers = factory production.
- Lamps: check whether the porcelain body is one piece or several sections joined with threaded rod; brass hardware quality; original shades (silk, 1960s) are a plus to local buyers, a liability to ship.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing/broken flower petals and leaves (nearly universal), broken fingers, toes, wings on putti, glued handles, filled chips repainted with cold paint (shows under UV), cracked bodies on lamps from over-tightened rods, corroded brass bases, frayed wiring, discoloured or split silk shades. Missing petals cut flower pieces 50–80%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
The crowned N has been used or imitated by hundreds of makers since the 19th century – it proves nothing. Asian copies carry "Capodimonte" stickers. "Antique" claims on 1950s–70s lamps are standard and wrong. Coalport/English bone-china flowers are mislabelled "Capodimonte" (and vice versa).

## Market
Out of fashion except for Hollywood Regency decorators; huge supply of lamps and flowers from 1960s–80s estates. eBay sell-through for Capodimonte lamps is low (asks of $200–400 sit for years), and lamps over 30 in cost $60–100 to ship, so they mostly sell locally for $50–150. Signed figurines have a small, steady collector base. No seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
- Sellers over-value nearly everything with "Capodimonte" on it (Lens medians of $227–495 for the lamp examples reflect dealer asks, not sales).
- What they miss: German Volkstedt/Bohne relief pieces called "Capodimonte", and signed Cacciapuoti flowers/figures sold as "porcelain flowers".

## Shipping / handling
Lamps: 28–36 in, 8–15 lb, must be disassembled and double-boxed – $50–100 per lamp, high breakage; shades separate (another $25–40). Flowers: extremely fragile petals, need custom foam – $15–25. Figurines $15–30. eBay 13%. Lamps are effectively local-only.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Call it "Capodimonte-style Italian porcelain" with the mark photographed; say 1950s–70s, not antique. List every missing petal, finger or chip; give height to socket and overall; say whether rewired and tested. For signed figurines, photograph the signature.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Lamps: round-trip shipping $100+ means even a $175 sold lamp loses money – pass every Capodimonte lamp unless Dylan can sell locally (then only ≤ $30). Flowers: pass (damage, shipping fragility, $20–50 sold). Buy only: signed Cacciapuoti/Cappé/Merli/Galli figurines or German Volkstedt/Bohne relief pieces, intact, ≤ 14 in, at ≤ $60. Pass anything with missing flowers/fingers (repair). 18th–19th c. claims need specialist authentication – pass.
