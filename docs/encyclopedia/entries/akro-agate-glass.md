## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Opaque marbleized ("slag") glass in swirled colors — green/white, blue/white, oxblood/white, orange — molded marbles maker's line of ashtrays, planters, powder jars, children's dishes. Mark: crow flying through letter "A" holding marbles, "Made in USA", mold number. Shapes: leaf ashtrays, shell ashtrays, "Westite" lines, Concentric Ring and Interior Panel doll dishes, Chiquita, "Stacked Disc". The example: blue-to-white opalescent ruffled leaf-shaped ashtray with molded veins and a single tubular cigarette rest — the translucent opalescent ombré is not typical Akro marbleizing; it reads more like a later opalescent leaf ashtray (possibly Fenton/Japanese/other) — unmarked in photo.

## Makers, brands and variants
Akro Agate (marbles 1911–, glassware 1932–1951); Vitro Agate, Master Marble, Westite (Westite leaf/shell ashtrays), Imperial "end of day" slag (1960s–70s), Houze, Boyd (later small pieces).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Akro: opaque, swirls go through the glass, crow mark, rough mold seams. Reproduction/other: translucent glow, uniform color, no mark.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim flea bites, chips on cigarette rests, scratches. Rare repairs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Akro doll dish reproductions by others; Westite and Imperial slag confused with Akro.
