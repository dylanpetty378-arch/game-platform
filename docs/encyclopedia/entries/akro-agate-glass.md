## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Opaque marbleized ("slag") glass in swirled colors — green/white, blue/white, oxblood/white, orange — molded marbles maker's line of ashtrays, planters, powder jars, children's dishes. Mark: crow flying through letter "A" holding marbles, "Made in USA", mold number. Shapes: leaf ashtrays, shell ashtrays, "Westite" lines, Concentric Ring and Interior Panel doll dishes, Chiquita, "Stacked Disc". The example: blue-to-white opalescent ruffled leaf-shaped ashtray with molded veins and a single tubular cigarette rest — the translucent opalescent ombré is not typical Akro marbleizing; it reads more like a later opalescent leaf ashtray (possibly Fenton/Japanese/other) — unmarked in photo.
- Company: organized in Akron, Ohio, in 1911; moved to Clarksburg, West Virginia, in 1914 for glass sand and cheap natural gas; first a marble maker (over 2 million marbles a week during the 1930s–40s Chinese checkers craze); pressed glassware (vases, flowerpots, containers) from the 1930s; boxed children's dish sets sold strongly in WWII when metal toys were scarce; production ceased 1951 (e-WV; Kovels).
- The crow mark sits as a raised moulded logo on the base (concave bases of teapots, creamers, sugars and plates on the CMOG set); lids are usually unmarked (CMOG). Some pieces are unmarked, and some carry "J.P." for J. Pressman Co., a toy company Akro made sets for (Kovel column).
- Children's dish patterns (Kovel column; CMOG; eBay titles): Stippled Band, Ribbed (Concentric Ring/Rib), Raised Daisy, Chiquita, Interior Panel, Octagonal, and the "H"-panel pattern of the Little American Maid sets. Sets came in large and small sizes.
- Colors (Kovel column): yellow, white, orange, cobalt, oxblood red, purple, light blue, topaz, green; pumpkin and oxblood red are rare. Children's sets mix colors on purpose (e.g. blue teapot with white lid, green cups and plates, white saucers on the CMOG set).
- Boxed sets: the "Little American Maid" box is marked "Manufactured by The Akro Agate Co., Clarksburg, W.VA." with a set number on the lid end (No. 335 on the CMOG example) (CMOG).

## Makers, brands and variants
Akro Agate (marbles 1911–, glassware 1930s–1951); Vitro Agate, Master Marble, Westite (Westite leaf/shell ashtrays), Imperial "end of day" slag (1960s–70s), Houze, Boyd (later small pieces).

### Quick ID checklist
- Crow-through-"A" mark visible on the base? Photo needed; many Akro pieces are marked, but not all.
- Opaque glass with swirls running through the body (marbleized), not translucent opalescent glow?
- Children's dish pattern recognisable (Stippled Band, Concentric Ring, Interior Panel, Octagonal, Chiquita, Raised Daisy)?
- Pieces in a "set" all the same pattern, size and maker mark? (Mixed sets are married.)
- Original box with Akro or Little American Maid printing and set number?
- Color: lemonade & oxblood swirl, pumpkin, or solid oxblood = rare; plain green, blue or white = common.
- Form: figural covered jars (Scottie dog, Colonial lady powder jars), Mexicali jars = better than ashtrays and flowerpots.
- "J.P." marking = Akro-made Pressman set.

### Golden-egg tells
- Complete boxed children's tea set in original box — ~10–20x a single ashtray ($60–300 vs $8–25; Terapeak boxed-set median $231) — printed Akro/Little American Maid box with insert; all pieces same pattern.
- Lemonade & oxblood boxed set — top ($450 in Terapeak sample vs $231 median) — yellow glass with red swirls throughout every piece.
- Pumpkin or solid oxblood colors — rare colors, upper single-piece band (general knowledge, verify; Terapeak lemonade/oxblood singles median $40, p75 $63 vs ashtray median $12) — orange-pumpkin or deep red opaque glass.
- Figural powder jars (Scottie dog, Colonial lady) and Mexicali jars — $25–80 vs $8–25 — figural lid; crow mark on base.
- Interior Panel and Octagonal large-size sets — above common Concentric Ring pieces (general knowledge, verify) — panels on the inside of cups and bowls; eight-sided plates.
- Not golden eggs: single marbleized leaf or shell ashtrays (Terapeak median $11.90), small flowerpots, unmarked opalescent "slag" ashtrays.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Akro: opaque, swirls go through the glass, crow mark, rough mold seams. Reproduction/other: translucent glow, uniform color, no mark.
- Akro glass is non-lead, translucent-to-opaque (CMOG); overall mold marks are normal on genuine pieces (CMOG), so seams are not a repro sign by themselves.
- Some shades were also made by other companies (Kovel column); color alone does not prove Akro.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim flea bites, chips on cigarette rests, scratches. Rare repairs.
- Boxes are often worn ("rough shape" on a nearly 100-year-old box is expected); a box in any condition still adds value (eBay.de listing; general knowledge).
- Mixed or replaced pieces in sets: check every piece matches in color, swirl, pattern, marks and size (Kovel column).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Akro doll dish reproductions by others; Westite and Imperial slag confused with Akro.
- Married sets assembled from loose pieces are more common than outright fakes (Kovel column advice to check matching).

## Market
Active collector club; common items oversupplied. Collector interest revived in the late 1960s; most pieces remain reasonably priced, with premiums for rare colors, rare forms and complete sets (Kovel column).

## What sellers usually miss
Boxed doll-dish sets; Scottie dog powder jars; oxblood/lemonade colors; "J.P." marks on Pressman sets.

## Shipping / handling
Small glass; double box; $9–14.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Crow mark photo, colors, dimensions, flea bites.

### What to tell the buyer
State that the piece carries the crow-through-"A" mark (photo) or say plainly that it is unmarked. Name the pattern and size (large or small set), list every piece and its color, and say whether the box is original and its condition. Note flea bites and chips piece by piece. Background that is safe to quote: Akro Agate was founded in Akron, Ohio, in 1911, moved to Clarksburg, West Virginia, in 1914, made glassware from the 1930s and closed in 1951.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). Single ashtrays never qualify. Fits easily.
