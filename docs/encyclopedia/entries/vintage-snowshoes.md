## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Bent ash frames, rawhide (or neoprene) lacing, leather bindings, two or more wooden crossbars. Shapes: Michigan/Maine (teardrop with a
long tail), bear paw (round/oval, no tail), Alaskan/Yukon (long, narrow, upturned toe), Ojibwa (pointed toe and tail), Huron (paddle/
teardrop) (Journal of Antiques). Typical pair: 36–56 in long; the MacMillan Tubbs pair of 1913 measures 48¾ × 12 in (Bowdoin).
Marks: stamped or burned into a crossbar or the frame tail, or a decal on the crossbar (Tubbs, Snocraft); Army pairs often stamped
"U.S." with contractor and date (general knowledge).
Title words: "vintage wooden snowshoes", "rawhide", "Tubbs", "Snocraft", "bear paw", "cabin decor".
Look-alikes: modern decor snowshoes (often small, varnished, synthetic lacing, Asian-made), 1970s–80s neoprene-laced recreation pairs.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Tubbs** — founded 1906 by Walter F. Tubbs in Norway, Maine (Bowdoin). Expedition use: a 1913 pair used by Donald MacMillan is
  "stamped Tubbs" (Bowdoin). Moved to Vermont (Wallingford, later Stowe); changed hands in 1968 and began using neoprene; later aluminium
  frames and plastic bindings; sold to K2 in 2003; manufacturing moved to China in 2004 (Liptak). So: **"Norway, Me." Tubbs = pre-Vermont**;
  wooden Vermont Tubbs = mid-century to 1980s; aluminium Tubbs = modern recreation gear.
- **Snocraft (Sno Craft)** — started by former Tubbs employees in the same Norway, Maine factory building; 40–50 workers; WWII US Army
  contracts ("tens of thousands" for the 10th Mountain Division); closed in the early 1980s (NewsCenter Maine). Norway's modern industry
  traces to farmer-maker Mellie Dunham (the article spells it "Millie").
- **Chestnut Canoe Co.** (Canada; Journal of Antiques says founded 1905) — also supplied the armed forces.
- **C. A. Lund** — made snowshoes for US forces in WWII (Journal of Antiques).
- **Floyd Westover** — popular US maker 1930s–1960s; created the "Modified Bearpaw" (Journal of Antiques).
- **Iverson** (Shingleton, Michigan), **Faber** (Quebec), Northland, Sherpa (aluminium, 1970s+ — general knowledge).
- **Native-made** — Huron, Ojibwa, Cree, Abenaki, Athabascan traditions; woodland designs were widely copied by traders and trappers
  (Journal of Antiques); Abenaki pairs from Vermont/Quebec are in the Vermont Historical Society (Liptak).

### Quick ID checklist
- Any stamp, burn or decal on the crossbars or tail: Tubbs, Snocraft, "Norway, Me.", Chestnut, Lund, Westover, "U.S."
- Lacing: thick cowhide rawhide vs fine babiche (gut, tight hexagonal weave) vs neoprene/nylon (black or grey, rubbery).
- Frame: one-piece bent ash with a tail joined by rivets/lashing; metal frame = 1950s+ (Journal of Antiques).
- Shape matches a named style (Maine/Michigan, bear paw, Alaskan, Ojibwa pointed, Huron).
- Bindings: original leather harness vs modern plastic/rubber.
- Measure length: the size rule allows ~56 in; longer Alaskan pairs may not fit.
- Mint "antique" with bright varnish and perfect lacing = suspect modern or restored (Journal of Antiques).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Native-made pairs with fine babiche lacing and decorated tufts/wool tassels** — $200–600 tier (old entry, general knowledge) vs $60–140 common — very fine tight hexagonal weave, painted or tufted toe, hand-carved crossbars, no maker stamp.
- **Early Tubbs stamped "Norway, Me." (1906–pre-Vermont)** — ~1.5–3× a common pair (general knowledge) — Tubbs stamp with Maine location on the crossbar; rawhide lacing, old dark patina.
- **WWII US Army issue (Snocraft, C.A. Lund, Chestnut contracts)** — military-collector premium (general knowledge) — "U.S." stamp, contractor name and date; olive/khaki bindings.
- **Snocraft (Norway, Maine) with decal intact** — $100–200 labelled tier (old entry) vs $60–140 unlabelled — Snocraft decal or stamp on the crossbar.
- **Westover "Modified Bearpaw"** — collector name premium (general knowledge) — short oval shape with slight tail, Westover stamp.
- Expedition/provenance pairs (e.g., MacMillan's Tubbs at Bowdoin) — museum tier — written provenance only.
- Neoprene-laced 1970s–80s Vermont Tubbs, aluminium Sherpa/Tubbs — not golden eggs; user value only.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Rawhide vs neoprene lacing; stamped/decal crossbar labels. Rawhide lacing dates mostly pre-1950s; nylon/man-made lacing and metal
frames are 1950s+ (Journal of Antiques); neoprene at Tubbs from 1968 (Liptak). Quality: tight even weave, steam-bent ash with no
cracks at the toe bend, mortised crossbars, varnished rawhide that is still taut. Old vs new: darkened, translucent-amber rawhide,
worn varnish, dry leather bindings with period buckles. Modern decor: thin frames, glossy lacquer, synthetic "rawhide", stapled lacing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken lacing, cracked frames, dry rot leather. Also: lacing chewed by rodents, sagging centre mesh, toe bend cracks, rewoven sections
(different colour or thickness of lacing), replaced bindings. Restoration is slow work (Journal of Antiques); damaged pairs still sell as
decor. Ask: any broken strands? toe cracks? photos of both sides?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern décor snowshoes; "made to look older" modern pairs (Journal of Antiques); restored pairs presented as original. No known faked
Tubbs stamps (general knowledge).

## Market
Seasonal (fall/winter peak). Buyers are cabin/lodge decorators first, then collectors of Native and military pieces; users buy neoprene
pairs. Best sold locally because of length.

## What sellers usually miss
Native-made pairs; maker stamps on crossbars; "U.S." Army marks; Maine-location Tubbs stamps.

## Shipping / handling
Long (40–56"), $35–50 per pair; marginal on passenger-seat size. Pack face-to-face with cardboard over the lacing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Length, maker, lacing condition. Also width, shape name, binding type, any stamps (photo), and wall-hanging suitability.

### What to tell the buyer
"Pair of ash-frame __-style snowshoes, each __ × __ in, laced in __ (rawhide/neoprene) with original leather bindings. Stamped '__'
on the crossbar (photographed). Lacing is taut with __ broken strands; frames have no cracks at the toe bend. Display-ready; usable
with __." For Tubbs: "Tubbs was founded in Norway, Maine in 1906; this pair's '__' stamp places it __."

## Buy rule
Shipping usually kills margin. Buy marked/Native/Army pairs ≤ ~$40 for local resale or confident shipping; pass common pairs over ~$40.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
