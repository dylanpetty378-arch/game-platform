## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Mid-century American semi-porcelain/earthenware: figural cookie jars, creamers, salt and pepper shakers, planters, vases and teapots for dime stores (Woolworth, Kresge, Sears).
- Look for: bright solid glazes (green, yellow, blue, pink), embossed designs, gold trim and cold-painted or decal accents, figural shapes (pigs, owls, Dutch kids, cats, elephants).
- Marks: "USA" with a mold number on the base (e.g., "USA 829"), "Shawnee USA", "Kenwood USA", "Pat. Smiley USA", "Corn King Oven Ware". Many pieces carry only "USA" and a number — the edge for Dylan.
- More marks (Hunker, Kovels): "Shawnee", "Shawnee U.S.A.", "Kenwood", "Great Northern", "Pat. Smiley USA", "Shawnee Smiley 60" / "Patent Smiley 60 — Shawnee USA". Pieces were not marked "Shawnee" until the 1950s, so a 1940s piece often reads only "USA". Originals never carry a year or artist signature on the base. Paper or foil labels read "Shawnee" or show an arrowhead with an Indian head (logo by in-house designer Louise Bauer, per Wikipedia).
- Base/foot (Hunker, Post-Bulletin): most pieces are glazed inside and out with a raised unglazed rim or foot ring where the piece sat in the kiln; large pieces may have an unglazed bottom showing clay and mould lines.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Company** (Wikipedia): Malcolm and Roy Schweiker bought the American Encaustic Tiling plant at 2200 Linden Ave., Zanesville, in 1937; production under the Shawnee name began August 1937; closed 1961 (overseas competition). About 90% of 1942–46 production went to Army Air Force contracts. Designer Robert Heckman joined in the WWII period. Sold through Woolworth, Kresge, McCrory and Sears; Sears commissioned Valencia dinnerware and an exclusive kitchenware line. Corn King and Pennsylvania Dutch are the most collectible lines (Wikipedia).
- **Kitchenware lines:** Corn King (1946–54) and Corn Queen (1954–61) — CORRECTION: the old entry said Corn King had the darker green husks and Corn Queen the lighter colours; Post-Bulletin describes the Corn Queen pitcher as darker green, and collector usage generally gives Corn Queen darker green husks and softer yellow kernels (verify against Mangus). Sequence: White Corn first, then Corn King, then Corn Queen (Post-Bulletin). Snowflake; Valencia (Sears, 1937); Fern.
- **Cookie jars:** Smiley pig (scarf colours, clovers, tulips, shamrocks), Winnie pig, Puss 'n Boots, Mugsey, Jo-Jo clown, Dutch Boy/Girl, Owl, Lucky Elephant, Muggsy dog. Gold-trimmed decal versions are worth 2–4× plain.
  - **Smiley detail**: designed by Rudy Ganz c. 1942 (Creators) and originally called "Smiling Pig" (Kovels). Early jars were cold-painted or plain with a triangular rim opening; later ones had round openings and were hand-painted or decal-decorated with apples, clover, flowers, plums, shamrocks, strawberries and coloured kerchiefs (Kovels). Companion pieces: Winnie, banks, shakers, pitchers (Creators). One reader's jar is about 11" tall with a green scarf and "butterscotch brown" pants (Creators). Also used as premiums, e.g. a 1950s coffee giveaway.
- **Teapots:** "Granny Ann", "Tom the Piper's son", Elephant; embossed floral teapots (rose, tulip, horseshoe) with hand-painted accents.
- **Art ware:** Chantilly, Kenwood (late 1950s, Elegance line), double dove vase #829, swan and bird planters.
- **Terrace Ceramics**: bought Shawnee moulds after 1961; made plain white undecorated Smiley jars (Kovels) and corn pieces lighter in colour than Corn King (Post-Bulletin) — $8–25 each retail.
- **Rival makers often confused:** McCoy, Hull, Robinson-Ransbottom, American Bisque (cookie jars), Regal China (Little Red Riding Hood is Hull/Regal).

### Quick ID checklist
- Base mark: "USA" + mould number, "Shawnee USA", "Kenwood", "Great Northern", "Pat. Smiley USA" / "Smiley 60"; never a year or signature.
- Glazed inside and out, with a raised unglazed foot ring (or unglazed bottom on large pieces).
- Smiley/Winnie: triangular rim opening = early cold-paint jar; round opening = later hand-painted/decal jar.
- Gold: fired gold on scarf, buttons, flowers, rim — note wear on high points; gold + decals is the top tier.
- Height: measure; repros run about 10% smaller from mould shrinkage.
- Crazing: uniform all-over crazing is a repro warning (Hunker).
- Plain white undecorated Smiley = likely Terrace Ceramics, post-1961.
- Corn: pale colours may be Terrace; Corn King vs Corn Queen by husk colour and mark; many other makers made corn ware with different moulds.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Gold trim: real 22K gold-fired trim, worn on high points — collectors prize "gold and decals".
- Cold-paint (unfired over-glaze paint) flakes; complete cold paint adds value.
- Glaze crazing is common and acceptable at low end; heavy staining is not.
- Good: crisp mold detail, even glaze, intact gold. Cheap: late reproductions with soft detail and heavier weight.
- Two-fire process 1937–1942 (Wikipedia): first firing, then china underglaze and paint; cold paint wears over time.
- Hunker notes original jar heads lack painted flowers (shamrocks, chrysanthemums, tulips) — floral decoration belongs on the body; flowers painted on a head is a repro warning (verify against Mangus, since Kovels lists shamrock/floral decoration on genuine jars).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips on lid rims and spouts, hairline cracks, worn gold, flaked cold paint, mismatched lids (cookie jars).
- Restorations: airbrushed lids and painted-over chips — check with a blacklight; restorations halve value.
- Corn King large teapot and butter dish are hard to find mint (Post-Bulletin).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Smiley/Winnie pig, Puss 'n Boots and Corn King cookie jars were reproduced from the 1990s ("USA" mark with smaller size because of shrinkage, poor gold, sloppy paint). Repros are about 10% smaller than originals.
- Planters and vases are rarely reproduced.
- Many fake Smiley jars are on the market (Kovels); repros are usually smaller and noticeably poorer (Post-Bulletin, Hunker); uniform crazing is a tell (Hunker).
- Terrace Ceramics post-1961 pieces from original moulds: legitimate but lower value.

## Market
- Collector base aging; plain pieces slow. Cookie jars with gold sell steadily. Prices down from 2000s highs.

## What sellers usually miss
- "USA" + number mark is Shawnee; sellers often don't know the maker for gold-trim figural pieces.
- Gold trim and decals multiply value; sellers price by shape alone.
- Corn pieces from other makers (and Terrace) get titled "Corn King" — a buyer-side trap, not an edge.

## Shipping / handling
- Ceramic: double box, 2" foam/bubble all round. Teapot 3–5 lb boxed, $12–18; cookie jars 6–8 lb, $18–25. Pack lids separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give mold number, colour, height, gold condition; photograph base and inside lid; state no chips/cracks/restoration (blacklight-checked).

### What to tell the buyer
Shawnee Pottery worked in Zanesville, Ohio from 1937 to 1961 and sold through Woolworth, Kresge, McCrory and Sears. Quote the base mark exactly. Many genuine pieces read only "USA" and a mould number, because the "Shawnee" name was not used as a mark until the 1950s. Give the height to the nearest 1/8 inch, since reproductions run about 10% smaller. Describe the decoration as cold paint, fired gold, or decals, and say how complete it is. For Smiley and Winnie jars, say whether the rim opening is triangular (early) or round (later). State that the piece has been checked under blacklight for restoration, and photograph the unglazed foot ring and the inside of the lid.
