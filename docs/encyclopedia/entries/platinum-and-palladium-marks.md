## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Platinum (Pt) and palladium (Pd) are white, non-tarnishing platinum-group metals. Platinum became the jewelry metal of choice from about 1900 (Edwardian "garland" style) through the Art Deco period (c.1920–1935); it was restricted for jewelry in the US during WWII as a strategic metal, when palladium and white gold replaced it, and it returned in the 1950s and again strongly in Japan and the US from the 1970s–90s (WWII and later dates general knowledge, verify). Palladium jewelry appears in the 1940s and again in 2000s–2010s wedding bands; UK palladium hallmarking started in 2010 (general knowledge, verify).

**Photo look:** a grey-white, slightly darker and softer white than silver or rhodium; fine pierced filigree, milgrain (tiny beaded edges), hand-engraved sides; old European, transitional and single-cut diamonds in bead or bezel settings; calibré sapphires, rubies or emeralds; often a **yellow-gold back with a platinum face** ("platinum-topped gold", "18K & PLAT", "PLAT TOP"). No tarnish in the crevices (silver goes black; platinum stays grey).

**Where the mark is:** inside the ring shank (often "PLAT", "IRIDPLAT", "10% IRID PLAT" or "PT950" with a maker), on the pin stem or back of a bar pin, on the clasp of a bracelet, inside the case of a watch ("PLATINUM" or "PLAT" inside the back cover; the movement may say otherwise), on the bail of a pendant.

**Seller words:** "silver ring with stones", "white metal", "silver tone", "antique filigree ring", "old ring with diamond(s)?", "CZ?", "unmarked", "white gold?", "marked PT (not sure what that means)", "marked IRIDPLAT", "costume Art Deco".

**Look-alikes:** 18K/14K white gold Deco filigree (very common — sellers call it "platinum" in the reverse error), sterling Deco-style reproduction filigree, rhodium-plated silver (modern), palladium, stainless steel and "white metal" costume with paste.

Platinum is now worth **less per gram than 14K gold** ($49 vs $79). Never let a "platinum" label push a price above what the stones support.

**National marks**
- **France — dog's head (tête de chien):** the French hallmark for platinum and pieces with platinum parts, used since 1912 (antiquejewel.com); minimum 950 (general knowledge, verify). A tiny dog's-head profile in a shaped cartouche, often on the pin stem or clasp of Deco brooches, beside an eagle's head (18K gold) on two-metal pieces. Sellers can't read it; it looks like a smudge. (Do not confuse with the Swiss St Bernard dog mark, which is a gold mark.)
- **UK — orb:** the traditional platinum symbol is an orb surmounted by a cross inside a pentagon shield, optional and often seen on older pieces, alongside the compulsory 950 fineness, sponsor's and assay office marks. UK platinum hallmarking began in 1975 after the Hallmarking Act 1973; before that, UK platinum was marked "Pt", "Plat", "PLAT/IRID" or "18/Pt" (Pragnell). Recognised UK/Convention platinum standards: 850, 900, 950, 999; palladium 500, 950, 999 (Assay Office London). Palladium's traditional mark is the head of Pallas Athene (general knowledge, verify).
- **Japan — Pt marks:** Japanese jewelry is stamped **Pt1000, Pt950, Pt900 or Pt850** (very often Pt900), with K18/K14 for gold. The Japan Mint hallmark (a stylised rising sun with characters for the metal) is voluntary, so most Japanese pieces carry only the maker's quality stamp (skyjems.ca). Older Japanese platinum is sometimes marked "Pm" (general knowledge, verify). Japanese Pt900 rings, chains and pendants with small diamonds or pearls come through US estates (post-war servicemen, travel) and are listed as "silver".
- **China:** Pt950, Pt990, 足铂 (general knowledge, verify for 足铂); metal symbol + fineness rule (skyjems GB 11887).
- **Russia (Imperial):** platinum assay marks of 88 or 92 (skyjems.ca); rare.

**Era variants**
- **Edwardian (c.1900–1915):** lacy garland and bow motifs, platinum-topped gold, millegrain, old mine and old European cuts, natural pearls. Marks often absent or "PLAT" only.
- **Art Deco (c.1920–1935):** geometric pierced filigree, calibré coloured stones, baguettes in the 1930s, "IRIDPLAT" / "PLAT" / "10% IRID" in US rings, dog's head on French pieces. Makers' marks of Tiffany, Cartier, Black Starr & Frost, Marcus, Oscar Heyman, Raymond Yard and J.E. Caldwell multiply value many times (general knowledge, verify).
- **Retro/WWII (c.1940–1950):** platinum scarce; "PALLADIUM" bands and settings, white gold (general knowledge, verify).
- **Modern (1970s–now):** Pt950 and Pt900 bands, Japanese Pt900 sets, PD950 men's bands.

### Golden-egg tells
- **"PLAT" / "IRIDPLAT" / "10% IRID" inside a Deco filigree ring listed as "silver ring with clear stones"** — melt alone ~$46–49/g (a 3–5 g ring ≈ $150–250) plus diamonds; with a 0.25–0.5 ct old European centre $800–2,500 vs a $20–40 asking price (~20–60×) (jewelry range general knowledge, verify). Tell: pierced geometric shank, milgrain edges, grey-white metal with no black tarnish, stones that sparkle in chunky "old cut" flashes.
- **French dog's head on a "white metal" Deco brooch or bar pin** — platinum at 950, often with diamonds; Deco diamond bar pins $400–2,000+ (general knowledge, verify) vs $10–30 costume. Tell: a tiny animal-head cartouche on the pin stem, often with an eagle's head on the gold catch.
- **Platinum Deco cocktail watch** sold as "silver ladies' watch" — platinum and diamond case $400–2,500 (general knowledge, verify) vs $20–50. Tell: tiny rectangular case surrounded by diamonds, cord or base-metal bracelet, "PLATINUM" or "IRIDPLAT" inside the case lid.
- **Japanese "Pt900"** on rings, pendants and chains listed as "silver" or "unmarked" because the seller reads "Pt" as a model code — $46.88/g plus stones; a 5 g ring with a 0.3 ct diamond $250–600 (general knowledge, verify). Tell: "Pt900" (often with "0.30" — the diamond's carat weight stamped beside it, a Japanese habit; general knowledge, verify).
- **"18K & PLAT" two-tone Edwardian piece** described as "gold ring with silver top" — the "silver" is platinum; value is gold + platinum + stones (~1.5–3× the seller's "gold" price; general knowledge, verify). Tell: white top plate with milgrain over a yellow shank.
- **Signed Deco platinum (Tiffany & Co., Cartier, Oscar Heyman "OH" with a pinwheel-style mark, Raymond Yard)** — 5–50× unsigned (general knowledge, verify). Tell: maker name or mark beside "PLAT" or "PLATINUM"; specialist authentication required.
- **Heavy plain Pt950 band** called a "silver ring" — 8–15 g = $395–740 melt vs $10–20 (~30×). Tell: thick, heavy for its size, grey-white, "PT950" or "PLAT" inside.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the mark (table above). "PLAT", "IRID", "Pt", "PT", "PD", "PALL", "dog's head", "orb" all mean a PGM; "925", "STERLING" mean silver; "18K", "14K", "750", "585" without "PLAT" mean white gold.
- Ask for the weight in grams and the ring size or dimensions. Platinum is very dense (21.45 g/cm³) — about **twice** silver (10.5) and **1.4–1.7×** 14K–18K white gold (≈ 12.6–16); palladium (12.0) weighs like 14K white gold, i.e. light for "platinum" (densities general knowledge, verify). A plain 6 mm band in size 7 weighs roughly 8–10 g in platinum, 6 g in 14K white gold, 4–5 g in silver (general knowledge, verify).
- Look at the colour at wear points: **white gold** shows a yellowish tint where rhodium has worn (shank bottom, prong tips); **platinum** goes uniformly grey with a satiny scratch "patina" and never yellows; **silver** tarnishes yellow-brown to black in crevices and looks brighter/whiter when polished; palladium is slightly darker grey than platinum.
- Magnet: all three are non-magnetic (some cobalt-platinum alloys are weakly magnetic; general knowledge, verify).
- Era check: pierced filigree, milgrain, old European or single-cut diamonds, calibré stones = Deco/Edwardian; laser-cut or cast "filigree" with CZ = modern repro.
- Ask: "Is there any stamp inside — PLAT, IRID, Pt, 18K, 925? Can you photograph it in focus?" and "What does it weigh in grams?"

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Hand-made Deco/Edwardian:** hand-pierced openwork with slight irregularity, hand engraving (wheat, scroll), milgrain wheel beading, bead-set stones with tiny raised grains; solder seams invisible in platinum work.
- **Cast/modern repro:** smooth mould marks inside, identical openings, CZs or modern round brilliants, sometimes "925" or "14K" with "Deco-style".
- **Platinum surface:** dull grey satin with fine scratches (platinum displaces rather than loses metal), no tarnish; edges of milgrain soften with wear.
- **Palladium:** lighter weight, grey-white, often modern bands "PD950"; 1940s "PALLADIUM" pieces with "Retro" styling (general knowledge, verify).
- **Platinum-topped gold:** a thin platinum plate on gold; the karat mark on the gold may be the only mark.
- **Stones:** old European cuts (small table, high crown, open culet visible as a dot), single cuts (17–18 facets), mine cuts (cushion shape). These signal age and real diamonds; modern CZ shows sharp, many-facet "rainbow" fire.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Worn or missing milgrain, thin shanks (re-sized many times), lost small diamonds (each missing melee costs ~$20–50 to replace; general knowledge, verify), cracked or chipped old-cut stones, broken filigree, solder repairs in white gold on a platinum ring (lowers melt value and looks whiter/yellowish), replaced shanks (half shanks of a different metal or karat — look for a seam on the band and a different stamp). Re-plated (rhodium) platinum hides wear. Ask for a photo of the underside (gallery) and of the shank seam.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Deco-style repros** in sterling, 14K white gold or base metal, sometimes stamped "PLAT" or "PT950" fraudulently (general knowledge, verify). Weight is the best check: a fake "platinum" ring is too light.
- **"Platinum plated"**, "PT plated", "platinum finish", "platinum-tone" — plating on silver or base metal; no PGM value.
- **Married pieces:** a Deco head on a new shank; a Deco watch case with a new movement.
- **Reverse error:** much 1920s–30s filigree is 18K white gold, which sellers call "platinum"; check the mark.

## Market
Deco and Edwardian platinum-and-diamond jewelry has a deep collector and bridal market (vintage engagement rings) on eBay, Etsy, 1stDibs and specialist auctions (Heritage); good pieces sell in days to a few weeks (general knowledge, verify). Plain platinum and palladium sell to refiners instantly at ~80–90% of melt (PGM refiners often pay a lower share than for gold on small lots; general knowledge, verify). Japanese Pt900 jewelry is plentiful and sells near melt plus a modest premium. Platinum spot (~$1,620) is well below gold, so most of the value of a platinum piece is in its stones and design.

## What sellers usually miss
- "PLAT", "IRIDPLAT", "10% IRID", "Pt900" and the French dog's head mean platinum; they list it as "silver" or "white metal".
- The weight: a heavy "silver" band can be 10 g of platinum.
- The old-cut stones are real diamonds, not "CZ" or "glass".
- Two-metal Edwardian construction: the "silver top" on a gold ring is platinum.
- Watches: the platinum is the case; they price the movement or the brand.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket-size: small box, padded, $5–6 to buyer; insure fully (USPS insured/registered) and require signature over $250. Don't write "platinum" or "diamond" on the outside. Pack so prongs and filigree can't snag.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State: metal and mark ("stamped IRIDPLAT — platinum with 10% iridium"), weight in grams, ring size, stone count, cut and estimated carat weight with the testing method (diamond tester, loupe), era and style, any maker mark, condition of milgrain and prongs, any re-sizing. Photograph the mark, the gallery (side view), the top under daylight and the stones under a loupe. Title keywords: "Art Deco", "platinum", "IRIDPLAT" or "PT950", "old European cut diamond", "filigree", "Edwardian", ring size, weight.
### What to tell the buyer
"Platinum, stamped [PLAT / IRIDPLAT / Pt900 / French dog's head], [5.6] g, size [6.5]. Set with [n] old European- and single-cut diamonds, tested as diamond on [tester], approx. [0.30] ct total. Hand-pierced filigree and milgrain, c.1925–35. Mark, gallery and stones pictured close-up."

## Buy rule
Buy when: a PGM mark (or the weight and the colour strongly indicate platinum, confirmed before paying) and price ≤ 40% of (melt + conservative stone value) for the scrap/plain route, or ≤ ~43% of the realistic sold price for a wearable Deco/Edwardian piece, so profit ≥ max($50, price). Pass when: no mark and no weight; "platinum plated"/"tone"; modern cast "Deco-style"; 14K/18K white gold priced as platinum; heavy damage to filigree or missing centre stone (value back to melt plus melee). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat in all forms.
