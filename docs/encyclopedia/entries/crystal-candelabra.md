## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass candleholders with two or three arms (lights), often a centre spire or finial, round "bobeche" drip pans and hanging cut prisms (spear, teardrop or "colonial" prisms). Mid-century American Depression/Elegant glass makers sold them in pairs as part of a console set (with a centre bowl).
Tells: twisted or ribbed stems, scalloped/fluted bases, removable bobeches hooked with prisms, mould seams on pressed pieces.

More detail:
- **Vocabulary**:
  - Candelabrum (singular) and candelabra (plural, though sellers use it for one).
  - "2-light" or "3-light" is the number of candle cups.
  - Bobeche: the drip collar, often separate, with holes or notches for prisms.
  - Prisms: spear, teardrop, "colonial" or button-and-prism chains.
  - Candleblock: a solid block with sockets, such as Heisey Saturn.
  - Girandole: a candelabrum with long prisms, often with a gilt-bronze or figural base, 19th century.
- **Factory blank numbers**: the same 2-light blank was sold plain or with various etchings, and the etching drives value.
  - Duncan & Miller #30: 2-light candleholder, 7" wide × 5" tall. Etched First Love, Indian Tree or Language of Flowers (NDGA).
  - Cambridge #647 "Keyhole": 2-light candlestick, 6" tall. Etched Rose Point, Chantilly, Elaine or Chintz. Identify it by the keyhole-shaped opening in the stem, which also appears on Cambridge vases and compotes (NDGA).
  - Cambridge #3400: 2-light candelabrum, about 6 1/8" tall. Replacements stocks it in Diane etch without bobeches or prisms at $129.95 each, a retail asking price.
- **Size**: most American 2-lights are 5–10" tall and 7–10" wide; 3-lights with spires reach 12–16". All fit a passenger seat.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **A. H. Heisey** (Newark OH, 1896–1957): Crystolite, Waverly, Orchid etch, Lariat; diamond-H mark on some (often absent on candelabra).
- **Cambridge Glass** (1902–1958): Caprice, Rose Point etch, Keyhole; C-in-triangle mark rare on candelabra.
- **Fostoria** (1887–1986): Baroque, Century, Navarre etch, Colony; paper labels only.
- **Duncan & Miller** (1893–1955): Canterbury, Sandwich, First Love etch; 2-light candelabra with prisms are classic Duncan.
- **Imperial**, **New Martinsville/Viking**, **Paden City**, **Westmoreland**, **L.E. Smith**: pressed versions.
- **Waterford, Baccarat, Bohemian/Czech (Bohemia Crystal)**: cut lead crystal, heavier, acid-etched marks; higher tier.
- **19th c. girandoles**: gilt-bronze or cut-glass bases with long prisms.

Further detail:
- **Heisey marks** (HCA FAQ): the moulded Diamond H is not always on the base or centred and can be hidden. Worn moulds and fire-polishing can blur or remove it, so no mark does not rule out Heisey. Blown pieces carried only paper labels. Heisey closed in 1957, so anything made later from Heisey moulds is a reissue. The Heisey mark is about twice as tall as it is wide; a square H on its corner is a Taiwan look-alike.
- **Heisey colours**: Zircon (a sparkly turquoise) is a Heisey colour used on the Saturn 2-light candleblock (NDGA). Colours such as Zircon, Sahara, Moongleam and Flamingo usually add value over crystal (general knowledge, verify).
- **Duncan & Miller**: a pattern catalog listing calls #41 "Sandwich" (NDGA). The spear-finial 2-light with long prisms seen in sweeps is the common Duncan form.
- **Etch vs plain**: an etched blank (Rose Point, First Love, Chantilly, Navarre) is worth clearly more than the same blank plain. Cambridge Rose Point is the most collected etch (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Is there a second, matching one? Pairs sell for 2–3× a single.
- Count the bobeches and prisms. Note whether every prism matches in shape, length and colour, and whether any hooks are missing.
- Look for a keyhole-shaped stem (Cambridge #647), a spear finial with long prisms (Duncan & Miller type), or a satin- or plate-etched floral (an etched pattern adds value).
- Look for a moulded Diamond H anywhere on the piece; it may be small and off-centre. An "HCA" or year mark means a post-1957 reissue.
- Check the rims of bobeches and cups and the base edge for chips and grinding.
- At the arm-to-stem joint, look for yellowish glue lines.
- Colour: crystal is common. Zircon, Sahara, Moongleam, cobalt or amber add value.
- For Waterford, Baccarat or Czech cut crystal, look for an acid-etched script mark on the base and deep, sharp cuts. Pressed glass shows mould seams and rounded "cuts".

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lead crystal rings when tapped, is heavier and brighter. Pressed glass shows mould seams and rounded "pressed" cuts. Prisms: old ones are hand-cut with slightly uneven facets and drilled holes; replacements are moulded and identical. Etched patterns (Rose Point, Navarre) add value over plain.
- **Antique vs modern** (Westland London): a pontil mark on blown parts, tiny trapped bubbles and slight irregularity, natural wear (small flakes, scratches, worn gilding), greater weight, and sharper cutting on true crystal. Crystal chimes when tapped and throws a strong prism effect.
- **American elegant glass** (1920s–50s) is mostly pressed or mould-blown and fire-polished, then sometimes etched or cut. Fire-polished edges are softly rounded. Etched patterns are acid-etched (frosted).
- **Prism tells**: old cut prisms have slightly uneven facets and a smooth, polished, drilled hole. Modern moulded or acrylic prisms are identical to each other, very light, sometimes have a moulded seam, and sound dull when tapped. Acrylic feels warm and is visibly lighter.
- **Bobeches**: original bobeches match the stem glass in colour and quality. A swapped bobeche from another pattern shows a different tint or notch spacing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on bobeche rims and base edges, missing/mismatched prisms, broken arms glued at the stem (look for yellow glue lines), cloudy "sick" glass, ground-down base chips. Bobeches often broken or swapped.
- Ground base chips show as a flat, matte, slightly uneven band on the foot rim.
- "Sick" (cloudy) glass cannot be cleaned out and should be priced as damaged.
- Replacements sells the candelabrum without bobeches or prisms, a sign that the base alone is the stable value unit and that prisms and bobeches are often replaced.
- What to ask: is there a second one? How many prisms are on each, and do they all match? Are there chips on the bobeche rims? Any glued joints? Are the bobeches original to the piece?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese/Indian candelabra with acrylic prisms sold as "vintage crystal". Fenton/Imperial reissued some Heisey moulds after 1957 (often marked IG or with Heisey Collectors' marks). Prisms are routinely replaced.
- **Correction (2026-10-13)**: Heisey's own collectors' sources name **Imperial Glass** and later the **Heisey Collectors of America (HCA)** as the reissuers of Heisey moulds after the 1957 closure. Fenton is not named, so treat Fenton as unverified. HCA reproductions are made in colours Heisey never used and are marked "HCA", the year, and a maker letter or symbol (HCA FAQ; Heisey Museum; NDGA "Imperial reproductions of Heisey patterns").
- Taiwan look-alikes carry an H in a square turned on its corner (HCA FAQ).
- Generic modern "crystal" candelabra from China and India usually have chrome or brass arms with acrylic drops, are very light, and have identical drops.

## Market
Elegant glass market has softened a lot since 2005. Singles are slow; complete pairs do OK around holidays and weddings.
Specialist buyers: Heisey, Cambridge and Duncan collector clubs (HCA runs auctions and an annual convention) and matching services such as Replacements, which buy low and sell high.

## What sellers usually miss
Whether there's a matching second one; the etched pattern name; Waterford marks on the base.
Also: Heisey coloured glass (Zircon, Sahara, Moongleam), the Cambridge keyhole stem, and blank numbers that tie a piece to a named etch.

## Shipping / handling
Very fragile: remove prisms and bobeches, wrap separately, double box. 3–5 lb, $18–25. Breakage risk high.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Count prisms, state any mismatched ones, note bobeche condition, height and span, maker/pattern if certain.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the height, arm span and number of lights. State "sold as a pair" or "single" clearly. Name the maker and pattern only when confirmed by a mark, blank number or reference book: "Cambridge #647 Keyhole 2-light, Rose Point etch". Otherwise say "American elegant glass, Duncan & Miller type, unmarked". Count the prisms on each piece, describe the prism type (spear, teardrop) and say whether all match. State whether the bobeches are present and original, and list every chip, ground edge and glued joint. For Heisey, say whether a Diamond H is visible and note that unmarked Heisey exists. For anything with an HCA mark, call it an HCA reissue with the year. Say the piece will ship with prisms and bobeches removed and wrapped separately.
