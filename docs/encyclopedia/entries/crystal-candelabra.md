## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass candleholders with two or three arms (lights), often a centre spire or finial, round "bobeche" drip pans and hanging cut prisms (spear, teardrop or "colonial" prisms). Mid-century American Depression/Elegant glass makers sold them in pairs as part of a console set (with a centre bowl).
Tells: twisted or ribbed stems, scalloped/fluted bases, removable bobeches hooked with prisms, mould seams on pressed pieces.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **A. H. Heisey** (Newark OH, 1896–1957): Crystolite, Waverly, Orchid etch, Lariat; diamond-H mark on some (often absent on candelabra).
- **Cambridge Glass** (1902–1958): Caprice, Rose Point etch, Keyhole; C-in-triangle mark rare on candelabra.
- **Fostoria** (1887–1986): Baroque, Century, Navarre etch, Colony; paper labels only.
- **Duncan & Miller** (1893–1955): Canterbury, Sandwich, First Love etch; 2-light candelabra with prisms are classic Duncan.
- **Imperial**, **New Martinsville/Viking**, **Paden City**, **Westmoreland**, **L.E. Smith**: pressed versions.
- **Waterford, Baccarat, Bohemian/Czech (Bohemia Crystal)**: cut lead crystal, heavier, acid-etched marks; higher tier.
- **19th c. girandoles**: gilt-bronze or cut-glass bases with long prisms.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lead crystal rings when tapped, is heavier and brighter. Pressed glass shows mould seams and rounded "pressed" cuts. Prisms: old ones are hand-cut with slightly uneven facets and drilled holes; replacements are moulded and identical. Etched patterns (Rose Point, Navarre) add value over plain.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on bobeche rims and base edges, missing/mismatched prisms, broken arms glued at the stem (look for yellow glue lines), cloudy "sick" glass, ground-down base chips. Bobeches often broken or swapped.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese/Indian candelabra with acrylic prisms sold as "vintage crystal". Fenton/Imperial reissued some Heisey moulds after 1957 (often marked IG or with Heisey Collectors' marks). Prisms are routinely replaced.

## Market
Elegant glass market has softened a lot since 2005. Singles are slow; complete pairs do OK around holidays and weddings.

## What sellers usually miss
Whether there's a matching second one; the etched pattern name; Waterford marks on the base.

## Shipping / handling
Very fragile: remove prisms and bobeches, wrap separately, double box. 3–5 lb, $18–25. Breakage risk high.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Count prisms, state any mismatched ones, note bobeche condition, height and span, maker/pattern if certain.
