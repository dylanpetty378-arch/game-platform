## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Simple roll-film cameras from c. 1900–1965: cardboard/leatherette box cameras (Brownie No. 2, Hawkeye), molded Bakelite or early plastic cameras (1930s–50s), and cheap twin-lens reflexes (Argoflex). Photo tells:
- **Bakelite** is glossy black or brown, heavy for size, with a molded textured pattern and art-deco ribs (Falcon/Spartus/Utility, Kodak Baby Brownie, Purma, Coronet). Colored Bakelite/plastic (green, blue, red, mottled brown) is the valuable exception.
- **Lens panel text** gives the maker: "UTILITY MFG. CO. NEW YORK" (Falcon/Spartus), "BIRDSEYE Flash" (Birdseye Camera Corp./Herbert George, Chicago), "argus" plate + "argoflex" (Argus, Ann Arbor MI), "Brownie"/"Kodak" on the strap or faceplate.
- **TLR (twin lens) cameras**: Argoflex E/EF/EM have two lenses geared together with toothed rings (focusing models); the Argoflex Seventy-Five (1949–58) is a box-shaped TLR with fixed focus; Argoflex Forty (1950–58) similar with flash.
- **Film size** printed on the camera or box: 127, 620, 120, 828. 127/620 cameras are mostly décor buys today (620 film is respooled 120).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Eastman Kodak (Rochester)** — Brownie No. 2/2A (1901–1930s, millions; $15–40), Baby Brownie / Baby Brownie Special (Bakelite, 1934–1950s; $10–25), Brownie Hawkeye (1949–61; $10–25), Brownie Holiday, Brownie Starflash (colors, 1957–65; $20–50 in colors). **Valuable:** Beau Brownie (1930–33, Art Deco enamel by Walter Dorwin Teague; rose/green most collectible, $85–650 per LoveToKnow), Kodak Petite/Vanity/Ensemble (colored 1920s–30s vest pocket cameras, $100–600 with cases), Bantam Special (1936–48, Teague Art Deco, $150–400), Super Six-20 ($1,000+).
- **Argus (Ann Arbor)** — Argoflex E (1940–48, Bakelite, geared lenses, Wollensak/Varex), Argoflex EF/EM (1948–51; EF coated lenses + flash sync; EM metal body, rarer — collectiblend lists "somewhat rare," $30–100 by condition), Argoflex Seventy-Five, Argoflex Forty, and the A-series/C3 "brick" 35mm (very common). Argus Collectors Group: "most Argus camera models are not worth a great deal."
- **Utility Manufacturing Co. (New York/Chicago)** — Falcon Miniature (late 1930s–1940s, 127 film, "Graf Lens 50mm" or Wollensak "Minivar 50mm"), Falcon Deluxe, Falcon Minette, Spartus (after 1940s reorganization as Spartus Corp.). Black Bakelite, very common.
- **Birdseye Camera Corp. / Herbert George Co. (Chicago)** — Birdseye Flash (c. 1954, 127 film, black plastic), Imperial, Flick-N-Flash, Roy Rogers; collectiblend estimates the Birdseye Flash at $5–20.
- **Coronet (Birmingham, UK)** — Coronet Midget (1935, colored Bakelite: blue, green, red, brown, black; $100–400 for colors), Coronet Vogue.
- **Purma (UK)** — Purma Special (1937, Bakelite, gravity shutter, $40–100).
- **Ansco/Agfa** — Ansco Panda, Agfa Clipper, Ansco Memo; mostly $10–40.
- **Others:** Ensign Midget (UK, $100+ for colors), Univex Mercury (US, $40–100), Kodak Jiffy, Sawyer's, Ciro.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Bakelite vs later plastic:** Bakelite (phenolic) is heavy, brittle, smells of formaldehyde when rubbed warm, and is mostly black/brown; post-1950 styrene cameras are lighter and often gray.
- **Color sells:** colored Bakelite or enamel (Beau Brownie two-tone geometric face, Coronet Midget mottled colors, Kodak Petite pastel leatherette) is the premium tell.
- **Completeness:** original box, instructions, flash unit, and case add 50–100% to common cameras; empty boxes of Argoflex 75 sell for ~$10.
- **Lenses:** clean glass vs haze/fungus — matters only to user-buyers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cracked Bakelite (corners, viewfinder housing), missing film spools, stuck shutters, broken winding knobs, peeling leatherette on box cameras, fungus in TLR lenses.
- Shutters on simple cameras are robust; the Argoflex E/EF/EM lens gearing often jams.
- Serviced (CLA) Argoflex E asking ~$195 vs $25–30 untested shows the repair premium — not for Dylan.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Few fakes in this category. Novelty "Bakelite-look" decor cameras and Lomography/Holga plastic cameras (modern, labeled) are not vintage.
- Repainted/recovered cameras sold as original colors — check for overspray in lettering.

## Market
- Very high supply, modest demand. Common cameras sit for weeks; decor buyers and film shooters buy the working ones. Colored Art Deco cameras sell fast to collectors.
- eBay is the main market; Etsy works for décor pieces with nice cases.

## What sellers usually miss
- Colored Art Deco Kodaks and Coronet Midgets are often in "old camera" lots priced like Brownies.
- Small colored vest-pocket Kodaks in fitted cases with compacts (Ensemble/Vanity sets).
- Sellers usually over-price common Falcons, Brownies and Argoflex 75s because they "look antique" — the commonest error.

## Shipping / handling
- Shoebox size, 1–3 lb; $8–15 Ground Advantage. Wrap the lens and Bakelite corners; box-in-box for Bakelite (cracks easily if dropped).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + model (Kodak Beau Brownie, Argus Argoflex E, Coronet Midget), colour if coloured, film size (620/127), "Bakelite"/"Art Deco", years, "tested"/"untested", "with case". Junk: "antique camera", "steampunk decor", "rare" on Hawkeyes. `Kodak Beau Brownie No. 2 Rose Art Deco Teague 1930s Box Camera 120`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (collectors search model names); Etsy for décor pieces with nice cases; common Brownies/Falcons lotted on FB local.
- **Price position:** common at the sold median ($8–25), lotted, cut fast; Argoflex E/EM and Starflash colours at $30–100 median, OBO; coloured Art Deco Kodaks/Coronet at the sold median to p75 ($100–650 by colour), firm 3 weeks then −10%.
- **Photos:** first: faceplate front, straight-on; then lens close-up, back open, inside (spools present), each Bakelite corner and viewfinder housing for cracks, leatherette, case and manuals; video of the shutter firing and winding.
- **State:** model, film size and current availability (620 = respooled 120), years, what was tested (shutter on I/T/B, winding, lens clarity with a flashlight), cracks/stuck shutter/missing spools by photo number, accessories included.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox, 1–3 lb, Ground Advantage $8–15; box-in-box for Bakelite with lens and corners wrapped.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Beau Brownie] in [rose], Walter Dorwin Teague's Art Deco faceplate, shutter firing — the black Brownie No. 2 is a $20 camera; the two-tone coloured ones are the $85–650 pieces."

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 ship to buyer − ~$12 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price).
- With most models selling $10–45, nothing common clears the rule even at $5. Pass on black Bakelite Falcon/Spartus/Birdseye/Imperial, Brownie Hawkeye/Holiday, Argoflex 75/Forty/E.
- Buy only colored Art Deco cameras (Beau Brownie, Coronet Midget colors, Kodak Petite/Vanity, Bantam Special, Ensign Midget) where sold median ≥ (price + ~$75) / 0.87.
- No authentication needed; shutters must at least fire (ask the seller).
