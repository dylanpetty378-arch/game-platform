## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1930s–60s PA, broadcast, ham and CB microphones: die-cast zinc heads with chrome or crinkle paint, swivel yokes, threaded stand mounts, cloth or rubber cables, often Amphenol or screw-type connectors. Shapes identify the model: Shure 55 "Unidyne" (fatboy/ "Elvis" grille), Shure 556/5575 (smaller), EV 950/951 "Cardax", RCA 44 (oblong ribbon box), RCA 77 (capsule), Astatic JT-30 (bullet harmonica mic), Shure 520 Green Bullet, Turner 33/34/99/S33D (teardrop body with angled horizontal slats). Example 7944197405 is a **Turner Model 33 / S33D**-type (Cedar Rapids, IA; late 1940s–50s): sloped grille with horizontal slats, grey crinkle paint heavily flaked, chromed swivel with metal nameplate, long rubber cable.
- Correction/nuance on the Shure 55 nicknames: the large original Model 55 (1939–1952) is the **"Fatboy"**; the smaller **55S** (from 1951) is the one called the **"Elvis"** mic (ProSoundWeb). The head shape is similar; size and base differ. The Fatboy 55B is about 7.7 × 3.2 × 3.5 in and 2.7 lb.
- Connector tells for age: early Shure 55s use a three-pin Amphenol-type connector; later bases have a built-in XLR (ProSoundWeb). Early JT-30s use the 5/8" Amphenol/Switchcraft screw-on "button" connector; later ones XLR (BluesHarmonica).
- Crystal vs dynamic: crystal mics (Rochelle salt) are high-impedance and often dead now; "D" in a Turner/Astatic model name usually marks a dynamic version (general knowledge, verify).
- Look-alikes: decorative chrome "vintage-style" prop mics (Shure 55 and RCA 77 shapes), theatre props built as RCA 44 shells (plastic, hiding a wireless transmitter; VCU prop article), Bakelite telephone mouthpieces on rods, Kellogg candlestick telephone transmitters.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Shure (Chicago)**: 55/55S/556S Unidyne, 5575, 520 Green Bullet, 708A. Model on nameplate on rear.
  - Model 55 chronology (ProSoundWeb): 1939 debut as 55A (35–50 Ω), 55B (200–250 Ω), 55C (high-Z); 1940 broadcast version 555 with isolation mount; 1947 broadcast version renamed 556 and the three impedance types replaced by one model with a rear multi-impedance switch; 1951 smaller 55S with 556S shock-mounted broadcast version; 1952 the large 55 discontinued; late 1970s impedance switch removed (low-Z only). Current production: 55SH Series II (cardioid, on/off switch, black foam) and Super 55 (supercardioid, no switch, blue foam).
  - Grille lining by era: reddish-brown silk (earliest), then blue silk, then black silk, then black or blue foam (newest). Finish: satin chrome standard; gold finishes offered for a time.
- **Electro-Voice (Buchanan MI)**: 630, 664, 950/951 Cardax, 911.
- **RCA**: 44A/BX, 77D/DX ribbons, 74B Junior Velocity — high value; often fakes/props. RCA 44B released 1936 and revised to the 44BX in 1938 (Mix). AEA makes the R44C, a modern re-creation of the 44BX (about $2,000 new at the time of the Mix review).
- **Turner (Cedar Rapids)**: 22, 33, 33D/S33D, 34X, 99, 211; common in ham/PA, moderate value. Founded 1931 by David Turner and Everett Foster; larger factory 1936; about 1,200 employees by the 1970s; sold to Telex 1978, ceased 1979. Model 22 (1940s–50s, crystal and ceramic, brushed nickel, Art Deco "shark fin" top); Challenger CD/BD/CX (1940–48); Model 33 (1940s–50s, crystal or dynamic, large chrome Art Deco body) (Wikipedia).
- **Astatic**: JT-30 bullet, D-104 "lollipop" (CB/ham; $50–150). Original JT-30 crystal elements have mostly failed and are often replaced with Shure CM (controlled magnetic) or CR elements — collectors and harp players want to know which element is fitted (BluesHarmonica).
- **American Microphone Co., Altec 639 "birdcage" (high value)**.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the rear/base nameplate for maker and model (55, 55S, 556S, S33D, JT-30, D-104).
- Size and shape: big Fatboy 55 vs smaller 55S "Elvis"; teardrop slatted Turner; bullet JT-30/520; oblong RCA 44.
- Connector: 3-pin Amphenol or screw-on button (older) vs XLR (later/modern).
- Grille lining colour visible through grille: brown/blue/black silk (older Shure) vs foam (modern).
- Impedance switch on the back of a Shure 55 = 1947–late 1970s.
- Weight and material: heavy die-cast zinc and steel vs light chrome-plated prop.
- Element: original crystal (likely dead), dynamic, or replacement CM/CR in a bullet.
- Paint: original crinkle/chrome vs repaint or heavy flaking.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original nameplates with model numbers, factory crinkle/chrome, matching connector. Old crystal elements (Rochelle salt) often dead; dynamic and ribbon elements survive better. Repainted bodies lower value.
- Prop/replica tells: plastic body, no element, no maker plate, wrong thumbscrew positions (the VCU RCA 44 prop's yoke screws sit lower than the original's), very light weight.
- Shure 55 old vs new: three-pin connector, impedance switch and silk lining = old; built-in XLR, no switch, foam = newer (ProSoundWeb).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dead crystal element, torn ribbon (RCA), dented grille, flaking paint (example), stripped swivel, replaced cable/connector. Untested electronics rule applies; dynamic mics can be tested with an ohmmeter only by a buyer with gear.
- Element swaps in bullets are normal and often preferred by players, but must be disclosed.
- Grille dents on Shure 55s are hard to repair invisibly; replated chrome looks too bright and smooth.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Decorative "vintage-style" chrome mics (Chinese prop microphones shaped like Shure 55 or RCA 77) sell $15–40; Shure's current 55SH Series II is a modern reissue ($100). Check for real nameplate, weight, and connector.
- Super 55 (blue foam, supercardioid) is a current model, not vintage.
- AEA R44C is a legitimate modern re-creation of the RCA 44BX; theatre/prop RCA 44 shells exist.

## Market
Active: decor, harmonica players (Astatic/Shure bullets), hams and vintage-PA collectors. Clean Shure 55s sell in days.

## What sellers usually miss
Harmonica players pay up for Shure 520/Green Bullet and Astatic JT-30 bullet mics; Altec 639 and RCA ribbons occasionally appear at estate prices; Shure 55 variants are often mislabeled "Elvis mic, decor".
- Large-body Fatboy 55 (1939–52) vs common 55S — size and the A/B/C suffix matter.
- Coloured anodised JT-30 volume-control bodies and original connectors.

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb; small box ~$10–14; pad the grille.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model number from plate, cable/connector type, whether tested (state "untested" honestly), paint condition, mount thread.

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker and model from the nameplate and the era it implies (e.g. "Turner Model S33D dynamic, Turner Co., Cedar Rapids, Iowa, 1940s–50s; Turner operated 1931–1979"; "Shure 55S 'Elvis' Unidyne, smaller version introduced 1951, with rear impedance switch"). State the element type and whether it is original, connector type, cable, mount thread, grille-lining colour, and paint/chrome condition with flaws pictured. Say "untested" unless it has been checked through an amp, and never present a prop or re-creation as vintage.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $12 − $12 − price ≥ max($50, price). A $30 Turner selling ~$70 nets ~$7 → pass. Only distinctive high-tier shapes (Shure 55, Altec birdcage, RCA ribbon, EV 950) under $60 qualify; Dylan should treat them as "untested electronics" unless the buyer market accepts "untested/as-is" (collectors often do).
