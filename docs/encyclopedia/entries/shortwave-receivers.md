## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tabletop or portable radios with a frequency display and bands beyond AM/FM (LW, SW, SSB). Models to recognise:
- **Radio Shack DX-394/DX-394B**: 1990s tabletop, black, "LSB/USB/CW" on the front. $150–250 working.
- **DX-302/DX-160** (1970s–80s): $80–150.
- **Sony ICF-2010 / ICF-SW7600GR / ICF-SW77**: $150–400. The 2010 is the cult portable.
- **Grundig Satellit 700/800**: $250–500.
- **Yaesu FRG-7/FRG-100**, **Kenwood R-1000/R-2000**, **Drake R8/R8B** ($500–900).
- **Hallicrafters S-38/SX- series** tube radios: collector $80–400; the SX-28/SX-42 bring more.

Sellers write "shortwave radio" or "ham radio receiver". Look-alikes: cheap multiband "world receiver" portables with analog dials (Coby, Kaito); a BFO or SSB switch is the tell for a real communications set (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Radio Shack / Realistic**: DX-394 is the desirable tabletop. The DX-375 portable is cheaper. DX-302 and DX-160 are older and $80–150.
- **Sony**: ICF-2010 (also sold as ICF-2001D outside the US) is the cult portable, $250–400 working. SW7600GR and SW77 are $150–400 (general knowledge on 2001D, verify).
- **Grundig**: Satellit 800 ($300–500), Satellit 700 ($250–500), YB400 ($100–250).
- **Yaesu FRG-7/FRG-100, Kenwood R-1000/R-2000**: solid-state communications receivers, $250–500.
- **Drake R8/R8B, AOR AR7030, Icom IC-R75/R8500, Collins 51S-1**: top tier, $500–1,500.
- **Hallicrafters**: S-38 is common ($15–60 untested). SX-28/SX-42 are the collector models.
- Generic: Kaito, Tecsun, Coby, $15–60.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Quality sets have a solid metal or heavy plastic cabinet, a smooth tuning knob with weighted flywheel feel, a digital readout to 1 kHz or better, SSB/BFO and filter switches, and an external antenna jack.
- On tube sets, original knobs, an intact dial glass, and no butchered wiring matter.
- The serial and model plate is on the rear or bottom.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- "Works some" means a fault (capacitors, display), and the electronics rule says pass.
- The Sony ICF-2010 is known to die from leaking batteries, so check the battery bay.
- Dim or dead LCDs, missing knobs, scratchy controls and dead bands are common.
- Tube sets usually need a recap. A recapped set is a plus if documented.
- Ask for a video tuned to a station on SW and SSB, and photos of the battery bay and the back.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No meaningful fakes. Rebadged clones exist: Grundig-branded radios were often Tecsun-made, and Radio Shack sets were made by others (general knowledge, verify). Grundig Satellit models were reissued with similar names, so check the exact model.

## Market
SWL hobbyists, hams and preppers buy them. eBay is best. eHam and QRZ classifieds suit ham-grade gear. Working, known models sell within weeks. Winter is stronger, because propagation and indoor hobby season raise interest (general knowledge, verify). There is a steady, niche, ageing buyer base.

## What sellers usually miss
Model desirability (DX-394 vs the cheaper DX-375 portable). "Works some" means a fault (capacitors, display), and the electronics rule says pass.

## Shipping / handling
DX-394 ~8 lb, $20–25. The Sony 2010 is a portable; check the battery bay for leakage damage. eBay 13%. Remove batteries before shipping, and double-box tube sets.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: "[Brand] [Model] Shortwave Communications Receiver SSB Tested Working". State the bands and modes tested, display condition, included accessories (antenna, power supply, manual), and any known issues. Photograph the front panel lit, the back and the serial, and the battery bay on portables. A short video tuning a station helps.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a fully working DX-394 is under $70, a working ICF-2010 is under $120, or a Satellit 800 is under $150. Pass when: "works some", untested, or missing knobs. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
