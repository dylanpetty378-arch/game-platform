## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Natural sandstone or bonded abrasive wheels on an iron axle. Floor-standing farm grindstones (wooden or cast-iron frame, 12-30" wheel, foot treadle and/or crank, water trough) were standard on 1880-1940 farms. Bench hand-crank grinders clamp to a table with gear housings. The Luther "Hummer" seated grinder has a bicycle-style seat and pedals.

## Makers, brands and variants
Luther Grinder Mfg (Milwaukee, seated/pedal "Dimo-Grit"), Keen Kutter (Simmons Hardware, emblem castings), Sears "Craftsman"/"Dunlap", Red Devil, Montgomery Ward, Bonney, Hubbard. Many farm grindstones are homemade frames with a purchased Ohio sandstone wheel.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast iron with embossed maker names and patent dates is the value driver; original paint/pinstriping adds value. Sandstone wheels crack and are heavy (a 12" x 2" stone ~20 lb, a 24" ~80+ lb). Modern bonded aluminum-oxide wheels indicate later replacements.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked or out-of-round stone, seized axle bearings, missing treadle linkage, rotten wood frames. A cracked stone can explode under power - don't motorize.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little faking. Cast-iron "Keen Kutter" decor reproductions exist in small items.

## Market
Local/farm-decor market; eBay sales limited by freight. Slow.

## What sellers usually miss
Little - prices are local and fair. Marked Keen Kutter bench grinders sometimes underpriced.

## Shipping / handling
Treadle grindstones: freight or local only, 50-150 lb. Bench grinders 8-15 lb, $20-30.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker castings, wheel diameter and condition (no cracks), local pickup terms.

## Buy rule
Floor/treadle units fail the passenger-seat and shipping rules - always pass. Bench hand-crank grinders: buy only marked brand units ≤ $25 with resale ≥ $100.
