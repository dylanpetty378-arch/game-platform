## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Containers filled with hot water, coals or a heated stone to warm beds and feet, 1800s–1940s. Example 7928977771: two oval galvanized-zinc/steel hot-water bottles with a brass screw cap and wire bail, central seam, mottled zinc patina — common European (German/Dutch/English) and American type, c. 1900–1940; seller titled them "canteens".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Zinc/galvanized ovals**: mostly unmarked; some German "Bettflasche" stamped makers, English "Rippingille", American "Lindsay". Common.
- **Stoneware "pigs"**: Henderson Foot Warmer (Dorchester Pottery, Boston; marked "Henderson's Foot Warmer"), Logan "The Ideal"/ "Uhl", Bristol-glazed; English Doulton/Price hot-water bottles with stamped names.
- **Pierced-tin foot stoves**: wooden mortise frame with punched tin box and coal pan, 1780–1860; New England. Paired punched designs (hearts, circles).
- **Brass/copper warming pans**: long wooden handle, engraved lid; many 20th-c. reproductions.
- **Soapstone warmers** with bail; **Clark heater** carriage warmers (coal-fueled, marked).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Age: hand-cut dovetails/pegs and square nails on foot stoves; heavy stoneware with impressed maker mark; brass caps with makers' names. Cheap/late: thin spun copper pans with machine-stamped decoration (1950s–70s decorative repros).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Leaks/rust-through on zinc, missing caps or gaskets, chipped stoneware spouts, cracked pigs, replaced handles on warming pans, broken wood frames.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Decorative warming pans widely reproduced 1920s–70s; foot stoves reproduced as "primitives" with modern punched tin and nails. Zinc bottles not usually faked (too cheap).

## What sellers usually miss
Marked stoneware pigs and early foot stoves get lumped in as "old jugs/boxes" and are priced low.

## Shipping / handling
Zinc ovals 1.5–3 lb each, bulky; $12–16 each. Stoneware needs heavy padding.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
