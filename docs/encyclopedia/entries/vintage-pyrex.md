## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Opal (milk) glass bakeware and mixing bowls with fired-on colour and patterns, 1936–1980s. Tells:
- **Logo**: embossed "PYREX" in capitals (often with "Corning N.Y.", "Made in U.S.A.", shape number such as 024, 444, 473, 475-B) on the base. Lowercase "pyrex" = post-1998 soda-lime glass, not vintage.
- **Shapes**: nesting mixing bowls (401–404), Cinderella bowls with spout tabs (441–444), round casseroles (471–475), oval casseroles (043, 045), refrigerator dishes (501–503), Hostess/Space Saver casseroles, divided dishes (1063), butter dishes, carafes.
- **Colour**: fired-on colour exterior with white interior (most) or white with printed pattern (later).
- **Promotional/limited patterns**: often sold as a single casserole in a cradle or with a lid as a give-away/limited release (e.g., Lucky in Love 1959, Atomic Eye/Atomic Stars, Golden Hearts, Starburst, Balloons, Bluebelle, Golden Honeysuckle, Golden Branch). Example 7981314114 (brown branch-and-blossom on almond, round tab-handled casserole) is a 1970s–80s pattern of this kind – identify the exact pattern by number on the base.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Primary colours** (1945–): red, yellow, green, blue mixing bowls; a full 4-bowl set $60–150; rare reverse colours higher.
- **Pink Daisy, Butterprint (Amish), Gooseberry, Snowflake, Pink/Turquoise Butterprint, Town & Country, Daisy** (1950s–60s) – $25–150 per piece.
- **Spring Blossom Green ("Crazy Daisy", 1972–79)**, Butterfly Gold (1972–79), Old Orchard, Harvest, Woodland, Forest Fancies, Verde, Autumn Harvest (1970s–80s) – $8–40 a piece; sets of nested casseroles $35–80 (example 7974315102).
- **Clear glass**: Pyrex Flameware (blue tint, 1936–79) coffee percolators $30–100; gold-band carafes (1960s) $15–35 (example 7952142584).
- **Corning Ware** (Pyroceram, 1958–): Blue Cornflower common; Spice of Life, Floral Bouquet, rare promos.
- **JAJ Pyrex** (UK) and **Agee Pyrex** (Australia) – own patterns; UK/AU collector demand.
- **Rare**: Lucky in Love (1959 shamrocks and hearts, $500–4,000), Pink Stems, Black Tulip promos, Turquoise Diamonds, Golden Hearts, Butterprint in rare colours, Hex Signs tall bowls.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bright, glossy colour = good; dishwasher-damaged ("DWD") pieces look chalky, faded and dull – value falls 60–80%. Pattern wear, utensil marks (grey lines inside), scratches and "crazing" lines (example 7981314114 shows lines – verify whether they are surface marks or cracks). Lids must match the shape number.

## Condition, damage and repairs
DWD, utensil marks, chips on rims and tabs, cracks (fatal), stains, faded gold trim, sticker residue, mismatched lids. No repairs possible; "Bar Keepers Friend" cleaning removes marks but not dulling.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Post-1998 "pyrex" reissues of vintage patterns (2016 Pyrex "Vintage Charm" line by World Kitchen/Corelle: Pink Daisy, Butterprint etc. reproductions, marked with modern lowercase logo). Fake "promotional" patterns created by decal over Pyrex blanks (rare, but exists). "Limited edition" in a listing title is often seller marketing.

## Market
Big, active collector market (Facebook "Pyrex Collectors" groups), but prices peaked ~2016–2020 and have softened; common 1970s patterns oversupplied. No seasonality (slight holiday bump).

## What sellers usually miss
Rare promotional patterns sold in "vintage casserole" lots; matching original lids and cradles. Sellers usually over-estimate 1970s patterns and DWD pieces, and call ordinary 1970s patterns "limited edition".

## Shipping / handling
Heavy and fragile: a 2.5-qt casserole with lid 5–6 lb boxed, $18–25; nested sets $25–40. Double-box. Shipping kills margins on anything under $60.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give pattern name, shape number, capacity, lid number, colour condition (state "no DWD" only if true), photos in daylight of interior and base.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$20) − ship to Dylan (~$20) − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs ~$105+ median. Buy only scarce 1950s–60s promotional patterns, complete primary/rare colour sets, or rare promos, glossy and undamaged, at ≤ 1/3 of median. Pass 1970s patterns, carafes, DWD, chipped or cracked pieces, post-1998 "pyrex".
