## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Anything a brewery made to advertise. Value depends on age, brand (regional and defunct beat national), graphics, and rarity.
- **Coaster sets / bar-ware:** 1970s plastic giveaways, like a bottle-shaped holder of six cork coasters (the example, Schlitz).
- **Signs:** tin, TOC, reverse-painted glass, lighted (plastic bubble, "motion" signs with rotating scenes like Hamm's bear).
- **Trays:** lithographed tin, pre-Prohibition (before 1920) trays are prized.
- **Tap knobs:** 1930s–50s enamel "ball knobs" are a strong collectible.
- **Cans:** cone-top and flat-top cans (1935–60s).
- **Dating clues in a photo:** "Pre-Prohibition" items (before 1920) often name the brewery company and city rather than a brand slogan, with ornate lithography; post-1933 items may carry "Internal Revenue Tax Paid" wording (cans/bottles, 1935–c.1950, general knowledge, verify); 1960s–70s items are plastic, vacuum-formed, with a cord and a UL tag; "Made in China", modern URLs or "Retro"/"Vintage style" = reproduction.
- **Tap knob shapes:** round "ball knob" (1930s–40s Bakelite/Catalin with metallic or ceramic insert), metallic-finish knobs (late 1940s–mid-1950s), larger "tin can" style and Kooler-Keg Sidewinder markers (early 1950s), plastic (mid-1950s on), acrylic/Lucite (early 1960s), then tall slim plastic handles (mid-late 1960s) (bavarianbrewery.org).

## Makers, brands and variants
National: Schlitz, Pabst, Budweiser, Miller, Hamm's, Olympia, Falstaff, Blatz, Old Style. Regional/defunct names multiply value (Grain Belt, Lucky Lager, Rainier, Iron City, Stroh's earlier, etc.). Sign makers: Price Brothers, Everbrite, Lackner (Cincinnati; tin-over-cardboard and celluloid signs — an earlier version of this entry said motion signs, probably wrong), American Art Works (trays).
- Lackner Co. attribution is general knowledge, verify. Other TOC/litho makers: Kaufmann & Strauss (New York). Tap knob maker named on the back: Fox Co., Cincinnati (metallic-finish knobs, late 1940s–mid-1950s); about a dozen makers produced Bakelite ball knobs 1930s–40s.
- **Hamm's motion signs (most collected lighted/motion line):** large scenorama (about 5 ft, excluded by size rule), standard scenorama (campsite, canoe, campfire smoke; round-logo version rarer), 1956 "TV box" rippler (grey cabinet), 1965 100th-anniversary rippler, "starry skies" (paper small and plastic large), sunrise/sunset (gold-goblet and clock versions), "flipper barrel" (changes scenes every 5–7 seconds), billboard and ice-skater bear signs (fragile bases), tuber bear, helicopter/parachute bear (reportedly about 100 made) (Collectors Weekly).
- **What multiplies value:** defunct regional brewery name, pre-Prohibition date, TOC or reverse-on-glass construction, working motion, ball knobs with intact inserts, cone-top cans in good paint, and rare variants (round-logo, limited runs).

### Quick ID checklist
- Brewery name and city readable? Defunct regional brands beat national ones.
- Maker credit line at the bottom edge (e.g. lithographer, Lackner, Kaufmann & Strauss, American Art Works, Price Brothers, Everbrite)? Photograph it.
- Material: tin, tin-over-cardboard (metal face with cardboard back and easel), glass, porcelain (magnet sticks to steel; aluminium = later or fake), vacuum-formed plastic.
- Back photo: old darkened cardboard/brown-black rust vs bright orange rust or clean new metal.
- Lighted/motion: original cord, label, working light and motion (rollers and motor inside)? Any cracked plastic edges or bases?
- Tap knob: Bakelite/Catalin ball with insert intact; maker name on the back (e.g. Fox Co.)?
- Words "Retro", "Vintage style", "Man cave", or a modern UPC = reproduction.
- Wear pattern: uneven fading (red fades first), scratches where it hung or was handled.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original paint, no fading, and legible maker marks. Lighted signs must work. Pre-1960 items often carry maker credit lines.
- Lithographed tin: fine registration, layered colours, rolled edges; modern repro tin is thin, has crisp digital printing, and often pre-punched corner holes with no wear around them.
- Porcelain (rarer in beer than in oil/soda): layered enamel visible in chips; chips spread naturally, not neatly on edges; steel base.
- Plastic lighted signs: vacuum-formed faces, metal or woodgrain-plastic frames; 1960s–70s.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fading, rust, scratches, cracked plastic, non-working lights or motion. Repaints and reproductions reduce value heavily.
- Motion signs: worn rollers and motors, water-damaged inner scenes, cracked woodgrain edges (1965 rippler), cracked bases (billboard and skater bears).
- Tap knobs: chipped Bakelite, missing or faded inserts, stripped threads.
- TOC signs: bent corners, cardboard separation, missing easel.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction tin signs ("Ande Rooney" and many others) are common and often sold as vintage. Reproduction trays exist. Breweries reissue retro designs.
- Fake signs show deliberate edge-only chips, orange-red rust, new-looking rivets, too-shiny red lettering, and no aluminium-vs-steel logic (aluminium signs are post-1950s); ask for a back photo (antiqueadvertisingexpert.com).
- Brewery-issued reissue trays and signs (often dated or marked with a modern copyright) are legitimate but low value.

## Market
Strong organised collector base (NABA, BCCA) for good pieces; common promos glut.
- Very large motion signs sell best locally (shipping risk). Collectors buy by brewery and by state; regional Facebook groups and breweriana shows are active.

## What sellers usually miss
Regional brewery names unknown to the seller, and old ball tap knobs in mixed bar lots.
- The maker credit line and the specific Hamm's sign variant (round logo, clock version, anniversary rippler).

## Shipping / handling
Coaster sets: light, $8–10. Signs: oversize, expensive.
- Motion signs: remove/secure moving parts, double-box, insure; plastic faces crack in cold.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, era, maker credit, dimensions, condition, working status for electricals.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the brewery, city and brand as printed, the item type and material (e.g. "tin-over-cardboard easel-back sign", "vacuum-formed plastic lighted sign"), the maker credit line if present, and the dimensions. For electricals state exactly what was tested (lights, motion, how long run) and whether the cord is original. Describe fading, scratches, rust and cracks plainly with photos of front and back. Give the era from evidence on the piece (slogan, maker, materials, tax wording) rather than guessing; do not call a piece pre-Prohibition unless it is clearly before 1920.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − shipping each way − price ≥ max($50, price). Common coaster sets never qualify. No untested lighted signs. Must fit in a passenger seat.
