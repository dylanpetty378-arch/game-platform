## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Teaching charts. Value tells: cloth (linen/canvas) backing, wooden or metal roller and header bar, publisher name (Denoyer-Geppert Chicago, A.J. Nystrom, Jung-Koch-Quentell, Hagemann), chromolithograph colour, series number. Paper posters (offset printed, glossy, copyright 1970s–2000s) are low value. The Iokai Shiatsu Center (Tokyo) "Shiatsu Meridian Chart / 経絡指圧診断治療要図" by Shizuto Masunaga ©1970 was sold to students for decades, so many survive.
Formats: (1) **pull-down charts**: printed paper laminated to cloth, wooden top batten and bottom dowel, sometimes a spring roller in a metal case with a pull ring; school sizes ~33×44 in to ~46×72 in (Frohse 62.5×41.5 in; D-G 1965 charts 46×72 in). (2) **Hanging wall charts**: cloth-backed with wooden battens top and bottom, no spring (the German and Dutch "Lehrtafel"/"schoolplaat" format). (3) **Paper posters and card charts**: shiatsu and acupuncture charts, pharma posters, framed prints.
Where marks sit: publisher and plate number in the bottom margin or lower corner ("Denoyer-Geppert Co., Chicago", "A.J. Nystrom & Co. Chicago", "Verlag Jung-Koch-Quentell" or "Hagemann Düsseldorf"); D-G anatomy series carry a code (e.g. "KL7"), editor ("Kampmeier") and artist ("Lariviere"); Frohse charts give the plate number and "Frohse" with Brödel's name; copyright year in the margin.
Look-alikes: new digital prints on canvas sold as "vintage school chart"; framed book plates (cut from anatomy atlases, small); laminated 1990s–2000s classroom posters (Nystrom and D-G still publish).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Denoyer-Geppert Co., Chicago (1916–)**: founded by L. Philip Denoyer and Otto E. Geppert, first for maps and globes. The 1940s **Anatomy Series** was edited by Otto F. Kampmeier (U. of Illinois College of Medicine) and painted by Pauline M. Lariviere (1906–1988, trained at Johns Hopkins under Max Brödel); charts are coded "KL" + number (KL7 Lymphatic, KL9 Digestive). D-G also made biology charts (One-Celled Animals, 1940s) and 1960s anatomy pull-downs on steel spring rollers. Rand McNally owned it and sold it on in 1985.
- **A.J. Nystrom & Co., Chicago**: publisher of the American **Frohse** anatomical charts: the series begun by German anatomist Fritz Frohse, with drawings by Max Brödel (the father of American medical illustration), plates numbered (No. 4 Circulatory 1918, No. 5 Ear & Eye 1918, No. 8 Digestive 1947), heavy canvas-backed paper, wood bar and spring roller, green canvas edge trim. The most decorative and collected American anatomy chart.
- **Jung-Koch-Quentell (JKQ), Germany**: the famous botanical and zoological teaching charts (Heinrich Jung, Friedrich Quentell and Gottlieb von Koch), printed through the 1950s–80s and published via Hagemann (Düsseldorf); canvas, wood and paper, ~44×32 in. Mostly natural history; anatomy charts exist.
- **Hagemann (Lehrmittelverlag, Düsseldorf)**: German school-chart publisher (JKQ and others).
- **Swedish, Dutch and other European school charts**; **J.F. Schreiber (Esslingen)**: 1880s–1950s; often lithographed.
- **Iokai Shiatsu Center (Tokyo)**: Shizuto Masunaga (1925–1981) meridian chart ©1970; reprinted for students.
- **Chinese acupuncture charts**: Foreign Languages Press, People's Medical Publishing House (1970s); sets of three (front, back, side).
- **Pharma posters**: Ciba (Frank Netter art); chiropractic and pharma company giveaways.

### Quick ID checklist
- Backing: cloth visible at the edges or back (good) vs paper only (low).
- Hardware: wooden top batten and bottom dowel, or a spring roller in a metal case with pull ring.
- Publisher line in the bottom margin: Denoyer-Geppert, A.J. Nystrom, Frohse, JKQ, Hagemann, Schreiber.
- Series code and credits: "KL"-number, Kampmeier/Lariviere, Frohse plate number, Brödel.
- Printing: under a loupe or zoomed photo, solid colour (litho) vs a rosette of halftone dots (offset).
- Copyright date: pre-1950 better; 1970s+ laminated plastic = classroom modern.
- Size: give height and width; anything over ~5 ft long on a roller needs a car check.
- Shiatsu chart: "Iokai Shiatsu Center", Masunaga, ©1970, printed on card = common.

### Golden-egg tells
- A.J. Nystrom Frohse chart with Max Brödel drawings, pre-1950 plate — the top-of-ladder American chart; est. 3–5× a 1970s laminated classroom chart (verify; 1stdibs listings show no prices) — "Frohse" and a plate number in the margin, green canvas edging, spring roller.
- Denoyer-Geppert 1940s Anatomy Series (KL codes, Kampmeier/Lariviere) — est. 2–3× a 1960s–70s D-G chart (verify) — "KL" code and "Kampmeier" in the margin, oil-painted look, cloth on wooden dowels.
- Sets of charts from one series (e.g. a set of 6 Frohse charts on 1stdibs) — sell as sets at more than the sum of singles (est., verify) — several matching rollers in one photo.
- 19th-c. lithographed European anatomy or medicine teaching charts (Swedish 1880s, Schreiber) — the existing $200–800 top tier vs $10–30 for reprints — no halftone dots, aged linen, Gothic or Swedish captions.
- Jung-Koch-Quentell canvas charts, especially striking subjects (snakes, spiders, frogs) — decorator favourite, listed on 1stdibs; est. 2–4× a generic paper poster (verify) — "Jung-Koch-Quentell" or "Hagemann" in the margin, canvas with wood battens.
- 1970s Iokai Masunaga shiatsu chart or Chinese acupuncture paper chart — the common version, $20–60 — card or paper, no backing.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cloth-backed > laminated board > paper. Lithographed (dot-free colour) older; halftone dots = offset. More: old charts are paper mounted on linen or canvas with glue, so the paper crazes and cracks in a grid where it was rolled; edges are bound with cloth tape (green on Frohse); the wood is varnished hardwood with old screws or tacks. Spring rollers are steel tubes in a painted metal case. Newer charts (1970s+) are plastic-laminated paper on a vinyl or plastic roller, with bright even colours and a copyright line in sans-serif type. Modern repros are giclée on canvas with stretcher bars or new raw wood dowels and no crazing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tears, flaking paint on cloth charts, water damage, broken rollers. Also: flaking along roll lines, brown staining along the bottom where the paper meets the cloth trim (seen on the 1947 Frohse), edge chipping, dead springs, missing pull rings and brackets, graffiti from students, tape repairs. Ask for a photo of the full unrolled chart, the margin with publisher text and the back.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Many reprint posters of vintage anatomical art; check for halftone dots and modern paper. Also: canvas giclée prints of JKQ and Frohse images sold as "vintage school chart"; publishers' own later reissues (Nystrom and D-G charts stayed in print for decades with the same images, so check the copyright date on the chart, not the image).

## Market
Decor/oddity buyers; moderate speed. Also: medical professionals (office decor), designers (1stdibs / Chairish for large canvas charts), and natural-history collectors for JKQ.

## What sellers usually miss
Cloth pull-down charts from school clear-outs. Also the publisher line and plate or series number (Frohse, KL codes), which is what separates a 1940s chart from a 1970s one with the same picture.

## Shipping / handling
Paper: tube $8–12. Pull-down charts 4–5 ft long — may exceed passenger seat when on roller. Long roller charts need a 5–6 ft box; roll image-side out to reduce cracking.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Publisher, date, size, backing, condition photos. Photograph the publisher line, plate number and copyright date close up; show the hardware; say whether the roller spring works.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Publisher] anatomical teaching chart, [title], plate/series [No.], [date per margin]. Printed paper mounted on [linen/canvas] with [wooden battens / spring roller]. Image [W × H] in, overall [W × H] in. Original classroom chart, not a reprint — [lithographed colour / publisher line in margin]. Condition: [roll-line crazing, stains, tears]. Ships rolled in a tube [or: local pickup due to roller length]."
