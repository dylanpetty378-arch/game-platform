## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Slip-cast red-clay earthenware with the signature green-black (or blue-black) "reduction drip" glaze in streaky vertical runs. The glazes are layered and flow in a high firing, so no two pieces match.
- **Common shapes**: stylised animals, swans, fish, tall bottle vases, jugs, and cream and sugar sets.
- **Marks**: impressed or moulded "BMP Canada" (a "BMP" logo) on the underside, or a paper label/hang tag (later "The Blue Mountain Collection, Collingwood, Ontario"). Many pieces lost their labels and are unmarked, so identification is then by glaze, form and red-clay base.
- **In a photo**: tall elongated swans with S-necks, leaping dolphins on waves, long-neck cats, ewers and bottle vases, all with a vertical streaked glossy glaze, green over black being the classic.
- **Title words**: "BMP", "Blue Mountain", "drip glaze", "Canadian pottery", "mid-century", "MCM", "swan", "dolphin".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **History**: founded in 1953 in Collingwood as an offshoot of Jozo Weider's Blue Mountain ski hill. The first entry said "c. 1947"; Wikipedia and the ROM both say early 1950s/1953.
- **People**:
  - Co-founders Denis (Dennis) Tupy, who ran the moulds and designed many shapes until 1960, and Mirek Hambalek, chief glazer. Both, like Weider, were Czechoslovak immigrants.
  - Dominic Stanzione was studio potter in the 1960s, making large wheel-thrown pieces.
  - David Bennett was master mould maker and designer from 1982. Robert Blair was manager from 1971 and the last president.
- **Ownership**: Weider sold to International Silver Company of Canada in 1968. Blair bought the assets and moulds in 1987 when International Silver went into receivership. The company closed on December 31, 2004 (the ROM says it operated until 2005).
- **Glazes**: classic green drip (deep blue-green over dark grey/black, said to evoke the evergreens on the Blue Mountains) is the commonest. Others include cobalt blue, mocha/brown, harvest gold, pewter, red/orange, white and "Canadiana".
- Late "Heritage"/"Blue Mountain Collection" pieces have printed tags.
- Commemoratives: the 1967 Canadian Centennial mug.

### Quick ID checklist
- Glaze: vertical streaky drip, glossy. Note green/black (common) vs cobalt, red, harvest gold, white or pewter (less common).
- Base: red clay showing at the foot rim, and a "BMP Canada" mark or a remnant label.
- Form: an animal sculpture (swan, dolphin, cat, fish, horse, rooster, bear or elephant) or a tall vase, vs small dinnerware.
- Height: get a measurement. Large (12 in+) figures and vases are the value pieces.
- Hand-thrown vs moulded: throwing rings and an incised signature suggest a Stanzione-era studio piece.
- Tags: "Blue Mountain Collection" printed tags mean late (1980s–2000s) production.
- Damage: ears, tails, beaks, dolphin fins and swan necks are the chip and break points.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Noah's Ark set (ark plus 13 figures on wooden stands)**: a complete set reportedly sold for $7,000 (Wikipedia, before 2014), hundreds of times a creamer. Photo tell: small paired animals on wooden plinths, or a single animal with "Noah's Ark" in the title (singles are sold as such).
- **Large dolphins on waves and tall swans (12–20 in)**: about 3–8× a small piece (mid/upper tier $30–100 to $80–250, general knowledge, verify). Photo tell: height next to a known object; dolphin arched over a wave base.
- **Non-green glazes (cobalt, red/orange, harvest gold, white, pewter)**: cobalt sold at a median of $26.50 vs $17 for the generic query (Terapeak, below), about 1.5× on small pieces and more on big forms. Photo tell: a blue-black or orange-red drip instead of green.
- **Stanzione-era wheel-thrown studio pieces (1960s)**: a premium over moulded production (no $ figure cited; verify). Photo tell: throwing lines, an irregular rim, an incised signature or initials on the base.
- **1967 Centennial mug and other dated commemoratives**: crossover Canadiana demand (no $ figure cited). Photo tell: the Centennial maple-leaf "C" symbol and "1867–1967".
- **Early (1950s–60s) pieces with a moulded BMP mark** over late printed-tag "Blue Mountain Collection": collectors prefer early (verify). Photo tell: an embossed mark vs a sticker or tag only.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Body**: lightweight red earthenware (Georgian Bay-area clays were tried early). Red clay showing at the foot distinguishes it from the white or buff bodies of many imitators.
- **Glaze**: glossy layered drip. Better pieces have deep, striking streaks; later pieces are thinner and smaller.
- **Old vs new**: early pieces are heavier, with crisp mould detail and the moulded BMP mark. Later production uses tags and lighter casting.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips on the ears and tails of animals, rim chips, crazing and glaze bubbles.
- Repaired swan necks and dolphin fins show as a glue line or colour-matched paint that lacks the glaze's gloss and streak.
- Ask for close-ups of every protrusion and the base.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked. Other Canadian potteries (Canuck, Evangeline, Royal Canadian Art Pottery) and Japanese imports have similar drip glazes. Japanese pieces usually have a white or buff body and a foil "Japan" sticker. Unmarked drip-glaze figures are often attributed to BMP optimistically, so check the clay colour and form against known BMP shapes.

## Market
Strong in Canada and moderate in the US; many listings; slow for common items. Mid-century-modern decorators buy the large swans and dolphins.

## What sellers usually miss
Large animals and rare glazes at estate sales; Noah's Ark singles; Stanzione thrown pieces.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile but small; $10–15. Large figures need a double box, with necks and fins individually padded.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Glaze, mark/tag, dimensions, condition.
### What to tell the buyer
"Blue Mountain Pottery, Collingwood, Ontario, Canada (1953–2004)." Give the glaze colour by name (classic green-black drip, cobalt, harvest gold, etc.), the height and width, and the mark exactly ("BMP Canada" moulded, paper label or hang tag, or unmarked). State that the drip glaze is unique to each piece. Note any chips, crazing or repairs at the ears, tails, necks and rims. For a Noah's Ark animal, say which animal and whether the wooden stand is present.

## Buy rule
Cream/sugar pair (~$25–35 sold) fails; pass. Buy large figures/rare glaze at ≤ $25 when median ≥ $100.
