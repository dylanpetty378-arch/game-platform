## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tin-plated steel containers sold full of product. Dating tells visible in photos:
- **Paper label on plain tin** (example 7935128376) – 1880s–1920s bulk/lard pails or later home use; worthless without a readable label.
- **Stencilled/japanned (painted) tin** 1860s–1890s; **offset lithography** on tin from the 1870s–80s, mass after 1895. Pre-1900 litho has slightly muted colours and fine detail; post-1960 litho is bright, photographic, with a UPC barcode (post-1974), nutrition facts box (post-1993), or "©" date and website (post-1995).
- **Construction**: soldered side seams and hinged lids with wire pins = old; lock-seamed, rolled-edge, slip-lid cookie tins with printed "Made in England/Hong Kong/China" = 1960s+.
- **Maker imprint** along the bottom edge: "Somers Bros. Brooklyn" (1869–1901), "Ginna & Co. NY" (1874–1901), "Hasker & Marcuse Richmond VA" (1870s–1900s), "American Can Co." (1901+), "Tindeco Baltimore" (1914–1940s), "Heekin Can Cincinnati", "Hudson Scott Carlisle" (British), "Huntley Boorne & Stevens Reading" (H&P biscuit tins), "Barringer Wallis & Manners Mansfield". A maker imprint plus graphic brand = real.
- **Nostalgia reproductions** (1970s–2000s): "Cracker Jack", "Hungry Jack", "Farmer's Almanac", "Shamrock Tea", "Pure Made Cocoa", "Gold Roast Coffee", "Pilgrim Brand Corn Syrup" – faux-antique labels, often with a © date, sold at Cracker Barrel, craft stores and as candy/cookie gift tins (example 7969524921 is a whole shelf of these).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Pocket tobacco (vertical, flip lid)**: Prince Albert (common, $5–15), Velvet, Union Leader (common) vs. Dixie Queen, Hiawatha, Forest & Stream (fisherman), Bagdad, Lucky Strike roly flat 1910s, Big Ben, Buckingham "Pinkie", Hi-Plane, Tiger, Taxi Cab, Tuxedo early – $50–2,000+.
- **Coffee (1 lb keywind or pry-lid, 1900–40)**: Folgers early, Hills Bros. early, Maxwell House common; regional brands with images (Mammy/Black-figure graphics are restricted on eBay – see below), Jersey, Lipton, Chase & Sanborn, Seal Brand, Bokar, Blue Banner. 3–5 lb store bins $100–600.
- **Tea**: Lipton, Tetley, Bushells (Australia, Sydney 1883–; 1953 coronation 1-lb tin with crossed Union/Australian flags and "Elizabeth R" shield, example 7933954228), Mazawattee, Ridgways.
- **Biscuit (British)**: Huntley & Palmers (figural "novelty" tins – books, clocks, vans, lanterns 1890s–1939), Macfarlane Lang, Jacob's, Carr's, Crawford's, Peek Frean. Novelty shapes $150–2,000+.
- **Toffee/candy (British)**: George W. Horner (Chester-le-Street, Co. Durham, "Dainty Dinah" brand, 1910–1960s; embossed crinoline-lady and Pekingese tins very common – example 7954927540), Mackintosh's, Sharp's, Edward Sharp & Sons, Thorne's, Riley's. Mostly $8–40; figural (bus, house, car) $60–300.
- **Spice/baking**: early Calumet, Clabber Girl, KC; spice tins with litho scenes $10–60; full store spice racks $100+.
- **Talc/medicinal**: early Mennen, Colgate talc, Dr. Hess, Watkins – $10–60.
- **Lard/peanut butter pails**: Swift's Silverleaf, Armour's, Sunny Boy/Toyland peanut butter pails (children's graphics, $100–600).
- **Gift/decor 1960s–present**: Life Savers storybook tins, Hershey/Reese's, Planters, Coca-Cola reissue, Christmas cookie tins, Danish butter cookies, Italian Vicenzi biscuit tins, Daher (Hong Kong) – $1–8.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Grading is everything (tin collectors use 1–10): 8.5+ = bright, no rust, only light scratches. Look for: fading (sun), rust bloom at rims, dents, lid-to-body match (same brand, same wear), paint loss on embossed high points (Horner tins), lithographic register. Light weight, perfect colours, sharp lettering and a "©19xx" line = reproduction/nostalgia. Original keywind coffee cans with key/strip intact add value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust through, heavy scratches, dents, lid mismatch, missing lids, flaking litho, writing/tape on lids (example 7935128376 "mother's" label), touch-up paint (look for brush strokes under raking light), clear-coat applied over rust (glossy unnatural surface). Repainting kills collector value. Odour (tobacco/coffee) is normal.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- 1970s–80s "Fake-Antique" lithographed nostalgia tins (often by Cheinco, Ballonoff, or labelled "Collector Series") – worth $2–8.
- Coca-Cola, Planters, Prince Albert, Folgers, Hills Bros. official reissue tins (1980s–2000s) say "reproduction" or have a modern © – $5–15.
- Chinese reproduction pocket tins (Forest & Stream, Hiawatha) since the 2000s: too-bright colours, no maker imprint, crude seams.
- Huntley & Palmers novelty tins reproduced in small numbers; check maker stamp and age wear consistency.

## Market
High volume, low value at the bottom (thousands of decorative tins listed, slow sell-through); a deep, knowledgeable collector market at the top (tobacco and coffee tin collectors, TCCA – Tin Container Collectors Association). Prices for good tins have softened ~30% since 2010. No strong seasonality except Christmas tins (Nov–Dec). Matthews Auctions, Morphy and Rich Penn handle the top tier.

## What sellers usually miss
- Pocket tobacco tins and small talc/spice tins mixed into "tin lots" – scan the lot photo for vertical flip-lid shapes and maker imprints.
- Figural British biscuit/toffee tins (vehicles, books, houses) lumped into "cookie tins".
- Children's peanut-butter/lard pails (Sunny Boy, Toyland, Kiddies) dismissed as "old pail".
- Sellers more often OVER-estimate: they call 1980s nostalgia tins "antique" (all of example 7969524921).

## Shipping / handling
Single tins 0.5–2 lb, $8–14; bulk lots 6–15 lb bulky, $20–35. Wrap individually – scratches and dents cut grade. eBay 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** brand, contents (coffee, tea, biscuit, toffee, talc), size (1 lb, pocket), maker imprint (Somers, Tindeco, Huntley & Palmers), shape (figural, keywind), decade, "lithographed". Junk: "antique" on 1980s nostalgia tins, "rare" on Prince Albert, "reproduction" omitted when the © is modern. `Vintage 1930s Huntley & Palmers Figural Biscuit Tin Motor Van Lithographed England`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for graphic pre-1940 and figural tins; rare pocket tobacco and store bins to Matthews/Morphy; 1970s–2000s decor tins only in lots (eBay or FB).
- **Price position:** at the sold median (tin lots $12–19, 1930s–60s candy/coffee $8–40, pre-1940 graphic $40–200, H&P novelty $150–800), OBO, accept −20%, floor $5; lot anything under $8; cut after 30 days.
- **Photos:** all sides in daylight; lid; base edge with maker imprint and ©/date line; interior; rust, scratches and dents close-up; hinge/lid fit; ruler.
- **State:** brand, contents, size (oz/lb), maker imprint, approximate date, honest grade, "reproduction" or "nostalgia tin" if the © is modern, rust, dents, scratches, paper label condition by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** single tins 0.5–2 lb $8–14; bulk lots 6–15 lb $20–35; wrap individually — scratches cut grade; shoebox class.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [1930s] [Huntley & Palmers] [motor van] figural tin, [maker imprint shown], grade [7/10] with original lithography; H&P novelty tins sell for $[150–800], and the 1980s cookie tins are $2."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$12) − ship to Dylan (~$12) − price ≥ max($50, price). A tin must sell for ≥ ~$85 to make $50 at $0 cost, so only collectible-tier tins work. Buy: pre-1940 graphic pocket tobacco or 1-lb coffee tins, H&P/British figural tins, children's peanut-butter pails, at ≤ $25 each and only with a visible maker imprint or period construction. Pass: any lot dominated by 1970s+ decorative tins, Christmas tins, Prince Albert/Velvet/Union Leader, rusted or paper-label tins, coronation/commemorative tins. Pocket-sized; no authentication issues except rare pocket tins (verify vs. reproductions before paying > $50).
