## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Most estate "antique stamp collections" are kids' collections from 1940s–70s: Harris "Statesman"/"Citation", Minkus "Supreme", generic "Postage Stamp Album" covers, stock books, glassine bags of off-paper common stamps (kiloware), and Golden Book stamp books (which are not postage at all — they are picture "stamps"). Value signals in photos: a heavy multi-volume binder set (Scott International, Scott Specialty, White Ace, Lighthouse/Davo hingeless), mounts on every stamp (serious collector), engraved 19th-century US (large banknote issues, Columbians 1893), early British Commonwealth (Victoria/Edward heads), and blocks/plate blocks.

## Makers, brands and variants
Scott (Specialty, International, National), Minkus, Harris, White Ace, Lighthouse, Davo, Schaubek; stock books (Lighthouse, Hagner sheets). Golden Book of Indian Stamps etc. are 1950s–70s children's activity books ($5–15).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Mint never hinged > mint hinged > used. Centering, perforations, gum condition. Modern US (post-1940) mint is worth at most face value as postage.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foxing/toning in humid storage, stuck-down gum, thinned paper from hinges, stamps glued to pages.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Forgeries mostly affect classic high-value stamps (needs expertising — professional authentication, which Dylan avoids).

## Market
Huge supply, declining collector base; most lots sit. Good classic material sells fast to dealers.

## What sellers usually miss
Mint US sheets (usable postage) and classic stamps hidden inside a plain album.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, light: $5–15 media/ground.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph every page with value; list counts, countries, date range; do not claim catalogue values as market value.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − $10 − $10 − price ≥ max($50, price). Requires page-by-page photos before buying. Classic high-value singles need expertising → pass.
