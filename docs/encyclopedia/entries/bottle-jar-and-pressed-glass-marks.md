Summary: The base and the seam decide whether a bottle or jar is a $3–20 machine-made common or a $50–600+ hand-finished pre-1900 piece (Terapeak sold: cobalt figural poison asks ~$100, threadless insulators $85 median against $21.50 for cobalt Hemingray). Logos split reissues from originals in pressed and carnival glass (Northwood carnival bowl $35 median, Millersburg $70) and in paperweights (antique Baccarat millefiori $238.50 median, against $30 desk weights).
Buy rule (short): Read the base, the seam and the logo before the title. Buy pre-1900 hand-finished bottles, true off-colour jars, logo-confirmed old carnival or Heisey, genuine Fire-King or McKee Jadeite, and signed or dated canes at ≤ 30% of the tier's sold median. Pass on seam-to-lip machine glass, irradiated "sun purple", unmarked "Jadeite" and anything with repro tells.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
This is a reading guide, not an item type. It covers the marks and manufacturing traces on glass that date it, name the maker or separate the original from the reissue. Use it with the type entries: antique-bottles, antique-canning-jars, carnival-glass, fenton-art-glass, depression-and-elegant-glass, eapg-pressed-glass, vintage-kitchen-glassware, desk-paperweights and glass-and-porcelain-insulators. Ceramic and art-glass factory marks are in ceramic-and-glass-marks-that-multiply-value.

**Where marks sit**
| Object | Where to look | What the listing photo usually shows |
|---|---|---|
| Bottles | **base** (pontil, maker's mark, date code, mould number); **neck and lip** (where the side seam stops) | a side view with the label; ask for a straight-down base photo and a lip close-up |
| Fruit jars | the front embossing (logo), the base (mould numbers, maker), the lip and closure | the front only |
| Pressed and carnival glass | **inside the centre of a bowl**, the underside of the base, or the stem; marks are moulded and faint | the top view; marks show only with raking light |
| Milk glass / Jadeite | the base: embossed oven-ware mark | a stack of mugs or bowls |
| Paperweights | **inside the design** (signature/date cane among the millefiori) and the base (concave ground, star cut, etched signature on studio pieces) | a top view; ask for a loupe shot of each odd cane |
| Insulators | embossing on the skirt or dome: maker, style number, patent date; drip points on the base | a side view; read the front and back |

**Words sellers use when they missed it:** "old bottle", "dug bottle", "purple glass", "blue jar", "green glass dish", "glows", "old candy dish", "carnival bowl", "milk glass mug", "green mug", "glass ball", "old paperweight", "glass insulator", "unmarked".

### Glass colour tells
| Colour | Means | Test / tell | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sun-coloured amethyst (SCA)** | clear glass decolourised with **manganese**, which turns lavender-purple in sunlight; c.1880s to **c.1915** (WWI cut the supply of German manganese and selenium replaced it) (general knowledge, verify) | soft, uneven lavender, darker on the sunlit side; on hand-finished bottles and EAPG | SCA confirms a pre-1915 date; it adds a little to bottles and a lot to rare EAPG |
| Straw / pale amber "clear" glass | **selenium** decolouriser, c.1915–1930s (general knowledge, verify) | faint yellow tint | dates a piece to after WWI |
| **Irradiated "sun purple"** | modern artificial irradiation (gamma or UV) of manganese glass, sold as "SCA" | **deep, uniform grape-purple**, often on **machine-made 1920s+ bottles** or Depression-era shapes | a fake colour: value **below** the same piece left clear |
| **Uranium glass** (vaseline when yellow-green and transparent; also custard and some green Depression glass) | uranium oxide colourant, 1830s–1943; US civilian use restricted 1943–58; still made today (Fenton, Mosser, Czech) (general knowledge, verify) | **bright green glow under a 365 nm UV torch**. Manganese glass glows dull and weak by comparison (general knowledge, verify) | Terapeak (sold): Bohemian uranium vaseline vase $355; Fenton topaz opalescent vaseline pieces $1,750–3,199 (fenton-art-glass). Green Cameo/Ballerina Depression vases: eBay **active** median $59.99 against $21 sold for general Cameo (Terapeak, n=50) |
The UV glow proves uranium, **not age**. Modern vaseline glass glows exactly the same. Date it by the maker's mark, the mould and the wear.

### Depression glass: reproduction tells (detail in depression-and-elegant-glass)
- **Colours never made in the pattern** are the biggest giveaway (Urban Art Antiques). Repros are often too light, too orange, too deep a green, or cobalt in a pattern that never had it.
- **Feel:** repros often feel oily or slick, heavier, with rougher bottoms (Urban Art Antiques).
- **Cherry Blossom:** repro flowers look **hand-drawn**, with outlines, plain circle centres and drawn stems. Genuine butter lids have **three mould lines about ¼ in. above the base**, repros often only one. On genuine bases the **pattern reaches the corners**; on repros it stops about ¼ in. short with a plain border. Check lids and bases separately, because married sets exist (Urban Art Antiques).
- **Madrid → Indiana "Recollection":** 1976 reissue first dated "1976", later undated, in amber, clear, blue, pink and teal. Teal and crystal-blue Madrid are always Recollection (general knowledge, verify).
- Other repro hotspots: Sharon butter and cheese dishes, Mayfair shot glasses and cookie jars, Miss America and Adam butter dishes, Avocado (Indiana/Tiara), Royal Lace cobalt, and Cameo salt shakers (general knowledge, verify). Comp: Royal Lace cobalt eBay **active** median $49.99 (n=50); real 3-legged bowls ask $300.

### Insulators (detail in glass-and-porcelain-insulators)
- **Embossing** on the skirt and dome gives the maker and style ("HEMINGRAY-42", "BROOKFIELD", "W.T.Co.", "McLAUGHLIN"), patent dates ("PAT MAY 2 1893") and sometimes a date code. **CD numbers** (Consolidated Design) are a **collectors' classification of the shape**, not embossed on the glass. Look the shape up to get the CD.
- **Drip points** (bumps on the base edge) mean a later Hemingray-type machine pressing. **Threadless** (no internal threads, pre-c.1870s) is the valuable early form. Terapeak (sold): **threadless median $85** (n=50, p75 $215) against **cobalt Hemingray $21.50** (n=50) and Locke porcelain $22.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Open pontil plus applied lip on a coloured (cobalt, amber, olive, emerald) medicine, bitters or ink** in a "box of old bottles": pre-1860, typically $50–600+ against $15–25 for a lot (Terapeak "assorted antique glass bottle lot" sold median $22.50; antique-bottles). Tell: a rough ring on the base and a lip with a drip.
- **Seam that stops below the lip** on an embossed soda, poison or city-named bottle: hand-finished pre-1915. Figural cobalt poisons ask around $100 (eBay **active** median, n=50).
- **Off-colour fruit jar** (true amber, olive, cobalt, milk) titled "old blue jar": $50–2,000 against $12.75 for aqua Ball Ideal (Terapeak sold), so about 5–100×. Tell: the colour is in the glass rather than a coating, with whittle marks and an old closure. Note that **the "cobalt Mason's Patent 1858" Terapeak median of $45 (n=26) is mostly reproductions**; a real one is far rarer.
- **Northwood underlined N in a circle** inside a purple or ice carnival bowl titled "carnival dish": old Northwood, $60–675+ (an eBay active ice-green Peacocks asks $675; Terapeak Northwood carnival bowl median $35 overall). Check the N against the fake-N list.
- **Heisey diamond-H** on a "pressed glass bowl" or candlestick: 2–5× unmarked (eapg-pressed-glass).
- **Fire-King Oven Ware embossing on Jadeite** titled "green mug" or "jade dish": genuine Restaurantware or Jane-Ray pieces $20–200 against $5–15 for modern (vintage-kitchen-glassware; general knowledge, verify).
- **Uranium glow plus a pontil or a classic-era mould**: a genuine Victorian vaseline piece, $100–3,000 (fenton-art-glass comps; general knowledge, verify).
- **"B1848", "SL1848" or a "C" cane** in a "glass ball paperweight": an antique French weight, $200–10,000+ (Terapeak Baccarat median $238.50; Clichy C cane about 2×).
- **Threadless insulator** in a mixed lot: $85 median against $3–10 commons.

### Quick ID checklist
- Ask for **a straight-down base photo, a lip close-up with the side seam visible, and a photo in daylight**. For pressed glass, ask for **raking light across the inside centre**.
- Pontil? → pre-1860. Seam stops below the lip? → hand-finished, before c.1915. Seam through the lip or an Owens scar? → machine-made, 1903 or later.
- Read the base code: diamond-oval-I or I-in-oval (O-I, 1929+, year to the right of the logo), H over A (1923–64), anchor over H (1938+), boxed anchor (1977–c.2000), Duraglas (1940+), "Federal Law Forbids" (1935–64).
- Purple? Uneven lavender on hand-finished glass = SCA, pre-1915. Deep, even purple on machine glass = irradiated.
- UV torch: bright green = uranium (any age). Then date it by the mark and mould.
- Pressed and carnival: find the logo, then check it against the fake-mark list (Northwood N variants; IG/LIG/ALIG; Fenton decade digit).
- Paperweights: a loupe on every odd cane (letter, number), plus the base (concave ground or star-cut = good; flat and rough = Chinese).
- Insulators: read the embossing, look up the CD, check the threads and drip points.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Old vs new glass:** seeds (bubbles), whittle (a hammered-looking surface from wood or cold iron moulds), uneven thickness and slightly crooked bodies are old. Perfect uniformity, sharp mould detail with modern fonts, and a slick, oily feel are new.
- **Wear on the base:** a true old base shows a random, even scuffed ring. **Artificial wear** (sandpaper) shows parallel scratches all in one direction (general knowledge, verify).
- **Carnival iridescence:** the classic radium and stretch finishes are thinner and subtler; revival iridescence is often thicker and more uniform.
- **EAPG flint glass** (lead, pre-c.1870) rings when tapped and is heavy; soda-lime glass after 1864 thuds (eapg-pressed-glass).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on the lip or the base ring, cracks (hold it to the light), **sickness/haze** (internal etching from contents, which cleans only by professional tumbling), stains, and **ground or polished lips or rims**, which hide chips and cut the value by 30–60% (general knowledge, verify). Reglued or polished rims show on pressed glass and paperweights (a polished-out bruise leaves a flat facet). Mould flaws and bubbles in old glass are not damage. Insulators: chips on the skirt or threads cut the value a lot (glass-and-porcelain-insulators). Paperweights: surface scratches can be polished out, but deep bruises cannot without changing the dome.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Repro **bitters, flasks and figural bottles** (Clevenger, Wheaton 1970s commemoratives, Taiwanese): too-crisp embossing, machine glass and fake pontils (general knowledge, verify).
- **Irradiated SCA**: deep, even purple on the wrong era of glass.
- **Coloured "Mason's Patent 1858" and Ball jars** in purple, red and cobalt: mostly modern, including Ball's own Heritage colour jars from 2013 on (general knowledge, verify; Appraisily warns about purple and red).
- **Fake Northwood N**, L.G. Wright and Mosser marks; Westmoreland moulds used by later makers; Imperial IG revival pieces sold as classic (Carnival Glass Worldwide).
- **Depression repros**: see the tells above.
- **Jadeite**: Chinese and Martha Stewart pieces, and "Fire-King" in the wrong font (vintage-kitchen-glassware).
- **Paperweights**: Chinese copies with fake date canes (desk-paperweights).
- **Insulators**: re-coloured (irradiated purple) and modern art-glass insulators (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Bottles and jars sell steadily on eBay and at bottle shows. Most of the volume is low-dollar, and the money sits in a few rarities that dealers know well. Carnival and Depression glass are soft markets for the commons and firm for rarities, sold to an older collector base. Jadeite and Pyrex-adjacent kitchen glass are trend-driven and active on Etsy and Instagram. Paperweights: antique French weights sell to specialists and at auction (Bonhams, L.H. Selman); studio weights sell steadily on eBay. Insulators: a niche of club collectors (NIA), with rarities quickly bid up. Glass ships badly, so collectors prefer sellers who state chips and cracks precisely.

## What sellers usually miss
- The base: pontils, Owens scars, date codes. Owners photograph the side.
- Where the seam stops on the lip.
- That uneven lavender means pre-1915 (and the reverse: they overprice irradiated purple).
- Faint moulded logos inside carnival bowls (the N) and on Heisey stems.
- Fire-King embossing on Jadeite; McKee marks.
- Signature and date canes inside millefiori weights.
- Threadless insulators and rare colours in mixed lots.
- The reverse error: they price "Mason's Patent 1858" as if 1858 were the jar's date, and treat "glows under blacklight" as proof of age.

## Shipping / handling
Fragility is high. Double-box: wrap in bubble wrap, then 2 in. of padding all round in an inner box, then an outer box. Bottles and jars 1–3 lb, $8–15. Carnival bowls and pitchers 2–5 lb, $12–20. Paperweights are heavy for their size (1–2 lb), $8–12, with the dome protected. Insulators are sturdy but chip at the skirt. Transit breakage on thin pressed glass is real; insure anything over $50.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the evidence: "open pontil base", "applied/tooled lip, seam ends below lip, c.1880–1910", "O-I diamond mark, plant 7, date code 4. = 1944", "Northwood underlined N in circle", "Heisey diamond-H on stem", "Fire-King Oven Ware embossed", "B1848 date cane". Add a UV photo for uranium, give height and diameter, and list every chip and crack precisely. Photograph the base square-on, the lip, and the mark in raking light. Title keywords: maker, mark, colour (cobalt, amber, SCA, vaseline, jadeite), age term (pontil, applied lip, threadless), pattern name and size.
### What to tell the buyer
"[Open pontil / tooled lip / maker's mark x], which dates it to [range]. Photographed in daylight and under UV. [No chips or cracks / chip described and pictured]. Packed double-boxed."

## Buy rule
Buy when: the base or mark photo confirms the tier (pontil, hand-finished lip, true colour, a genuine logo, a signature cane) and the price is ≤ 30% of that tier's sold median, with profit ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Pass when: the seam runs through the lip and the piece is not a known rarity; the purple is irradiated; the "Jadeite" is unmarked; there are Depression repro tells or a fake-N or IG revival mark sold as classic; there is no base photo; the piece is chipped, sick or ground-rimmed at more than a common price; or it is bulky and thin (punch bowls) at a low tier. Everything here fits a passenger seat.
