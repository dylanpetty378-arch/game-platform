## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Plates or frames attached to a belt with a hook/bar on the back. Tells:
- **Back stamps**: maker, copyright year, metal ("Solid brass", "Pewter", "Sterling", ".925", "Silver overlay", "German silver/nickel silver" – NOT silver), issue numbers ("12 of 500").
- **Pewter/zinc-alloy cast plates** with relief image and advertising text (example 7957572077 Caterpillar dozer "Quality Products", 1980s), often by Siskiyou, Spec-Cast, Hit Line.
- **Enamelled/epoxy-domed plates** with event printing (examples 7957795823, 7959028992: Ocean State Motorcyclist's Association poker run buckles 1989 and 1990) – local event souvenirs.
- **Trophy buckles**: oval, engraved event/year/name, silver overlay or sterling with gold figures, made by Montana Silversmiths (low), Gist, Comstock, Clint Orms, Vogt (high).
- **Navajo/Zuni**: hand-stamped sterling, cast silver ("ketoh"-style), inlay or petit point turquoise; artist hallmarks on the back.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Western high end**: Edward H. Bohlin (Hollywood, 1920s–1980s), Clint Orms, Vogt Western Silver, Comstock Heritage, Gist – $300–5,000+.
- **Native American**: Navajo (stamped, cast, sandcast), Zuni (inlay, needlepoint), Hopi (overlay); hallmarked artists command premiums – $100–2,000+.
- **Rodeo**: Hesston NFR (1974–present; early years $50–150, common years $15–40), named champion trophy buckles $100–1,000.
- **1970s brass**: Bergamot Brass Works (Wisconsin), Award Design Medals (ADM), Lewis Buckle Co. (Chicago), Indiana Metal Craft – $10–40; railroad, beer, tractor (John Deere, IH, Caterpillar) designs $10–40.
- **Military**: US Civil War buckles (US/CS – heavily faked), WWII GI buckles, Boy Scout – $10–500.
- **Tiffany Bergamot** etc.: some 1970s Bergamot buckles are marked "Tiffany" in reference to the series – not Tiffany & Co.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling tarnishes black-brown, is soft; German silver and silver plate show brass at wear points. Hand-stamped Native work has small irregularities; die-struck imports are perfectly regular. Weight: sterling trophy buckles 2–4 oz.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent hooks/keepers, re-soldered loops, worn plating, lost turquoise stones or cracked inlay, chipped epoxy domes, scratched enamel.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Civil War buckles (dug-look repros), fake Navajo (Asian-made "Native style" stamped sterling and stabilized/block turquoise), "sterling" stamps on nickel silver, Bohlin marks on unrelated buckles. High-value Native and Bohlin pieces may need professional appraisal – buy only with clear hallmarks and price support.

## Market
Western/Native silver has a strong, steady market (and a Southwest-themed fashion bump); pewter advertising buckles are plentiful and slow. Slight fall/holiday bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling marks on Western and Native buckles sold as "silver-tone"; early Hesston NFRs (1974–1976); artist hallmarks. Sellers usually over-estimate event and advertising buckles ($60 for a $15 buckle).

## Shipping / handling
Small, 3–8 oz; $6–10 Ground Advantage.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the back stamps; give dimensions, weight, metal (tested), belt width fitting (1½" standard, 1¾"), condition.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$8) − ship to Dylan (~$8) − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs ~$75+ median. Buy only sterling Western/Native buckles with marks, early Hesston, named trophies, at ≤ 1/3 sold median. Pass pewter advertising/event buckles over $10. High-end Bohlin/Native claims need clear hallmarks; anything needing appraisal at > $200 value – pass.
