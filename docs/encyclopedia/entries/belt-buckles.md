## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Plates or frames attached to a belt with a hook/bar on the back. Tells:
- **Back stamps**: maker, copyright year, metal ("Solid brass", "Pewter", "Sterling", ".925", "Silver overlay", "German silver/nickel silver" – NOT silver), issue numbers ("12 of 500").
- **Pewter/zinc-alloy cast plates** with relief image and advertising text (example 7957572077 Caterpillar dozer "Quality Products", 1980s), often by Siskiyou, Spec-Cast, Hit Line.
- **Enamelled/epoxy-domed plates** with event printing (examples 7957795823, 7959028992: Ocean State Motorcyclist's Association poker run buckles 1989 and 1990) – local event souvenirs.
- **Trophy buckles**: oval, engraved event/year/name, silver overlay or sterling with gold figures, made by Montana Silversmiths (low), Gist, Comstock, Clint Orms, Vogt (high).
- **Navajo/Zuni**: hand-stamped sterling, cast silver ("ketoh"-style), inlay or petit point turquoise; artist hallmarks on the back.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Western high end**: Edward H. Bohlin (Hollywood, 1920s–1980s), Clint Orms, Vogt Western Silver, Comstock Heritage, Gist – $300–5,000+.
- **Native American**: Navajo (stamped, cast, sandcast), Zuni (inlay, needlepoint), Hopi (overlay); hallmarked artists command premiums – $100–2,000+.
- **Rodeo**: Hesston NFR (1974–present; early years $50–150, common years $15–40), named champion trophy buckles $100–1,000.
- **1970s brass**: Bergamot Brass Works (Wisconsin), Award Design Medals (ADM), Lewis Buckle Co. (Chicago), Indiana Metal Craft – $10–40; railroad, beer, tractor (John Deere, IH, Caterpillar) designs $10–40.
- **Military**: US Civil War buckles (US/CS – heavily faked), WWII GI buckles, Boy Scout – $10–500.
- **Tiffany Bergamot** etc.: some 1970s Bergamot buckles are marked "Tiffany" in reference to the series – not Tiffany & Co.

### Golden-egg tells

Bohlin gold-overlay/presentation buckle, hand-chased 1930s–50s — $18,750–47,200 sold (Bonhams 2013 Larry Hagman sterling/10k; Lebel 2022 Bohlin's own filigreed gold) vs $8–30 common (~1,000×+) — small oval stamp on the back "BOHLINMADE / HOLLYWOOD, CALIF" (later "BURBANK" or "Bohlin Made") plus karat stamps (10K, 12K, 18K, 22K); raised gold figures/initials over engraved scrollwork, heavy multi-piece construction; beware: fraudulent Bohlin marks are increasingly common, so a stamp alone proves nothing without a pre-1960 look and provenance (https://www.bonhams.com/auction/20984/lot/3311/a-larry-hagman-bohlin-jr-belt-buckle ; https://www.oldwestevents.com/highlights/2022/5/31/edward-h-bohlins-personal-filigreed-gold-buckle)

- Civil War "US" oval belt plate (Model 1839), lead-filled back — $300–350 for a plain genuine one, Confederate rarities to $35,000 vs $10–20 repro (~15–30×) — lead-filled back with thick brass arrow-shaped or bar hooks, softened letters from wear; beware: razor-sharp letters, thin flat hooks cut from sheet brass, steel hooks or a bevelled edge = modern repro (https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/roadshow/articles/fascinating-fasteners-civil-war-belt-buckles)
- Navajo sterling oval buckles with turquoise/coral, third quarter 20th c., artist-hallmarked — ~$200 each sold (Hindman 2022, three for $594) vs $8–30 (~7–10×) — "STERLING" plus artist initials stamped on back (e.g. "DKL", conjoined "DB"), stamped/appliquéd leaves, petit-point turquoise clusters, 40–50 dwt weight; beware: "German silver"/"nickel silver" and "silver overlay" stamps are not sterling (https://hindmanauctions.com/items/10569888-navajo-silver-turquoise-and-coral-belt-buckles)
- 1974 Hesston National Finals Rodeo buckle (first year, giveaway) — documented at $400 in 1983 and complete sets at $1,500 (today expect $50–150 for 1974–76) vs $15–40 for common later years (~3–5×) — "1974" date and "Hesston" in the design, enamel colours on brass; only the 1974–76 dates matter (https://www.farmshow.com/a_article.php?aid=2897)
- 1970s Bergamot Brass Works / Award Design Medals novelty brass (band, motorcycle, hunting) — asking $20–60 vs $8–30 (~2×) — back cast "BERGAMOT BRASS WORKS" with date and design number (e.g. "1974 S-48"); band names (e.g. Atlanta Rhythm Section) and psychedelic/demon designs are the better ones; asking only (https://www.ebay.com/itm/277013374578)
- Pewter/zinc advertising, poker-run and event buckles (Siskiyou, Spec-Cast, Caterpillar/John Deere) — ~1× ($8–30) — dull grey cast plate, "Siskiyou"/"Spec-Cast" and copyright year on back; do not flag (entry comps)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling tarnishes black-brown, is soft; German silver and silver plate show brass at wear points. Hand-stamped Native work has small irregularities; die-struck imports are perfectly regular. Weight: sterling trophy buckles 2–4 oz.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent hooks/keepers, re-soldered loops, worn plating, lost turquoise stones or cracked inlay, chipped epoxy domes, scratched enamel.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Civil War buckles (dug-look repros), fake Navajo (Asian-made "Native style" stamped sterling and stabilized/block turquoise), "sterling" stamps on nickel silver, Bohlin marks on unrelated buckles. High-value Native and Bohlin pieces may need professional appraisal – buy only with clear hallmarks and price support.

## Market
Western/Native silver has a strong, steady market (and a Southwest-themed fashion bump); pewter advertising buckles are plentiful and slow. Slight fall/holiday bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling marks on Western and Native buckles sold as "silver-tone"; early Hesston NFRs (1974–1976); artist hallmarks. Sellers usually over-estimate event and advertising buckles ($60 for a $15 buckle).

## Shipping / handling
Small, 3–8 oz; $6–10 Ground Advantage.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker (Bohlin, Clint Orms, Montana Silversmiths, Hesston), metal (sterling, brass, pewter), style (Western, Navajo, trophy, NFR year), stones, size, belt width. Junk: "Tiffany" on Bergamot series, "silver" on silver-tone, "rare" on advertising buckles. `Vintage Navajo Sterling Silver Turquoise Belt Buckle Signed 3x2 in Fits 1.5 in Belt`
- **Where:** eBay — 7-day auction for Bohlin/Orms/Navajo sterling (strong collector base), BIN with offers for Hesston and brass; pewter advertising buckles in lots or FB local; slight fall/holiday bump.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (Bohlin $895, Clint Orms $875, Navajo sterling $100–600, early Hesston $50–150, brass $15–60, advertising $8–30), OBO, accept −15%, floor is silver weight or $8; cut after 30 days for brass.
- **Photos:** front straight-on; back stamps and hallmarks close-up; loop and prong; edge for plating wear; stones close-up; buckle on a gram scale; ruler.
- **State:** maker and marks, metal tested, dimensions, weight, belt width fitting (1½ in. standard, 1¾ in.), year for Hesston, stone type, bends, plating loss by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** pocket, 3–8 oz, padded mailer $6–10; insure sterling.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [hallmarked Navajo] sterling buckle, [42 g], with [natural turquoise], c. [1970s]; signed Navajo sterling sells for $[100–600], and the silver-tone pewter copies are $10."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$8) − ship to Dylan (~$8) − price ≥ max($50, price). Needs ~$75+ median. Buy only sterling Western/Native buckles with marks, early Hesston, named trophies, at ≤ 1/3 sold median. Pass pewter advertising/event buckles over $10. High-end Bohlin/Native claims need clear hallmarks; anything needing appraisal at > $200 value – pass.
