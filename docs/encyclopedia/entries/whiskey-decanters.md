## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ceramic (occasionally glass) figural bottles sold full of bourbon or scotch from the 1950s to 1990s, now mostly sold empty as collectibles. Photo tells:
- **Jim Beam**: shaped like animals, cars, trains, states, political donkeys/elephants, sports, clubs. Paper label near the base reading "Beam ... Kentucky Straight Bourbon Whiskey", proof, and often "Regal China" on the base or label. 1950s–60s glass "Beam's Choice" decanters with painted portraits (composers Mozart, Beethoven, Brahms; Vermeer, Remington) have a red/green crackle-painted glass body.
- **Ezra Brooks**: heavier, glossier figurals (Shriners, sports, Indian chiefs) with an "Ezra Brooks" label; generally lower value than Beam.
- **Ski Country (Colorado, 1973–90s)**: the best-made: finely modelled birds, owls, eagles, Indian figures, with crisp painting. Base reads "Ski Country Limited Edition".
- **Wild Turkey (Austin Nichols, 1971–1980s)**: turkey figurals in series #1–#8 and minis, often boxed.
- **Old Fitzgerald**: "1849" stopper; Monticello, Irish/Old Ireland and hillbilly jugs.
- **Lionstone (1969–80s)**: Western and sporting figures with metal accessories.
- **McCormick**: Elvis, Jupiter train, US presidents.
- **Bell's Scotch (Wade ceramics, UK)**: brown/cream bell shape with gold base.
- **Other**: leather-wrapped bottles with photo panels (Spanish brandy/novelty bar ware), Grenadier soldiers, Garnier figurals (French, pre-war Garnier can be $50–200).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Jim Beam decanters**: first in 1953–55 (bowling pin, Black Royal Regal China 1955). Regal China (Antioch, Illinois) made the bulk until 1992; Wade took over afterwards. Series: Trophy (animals, birds, fish), State, Political (donkey/elephant every election, e.g. 1972 football donkey), Wheels (cars, trains, trucks), Club and Convention (International Association of Jim Beam Bottle & Specialties Clubs), Customer Specialties (banks, casinos), Ducks Unlimited (1974–1990s annual), Beam's Choice glass portraits (1960s–70s). Dates are often on the base as two digits.
- **Rare Beam pieces**: First National Bank of Chicago 1964 (117 made, up to $3,500), 1970 Dodge Hot Rod, 1991 Gold Semi, convention editions numbered x/500 (Michigan Auction Co.; TX Antique Mall).
- **Ezra Brooks (1960s–80s)**, **Lionstone**, **McCormick**, **Old Commonwealth**, **Kentucky Gentleman**, **Hoffman**: mass production; $5–30.
- **Ski Country**: higher craft; empties $20–80, large eagles/owls more.
- **Wild Turkey Series**: $20–60 empty, more with box.
- **Bell's (Wade)**: "now worth less than £30" (Mark Littler); Christmas editions slightly more.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Regal China and Ski Country are vitreous china with crisp moulding; cheaper makers show blurry detail and thick glaze.
- Original stopper (cork with ceramic top) and intact paper label matter. Missing stopper = –30–50%.
- Tax strip: a paper federal strip over the stopper means sealed and full; a broken strip means opened.
- Beam's Choice glass decanters have a crackle "antique" finish that is factory, not damage.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips at stopper rims, ears, beaks, wings; hairlines from evaporating whiskey freezing/expanding are rare but occur.
- Leaking or evaporated full bottles leave sticky staining under labels.
- Lead: old ceramic decanters may leach lead into whiskey; don't advise drinking (Michigan Auction Co.).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked: values are too low. Confusion pieces exist (a 1971 Masonic decanter is often mistaken for Beam, ~$30). Club "convention" pieces were made in small runs; check numbering and documentation.

## Market
Massive supply, aging buyer base, prices fell from the 1970s peak (one piece: $160 in the 1970s, $48 by 1995 – Michigan Auction Co.). eBay sell-through for common empties is poor; many listings sit. Lots sell slowly and shipping heavy ceramics kills margins. Full sealed bottles have a lively "dusty bourbon" collector market, but only through licensed channels.

## What sellers usually miss
- Usually the reverse: sellers think decanters are valuable because of 1970s price guides.
- Real misses: a still-sealed (tax strip intact) Bottled-in-Bond decanter, which whisky collectors value for the bourbon, not the ceramic; a numbered convention/club piece mixed into a lot; Ski Country pieces in a "liquor bottle" lot (much better quality than Beam).

## Shipping / handling
Ceramic figurals are 2–4 lb, irregular, with breakable ears/beaks/tails: $12–20 each, double-boxed. Never ship full bottles (USPS prohibits alcohol; carriers require licensed shippers). Empty and rinse before shipping; state "EMPTY" in the title (eBay requires it).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title with brand, year, series and "EMPTY" (e.g. "Jim Beam 1972 Democratic Donkey Football Decanter Regal China EMPTY"). Photograph label, base date and stopper. Disclose chips and missing stoppers. Don't make health claims about drinking from it.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- An empty $25 decanter nets ~$22 − $30 shipping = a loss. **Pass on every common empty decanter regardless of price.**
- Buy only: a Ski Country large eagle/owl or a documented club/convention edition with recent sold ≥ $100 at ≤ $20.
- Never buy full bottles to resell; Dylan has no liquor licence and eBay bans alcohol.
- Lots: only if the lot price is ≤ $3 per piece and it contains identified better pieces; still bulky (often fails the passenger-seat rule).
