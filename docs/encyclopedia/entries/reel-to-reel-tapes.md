Buy rule (short): Buy 10.5" aluminum audio reels (brand on flange) under ~$5 each and prerecorded 4-track jazz/rock under ~$5 each; pass on plastic reels and computer tape.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Audio tape is 1/4" wide on 5", 7" (plastic, small cine hub) or 10.5" reels (metal or plastic, large 3" NAB center hole with 3 slots). 9-track computer tape is 1/2" wide on 10.5" reels with a small hub, a groove on the back for the write-enable ring, and usually a plastic "tape seal" belt or hanging canister with a wire hanger — storage from data centers (Memorex, IBM, 3M "Scotch", Graham, BASF). Prerecorded 4-track tapes come in printed boxes with album art (Ampex, Columbia, RCA, "Barclay-Crocker").
- Prerecorded formats (PS Audio Copper): **2-track (half-track) stereo** plays one direction across the full tape width; **4-track (quarter-track)** stereo plays both directions. Major-label prerecorded tapes ran from about 1953–54 to the mid-1980s. 7.5 ips was used for classical and prestige titles; 3.75 ips for lesser pop titles. About 97–98% carry no printed date, and catalogue numbers do not follow release order.
- Dating clues on prerecorded tapes (PS Audio Copper): early boxes are heavier card with cloth hinges and thicker spools; later boxes and spools are thinner and the print poorer. Sealed tapes from the mid-1960s to mid-1980s have no leader, only a sticker holding the tape end; printed leader appears on a few 1950s titles.
- Audio vs computer at a glance: 1/4" tape with a brand box = audio; 1/2" tape, hanging canister, write-ring groove, white label strip with job numbers = computer.
- Seller words: "reel to reel tapes lot", "studio tape", "open reel", "metal reels", "computer tape".

## Makers, brands and variants
Audio: Ampex (456/499 Grand Master — studio favourites, but beware sticky-shed), Scotch 3M (206/250), Maxell UD/XL, TDK Audua/GX, BASF/AGFA, Memorex Quantum. Metal empty reels: TEAC RE-1002, Maxell, Ampex, Scotch — collectors pay most for TEAC and Maxell metal reels, and Tascam/Studer/Otari ones.
Computer: Memorex, IBM, 3M 777, Graham — mostly hobby-value; a tape containing early software (e.g. old OS/ARPANET dumps) can be valuable to archivists but is unknowable from photos.
- Prerecorded labels: mainstream (RCA, Capitol, Mercury, Columbia) and smaller audiophile labels such as Livingston (PS Audio Copper); later audiophile/reissue open-reel sources include Barclay-Crocker (Dolby), The Tape Project (15 ips 2-track), Opus3, IPI, Horch House and the Rhino open-reel series (forum and Stereophile titles).

### Quick ID checklist
- Reel diameter (5", 7", 10.5") and hub: big 3-slot NAB hole vs small cine hole.
- Reel material: aluminum with brand embossed/printed on the flange vs plastic.
- Tape width: 1/4" (audio) or 1/2" (computer); hanging canisters and write-ring grooves = computer.
- Prerecorded: label and box art present; does the box say 2-track or 4-track, and 7.5 or 3.75 ips?
- Flanges flat (look edge-on) and free of dents.
- Original reel box (TEAC, Maxell, Ampex) present?
- Tape formulation printed on the box (Ampex 456, Scotch 206, etc.) — sticky-shed risk.
- Any smell or white powder/mold on the pack (ask).

### Golden-egg tells
- TEAC RE-1002 (and similar TEAC/Maxell/Studer/Tascam/Otari) 10.5" metal reels with boxes — ~2x a generic metal reel ($25–60 vs $12–25 each; new-old-stock TEAC asks seen €69–79 and $125 on eBay.de, asking only) — brand printed on the flange, silver aluminum, NAB hub, original box.
- 2-track (half-track) stereo prerecorded tapes — "generally much more valuable" than 4-track (WhatsBestForum) — box marked "2-track" or "stereo tape" from the 1950s, heavy box with cloth hinge.
- Specific prerecorded titles: rare jazz and pop/rock 4-track (e.g. Beatles on 4-track) — $100–400+ vs $10–60 — album artwork on the box; label (Capitol, Columbia, RCA); 7.5 ips.
- Tape Project / 15 ips audiophile 2-track — top tier ($100–400+) — 10.5" reels, 15 ips marking, audiophile packaging.
- Empty branded metal reels in a lot of recorded tape — worth more than the tape on them (existing entry) — silver flanges among black plastic reels.
- Not golden eggs: 9-track computer tapes; 7" plastic used blanks; most 4-track easy-listening and classical titles ("most are not" valuable, WhatsBestForum).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Aluminum reels with brand embossed/printed on flange = value. Plastic reels = low. Check tape width (1/4" audio vs 1/2" computer). Hanging plastic canisters with wire hangers and white label strips = computer tape.
- 1950s–60s major-label prerecorded tape stock is about as durable as 1980s stock (PS Audio Copper); age alone is not a defect.
- Polyurethane-binder tapes of the 1970s–80s absorb moisture and become sticky (Everpresent); see the list below.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent flanges kill metal reel value; sticky-shed in 1970s–80s Ampex/Scotch back-coated tape; mold on tape pack; vinegar smell (acetate). Reels are visual inspection only.
- Sticky-shed formulations (Everpresent): Scotch/3M 206, 207, 226, 227, 262, 808, 966, 967, 986, 996; Ampex/Quantegy 406, 407, 456, 457, 499; reportedly some Sony (PR-150, SLH, ULH, FeCr). First symptom is squealing on playback; it is treated by professional baking.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction "studio master" claims; new-production reels by RTM/ATR/Recording The Masters (sold new $30–60) set a ceiling for used.
- Chinese-made "TEAC" style reels and new-old-stock claims appear on eBay; check flange printing and box (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Steady audiophile demand for metal reels; slow for computer tape. 10–40 days for metal reels.

## What sellers usually miss
Branded TEAC/Maxell aluminum reels; Tape Project / prerecorded jazz boxes; empty metal reels worth more than the tape on them; whether a prerecorded tape is 2-track or 4-track.

## Shipping / handling
10.5" reels ship in 12×12 boxes; 1–2 lb each; flanges must be protected from bending.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, reel material, size, hub type (NAB/cine), tape width, whether recorded, any bends.

### What to tell the buyer
State reel size, hub type, reel material and brand as printed on the flange, and whether the original box is included. For tape, give the formulation from the box, tape width, whether it is recorded, and for prerecorded tapes the label, title, 2-track or 4-track, and speed. Say that playback has not been tested (if so) and that 1970s–80s Ampex and Scotch formulations can need baking. Describe any flange bends edge-on and confirm no mold or vinegar smell.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). Six mixed reels need ~$110+ resale to clear at $15; only all-metal branded lots do. No untested-electronics issue. Fits easily.
