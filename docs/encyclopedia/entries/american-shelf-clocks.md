## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Spring- or weight-driven pendulum clocks made in Connecticut (Bristol, Plymouth, Thomaston, Waterbury, New Haven) c.1820–1930.
- **Sharp gothic / steeple (1840s–1900s)**: pointed case with two turned finials on side columns; rosewood or mahogany veneer; lower glass tablet (reverse-painted, stencilled or transfer) and paper label inside on back board. "Steeple-on-steeple" (double steeple) is earlier and worth more.
- **Round gothic / beehive (1840s–1860s)**: rounded arch, bezel round dial, often ripple moulding; Brewster & Ingrahams, Chauncey Jerome, Sperry & Shaw (NY retailer). Etched/frosted lower glass with basket or urn motif is typical.
- **Ogee (OG) (1840s–1910s)**: rectangular wall/shelf case with S-curve moulding, weight-driven 30-hour; very common.
- **Cottage, gingerbread/kitchen (1880s–1915)**: pressed oak "gingerbread" cases, 8-day; common.
- **Black mantel (1890s–1920s)**: iron, marble or "Adamantine" celluloid-veneered; heavy.
- **Tambour/humpback (1910s–40s)** and **1950s–80s reproductions**: Seth Thomas, New Haven and Howard Miller made "colonial" steeple reproductions with printed dial plus small printed lower picture, often electric or later key-wound with stamped plate movements, sometimes quartz conversions.
- Label: printed paper label glued inside the case back (maker, city, instructions) is the key ID; dial holes: 30-hour time-and-strike and 8-day both have two winding holes, so check movement size (8-day brass movements are larger, wound by key with springs; 30-hour often have a pull-cord/weights for OG).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Brewster & Ingrahams** (Bristol 1844–52): beehive and steeple, 8-day spring movements with "Brewster's" springs; strong collector names.
- **Elisha Manross**, **Birge & Fuller** (wagon-spring, steeple-on-steeple), **J.C. Brown / Forestville Mfg** (ripple fronts), **Atkins** (wagon spring, rolling pinion).
- **Chauncey Jerome** / **Jerome & Co.**: Jerome & Co. name used by New Haven Clock Co. after Jerome's 1855 failure, labels say "Jerome & Co. New Haven Conn" (1850s–1904). Huge volume, mostly 30-hour.
- **Seth Thomas** (Plymouth Hollow/Thomaston): everything; labels and stamped movements dated in some years.
- **E.N. Welch**, **Waterbury**, **Ansonia** (Bristol then Brooklyn), **Gilbert**, **E. Ingraham** (later Ingraham), **New Haven**, **Sessions** (successor to Welch, 1903).
- **Sperry & Shaw** (New York, c.1844–48): retailer/assembler with Connecticut movements; beehive and steeple; label prominence makes them modestly desirable.
- **Eli Terry / Terry & Sons** pillar-and-scroll (1810s–30s): wooden movements, $800–3,000+.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Veneer: rosewood (dark streaked) and flame mahogany over pine; check for lifting and missing chips.
- Tablets: original reverse-painted, stencilled gold or etched glass tablets add 20–40%; modern decal replacements are flat-coloured and crisp.
- Dials: original painted zinc or paper-on-tin with crazing/flaking is preferred over a repaint; reproduction paper dials are bright white.
- Movement: brass 8-day with 2 mainsprings, maker stamp; 30-hour spring or weight; wooden movements = pre-1840.
- Label intact and legible adds value; torn or replaced labels reduce it.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Untested/not running is the norm; a cleaning and bushing service costs $150–400. Missing pendulum bob, key, bell or gong, broken springs, replaced hands, cracked or replaced glass, lifting veneer, missing finials, refinished cases. Mechanical service needed makes these "repair" items unless running for 8 days.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mid-20th-c Seth Thomas, New Haven, Howard Miller and Waltham "colonial" steeple and beehive reproductions with stamped-plate or electric movements (printed dials, printed lower pictures, no paper label or a modern one). Modern decorator "antique-style" quartz clocks. Marriages (wrong movement in an old case) are common; screw holes and filler blocks show it.

## Market
Weak and aging collector base; prices have dropped since the 2000s. Running, original examples sell; non-running clocks sit. Shipping costs and fragility deter many buyers; local sale is often better.

## What sellers usually miss
- Wagon-spring or fusee movements in an "old clock".
- Original Brewster & Ingrahams or Birge & Fuller labels on "steeple clocks".
- They usually overprice: old labels and "antique" titles get asks of $150–300 that sell at half.

## Shipping / handling
15–20" tall, 4–8 lb; remove pendulum and weights, secure the hammer, pad the movement and glass; box in box. $25–35 each way. Fits passenger seat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the label, movement front, dial, tablet and back; say clearly whether it runs and for how long; whether 30-hour or 8-day; whether key and pendulum included; disclose replaced parts.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$30 to buyer − ~$30 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Mechanical clocks needing service count as repairs: pass unless sold as running with video.
- Reproductions (printed dial and printed lower picture, no paper label): pass.
- 8-day beehive/double steeple with original label and tablet, running: buy at ≤ $80 if resale ≥ $250.
- 30-hour 19th-c steeples: rarely clear the $50 profit bar after $60 of shipping: pass unless ≤ $20.
