## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
"Vintage Christmas" covers a century of goods, but value concentrates in a few groups:
- **Pre-1940 German blown-glass figurals** (Lauscha): birds with spun-glass tails, fruit, Santas, clip-ons. Paper-thin glass, silvering inside, a pressed metal cap stamped "Germany". $20–300 each.
- **Dresden ornaments** (embossed gilded or silvered cardboard, 1880–1910): $100–1,000+.
- **Belsnickles and papier-mâché Santas, candy containers:** chalk/compo faces, mica-flecked coats, German. $200–2,000+.
- **Feather trees** (German goose-feather trees on red or white turned bases, 1900–1940): 2–3 ft $200–800.
- **Shiny Brite** (Max Eckardt, New York, 1937–1962): mass-produced US glass balls in printed boxes ("Shiny-Brite", Uncle Sam boxes in WWII with unsilvered glass and cardboard caps). A box of 12 sells $25–60. Indents, stencilled "Merry Christmas" and teardrops bring more.
- **Japanese 1930s–50s:** glass figurals, celluloid Santas and reindeer, Putz cardboard houses with mica "snow" (stamped "Japan" under the base) $15–60 each.
- **Lighting:** NOMA, Paramount and Royal bubble lights (1946–60s), Paramount plastic light-up Santas and stars, and figural milk-glass bulbs (1920s–30s Japan) $15–100+.
- **Ceramic trees:** 1960s–70s hobby-ceramic lighted trees with plastic bird or bulb lights on a music-box or lighted base. **Atlantic Mold** and **Holland Mold** marks are inside the base, sometimes with the hobbyist's name and year. 12–18" sizes sell $60–200 intact. The bases chip.
- **Blow molds** (Union Products, Empire, General Foam, 1960s–80s): too big for shipping. Local only.
- **Holt Howard, Lefton, Napco** (Japan, 1950s–60s): pixie Santas, Christmas candle-huggers, and "Holt Howard Merry Whiskers" pieces $20–80.
- **Low-value modern:** 1980s–2000s resin and polyresin "Victorian-style" ornaments, raffia-bowed craft ornaments, Jasco and Enesco ceramic trivets, Color Clings window clings, wreaths, Hallmark Keepsake (most $5–15, though a few early series sell for $30–100).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Shiny Brite** (1937–62; boxes say "Shiny-Brite" with a Santa on the lid, later boxes by Corning-made glass). WWII unsilvered pastel balls with paper caps are a sub-variety.
- **Rauch Industries** (NC, 1950s–2000s) and **Coby / National Tinsel**: common US balls. $10–25 a box.
- **Christopher Radko** (1986–): modern Polish glass. Retired pieces sell $20–150. These are not vintage, but sellers call them "vintage".
- **Dept 56** villages: most are low ($10–40), with exceptions.
- **NOMA / Paramount / Royal:** lighting. **Bradford Novelty:** plastic décor.
- **Atlantic Mold, Holland Mold, Duncan, Byron:** ceramic hobby moulds. The tree is valued by size, colour and intactness, not by mould maker.
- **Jasco** (Japan/Taiwan, 1970s–80s): ceramic trivets, music boxes, bisque bells. Low.
- **Color Clings Inc.** (USA, 1980s–90s): static window clings. Low.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old glass is extremely thin, with a hand-blown irregular shape, a silvered interior often faded or "foxed", and a metal cap with "Germany", "Made in Japan" or "Shiny Brite" on it. Modern glass (Poland, China) is thicker, with even paint and glitter glued on.
- Dresden: embossed gilded cardboard with fine detail, 3D-assembled.
- Papier-mâché: a moulded seam, chalky compo faces, real fabric or mica-flake trim. Resin is heavy, smooth and cold-cast.
- Ceramic trees: original small plastic "bird" or "twist" bulbs, a felt or cork base, a cord with an old-style switch. A tree that lights with an intact base is worth the most.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Glass: loss of silvering, paint loss, cracked or missing caps, broken spun-glass tails. Repainted figurals lose most of their value.
- Ceramic trees: chips on branch tips and base edges, missing bulbs (replacement bulbs are cheap but originals add value), cracked bases, rewired cords.
- Bubble lights: dead bulbs, missing fluid (counts as "untested electronics" for Dylan's rule).
- Paper and Dresden: crushing, foxing, water stains.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Christopher Radko, Inge-Glas and Old World Christmas (1980s–present) make faithful-looking German-style figurals. Read the cap and the box.
- Reproduction Belsnickles and feather trees (Bethany Lowe, others) are heavier, with modern paint and a resin feel.
- Shiny Brite was relaunched by Christopher Radko in 2001 ("Shiny Brite by Radko"). The boxes say Radko.
- "Victorian-style" cherub, angel and Santa ornaments in resin and craft materials are 1980s–2000s décor, not Victorian.

## Market
Strongly seasonal. Demand and prices rise from mid-September through mid-December and fall by half after Christmas. Volume is very high: tens of thousands of ornament and décor listings in Q4. Shiny Brite and ceramic trees have broad buyer pools. Dresden and Belsnickles go to specialist collectors and auction houses.

## What sellers usually miss
- **German pre-war figurals mixed into a "box of old ornaments"** (look for thin glass, birds with clip-on bases, odd shapes, worn silvering).
- **Dresden pieces** that look like "gold cardboard ornaments".
- **Japanese 1930s celluloid and putz houses** sold as "old village pieces".
- **Original boxes**: a Shiny Brite or bubble-light box can be worth as much as the contents.
- The reverse: sellers call 1980s–2000s craft ornaments "Victorian" or "vintage". The cherub, raffia and Santa-wreath type is low value.

## Shipping / handling
- Glass ornaments: very fragile. Double-box with every ornament wrapped. A box of 12 is about 2 lb, $12–18.
- Ceramic trees: 5–10 lb, fragile. Double-box with foam, $20–35. The tree fits a passenger seat but should not ship unless resale is over $120.
- Clings, trivets, flat items: $6–10.
- Wreaths and blow molds: bulky. Local only.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the maker and the cap or base marks ("Shiny Brite caps", "Germany" cap, "Japan" stamp, "Atlantic Mold 19xx"), the size, and the count.
- Show silvering and paint loss honestly, and say whether lights work (with a photo of them lit).
- List from late September to early December, and auction strong pieces the first two weeks of December.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). With glass ornaments costing ~$15 each way and ceramic trees ~$30 each way, buy only when the photo shows pre-1960 German, Japanese or US glass figurals or Dresden or papier-mâché in a mixed lot priced at ≤ $40, a boxed Shiny Brite or bubble-light set at ≤ $15, or a ceramic tree under 18" that is intact and lit, at ≤ $25 locally. Buy in September–November only. Pass on resin and craft "Victorian", clings, trivets, wreaths, Hallmark/Dept 56 lots, blow molds (size), and anything electric that is untested.
