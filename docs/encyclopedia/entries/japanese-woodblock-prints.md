## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Originals: Edo/Meiji prints (Hiroshige, Hokusai, Utamaro, Kunisada) on thin mulberry paper, visible woodgrain, publisher seal and censor seals in the margin; 20th-c. shin-hanga (Hasui, Yoshida, Koson) with publisher seal (Watanabe) and often a pencil signature. 1950s–80s reprints (Uchida, Adachi, tourist-shop "Utamaro" beauties) are crisp, bright, on heavier paper, often in bamboo frames – these are $20–60. Framed-with-mat pairs on craigslist are nearly always reprints. Formats: oban (~10x15"), chuban, triptychs (three oban panels). Also: offset/photomechanical reproductions (dot screen under magnification – no value).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Edo ukiyo-e** (Hokusai, Hiroshige, Utamaro, Kuniyoshi, Sharaku) – originals rare; later editions/re-cuts common.
- **Meiji** (Kunisada/Toyokuni III, Yoshitoshi, Chikanobu) – triptych panels are the accessible original tier; Yoshitoshi commands more (general knowledge, verify).
- **Shin-hanga** (1915–1950s): Kawase Hasui, Hiroshi Yoshida (self-published, pencil "jizuri" seal), Ohara Koson, Ito Shinsui – publisher Watanabe Shozaburo; seal style dates the edition (general knowledge, verify).
- **Sosaku-hanga** (creative prints: Munakata, Saito Kiyoshi) – signed, separate collector market.
- **Reprint publishers**: Uchida, Adachi, Unsodo, Takamizawa – legitimate woodblock re-editions, low value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original: thin washi, woodgrain and baren marks, slight registration variation, embossing (karazuri), faded fugitive colours (purples, reds) on Edo/Meiji; seals in margins (publisher, censor, date). Reprints: brighter colours, thicker paper, uniform impression, modern publisher seals. Offset reproductions: dot pattern. Trimmed margins reduce value. (general knowledge, verify)

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fading, toning, foxing, wormholes, trimmed margins, backing/laid-down sheets, glue stains from old mounts, tears and repairs; mat burn from acidic frames. Ask for unframed photos front and back, margins, seals closeup.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Re-cut blocks and later editions of Hiroshige/Hokusai; Meiji-era and 20th-c. reprints sold as Edo; photomechanical reproductions; posthumous/later Hasui editions with later Watanabe seals (worth less than lifetime editions). Bamboo-framed bright pairs titled "antique" are reprints.

## Market
Healthy eBay and auction market for shin-hanga and Meiji originals; reprints sell slowly and cheaply. Buyers: collectors (seal-savvy), decorators. Specialist dealers and auction houses (Bonhams, Ronin Gallery) set the top. (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
Publisher and date seals, and the artist. Conversely, sellers call reprints "antique" – check paper and seals before believing a title.

## Shipping / handling
Framed: $25–35, glass risk. Unframed: $10 flat in a rigid mailer. Ship unframed where possible.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State artist, title/series, publisher and seal type, edition (lifetime vs later), paper size, condition (fading, margins, backing). Photograph the full sheet unframed, seals and signature close, verso, any damage. Title keywords: "original Japanese woodblock print", "Hasui Watanabe seal", "Meiji triptych", "oban", "shin-hanga".

## Buy rule
Buy when: a print with a Watanabe/period publisher seal visible in the photo is under $100. Pass when: bamboo-framed bright pairs titled "antique" – they are reprints. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat.
