## Makers, brands and variants
### Quick ID checklist
- Name the object type and find its row below.
- Ask for (or zoom into) **every** photo in the "What to photograph" column. A front-only listing is unpriced.
- Decode each mark with the linked entry. When two entries apply (a gold watch has case marks and a movement serial), use both.
- Write the mark exactly as it reads into the item rubric (`marks_visible`, `identification`).
- Check that the date implied by the mark agrees with the construction (country-of-origin-patent-and-registration-marks; construction-and-tool-marks-for-dating).
- Only then apply the type entry's buy rule.

### Golden-egg tells
Each linked entry lists its own tells with multiples. The ones that come up most often across the catalogue:
- **Unread fineness marks on "gold tone" or "costume" jewelry** (56, 583, 585, 750, K18, 足金): ~20–100× (precious-metal-marks-reading-guide).
- **Solid gold watch case marks inside the back lid** (Helvetia head, squirrel, 0.585/0.750, plain 14K with no "filled"): melt alone runs $1,550–3,100 for a typical 14K hunter case (watch-case-marks).
- **Genuine factory or artist marks on "vase" or "bowl" listings**: from $10–60 for a look-alike to $1,000–150,000 for a period or artist mark (ceramic-and-glass-marks-that-multiply-value).
- **Period country marks** (Nippon, US Zone, Occupied Japan, the "England" only form, the registration diamond): ~1.5–5× over a later mark (country-of-origin-patent-and-registration-marks).
- **Edition, printer and first-state marks on prints, books, records and comics**: often the gap between a $10 copy and a $500+ first (art-backs-labels-and-printmaking-marks, book-photo-and-paper-ephemera-marks, records-comics-cards-and-media-identifiers; in progress).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
How a mark was applied is a quality and age tell across every object type: struck (sunk and crisp) vs cast-in (soft and raised); impressed or underglaze vs overglaze ink; engraved vs acid-stamped vs laser-etched; woven labels vs printed. Each linked entry gives the specific tells for its marks. The cross-type rule: a mark added after manufacture (overglaze stamp, later engraving, sticker) proves nothing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Marks hide in the places that wear first: bases, shank insides, clasps, backs. Polishing, grinding, relining, rebacking, re-tagging and replated surfaces remove or obscure them. Ask for raking-light photos before deciding a piece is unmarked. The detail for each object type is in the linked entries.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Every major mark family is faked, and each linked entry lists its own fakes: fake fineness stamps and Fabergé marks (precious-metal-marks-reading-guide), fake Meissen, Sèvres, Tiffany and Gallé marks (ceramic-and-glass-marks-that-multiply-value), fake Nippon and Occupied Japan marks (country-of-origin-patent-and-registration-marks), married watch cases (watch-case-marks), and later prints with forged signatures, record reissues and comic reprints (in-progress entries). The cross-type rule: a mark must agree with the material, the construction and the style. When it doesn't, believe the object.

## What sellers usually miss
- They photograph the front only.
- They don't open lids, case backs, drawers or battery doors.
- They read abbreviations and numbers (56, K18, DRGM, Rd No, runout numbers, Zippo codes) as "model numbers" or decoration.
- They call a piece "unmarked" when the mark is faint, on a sub-part, or was a label.
- The reverse error: they treat look-alike marks (Bavaria decorator, "Dresden", pseudo-hallmarks, "14K GF") as the real thing.

## Shipping / handling
Varies by object type: see each type entry. General rule: photograph every mark before packing, because buyers and returns disputes both turn on them.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Whatever the type: quote each mark exactly as it reads, say what it means and which reference supports that, and photograph each one square-on with a ruler. Put the decoded mark words in the title ("585", "Nippon", "US Zone", "first edition", "sterling"), because that is what collectors search for.
### What to tell the buyer
"Marks pictured: [exact wording]. These indicate [maker/metal/date/edition], per [reference]."

## Buy rule
Buy when: the marks have been seen and decoded with the linked entry, they agree with the object, and that entry's buy rule passes (profit ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways; Dylan's rule). Pass when: the marks can't be seen and the seller won't send photos, the marks contradict the object, or the item is a Nazi or other hate-symbol piece (policy kill). Must fit a passenger seat.
