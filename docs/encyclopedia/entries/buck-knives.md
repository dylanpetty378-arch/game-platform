## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
The Buck 110 Folding Hunter (introduced 1963–64) is a lockback with brass bolsters, dark wood (Macassar ebony, later Crelicam ebony/"Dymondwood") scales, 3 3/4" clip blade, 4 7/8" closed. The 112 Ranger is the smaller version (3" blade). Fixed blades: 119 Special, 120 General, 105 Pathfinder, with black phenolic handles and aluminium pommels.
Dating hinges on the blade tang stamp: requires a clear close-up photo.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Tang stamps for 110 (verify on Buck's site): early 1960s two-line "BUCK / U.S.A." variants; later three-line stamp with model number; ~1972–1986 single-line "BUCK 110 U.S.A."; 1986 onward a date-code symbol after "U.S.A." (each year a different symbol). A seller saying "1994" is likely reading a date-code chart → modern era.
- Pin count, "nail nick", bolster shape and Macassar vs Dymondwood scales help date. Brass bolsters with 3 pins per side on many vintage; Dymondwood (laminated) from ~1980s.
- Fixed blades: 1940s–60s Hoyt H. Buck and early Buck & Son (San Diego) knives are high-value.
- Imports & clones: Frost, Pakistan "Buck-style", Schrade LB7 (different, also collectible), Gerber/Case lockback lookalikes.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
420HC blade (modern), 440C (some vintage), brass bolsters, ebony/Dymondwood/Macassar scales. Original Buck sheath (black or brown leather, Buck embossed) and box add 20–50%.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Sharpened-down blades ("smiles"), loose lockup, blade play, bolster dents, scale cracks, pitting. Buck's lifetime warranty means many were factory-replaced with new blades/scales — check that marks match period.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Buck reissues: "110 50th anniversary", limited editions with early-style marks; Chinese counterfeits of 110 with poor stamping, aluminium-look bolsters. Check stamp crispness.
