## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ceramic pieces made to advertise a product, company or service, or carrying a company's logo as part of its service ware.
- **Beef-extract and tonic mugs (c.1890–1915):** small handled porcelain mugs or cups-and-saucers from Bavaria/Austria printed with a brand — Armour's Vigoral (Chicago, 1890s), Cudahy's Rex / Soma, Liebig Company's Extract of Meat, Bovril. Sold or given with the product for "beef tea". Triage: $20–50 each, no premium for pairs.
- **Calendar plates (c.1906–1929, peak 1909–1912):** a year calendar printed around a central scene, with a local merchant's name — US potteries and German blanks.
- **Restaurant, hotel, railroad and airline ware:** heavy vitrified china (Syracuse, Shenango, Buffalo, Hall, Sterling, Mayer, Scammell's Lamberton) with a logo; railroad dining-car china is the money end — "top-marked" (logo on the face) is worth much more than "back-stamped only".
- **Brand-character china:** Buster Brown, Campbell Kids, Kellogg's, Cracker Jack children's dishes and mugs.
- **Old Sleepy Eye:** Sleepy Eye Milling Co. (Minnesota) premiums made by Weir/Western Stoneware/Monmouth, blue-on-grey salt glaze with an Indian chief's profile, c.1899–1937 (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Beef extract:** Armour & Co. (Vigoral), Cudahy Packing (Rex, Soma), Liebig's Extract of Meat Co. (also famous for trade cards), Bovril (UK), Valentine's Meat Juice. Blanks from Bavarian/Austrian/Thuringian porcelain factories, often unmarked or with a small export mark.
- **Restaurant/hotel bodies:** Syracuse China (O.P.CO.), Shenango (New Castle PA), Buffalo China, Hall, Sterling, Scammell's Lamberton (B&O Centenary).
- **Railroad dining china:** B&O Centenary (Scammell's/Shenango; widely reproduced), Santa Fe Mimbreno, Great Northern Glory of the West / Mountains and Flowers, Union Pacific Harriman Blue, Pennsylvania RR Purple Laurel, Northern Pacific Yellowstone (general knowledge, verify).
- **Airline:** Pan Am, TWA, United, American (Rosenthal, Noritake, Hutschenreuther); first-class pieces.
- **Calendar plates:** various US potteries; local merchant imprint.

### Golden-egg tells
- Top-marked railroad dining-car china (Santa Fe Mimbreno, GN Mountains and Flowers, UP Harriman Blue, NP Yellowstone) — ~5–20× plain restaurant ware ($75–500+, general knowledge, verify) — railroad logo or pattern on the face plus railroad backstamp naming the road and the china maker.
- Old Sleepy Eye original (Monmouth/Western Stoneware, pre-1937) — $200–1,000+ vs $20–40 for the many reproductions (general knowledge, verify) — salt-glaze blue-on-grey; originals have crisp, fine detail and correct maker marks; repros are smoother with a modern glaze and often reddish clay.
- Brand-character china (Buster Brown, Campbell Kids, early Kellogg's) — ~2–4× a generic advertising mug ($50–150, general knowledge, verify) — the character printed on children's plates/mugs, maker backstamp (e.g. German or Buffalo Pottery).
- Calendar plates with scarce years (1906–1908) or unusual towns/states — ~2–3× a 1910 common plate (general knowledge, verify) — year in the centre or border; local merchant name.
- Beef-extract mugs with full brand text and intact gilt (Vigoral, Cudahy Rex, Liebig) — $20–50 vs $5–10 for faded or chipped (triage) — crisp transfer, gilt rim, no hairlines.
- Airline first-class china by Rosenthal/Noritake with logo (Pan Am, TWA) — $25–80 vs $5–15 for logo coach ware (general knowledge, verify) — airline backstamp with the china maker.

### Quick ID checklist
- Face photo: logo on the front (top-marked) or only a backstamp?
- Backstamp: china maker (Syracuse, Shenango, Buffalo, Rosenthal), railroad/airline name, date code.
- Body: heavy vitrified restaurant china vs thin Bavarian porcelain (c.1900 premium) vs salt-glazed stoneware (Old Sleepy Eye).
- Calendar plate: read the year and merchant.
- Damage: rim chips, hairlines, worn gilt, utensil marks on restaurant ware.
- Any caricature of Black people (or other racist imagery) → policy kill.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bavarian/Austrian hard-paste porcelain (beef-extract mugs) — thin, translucent, transfer printed and gilt; vitrified restaurant china — thick, heavy, chip-resistant, decal or underglaze printed; salt-glaze stoneware — orange-peel surface with cobalt. Old pieces show utensil scratching and crazing; new repros are pristine with modern decal edges.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, hairlines, worn transfers and gilt, staining under the glaze (restaurant ware), restored chips on railroad china (check under UV). Condition drives price strongly for mugs and plates.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Old Sleepy Eye (pitchers, mugs, steins) heavily reproduced since the 1950s–2000s; B&O Centenary and other railroad patterns reproduced (Shenango reissues and fakes with spurious marks); fake airline and Coca-Cola restaurant ware with decals.

## Market
Advertising and railroadiana collectors are active; beef-extract mugs and calendar plates are slow (4–8 weeks). Railroad dining china sells fast to collectors when top-marked.

## What sellers usually miss
That railroad china is only valuable with the right marks (and that unmarked "railroad china" is just restaurant ware); top-marked pieces mixed into restaurant china lots; Old Sleepy Eye vs repro differences.

## Shipping / handling
Mugs and plates: box-in-box with bubble wrap, $8–12. Fragile.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Face and backstamp photos, maker, date or date code, size, chips/hairlines (check with light), crazing.
### What to tell the buyer
"Antique advertising mug for [Armour's Vigoral], c.1900, German/Austrian porcelain, [dimensions]. Transfer and gilt [condition]; no chips or hairlines found under strong light. Backstamp pictured."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $10 − $10 − price ≥ max($50, price). Beef-extract mugs and calendar plates at $20–50 sold can't clear $50 profit singly — lots only at ≤ $5 per piece. Top-marked railroad china: buy ≤ $25 when sold median ≥ $100. Old Sleepy Eye: only with clear photos of the marks and body, ≤ $40. Kill racist imagery (policy). Fits easily.
