## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Gold and silver from India to the Levant, marked in Western numerals, Arabic-Indic digits, Persian digits, Hebrew, or with state symbols (BIS triangle, tughra, lion and sun, cat, lotus). Sellers who can't read the digits or the symbol call it "brass", "gold tone", "white metal", "metal tray" or "tribal jewelry".

**Arabic-Indic digits (learn them — they are the whole trick):** ٠ = 0 (a dot) · ١ = 1 · ٢ = 2 · ٣ = 3 · ٤ = 4 · ٥ = 5 (a small circle) · ٦ = 6 · ٧ = 7 (a V) · ٨ = 8 (an inverted V) · ٩ = 9. Persian forms differ for 4, 5, 6: ۴ ۵ ۶ (general knowledge, verify). So:
- **٢١ = 21K** (reads like "r1"), **٢٢ = 22K** ("rr"), **١٨ = 18K** ("1Λ"), **٢٤ = 24K**.
- **٨٧٥ = 875 (21K)** ("ΛVo"), **٧٥٠ = 750 (18K)** ("Vo·"), **٩١٦ = 916 (22K)** — reads like "917" to a Western eye, **٩٩٩ = 999**.
- Silver: **٨٤ = 84** (Persian/Russian zolotnik standard, .875), **٩٠ = 90** (.900), **٨٠ = 80** (.800), **٦٠ = 60** (.600, Egyptian).

**India:**
- **22K "916"** — 22/24 = 91.6% gold. The usual Indian jewelry grade; colour a deep warm yellow. Also 18K "750", 14K "585", and 24K coins/bars "999" (BIS grades per IIFL: 22K916, 18K750).
- **BIS hallmark (current, "HUID" format)** — three marks together: the **BIS triangle** logo, the **purity grade** (e.g. 22K916, 18K750), and a **6-character alphanumeric HUID** unique to that article and recorded in the BIS database; "HUID-based hallmarking became the standard from 2021"; mandatory hallmarking covers 380 districts as of March 2026; pre-2021 stock must be re-hallmarked before resale in covered districts; verify any HUID free in the **BIS CARE** app (IIFL).
- **Older BIS hallmark (2000–2021)** — BIS triangle + fineness (916, 750, 585) + assay-and-hallmarking-centre logo + a year letter + the jeweller's mark; the year letter was dropped c.2017 (general knowledge, verify; IIFL confirms only that the older system marked the jeweller and centre).
- **"KDM"** — not a purity mark: it means the piece was soldered with a cadmium alloy, a 1990s selling point for "22K all through" before BIS hallmarking; cadmium solder is now banned in India; a KDM stamp says nothing certified about karat (general knowledge, verify). Still a strong hint of 22K Indian work.
- **Unmarked older Indian gold** — pre-2000 family jewelry is very often unmarked or marked only with the jeweller's initials or "22" / "916" in tiny digits (general knowledge, verify). Test it.
- **Indian silver** — modern BIS silver grades 999/970/925/900/835/800 (general knowledge, verify).

**Colonial Indian silver (c.1780–1947):**
- **Hamilton & Co., Calcutta** (from the early 19th c.): English-style marks with a maker's mark, a **tiger or elephant** punch and other pseudo-marks; the **elephant** punch was misread in early publications as an Inverness town mark (Lyon & Turnbull footnote, 2025); tiger mark general knowledge, verify. Butter coolers, tea wares, presentation pieces.
- **P. Orr & Sons, Madras** — "swami" silver with applied Hindu deities on a chased ground; also Indian-pattern hollowware (general knowledge, verify).
- **Kutch (Bhuj) — Oomersi Mawji** and family: dense chased hunting and floral scenes, figural handles; marked **"O.M"** (Bonhams 2021). The collected names; much "Kutch-style" is unsigned.
- **Kashmir (Srinagar), Lucknow, Cutch, Bombay** engraved and chased silver; **Cooke & Kelvey**, Calcutta, retailer (general knowledge, verify).
- **Burmese silver** sits with Southeast Asia: see `east-and-southeast-asian-gold-silver-marks`.
- Fineness is often near rupee silver (.917) and is not assayed (general knowledge, verify).

**Pakistan and Bangladesh:**
- Gold is sold by the **tola (11.66 g)** and grams, mostly 22K and 21K; hallmarking is not compulsory in Pakistan and stamps are jeweller's own (21K, 22K, 875, 916) (general knowledge, verify).
- Bangladesh grades jewelry as 22K, 21K, 18K and "traditional" (sonaton, lower karat) under BAJUS pricing; BSTI hallmarking applies (general knowledge, verify).

**Gulf gold (Saudi, UAE/Dubai, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman):**
- **21K (875)** is the classic Gulf grade — "a rich, saturated yellow… deeper than 18K", stored-wealth and bridal (mahr) jewelry, priced primarily by weight and fineness with modest making charges (Sky Jems, 21K). Dubai sells 24K/22K/21K/18K (general knowledge, verify).
- Stamps: **21K / 21Kt / 875**, **18K / 750**, **999** for 24K; **SA** = Kingdom of Saudi Arabia compliance; a **factory/maker logo** (QZS Saudi). Arabic-Indic forms ٢١ / ٨٧٥ / ٧٥٠ / ١٨ appear on older and on some current pieces (general knowledge, verify).
- **Kuwait** — the Ministry of Commerce assay office stamps karat (often 21/22/18) with a state mark (general knowledge, verify). **Bahrain** — the government assay office marks, a stronger guarantee (Sky Jems Bahrain page seen in search, not fetched).
- Typical forms: wide bangles in sets, "coin" necklaces (Saudi/Gulf sovereign-style), mesh "Turkish/Saudi" necklaces, Dubai bridal sets.

**Ottoman Empire (to 1922) — tughra and sah marks:**
- The **tughra** is the sultan's calligraphic monogram, struck on silver (and some gold) at the assay office as a guarantee of standard; each sultan's tughra differs, so it **dates the piece to a reign** (Pushkin dates its Aznavour tray, 2,730 g, 900 standard, to Abdulhamid II, 1876–1909). Many tughras include a **regnal-year numeral** and the accession year (general knowledge, verify).
- **Sah / sahh (صح, "correct")** — a small companion stamp confirming the standard, often found near the tughra; Pushkin calls it "rare" (Pushkin; meaning general knowledge, verify).
- Reigns to know: Mahmud II 1808–39 · Abdulmecid I 1839–61 · Abdulaziz 1861–76 · Murad V 1876 · Abdulhamid II 1876–1909 · Mehmed V Reşad 1909–18 · Mehmed VI 1918–22 (general knowledge, verify).
- Tughra silver is typically .900 (Pushkin tray) and was also used in Egypt before 1916 (silvercollection.it) and in Ottoman Balkans/Levant. **Large trays, bowls, hammam bowls, coffee-cup holders (zarf), rosewater sprinklers** — often listed as "metal", "tray", "white metal Turkish tray".
- **Aznavour** pattern — a named 19th-c. Istanbul decoration (Pushkin).
- After 1923 Turkish silver uses numerals (900, 925) and assay stamps (general knowledge, verify).

**Persia / Iran:**
- **84** — Persian silver commonly marked **84**, the Russian zolotnik standard = **875/1000** (Leland Little lot cites an 84 mark for 875 fineness); **90** = .900 (general knowledge, verify; Sky Jems says Persian silver "most commonly bore 800 or 900 fineness marks").
- **Lion and sun** — a lion in profile holding a sword with a stylised sun rising behind, in a small circular or oval cartouche; the Qajar imperial symbol continued under Reza Shah after 1925; ended with the 1979 Revolution, so a **lion-and-sun piece is pre-1979**; post-1979 marks use Islamic-republican calligraphic designs, with hybrid late-1970s marks (Sky Jems, Persian). Assay offices: Tehran, Isfahan, Shiraz and others; some pieces add a date, assay-master initial, bazaar or workshop seal (Sky Jems).
- **Isfahan engraved silver** — the technique is **qalamzani** (chased/engraved pen-work with figures, hunting scenes, Persepolis and Safavid miniature motifs). Note: *qalamkar* is Isfahan's block-printed textile, so a seller saying "qalamkar silver" usually means qalamzani (general knowledge, verify). Masters' signatures in Persian script on the piece: **Bagher Parvaresh** (Leland Little tray), Lahiji, Vartan Kalustian and others (Lahiji/Vartan general knowledge, verify). Signed master pieces carry a large premium.
- 21K "عيار ٢١" also appears on Iranian gold (Sky Jems, Persian).

**Egypt (silvercollection.it unless noted):**
- Pre-1916: Ottoman tughra; early marks a "lion" with the town name under the fineness.
- **1916: British-style date-letter system** — a **standing cat facing left** in a cut-corner square for silver; the town name above two Hindu-Arabic numerals giving fineness; **date letters in Latin capitals from 1916 (first letter R)**, switching to **Arabic letters from 1941**.
- **Gold: the ibis** (occasionally used for silver c.1920). **Correction to the brief: the lotus is the silver mark from October 1946, not a gold mark** (cat still seen to at least 1949). **From 1966: "lotus with dots"** at the sides, with dots added to the date letters.
- Fineness in hundredths (60, 80, 90) then, possibly after 1975, thousandths (600, 800, 900); 925 more recently.
- Town marks: Cairo, Alexandria, Assiut, Beni Souef, Mansura, Qena, Tanta, Zagazig.
- Most US finds: 1950s–80s souvenir silver (cartouche pendants, scarab bracelets, filigree) at .800/.900, and 18K gold cartouches with the ibis/ fineness (general knowledge, verify).

**Israel:**
- **925** (sterling) and **935** (a slightly higher standard used by some Israeli makers; general knowledge, verify). "Sterling Israel", "925 Israel", Hebrew maker marks. Mid-century Judaica: Hadad Brothers, Yemenite filigree workshops in Jerusalem (general knowledge, verify).
- **Bezalel School of Arts and Crafts, Jerusalem (founded 1906; the classic metalwork period c.1906–1929)** — filigree and repoussé Judaica (megillah cases, mezuzot, spice boxes, Hanukkah lamps, jewelry) marked **"Bezalel" in Hebrew** (Kestenbaum lot: "Bezalel" in Hebrew on the pull; founding date general knowledge, verify). Bezalel brass and copper pieces are also collected but are not precious metal.

**Yemenite and Bedouin silver:**
- **Yemenite (largely Jewish silversmiths before the 1949–50 exodus)** — fine filigree and granulation, box-spacer necklaces, **lazim** bridal necklaces, labbeh chokers, often gilt and set with coral or glass; silver commonly from melted **Maria Theresa thalers (.833)** (general knowledge, verify). Dorotheum sold two restrung Yemeni bead-and-box necklaces for €375 (2017).
- **Bedouin (Sinai, Negev, Jordan, Saudi, Oman)** — heavier, coarser work, coin-hung necklaces, amulet boxes (hirz), anklets; silver content varies widely from good silver to low-grade alloy (Dorotheum calls one necklace "good silver alloy") — test before paying silver money (general knowledge, verify).
- **Omani** silver khanjar and kohl pots are covered under `middle-eastern-daggers` (general knowledge, verify that entry's scope).

**Afghan, Turkmen and Uzbek:**
- **Turkmen Tekke (Teke) and Yomut** jewelry, late 19th–early 20th c.: **parcel-gilt silver** (gilt fire-gilded bands on silver) with **carnelian** and glass/paste; armbands (bilezik), triangular amulet holders (tumar/tumors), necklets (bukov), dorsal plait ornaments, headdress plates. **Unmarked** — value rests on form, gilding, carnelian setting quality and tribe (Bonhams 2024 group: Teke armbands, tumar and bukov plus a Yomut plait ornament, £1,280).
- **Uzbek (Bukhara, Khiva)** — silver-gilt with turquoise and coral, temple pendants, tilla-kosh brow bands; often lower-grade silver (general knowledge, verify).
- **Afghan "Kuchi" / tribal** jewelry sold in the US is mostly 20th-century and tourist alloy (low silver or white metal) with glass "lapis"; the old collected Afghan/Turkmen pieces are the minority (general knowledge, verify).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Indian 22K "916" or BIS-triangle gold listed as "gold tone"** — 3–10× the asking; melt ≈ $124/g (computed $4,203/oz ÷ 31.103 × 0.916) — deep warm yellow, BIS triangle with 22K916 and a 6-character code, or bare "916", "22K", "KDM"; a 20 g bangle pair ≈ $2,480 of gold.
- **Gulf 21K with Arabic digits ٢١ / ٨٧٥ (or 22K ٩١٦ read as "917")** — melt ≈ $118/g (21K, computed) — V-and-circle digit shapes on the clasp tag; Saudi "SA" or a factory logo; heavy bangles in sets.
- **Ottoman tughra on a "metal tray" or "white metal bowl"** — melt is the floor: a 2,730 g .900 tray (Pushkin, Abdulhamid II) holds ≈ $4,800 of silver (2,730 × .900 × $1.953/g, computed) — calligraphic monogram punch with a small sah stamp, Ottoman floral (Aznavour) chasing.
- **Persian 84 / 90 with lion-and-sun on "Middle Eastern metal" pieces** — melt ≈ $1.71/g (84) and $1.76/g (90); signed Isfahan masters (Parvaresh, Lahiji) bring several × melt (general knowledge, verify; Leland Little 2019 22.1 ozt Parvaresh tray, price not shown) — fine figural qalamzani engraving, Persian-script signature in a cartouche, ۸۴ digits.
- **Kutch silver by Oomersi Mawji ("O.M")** — ≈ 8× melt: £5,737.50 for a 515 g jug (Bonhams 2021) vs ≈ $905 melt (515 × .900 × $1.953) — dense chased jungle/hunt scenes, figural goats and peri on the handle.
- **Hamilton & Co. Calcutta** — ≈ 1.4× melt for plain pieces: £1,386 for a 23.4 oz butter cooler, c.1820 (L&T 2025; melt ≈ $1,300 at .917, computed) — English-looking marks with an elephant or tiger punch; mistaken for British provincial silver.
- **Bezalel Jerusalem filigree Judaica** — $3,000 for a 13.5 cm megillah case (Kestenbaum 1999) — Hebrew "בצלאל" stamp, fine filigree with bosses and a crown.
- **Turkmen Tekke parcel-gilt silver with carnelian** — unmarked, so sellers call it "tribal costume"; £1,280 for a six-piece group (Bonhams 2024) — fire-gilt bands on silver, cabochon carnelians in raised collets, triangular tumar amulets, wide armbands.
- **Egyptian cat-mark (1916–46) silver hollowware** — melt plus a dating premium (general knowledge, verify) — left-facing cat in a cut-corner square, Latin date letter, town name.
- **Yemenite lazim bridal necklaces (gilt, coral, granulation)** — collector premium over the €375-type bead necklace (Dorotheum 2017; lazim premium general knowledge, verify; Michael Backman offers one at https://michaelbackmanltd.com/object/large-yemeni-bridal-gilded-silver-coral-necklace-lazim, not fetched).

### Quick ID checklist
- Get a **gram weight** and a **macro photo of every stamp**.
- Triangle + "22K916" + 6 letters/digits → BIS-hallmarked Indian 22K; check the HUID in BIS CARE.
- "916" / "KDM" / "22K" alone → probably Indian 22K, test.
- V-shapes, circles, dots and r-shapes (٢١ ٨٧٥ ٧٥٠ ٩١٦) → Arabic-Indic karat digits: 21K, 875, 750, 916.
- "SA" + 21K/875 → Saudi gold.
- A swirling calligraphic monogram punch → Ottoman tughra → name the sultan, date the reign.
- 84 or ۸۴ / 90, or a lion with a sword and rising sun → Persian silver, pre-1979 if lion-and-sun.
- Cat in a square → Egyptian silver 1916–49; ibis → Egyptian gold; lotus → Egyptian silver after 1946; dots → after 1966.
- Hebrew letters בצלאל → Bezalel; "935" or "925 Israel" → Israeli silver.
- Unmarked gilt-and-carnelian armbands/amulets → Turkmen; check gilding and silver content.
- English-style marks with an elephant/tiger and no British duty/date logic → Calcutta colonial (Hamilton etc.); "O.M" → Oomersi Mawji.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Melt values (computed at $4,203/oz gold, $60.73/oz silver; ≈ $135.1/g pure gold, $1.953/g pure silver):** 24K ≈ $135/g · 22K/916 ≈ $124/g · 21K/875 ≈ $118/g · 18K/750 ≈ $101/g · silver 925 ≈ $1.81/g · 917 ≈ $1.79/g · 900 (Ottoman, Persian 90) ≈ $1.76/g · 875 (Persian 84) ≈ $1.71/g · 833 (thaler silver) ≈ $1.63/g · 800 ≈ $1.56/g · 600 (Egyptian 60) ≈ $1.17/g.
- **High-karat gold** (21–22K) is soft and orange-yellow; Indian pieces often have hollow construction (lac- or wax-filled bangles, hollow beads) — the weight includes the filling; ask whether filled (general knowledge, verify). Kundan/polki pieces include stones and lac — melt only the gold weight.
- **Gilding:** Turkmen fire-gilding sits in bands and wears on high points; modern copies use electroplate that is uniform and bright.
- **Silver grade:** tribal and souvenir silver ranges from .900 to base metal — test any piece bought for silver value.
- **Old vs new:** genuine tughra and assay punches are struck into the metal with crisp edges; cast-in "marks" on reproduction trays are soft and part of the casting.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Gold: dents in hollow bangles, broken clasps, worn stamps — melt unaffected except for filled/hollow pieces whose gold weight is unknown.
- Hollowware: dents, splits in thin-gauge trays, re-soldered feet and handles, removed inscriptions; Persian engraving worn by polishing.
- Tribal jewelry: missing carnelians/pendants, restrung necklaces (Dorotheum lot restrung), replaced chains, married parts from several pieces.
- Ask for weight, whether the piece is filled, and a photo of the back and every punch.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Fake BIS stamps** on underkarat gold — the HUID check in BIS CARE exposes them (IIFL).
- **Underkarat Gulf/Indian "22K"** without an assay mark — test (general knowledge, verify).
- **Reproduction Ottoman/Persian silver** — modern pieces with cast or invented tughras and lion-and-sun marks, sometimes silver-plated copper (general knowledge, verify).
- **Egyptian souvenir pieces** with genuine modern marks are not fakes but are low value.
- **Modern Turkmen/Afghan copies** — plated base metal with glass "carnelian", made for the tourist trade; Kuchi assemblages (general knowledge, verify).
- **"Bezalel style" filigree** — later Jerusalem workshops copied the style without the mark.

## Market
- **Indian and Gulf gold:** large, fast, spot-linked; South Asian and Arab diaspora buyers, gold stackers and refiners; sells in days at near melt. Wedding season (Indian Oct–Feb, Diwali/Akshaya Tritiya peaks) lifts demand (general knowledge, verify).
- **Ottoman/Persian/Egyptian silver:** steady at melt-plus on eBay; signed and early pieces go to Islamic-art auctions (Bonhams, Christie's, Sotheby's, Pushkin as dealer).
- **Colonial Indian silver:** specialist UK collector market (L&T held a single-owner Indian silver collection in 2025); Kutch is the strongest name.
- **Judaica:** dedicated auction houses (Kestenbaum, Greenstein, Kedem, Genazym); Bezalel is a steady name.
- **Tribal (Turkmen, Yemenite, Bedouin):** niche ethnographic buyers; slow on eBay, better at tribal-art sales (Dorotheum, Bonhams, tribalart.de).

## What sellers usually miss
- "916", "KDM", the BIS triangle and HUID — listed as "gold tone Indian costume".
- Arabic-Indic digits ٢١ / ٨٧٥ / ٩١٦ — read as decoration or "917".
- Tughra punches — listed as "metal tray", "brass-colour tray", "white metal Middle Eastern bowl".
- 84 / ۸۴ and the lion-and-sun — listed as "Indian metal" or "Persian brass".
- Egyptian cat mark — read as an animal decoration.
- Hamilton & Co elephant/tiger punches — read as British provincial silver.
- Hebrew "Bezalel" — listed as "filigree box".
- Turkmen gilt silver — listed as "tribal costume jewelry, brass and glass".

## Shipping / handling
Jewelry: padded mailer/small box $5–6, insured with signature for gold over $200. All within passenger-seat size except the very largest trays (check dimensions; 68 cm trays still fit a seat).

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ship out ($6 jewelry, $15–30 trays) − ship in (same) − price ≥ max($50, price) (Dylan's rule). For melt-priced metal: **price ≤ (0.87 × melt − both shipping) ÷ 2 ≈ 40% of melt**. Example: 10 g 22K chain, melt ≈ $1,240 → pay ≤ (1,079 − 12) ÷ 2 ≈ $533. Example: 2 kg tughra tray at .900, melt ≈ $3,515 → pay ≤ (3,058 − 50) ÷ 2 ≈ $1,500. Pay more only for a named collector type with a cited sold comp (Kutch, Hamilton/Orr, Bezalel, signed Isfahan master, Teke gilt silver). Pass on: unweighed gold, filled/hollow bangles priced as solid, unmarked tribal "silver" without a test, souvenir Egyptian pieces at collector prices, dealer-retail pricing.
