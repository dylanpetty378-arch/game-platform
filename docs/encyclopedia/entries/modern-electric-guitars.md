# Modern electric guitars (Kramer, Fender/Squier, Gibson/Epiphone, Ibanez, ESP/LTD, Jackson, Yamaha, Cort, Samick) — 2000s–present, import and budget US

Slug: modern-electric-guitars · Aliases: electric guitar, shredder, Floyd Rose guitar, Strat copy, Les Paul copy, MIJ guitar, Kramer Pacer
Summary: No-name/First Act $30–80; budget imports (Squier, Epiphone, Cort, Samick, GIO) $100–200; Pacer Classic, MIM Fender, Ibanez RG, Pacifica 612 $300–600; Japanese/US Fender/Gibson/Ibanez Prestige/PRS/1980s Kramer $800–3,000+.
Buy rule (short): Buy Japanese- or US-made guitars priced as imports (≤ $200) or anything at ≤ 25% of Reverb used; pass current imports at ≥ 40% of used value.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Headstock logo + body shape. Kramer (Gibson-owned since 1997): pointy "banana"/hockey-stick headstock, Pacer Classic (two humbuckers, Floyd Rose Special, metallic finishes, made in Indonesia/China, new $500–650) vs 1980s US/Japan Pacer (Neptune NJ, ESP-made, Floyd Rose original, $600–2,000). Cort, Samick/Greg Bennett, Yamaha Pacifica, Squier, Epiphone, Ibanez GIO, Jackson JS, ESP LTD 10–200 series = budget imports, used 35–50% of new. The money in modern guitars is Japanese-made (Fender Japan, Ibanez Prestige, ESP Japan), US Fender/Gibson, and discontinued cult models (Yamaha SG, Ibanez RG550 '87–'94 Japan). Sellers usually know Gibson/Fender; they do not know "Pacer Vintage vs Pacer Classic" or "MIJ vs MIC". Sellers' words: "electric guitar", "shredder", "Floyd Rose".

Where to look: country of origin on the back of the headstock or neck plate; serial number on headstock back or neck plate dates it (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Kramer**: 1980s US/Japan Pacer (Neptune NJ; Deluxe/Imperial $700–1,500) vs modern Pacer Classic (Indonesia/China, used $300–450) and Baretta Special (budget, $100–200).
- **Fender / Squier**: Squier budget ($100–200); Fender MIM Player ($400–550); Fender Japan and US top tier.
- **Gibson / Epiphone**: Epiphone LP Special (budget), Epiphone Standard (collectible tier); Gibson US top.
- **Ibanez**: GIO (budget), RG standard (collectible tier), Prestige/JEM (top), RG550 '87–'94 Japan (cult).
- **Jackson JS, ESP LTD 10–200**: budget imports; ESP Japan top.
- **Yamaha**: budget Pacificas; Pacifica 612 (collectible tier); Yamaha SG (cult).
- **Cort, Samick/Greg Bennett**: budget imports ($120–250 used).
- **PRS core** (US): top tier.
- **First Act, Rogue, Glarry, no-name copies**: junk tier. Silvertone Pro (modern budget reissue brand) also low (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bolt-on vs set neck; solid vs laminated (plywood) body on budget guitars; Floyd Rose "original" (licensed plate, German/US) vs "Floyd Rose Special" vs no-name locking tremolo; branded pickups (Seymour Duncan, DiMarzio, EMG) — often aftermarket upgrades that add some value (general knowledge, verify). Gloss nitro finishes on vintage/top models; poly on imports. Quality tells: tight neck pocket, even fret ends, country of origin.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Headstock cracks/breaks (the classic killer, especially on Gibson-style angled headstocks), neck relief/warp, worn frets, truss rod maxed out, electronics crackle, Floyd Rose knife-edge wear, missing trem arm, refinishes, replaced pickups/parts (lower value on vintage, neutral on budget). Ask for: headstock back and neck heel photos, a sound clip, whether truss rod adjusts (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese counterfeit Gibson/Fender/PRS are common on marketplaces — tells: wrong headstock shape/logo, serial format, bolt-on neck on a "Les Paul", cheap hardware, price too good (general knowledge, verify). "Partscasters" (mixed parts) sold as genuine. Modern Kramer reissues ("Kramer Original Collection") vs genuine 1980s: check country of origin and hardware.

## Market
Very liquid on Reverb, eBay and local (Facebook Marketplace, Guitar Center trade-in); buyers are players first, collectors for MIJ/1980s/US (general knowledge, verify). Budget imports are flooded and price-sensitive; local sales avoid the $100+ round-trip shipping. Holiday season and tax-refund season tend to lift demand (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Country of manufacture and series name. Headstock back/neck plate says "Made in Japan/USA". A "Kramer Pacer" title can be a $350 Indonesian Classic or a $1,200 1985 Neptune NJ – the pointy headstock with "Kramer" in script, brass-era hardware, and the Floyd Rose "original" licensed plate are the tells.

## Shipping / handling
10–14 lb in a box; $50–80 each way with a case/gig bag; fragile (headstocks break). Loosen strings, pad the headstock, use a guitar box. Reverb ~10% + payment. Shipping both ways eats $100+, so only sub-$200 guitars worth $500+ clear Dylan's rule.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State make, model, year (serial), country of origin, pickups and tremolo type, weight, neck profile if known, fret wear, action/setup and tuning, any mods, case/gig bag included. Photograph front, back, headstock front and back (serial), neck plate/heel, frets, electronics cavity if mods. Title keywords: "1986 Kramer Pacer Imperial USA Floyd Rose", "Fender Japan ... MIJ", "Ibanez RG550 MIJ".

## Buy rule
Buy when: a Japanese- or US-made instrument is priced as an import (≤ $200) – check neck plate/headstock back; or any guitar at ≤ 25% of Reverb used. Pass when: current import model at ≥ 40% of used value (Kramer Pacer Classic $160 = pass; profit ~$80 after $100 shipping, below the $160 required). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (a guitar in a case does).
