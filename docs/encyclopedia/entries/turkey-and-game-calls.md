## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Wooden box calls (a lidded box with a paddle), pot/slate calls (a round pot with a striker), and duck/goose calls (wood or plastic barrels). The pivoted-lid box call was patented by Henry C. Gibson of Arkansas in 1897, and the basic design is unchanged (onX).
- Mass-market makers (Quaker Boy, Primos, Knight & Hale, Lynch pre-2000) are worth $10–30.
- The collectible ones are hand-made by named callmakers:
  - M.L. Lynch early (Birmingham AL, pre-1960s)
  - Neil Cost, Tom Turpin, Ben Rodgers Lee, Earl Mickel, D.D. Adams, Lamar Williams
  - Ike Ashby (Mississippi, "Ashby Gobbler Box", signed and numbered)
- Collectible duck calls: Olt, Faulk's, Cajun, early Reelfoot (Johnny Marsh, Earl Dennison), $100–2,000. The most sought-after are 19th/early-20th-century Illinois River and Reelfoot Lake calls (Glodo, Beckhart, Perdew, Allen, Ditto, Fuller) (DU "Legends of the Call").
- Duck call anatomy: barrel + insert (tone board) + reed + wedge. Reelfoot style = barrel with extended tone board, hand checkering, often a carved tier; Arkansas style (popularised by Olt) = one-piece insert, straight reed, curved tone board.
- Seller words: "old turkey call", "box call", "duck call". Look-alikes: modern "heritage" reissues printed with old model names, and decorative carved calls never meant to be blown.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **M.L. Lynch** (Michael Leroy Lynch, homebuilder; first box call 1940; Birmingham/Homewood AL; some early calls inscribed with his Edgewood Boulevard address): "World Champion" box calls. Early boxes (pre-1960s) are collectible. Later production, through the 1980s, is mid-range. Modern Lynch is mass-market. Company history for dating: Alabama 1940s–1960s; sold to Allen Jenkins 1970 and moved to Liberty, Mississippi; moved to Thomasville, Georgia in the 1990s; Amish-made in Pennsylvania from 2013; bought by Johanna Gaines 2021 (Boston). Models: No. 101 "Fool Proof" (single-sided box), No. 102 "World Champion" (two-sided box, gobbles using rubber bands), No. 100 "Jet Slate". The Squirrel Chatter Box (12 holes, cotton-filled) is very rare; most sold above $6,000 (onX). The location printed on the call/box (Birmingham AL vs Liberty MS vs Thomasville GA) is the first dating clue.
- **Neil "Gobbler" Cost** (1923–2002; born Fort Sill OK; lived his last decades in Greenwood, South Carolina): hand-made boxes, highly collected; Cost-Lynch pieces are the top (unverified, verify). Started selling in the late 1950s under the "Gob-El-R" name; 1960s shop "The Gobble Shop". Each call carries a quirky hand inscription naming the wood plus a pithy saying. Models: "Old Hen" box (best known), boat paddles, scratch boxes, single-sided boxes, slates, a few yelpers. Market note: calls that sold for four figures on eBay ~20 years ago now bring no more than about half; collector interest peaked c.2000 (Sporting Classics).
- **Tom Turpin** (Memphis TN): made turkey calls before starting duck calls in the 1920s; Reelfoot-style duck calls built longer than normal, preferred South American woods (DU).
- **Ben Rodgers Lee** ("Lee's Champion Gobble Box", Wild Turkey Archives), **Earl Mickel, D.D. Adams, Lamar Williams**: named callmakers.
- **Ike Ashby**: signed, numbered gobbler boxes, often with a letter; $100–250 with the letter.
- **Duck calls**: P.S. Olt (Pekin, Illinois, 1904+; adjustable-tone patent 1905; D-2 duck and A-50 goose are the common models; early hard rubber, later wood and moulded acrylic), Faulk's (Lake Charles, Louisiana; Patin and Dud Faulk, cane calls from the mid-1930s, later wood and early plastic), Cajun, Reelfoot-style checkered calls (Tennessee: Johnny Marsh, Earl Dennison) (general knowledge, verify for dates).
- **Rare early duck/goose calls**: Victor Glodo (Reelfoot Lake from the early 1890s; copper reeds, "duck wing" checkering, classic Reelfoot barrel shape), J.T. Beckhart (Big Lake AR, early 1900s–1920s; monel reeds, four carved tiers, hand checkering; extremely rare), Charles Perdew (Henry IL; checkered walnut, carved ducks, "crown" stopper; d.1963), F.A. Allen (Monmouth IL, 1880s; first all-metal nickel-plated call), Charles Ditto (Keithsburg IL, Eureka models c.1900–mid-1920s, hard-rubber inserts, brass reeds), David Fuller (Chicago, 1885 goose call patent, nickel-plated brass with boxwood mouthpiece), Chick Major (Stuttgart AR, "Dixie Mallard", early hand-turned acrylic c.1970s), Ken Martin (Olive Branch IL, walnut, town stamped on call), Herter's "Vit Glodo" (Waseca MN, catalog production) (DU).

### Quick ID checklist
- Inside of lid / bottom of box: burned, stamped or pencilled signature, date, number, wood name or saying (Cost inscriptions name the wood).
- Printed maker location on a Lynch call or its box: Birmingham/Homewood AL (early), Liberty MS (1970–90s), Thomasville GA (1990s+), Boston (2021+).
- Model number/name: Lynch 101 Fool Proof, 102 World Champion, 100 Jet Slate; Olt D-2, A-50; Major Dixie Mallard.
- Construction: hand-cut joints, figured hardwood and hand-fitted paddle vs CNC-uniform parts and printed logos.
- Duck calls: hand checkering (slightly irregular) and carved tiers = Reelfoot hand work; metal (copper, monel, brass, German silver) reed = early; plastic insert/acrylic barrel = later.
- Original box, paperwork, instruction sheet or maker's letter present.
- Wear: chalked paddle and hand-worn edges are normal; glossy refinish or new screws are not.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Hand-made boxes: figured hardwoods (walnut, cedar, poplar), hand-cut joints, a signature burned or pencilled inside the lid, sometimes dated and numbered.
- Mass-market calls carry printed logos, plastic parts and uniform machining.
- Duck calls: checkering by hand (Reelfoot) vs machine; hard rubber vs plastic. Early inserts are hard rubber or wood; moulded acrylic and plastic arrive mid-century onward (Olt, Faulk, Major). Metal bands on early cedar calls (Perdew). Early reeds are metal (copper, monel, brass, German silver); later ones are Mylar/plastic.
- Age tells: oxidised, darkened wood with hand-polish wear, square or slotted screws, cork or cloth-lined boxes; vs bright lacquer, Phillips screws, laser engraving on modern pieces (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cracked boxes, replaced lids, worn or re-chalked paddles (normal), missing strikers or reeds. Original paperwork or a letter adds value.
- Heavy use is accepted. Refinishing lowers value (general knowledge, verify).
- Ask for: a photo of the hinge screw and rubber band/spring (replacements common), the inside of the box, and for duck calls the reed and wedge (replaced reeds are common and lower value slightly); whether the call still sounds.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Forged signatures on unsigned boxes in the style of Cost or Lynch exist. Collectors rely on provenance and the maker's handwriting (general knowledge, verify).
- Lynch reissued its classic models (commemorative box sets were made for the 2025 Hall of Fame induction), so "World Champion" alone does not mean old; the address and construction date it.
- Herter's "Vit Glodo" is a catalog version of the Glodo call, not a Glodo.

## Market
A small, knowledgeable collector community (Callmakers & Collectors Association, NWTF auctions) plus hunters. eBay moves common calls. Interest peaks before the spring turkey season (Feb–Apr) and duck season (Oct–Nov) (general knowledge, verify). Top-name turkey call prices have softened since about 2000 (Sporting Classics).

## What sellers usually miss
- A signature burned or pencilled inside the lid; the maker's letter.
- "Old turkey call" listed with no name.
- The Lynch address/location line that separates Alabama-era calls from later ones; metal reeds and hand checkering on "old duck call" listings.

## Shipping / handling
- Under 1 lb, $8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker, signature and number, date, wood, dimensions, original letter or box, how it sounds (a short audio clip helps).
- Photograph the inside of the lid, the signature, both ends and the paddle.
- Title keywords: "Neil Cost Turkey Box Call Signed", "Ike Ashby Gobbler Box", "Vintage Lynch World Champion Box Call", "Olt Duck Call".

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] [model] [box/slate/duck] call, [wood], [dimensions]. [Signed/inscribed] inside the lid: '[exact text]'. [Lynch: marked Birmingham, Ala., which places it in the Alabama period before the 1970 move to Liberty, Miss.] Original [paddle/striker/reed]; [no cracks / note cracks]; not refinished. Plays [well / untested]. Comes with [original box / letter / paperwork]." Quote inscriptions exactly and photograph them; do not attribute an unsigned call to a maker.
