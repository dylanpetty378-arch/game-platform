## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Baskets made of steel, galvanised, tinned or aluminum wire. The cheap, plentiful kind is a mid-century or modern machine-made aluminum oval bread basket with a hex-mesh body and a wrapped rope-like rim (like the example). The valuable kind: 19th/early-20th c. French hand-twisted wire baskets (scrolls, egg baskets, fruit baskets with feet), primitive American egg/clam baskets with old patina, and silver/silverplate woven baskets.

Forms to recognise:
- **French pedestal / compote egg basket (c. 1890–1910)**: a bowl of concentric wire rings on a wheel-form or splayed foot, twisted-wire rim; typical ~9" diameter × 6" high (1stdibs example). Often slightly off-round from age.
- **French tiered egg stands**: two or three stacked wire tiers on a central stem or legs, usually painted black or left dark iron.
- **Pedestal urn baskets**: urn-shaped, black twisted wire, late 19th c. (Chairish).
- **Heavy-gauge steel gathering/potato baskets**: thick wire, bail handle, flat or rounded bottom; French, German and American.
- **Collapsible wire baskets** (German and American, early 20th c.): fold flat with hinged sides; popular with decorators.
- **American clam/egg baskets**: galvanised mesh or heavy wire, wire bail with wooden grip.
- **Aluminum woven bread baskets** (1950s onward): light, dull silver, hex or lattice weave, rope-twist rim — the common low-tier type.
- **Industrial aluminum crates/baskets** (e.g. Zarges, Germany): riveted aluminum, not woven; a separate industrial-decor niche.

Words sellers use: "French wire", "wirework", "egg basket", "panier", "farmhouse", "primitive", "industrial", "collapsible".

## Makers, brands and variants
Mostly unmarked. Aluminum woven baskets: 1950s-70s US (Everlast-style giftware) and 1990s-2010s imports. French wire: unmarked, sold as "panier en fil de fer". American egg baskets: farm-made. Silverplate woven (Wilcox, Reed & Barton bread trays) are a different category; Kalo Shop (Chicago Arts & Crafts silversmith) pieces are sterling hollowware, also a different category — check hallmarks and weigh, never price as wire.
- **Zarges** (Germany): industrial aluminum baskets/crates with attribution marks, 1950s–60s; dealer asks ~$280 (Chairish, asking).
- **German collapsible wire baskets**: unmarked, early 20th c.

### Quick ID checklist
- Joins: hand-twisted wraps and irregular spacing (old/French) vs spot welds and perfectly uniform mesh (modern).
- Weight and colour: aluminum is very light and dull grey-silver; iron/steel is heavier, dark, takes a magnet.
- Foot: a wheel-form or scrolled foot on a bowl of concentric rings = French pedestal egg basket.
- Tiers: two or three stacked tiers on a stem = French egg stand.
- Rust: even, deep, slightly pitted patina in protected areas vs fresh, uniform orange rust everywhere (repro).
- Paint: old black paint worn to metal at contact points vs fresh glossy black or powder coat.
- Shape: slightly off-round from age is normal on 19th-c. pieces.
- Labels or glued tags, "Made in China/India" = modern decor.

### Golden-egg tells
- French 19th-c. triple-tier twisted-wire egg stand — several times a plain wire basket (dealer asks in the hundreds, 1stdibs) — three stacked tiers, black iron wire, twisted joins, worn paint at feet.
- French pedestal/urn "compote" egg basket c. 1890–1910 — ~3–6× a modern egg basket (estimate, verify against sold) — concentric rings on a wheel foot, twisted rim, off-round.
- Heavy-gauge French/European gathering basket with scrollwork — ~3× — thick wire, scroll motifs, bail handle with old patina.
- Early-20th-c. collapsible wire basket (German/American) — ~2–3× a fixed modern basket — hinges at the sides, folds flat.
- Zarges or other marked industrial aluminum basket — dealer asks ~$280 vs $15 for a woven bread basket — riveted sheet/rod aluminum with a maker plate, not woven.
- Sterling woven basket (Kalo or other silversmith) — melt value plus maker premium — hallmarks on base; not wire at all.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: rust patina, hand-twisted joins, irregular spacing, solder blobs, tinned surfaces with dark oxidation, scrollwork. New: perfectly uniform hex mesh, bright aluminum or powder coat, spot welds, glued labels. Aluminum is very light and dull silver; galvanised has spangle.
- Hand-formed French wire uses concentric rings bound to radial wires with tight twisted wraps; machine pieces use welded crossings.
- Tinned wire darkens to grey-black; galvanised shows crystalline spangle; aluminum never rusts (white oxide only).
- Magnet test separates steel/iron (sticks) from aluminum (doesn't).
- Quality: tight, even twists; strong foot that keeps the bowl level; no sharp cut ends.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent rims, broken wire ends (sharp), rust holes. Repairs are visible as new wire. Paint over rust lowers value to decor-only.
- Ask for photos of the foot and joins; broken radial wires let the basket collapse.
- Off-round shape is acceptable on antique pieces; crushed tiers are not.
- Recent repainting hides repairs; check for bright wire under the paint.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Huge decor-market reproduction of "French farmhouse" egg baskets and painted chicken baskets (2000s-present). Fresh, even rust is a sign of a repro.
- Repro tells: spot welds, identical wire gauge everywhere, uniform fake rust, glued tags, perfectly round.
- New "tiered egg stands" are sold by home-decor chains; they lack twisted joins.

## Market
Slow decor category; buyers are farmhouse decorators. 30-90 days for low tier. Antique French wire sells best through dealer platforms (1stdibs, Chairish, Etsy) to decorators.

## What sellers usually miss
Rarely under-priced; sellers usually over-call modern pieces "antique". The occasional miss: a tiered French egg stand or a collapsible basket sold in a box lot of "old wire stuff".

## Shipping / handling
Light (1-2 lb) but bulky; $10-15 to ship. Crushes easily without a box. Tiered stands need a box with all tiers supported.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give dimensions, metal (aluminum vs steel - magnet test), age evidence, close-ups of joins.

### What to tell the buyer
State the form (pedestal egg basket, tiered egg stand, gathering basket, collapsible), the metal (iron/steel by magnet test, or aluminum), and the construction (hand-twisted joins vs welded). Give diameter, height and number of tiers. Describe patina honestly ("original dark patina with light surface rust; slightly off-round from age") and photograph the foot, the joins and the base. Only call it "French" or "19th century" when the construction supports it.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ $50 needs resale ≥ ~$100 at a $15 buy - only antique French/primitive pieces reach that. Pass on aluminum bread baskets at any price.
