## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Framed or mounted Christian images sold as "vintage Jesus picture". Nearly all are reproductions of a handful of famous paintings:
- **Heinrich Hofmann (German, 1824–1911)**: "Christ in Gethsemane" (1890, Riverside Church NYC – Jesus kneeling at a rock, red cloak over white, light from above), "Christ in the Temple" / "Jesus at Twelve" (1881, head and shoulders of a red-haired boy with a halo, one hand on a book). Printed endlessly 1900–1970s as lithographs, textured "canvas-look" prints and lacquered-on-wood plaques.
- **Warner Sallman (American, 1892–1968)**: "Head of Christ" (1940, profile, brown-gold background – over a billion copies), "Christ in Gethsemane" (1941, Jesus with clasped hands on a rock), "The Lord Is My Shepherd", "Christ at Heart's Door". Prints say "© Kriebel & Bates" or "Warner Press" in the margin.
- **Leonardo "Last Supper"** prints, "Praying Hands" (Dürer), "Grace" (Enstrom praying man).
- **Lighted pictures (1950s–60s)**: print under glass in a deep gold-tone pierced-metal or moulded frame with a lamp hood or hidden bulb and a cord (makers: Turner Mfg, Emdur Metal Products, Electric Picture Co.).
- **Wood-slice plaques (1960s–80s)**: a print decoupaged onto a cross-section of a log with bark edges, shellacked.
- **Valuable outliers**: hand-painted Mexican retablos (oil on tin or copper, 19th–early 20th c.), Russian/Greek icons (egg tempera and gesso on a wooden panel, often with a metal riza cover), Spanish colonial or Italian old-master oils, original Sallman sketches/paintings.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Print publishers**: Kriebel & Bates (Indianapolis, Sallman 1940s–50s), Warner Press (Anderson IN), Gospel Trumpet Co., Brown & Bigelow, Perry Pictures (small c.1900 art prints), C. Klackner, Fratelli Alinari (Italy).
- **Lighted picture makers**: Turner Manufacturing Co. (Chicago Heights, 1930s–60s), Emdur Metal Products (filigree metal frames), assorted 1950s novelty makers.
- **Retablos**: anonymous Mexican provincial painters, mostly 1820–1920, oil on tinplate; small (7×10" typical). Popular subjects: Guadalupe, Santo Niño de Atocha, Holy Trinity, San Antonio. Ex-votos (painted thanks with a written story) are a related, sought type.
- **Icons**: Russian 18th–19th c. (Old Believer, Palekh, Mstyora schools), Greek and Balkan. Modern Greek monastery icons and Russian "souvenir" icons are prints on wood.

### Golden-egg tells
- Original Warner Sallman painting or chalk/charcoal study (not a print) — $35,000–100,000 sold (oil $100,000 + chalk $35,000, private sale to Jerry Jenkins, March 2018) vs $8–40 for a Sallman print (~1,000×+) — NO "© Kriebel & Bates" or "Warner Press" copyright line in the margin; visible brush ridges (oil) or powdery chalk strokes on paper/board under raking light; usually the Head of Christ profile; came out of church offices, Bible colleges and Covenant-church estates (https://news.wttw.com/2018/03/29/rare-head-christ-originals-found-chicago-thrift-store-sell-135k)
- Russian icon with a hallmarked silver oklad ("84" standard) over a painted wooden panel — £1,560 sold (Bonhams 2010, 26.5×22 cm, Moscow 1858 silver-gilt); dealer estimates $2,200–7,500 for others vs $8–40 print plaque (~40×) — tiny stamps on the oklad's lower edge or the halo: "84" plus a 4-digit year (1858, 1881...), a St George city mark (Moscow) and Cyrillic initials for the assay master and maker; the metal cover is cut out only for faces and hands; the panel behind is thick wood with a recessed field. beware: brass/tin "riza" covers with no 84 stamp over a paper print, and modern Greek gilt-print icons (https://www.bonhams.com/auction/17810/lot/150/the-mother-of-god-iverskaya-russian-makers-mark-cyrillic-ii-moscow-assay-master-cyrillic-ia-1858)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Print vs painting**: under magnification prints show a dot rosette or uniform texture-embossing ("canvas-texture" prints have a regular weave pattern stamped in). Paintings show raised brush strokes and irregular edges, especially in highlights.
- **Period of print**: c.1900–1930 chromolithographs have rich layered colour, a slightly grainy look, and come in original oak, gesso or gilt frames, often "yard long" (narrow and long). 1940s–60s prints have the publisher's copyright line and are on thin paper or board. 1970s plaques are lacquered photo-mechanical prints.
- **Lighted pictures**: original cloth cord, a two-prong non-polarised plug and a working bulb are pluses; rewired is fine for decor buyers.
- **Retablos**: real tin is thin, sometimes rusted at edges with nail holes; paint has crackle and losses. Modern copies have bright even paint on new, shiny tin or are printed.
- **Icons**: real panels are thick wood with a slight concave face (kovcheg = recessed field) and wooden cross-splints (shponki) on the back; old gesso cracks follow the wood grain.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Foxing, water stains and fading on prints; flaking shellac and cracked bark on wood slices; dented filigree, broken glass, dead or frayed wiring on lighted pictures (do not ship glass; swap to plexi or ship the print only).
- Icons: overpainting, "white-washing" (stripped to look older), missing riza parts.
- Retablos: paint loss, rust, dents, patched holes – buyers tolerate it (a patched retablo still sold for $774 at Turner Auctions).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Sallman and Hofmann prints are reproductions by definition; nobody fakes them because they are cheap.
- Icons: modern prints glued to wood, thickly varnished and artificially aged; student copies with fake aging; risas painted only where the openings show; 18th–19th c. copies passed off as earlier (russianicon.com). Buying icons from a photo is high-risk.
- Retablos: modern hand-painted tin "retablos" sold in tourist markets since the 1970s and printed tin plaques; the paint is too fresh and the tin too clean.

## Market
Prints and plaques: huge supply, low demand, slow (months). Sells mostly to decor/kitsch buyers on Etsy. Lighted pictures have a small mid-century-kitsch following. Retablos and icons have active specialist auction markets (Turner Auctions, Vogt Auction Texas "Hecho en Mexico" sales, Jasper52), selling within days at auction.

## What sellers usually miss
- Hand-painted vs printed: an "old Jesus picture" on tin is the one to stop for. Ask for a raking-light photo of the surface and a photo of the back.
- Icons sold as "religious plaque": thick panel, recessed field, back splints.
- The original painting outlier (Sallman originals were in church offices; Sallman studies come up rarely).
- The reverse is the norm: sellers price Sallman/Hofmann prints at $40–100 because they are "vintage".

## Shipping / handling
Framed pictures are flat but glass is the risk: remove glass or use plexi, corner protectors, 2" padding. 16×20" framed ≈ 6–10 lb, $20–35. Lighted pictures are deep and heavy (8–15 lb), $25–40. Retablos and small icons ship cheaply ($10–15). Wood slices $10–15.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the artist and painting ("after Heinrich Hofmann, Christ in Gethsemane"), state "print/lithograph" or "hand painted" honestly, give dimensions with and without frame, and show the publisher line and back. For lighted pictures, say whether it lights and whether the wiring is original. For retablos/icons, describe as "attributed" and "period" only when the tells are clear.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Printed Sallman/Hofmann/Last Supper pictures, wood slices and lacquered plaques: **pass at any price over ~$10** (a $60 sale nets ~$52 − $40 shipping both ways).
- Lighted pictures: pass unless ≤ $15 and local pickup.
- Buy: an old hand-painted tin or copper retablo at ≤ $60, or a period yard-long chromolithograph in its original frame at ≤ $20.
- Icons: hand-painted, panel tells visible, ≤ $60; anything claimed 18th c. or priced higher needs a specialist = pass.
- Size: framed prints up to ~24×30" fit the passenger seat; bigger = pass.
