Buy rule (short): Buy lots of genuine (plate-marked or letterpress-backed, foxed, hand-coloured) atlas leaves at ≤ $3–5 per map, or any single genuine American state/Texas map ≤ $40; never pay for prints.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pages removed from 19th-century atlases. Genuine tells: hand-applied outline or wash colour that bleeds slightly over lines, steel- or copper-engraved fine lines (or lithographic transfers after ~1850), plate mark on copper engravings, page numbers/text on reverse, natural foxing and uneven toning, laid/wove rag paper with deckle or trimmed atlas edges. Reproductions: uniform tan "parchment" ground, printed colour with halftone dots under a loupe, no plate mark, glossy or modern paper, sizes matching standard poster frames. Example 7945764934: about a dozen small atlas leaves (c. 7×9") in five frames (several triptych frames ~12×36", plus one single framed "China" map); outline hand-colouring in yellow/green/pink, uneven foxing and toning, German-style title cartouches — consistent with a genuine mid-19th-c. German small atlas such as Meyer's Zeitungs- / Universal-Atlas (Bibliographisches Institut, Hildburghausen, 1840s–50s), matching the seller's "1849". Frames are cheap modern store frames.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **American**: S. Augustus Mitchell (1840s–60s, hand-coloured, decorative borders after 1860), J.H. Colton, A.J. Johnson, H.S. Tanner, Rand McNally (1880s+ wax engraving, low value), Asher & Adams, Gray.
- **German**: Meyer (Hildburghausen), Stieler (Justus Perthes, Gotha), Kiepert, Andree.
- **British**: A.K. Johnston, Black, Arrowsmith, SDUK (1830s–40s), Tallis (decorative vignettes, high value).
- **French**: Malte-Brun, Levasseur (decorative border scenes, $50–200).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand colour vs printed colour (loupe: dots = printed). Engraved lines are crisp and raised slightly. Watermarks/chain lines in early paper. Value drivers: subject (US states, Texas, the West, California, cities) and decorative elements; Europe/Asia small leaves are the lowest tier.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foxing, toning, centrefold splits, trimmed margins into image, tape stains, mat burn, water stains. Framing in acidic mats causes burn lines.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Many "antique" maps are 20th-c. reproductions (Rand McNally facsimiles, Hammond, National Geographic reprints, decorator prints on parchment paper). Reproductions often come pre-framed in sets of 2–3 matching frames — check that the images vary in toning and colour like real leaves.

## Market
Steady; collectors buy by geography. Individual European leaves sell slowly (weeks–months); lots of 10+ genuine leaves sell to dealers at ~$8–15 per map.

## What sellers usually miss
That a genuine 1840s–60s leaf of a US state or Texas is worth 5–10× a Europe/Asia leaf; and the reverse: that their "antique" frames hold reproductions.

## Shipping / handling
Unframed: flat between rigid boards, $6–10. Framed with glass: risky and $25–45 each — remove from frames before shipping. Large frames fit a passenger seat but don't ship well.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Atlas, date, cartographer, size of sheet, hand-coloured vs printed, condition (foxing, folds), reverse text. Photograph under raking light to show plate/engraving.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Framed sets need the seller to unframe and ship flat (~$15). A lot of ~12 genuine small leaves at $20 total → lot resale ~$120–200 → profit ≈ $50–110 (passes; Tier A if ≥ $40). At $20 EACH it fails. No authentication needed; confirm with close-ups (loupe/raking light, reverse side).
