## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wristwatches with a cartoon, cowboy, comic or political figure on the dial, mostly cheap pin-lever mechanical movements (wind-up), later quartz.
- **1933–40s**: Ingersoll Mickey Mouse (1933, Mickey's hands are the watch hands), Big Bad Wolf, Popeye, Dick Tracy, Superman (New Haven 1939), Buck Rogers; small chrome cases, leather straps sometimes with a metal figural strap ornament.
- **1949–55 cowboy boom**: Hopalong Cassidy (U.S. Time, 1950; Hoppy bust on dial; famous saddle display box), Roy Rogers (Ingraham/Bradley), Dale Evans, Gene Autry (Wilane), Lone Ranger (New Haven), Davy Crockett (U.S. Time).
- **1970s**: Bradley and Timex Mickey, Snoopy, Big Bird; political novelty: Spiro Agnew (Dirty Time Co., 1970), Nixon, Republican elephant / Democratic donkey dial watches with patriotic straps.
- **1980s–90s**: Lorus Disney (quartz), Fossil limited editions, Burger King/McDonald's premiums — low.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Ingersoll / Ingersoll-Waterbury** (USA, 1933–40s) and **Ingersoll UK** (1930s–50s Mickey, Dan Dare 1953): original Mickey 1933 with metal band $300–800 boxed $800–2,000.
- **U.S. Time** (Timex predecessor, 1949–60s): Hopalong Cassidy, Davy Crockett, Mickey "Birthday Series" 1958; dial marked "U.S. Time" small. Hoppy watch loose $50–120, with saddle box $200–500 (estimate).
- **New Haven Clock & Watch**: Superman, Dick Tracy, Lone Ranger, Popeye.
- **Ingraham, Bradley (Elgin)**: Roy Rogers, Mickey 1970s.
- **Dirty Time Co.**: Spiro Agnew 1970 ($30–80), others.
- **Timex 1970s**: character and promotional watches $15–60.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Original dials: lithographed or printed paper/metal, soft ageing, period fonts; maker name small on dial. Re-dials: too bright, too crisp, wrong font, maker missing.
- Original straps (leather with figural metal buckle or ornament) add value. Replacement straps lower slightly.
- Cases: chrome-plated brass (wear to brass), painted cases on some 1950s models.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Not running (cheap pin-levers may need a full service costing more than the watch), rusted movements, cracked crystals, faded or water-stained dials, missing hands, broken stems. Re-dialed or reproduction dials on genuine movements are common.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction dials (Mickey, Hoppy, Roy Rogers) installed in old cases.
- Official reissues: Timex/Disney 1990s "re-creations" of 1933 Mickey; Fossil limited editions; Hopalong reissue watches 1980s–2000s with modern quartz. 
- Fantasy political watches (modern quartz with retro dials).

## Market
Steady collector base for cowboy and 1930s characters (aging buyers; prices flat to soft). Boxes and running condition drive value. 1970s novelties sell slowly.

## What sellers usually miss
- "Old Mickey watch" may be a 1933 Ingersoll (hands are Mickey's arms; small seconds disc with three Mickeys).
- Original boxes and paper inserts in a drawer.
- Reverse: modern reissues listed as originals.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket-size, under 4 oz; padded box $5–8. Boxed watches need a sturdy box ($8–12).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, year, whether it runs (wound and timed for 24 h), dial originality, strap/buckle originality, case wear, box. Video of it running.

## Buy rule
Watch shipping is cheap (~$6 each way), so profit = sold × 0.87 − 12 − price ≥ max($50, price). A $25 buy needs ~$100 sold. Buy only 1930s–50s characters that run (ask seller to wind it and confirm) and have original dials, at ≤ $30, or boxed examples at ≤ $100. Mechanical watches are testable by winding — not "untested electronics" — but non-runners are repairs: pass. These are not high-end watches; no professional authentication needed, but re-dial risk must be judged from photos.
