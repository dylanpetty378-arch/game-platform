## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Souk-made decor: hand-tooled white metal ("nickel silver"/brass), panels of dyed camel bone (orange/amber tones, visible pores) tacked with metal bands, glass "jewel" studs (red, green, blue). The example: ~12–14 in candlestick, white-metal stem with drip tray, red glass bead accents, amber camel bone panel section — tourist-trade c. 1970s–2000s. Lanterns: pierced brass/tin with colored glass. Thuya wood boxes (burl, Essaouira).

## Makers, brands and variants
Unmarked souk artisans; Tuareg (Niger/Mali) silver crosses and boxes; Berber (Tiznit) silver/enamel. Western importers (Pier 1, Pottery Barn) sell new look-alikes.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Camel bone has dark pores and slight grain; resin imitations are uniform. Old pieces: hand-forged nails, worn patina, heavier silver content (test). Most "silver" is nickel silver/alpaca (no silver).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Loose/missing bone panels, lifted metal bands, missing glass studs, dented trays.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Market dominated by new production made in the same way; "antique" claims rarely true.
