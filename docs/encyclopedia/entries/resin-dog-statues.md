## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast polyresin (or older chalkware/plaster) dog figures, hand-painted with glass or painted eyes, open-mouth tongue, sculpted fur texture. Homco (Home Interiors & Gifts, 1970s–90s) dogs have a gold foil "Homco" sticker and copyright year cast under the base (1985/1986 common). Sandicast (San Diego, 1980s–2000s) is signed "Sandra Brue" with breed and year.
- **Homco** is the giftware division of Home Interiors & Gifts (home-party sales). Bases typically carry a molded "© year" plus a 4-digit item number (e.g. "© 1991 8799"; pug #1287, 1997), often with a "Made in USA" sticker and sometimes felt under the base; later production moved to Mexico. Not every piece is resin: many Homco animals are the bisque "Masterpiece Porcelain" line (e.g. 1983 basset hound). Sticker style varies — absence of a foil sticker does not rule out Homco.
- **Sandicast** (San Diego, CA): "Sandra Brue" engraved/cast on the underside with the year (1983, 1985, 1986, 1988, 1992 seen) and often a model code (e.g. J380 mastiff pup). Realistic breed sculptures, sleeping/lying poses common, from small "Lil Snoozer" pieces to 16"+ pups and life-size busts.
- **Border Fine Arts** (Langholm, Scotland, 1974–2016): resin (cold-cast) with very fine detail on wooden or resin bases; sculptor's name moulded on the piece; model numbers like B0668.
- **Beswick / Royal Doulton**: glazed ceramic (not resin) with printed backstamp.
- **Chalkware** (1930s–50s carnival prizes): plaster, very light and chalky, flaking paint, often hollow.

Look-alikes: generic Chinese polyresin dogs (no signature, glossy uniform paint), garden concrete dogs (too heavy and outdoor).

## Makers, brands and variants
Homco (1980s resin cocker spaniels, labs, golden retrievers – mostly 12–18" long); Sandicast (realistic breed sculptures, smaller and life-size); Castagna (Italy, resin cold-cast); Leonardo Collection (UK); Border Fine Arts (Scotland, high detail, signed sculptors e.g., Margaret Turner, Ray Ayres); Royal Doulton/Beswick "Champion" dogs (ceramic, backstamp); chalkware carnival dogs (1930s–50s).
- **Homco**: most common; resin and "Masterpiece Porcelain"; low value.
- **Sandicast**: Sandra Brue sculptures, 1980s–2000s; breed-specific buyers; rarer breeds and large/life-size sizes command more.
- **Border Fine Arts**: two value worlds — handmade/Classic Scottish pieces (hand-finished, slight colour variation) and limited editions are wanted; the later **Studio** range made in China is worth much less (often under £50, per PM Antiques). Ray Ayres is the best-known sculptor (Tennants). Limited-edition farm tableaux reach £400–1,700 at UK auction, but those are mostly farm/livestock, not dogs.
- **Castagna** and **Leonardo Collection**: mid-market giftware.

### Quick ID checklist
- Flip it: Homco = molded © year + 4-digit number; Sandicast = "Sandra Brue" signature + year (+ model code); BFA = sculptor name moulded, model number, "Border Fine Arts" mark.
- Material: resin (warm, matte, white/grey core at chips) vs glazed ceramic (Beswick/Doulton) vs chalky plaster (chalkware).
- BFA: check for "Studio" or "Made in China" — that is the low tier.
- Size: measure length; Sandicast large/life-size pieces are a different tier.
- Breed: popular breeds (cocker, lab, golden) are common; rarer breeds draw fewer but keener buyers.
- Paint: crisp airbrushed shading and natural eyes = quality; thick single-colour paint and plastic eyes = generic.
- Box/COA: BFA original box and certificate add value.

### Golden-egg tells
- Border Fine Arts handmade/Classic or limited-edition piece — several times a Studio (China) BFA piece; Studio pieces often under £50 (PM Antiques) — base marks without "Studio"/China, limited-edition number, original box/certificate.
- Beswick / Royal Doulton glazed ceramic "Champion" breed dog — ~2–5× a Homco resin dog (general knowledge, verify) — glossy glaze and printed backstamp under the base.
- Sandicast large or life-size breed sculpture — ~$60–150 vs $25 for a Homco (entry tiers) — "Sandra Brue" signature and year, 16"+ length or life-size bust.
- Sandicast rarer breed (e.g. Shar Pei, Old English sheepdog, mastiff) — breed buyers pay above the $25–80 Sandicast norm (dealer listings on 1stdibs, asking) — breed named on the base with signature.
- Homco cocker/lab resin — this is the common version, ~$20–30 sold (Terapeak) — molded © year and 4-digit number.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Polyresin: heavy-ish, slightly warm feel, airbrushed paint. Quality tells: crisp hair detail, natural eye shine, signed base. Cheap: thick paint, plastic eyes, molded seams visible.
- Cold-cast resin (BFA, Castagna) has a stone-like weight and finer detail than hollow polyresin.
- Bisque porcelain (Homco Masterpiece) is unglazed, matte and cool to the touch; chips show white porcelain.
- Old vs new: 1980s pieces show dust in recesses and slight paint fading; modern imports are glossier and lighter.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chipped ears/tail tips, paint rubs on nose, fading, glue-repaired breaks. Resin chips show white/grey core.
- Ask for photos of ear tips, tail, paws and the underside; repaired breaks show glue lines and paint-matching halos.
- BFA buyers reject damaged or extensively repaired pieces (Tennants).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not a faking market; many generic imports styled like Homco/Sandicast. BFA "Studio" pieces made in China can be passed off as Scottish handmade — read the base.

## Market
Breed-specific buyers (cocker spaniel owners), moderate volume; slow 30–60 days. BFA has a strong UK collector market (closed 2016, secondary market only).

## What sellers usually miss
Border Fine Arts and Beswick pieces sold as "dog figurine"; rare breeds in Sandicast.

## Shipping / handling
18" resin dog ~5 lb in 22×12×12 box → $20–30; ears/tails fragile, double-box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, year on base, dimensions, breed, any chips.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker and quote the base mark exactly (e.g. "Sandra Brue Sandicast, 1986" or "Homco © 1986"). Give breed, pose, colour, length and height, and material (resin, bisque porcelain or glazed ceramic). Disclose every chip, rub and repair with close-ups of ears, tail and paws. For Border Fine Arts, state the sculptor, model number, whether it is a handmade/Classic or Studio piece, and whether the box and certificate are included.
