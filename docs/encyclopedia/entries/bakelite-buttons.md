## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Buttons made c. 1925–1950 from phenolic resins (Bakelite, Catalin), celluloid, galalith (casein) and later acrylic (Lucite). Desirable categories: carved colourful Bakelite (butterscotch, cherry red, green), laminated/polka-dot "dots" (inset contrasting rods), "apple juice" translucent yellow Bakelite with reverse carving and painting, realistic figural "goofies" (shaped as fruits, animals, sports items), card sets.
- **Size**: coat buttons 1–1.5 in., dress buttons 5/8–7/8 in.; realistics usually 3/4–1.5 in. Collectors measure diameter in inches (or NBS "lines").
- **Shanks**: Bakelite/Catalin buttons usually have a self-shank (drilled through a moulded or carved boss on the back) or a metal loop set into the back. Two- and four-hole sew-throughs are common on cheaper plastic. Realistics often have an inserted wire loop or a moulded shank; painted plastic with an inserted wire loop shank is a typical realistic construction (austbuttonhistory).
- **Backs**: old phenolic backs show tool and polishing marks, wear and an even, aged colour. Moulded later plastics have mould seams and sprue nubs.
- **Cards**: original cards carry the brand name (La Mode, Le Chic, B.G.E. Originals, Rich Art, Schwanda, etc.), and the card is the best maker evidence. Off the card, maker attribution of realistics is usually a guess.
- Tests: Simichrome or 409 on a cotton swab turns yellow on Bakelite; hot water releases a formaldehyde/carbolic smell; Bakelite clunks; Lucite is clear, light and fails tests; celluloid smells of camphor.
- Seller title words: "bakelite", "catalin", "carved", "dot", "apple juice", "realistic", "goofy", "figural", "button card", "button jar lot", "tested". "Bakelite" in a title is often unverified.
- Look-alikes: Lucite (water-clear, light, 1950s+), casein/galalith (flat, cut from sheet, dull surface, often dyed; sometimes smells of burnt milk under hot pin, which sellers should not do), celluloid (thin, light, can be ivory-grained, camphor smell), modern resin "fakelite" (bright, perfect, no wear).

### Quick ID checklist
- Is there an original card with a brand name (La Mode, Le Chic, B.G.E., Rich Art, Schwanda)? Photograph the card front and back.
- Colour: aged Bakelite shows warm, slightly yellowed tones (white becomes butterscotch, blue looks greenish); bright, perfectly even colour suggests new resin.
- Back: drilled self-shank or set-in metal loop and tool marks = old; mould seams and sprue nubs = later moulded plastic.
- Wear: small scuffs and edge rounding on the high points. A flawless piece is suspicious.
- Test photos: Simichrome swab turning yellow, plus the hot-water smell report (black Bakelite often fails Simichrome, so the hot-water test matters).
- Realistic figural: is it celluloid (light, thin, painted) or phenolic (heavy, carved)? Both can be good; state which.
- Count, size in inches and matching (a set of 6–8 matching beats odd singles).
- Paint on reverse-carved apple juice: intact or worn?

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bakelite ages: surface oxidation darkens colours (white becomes butterscotch); polished deep carving; heavier. Seams/sprue marks on moulded plastic indicate cheaper later production. Sets on original cards add 50–100%.
- **Bakelite/Catalin**: cast phenolic, carved and polished by hand or lathe, dense, "clunks" against another piece. Patina (oxidized surface) is what reacts to Simichrome.
- **Celluloid**: thin, light, sometimes laminated or ivory-grained; can crack, shrink and "off-gas" (crystallise) with age; camphor smell.
- **Casein/galalith**: cut from sheet, flat, often dyed and lightly incised; dull surface.
- **Lucite/acrylic**: water-clear or bright, light, often with inner carving; 1950s and later.
- Testing limits (Etsy Bakelite Love forum): Simichrome reaction depends on patina, so a recently polished piece may not react for months; black Bakelite often does not react; 409's formula changed and it is less reliable; a heavily carved fake has passed Simichrome; some fakes are washed or aged to mimic the reaction. Use several tests together (hot water smell, weight, sound, wear).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, cracks, missing shanks, paint loss on reverse-carved designs, re-polishing.
- Re-polishing removes patina and may stop the item testing positive; dull spots from cleaners (Scrubbing Bubbles noted) are damage.
- Celluloid realistics: cracks, shrinkage, crystallisation, flaking paint.
- Replaced or re-glued shanks reduce value; ask for back photos.
- Cards: stains, tears and missing buttons (empty stitch holes) reduce set value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
"Fakelite" — modern cast resin realistics and polka dots (1990s–present, often from Asia); "Bakelite" claims on any old plastic. Fakelite often fails Simichrome and lacks oxidation.
- Some fakes are washed or treated so they react to Simichrome, and patina can be faked; a positive swab alone does not prove age (Etsy forum).
- Tells of fakelite: too-bright colours, no wear, sharp unworn carving, light weight, "off" smell in hot water, and uniform batches sold in quantity.
- Lucite and casein are often sold as "Bakelite" in good faith.

## Market
Dedicated button collectors (NBS); eBay listings sell modestly; button jar lots sell well. Crafters and jewellery makers also buy carved Bakelite. Designer realistics are a specialist niche; the NBS and regional button clubs are the core buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Bakelite in "grandma's button jar"; carded sets; sellers usually over-call Lucite as Bakelite.
- The card brand (La Mode, Le Chic, B.G.E.) and the designer (Marion Weeber) are rarely named in titles.
- Celluloid realistics are often dismissed as "plastic" though designer sets are collected.

## Shipping / handling
Envelope, $5. Use a padded mailer or small box for carded sets so the card does not bend.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Size in inches, test results (Simichrome photo), shank type, material, quantity.
- Photograph front, back (shank) and edge; the card front and back; a ruler for scale; the test swab.
- Title keywords: "Vintage Bakelite Realistic Buttons Carded La Mode", "Apple Juice Reverse Carved Bakelite Button", "Polka Dot Bakelite Buttons Set of 6".

### What to tell the buyer
State the material and how it was tested (for example: "Simichrome turns the swab yellow; hot-water test gives the phenolic smell"), or say "untested" plainly. Give count, diameter in inches, shank type and whether the set matches. Name the card brand only if the card is present; without a card, describe the button (e.g. "1940s-style realistic") rather than naming a maker. Note wear honestly (scuffs, paint loss, re-polishing). Do not call celluloid or Lucite "Bakelite".
