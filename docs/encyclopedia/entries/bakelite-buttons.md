## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Buttons made c. 1925–1950 from phenolic resins (Bakelite, Catalin), celluloid, galalith (casein) and later acrylic (Lucite). Desirable categories: carved colourful Bakelite (butterscotch, cherry red, green), laminated/polka-dot "dots" (inset contrasting rods), "apple juice" translucent yellow Bakelite with reverse carving and painting, realistic figural "goofies" (shaped as fruits, animals, sports items), card sets.
Tests: Simichrome or 409 on a cotton swab turns yellow on Bakelite; hot water releases a formaldehyde/carbolic smell; Bakelite clunks; Lucite is clear, light and fails tests; celluloid smells of camphor.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bakelite ages: surface oxidation darkens colours (white becomes butterscotch); polished deep carving; heavier. Seams/sprue marks on moulded plastic indicate cheaper later production. Sets on original cards add 50–100%.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, cracks, missing shanks, paint loss on reverse-carved designs, re-polishing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
"Fakelite" — modern cast resin realistics and polka dots (1990s–present, often from Asia); "Bakelite" claims on any old plastic. Fakelite often fails Simichrome and lacks oxidation.
