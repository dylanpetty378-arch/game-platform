## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Canvas or cotton duck bag with two handles. Value tells: L.L.Bean Boat and Tote with contrast bottom/handles and sewn-in "L.L.Bean Freeport Maine" label (older without size code); USPS letter-carrier satchels with leather straps and "U.S. Mail" stamping; screen-printed graphics from known events, bands or brands.

L.L.Bean history: L.L. Bean created the bag in fall **1944** as **"Bean's Ice Carrier"** for hauling blocks of ice; it was dropped after one season and returned in **summer 1965 as the Boat and Tote**. So a "Boat and Tote" is 1965 or later; a genuine 1940s Ice Carrier is a rarity (AOL/AP). The company still makes them in Maine (current natural "oatmeal" canvas with coloured trim; new price $44.95 in the AOL piece), and sells a "1944 Boat and Tote" reissue (llbean.com).

Photo routine: label close-up (inside top seam), the trim colour and handle style, bottom panel, size relative to a person, any monogram, and stains/patina.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **L.L.Bean Boat and Tote** (Ice Carrier 1944; Boat and Tote since 1965). Old labels: "L.L.Bean Freeport, Maine" with no RN/size; colours like Kelly green/navy. Variants: sizes small to extra-large, open-top vs zip-top, long-handle, leather-handled, "Gardening Boat and Tote" (pockets), monogrammed (AOL lists vintage leather-handled and gardening versions). The highest asking prices attach to 1970s–80s bags with heavy "sea-salted" wear and patina (AOL); the 24-piece 80th-anniversary vintage collection sold out in under 24 hours (AOL; Mr Mag).
- **USPS carrier satchels** (leather, then canvas/vinyl) — postal collectors.
- **NALC / union convention totes** — low value, niche.
- **Corporate/conference giveaways** (1990s) — near nil.
- **Museum / bookstore / band / brand graphic totes** (1970s–80s screen-prints) — value depends entirely on the graphic.

### Quick ID checklist
- Label: "L.L.Bean, Freeport, Maine" without RN/size and without care tag = older; modern labels carry RN, size and fibre/care information (general knowledge, verify).
- Canvas weight: heavy, stiff duck with double-layer bottom = Boat and Tote; thin cotton = promo.
- Trim colour and fading: darkly faded, frayed, "sea-salted" patina is what vintage buyers pay for (AOL).
- Variant: leather handles, gardening pockets, zip top, extra-large long-handle = less common.
- Monogram: personalised initials are common; note them rather than hiding them.
- USPS: real carrier satchel has leather straps/hardware and "U.S. Mail" or Post Office Department stamping; a printed tote with a USPS conference name is promo.
- Graphic totes: date line, event year, band or museum name readable in a photo.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Original 1944 "Bean's Ice Carrier"** — far above any Boat and Tote (one-season product; no sold comp found) — Ice Carrier wording or pre-1965 provenance; handle and label details to be confirmed by an L.L.Bean collector.
- **Heavily worn 1970s–80s Boat and Tote with old Freeport label** — ~3–7× the $30–90 tier asked in the market (listings at $248–650 asking, AOL; verify against sold) — soft, faded, frayed canvas, old Freeport, Maine label with no RN or size code.
- **Leather-handled or Gardening Boat and Tote (vintage)** — upper end of the vintage band (listed at $250–600 asking, AOL) — leather handles or exterior tool pockets.
- **USPS/Post Office Department leather carrier satchel** — ~3–8× a union tote ($40–150 vs $15–40) — thick leather strap and hardware, "U.S. Mail" stamping.
- **Sought 1970s–80s graphic tote (museum, band, brand)** — ~2–5× a promo tote ($25–100 vs $5–20) — screen-printed art with a readable name and date.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Heavy 24-oz duck canvas, double-stitched, leather/vinyl trim = quality; thin cotton canvas with screen print = promo. Boat and Totes are rated by the company to hold up to 500 lb (AOL). Old vs new: softness and fading from decades of use, older label wording, and absence of modern care labels; new bags are stiff and bright (current natural canvas with coloured trim).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Stains, yellowing, fraying handles, print cracking, mildew odour. For Bean totes, wear is part of the appeal (the "sea-salted" look), but holes through the bottom, rot and mildew smell are not. Ask about smells and for a photo of the bottom panel inside and out.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
L.L.Bean reissues (current production) look similar; label details date them. The "1944 Boat and Tote" is a current reissue, not a 1944 bag. Generic heavy canvas totes in Bean colours exist; no label = not Bean.

## Market
Fast for L.L.Bean, dead for promo totes. Demand for the Boat and Tote rose over 30% year over year in 2024 per the company, driven by fashion/social-media interest (AOL/AP).

## What sellers usually miss
Old L.L.Bean label variants; USPS leather satchels buried in "old bag" listings. Also that heavy wear raises, not lowers, value on vintage Bean totes.

## Shipping / handling
Light, soft; poly mailer $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Label close-up, measurements, stains, print condition.

### What to tell the buyer
State the exact label wording (photograph it), size (small/medium/large/XL) with height, width and depth, trim colour, handle type (canvas, long, leather), any monogram, and whether it is open-top or zip-top. Describe wear honestly in collector terms ("faded, softened canvas, frayed handle edges, no holes, no odour"). Give history accurately: the bag began as Bean's Ice Carrier in 1944 and has been sold as the Boat and Tote since 1965. Do not date a bag to the 1970s without label evidence.

## Buy rule
Promo/union totes: median $15 → pass at any price. Bean or USPS satchel: buy if comps ≥ $80 and price ≤ $15.
