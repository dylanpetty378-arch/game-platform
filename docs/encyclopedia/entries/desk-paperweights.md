## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A heavy desk object. Two families:
- **Glass**: domed clear glass encasing millefiori canes, lampwork flowers, fruit, or sulphides. Antique French (c.1845–1860: Baccarat, Clichy, St. Louis) have date canes ("B1848"), concave ground bases, crisp canes, star-cut or polished bases. American (New England Glass, Sandwich, Millville). Studio (1970s–today): Lundberg Studios, Orient & Flume, Perthshire (P cane), Caithness (etched signatures), Rick Ayotte, Paul Stankard (top), Murano/Chinese (low).
- **Figural metal/stone**: brass/bronze/iron animals, apples, advertising weights (company names cast in), marble/onyx shapes. The example is a red-painted cast metal apple with brass stem and leaf — a 1970s–90s teacher-gift novelty.
- Glass design words: **millefiori** (slices of patterned canes), **close-pack/concentric/scrambled** (cane arrangements), **lampwork** (flame-worked flowers, fruit, animals), **sulphide** (ceramic cameo portrait inside), **overlay** (coloured outer layers cut with windows), **faceted** (cut flat windows on the dome), **crimp/Millville rose** (an upright crimped rose, early 1900s Millville NJ).
- Classic period 1845–1860: Baccarat, Saint-Louis and Clichy made most of them, roughly 15,000–25,000 weights in all (Wikipedia). So true French antiques are scarce relative to what is titled "antique French".
- Advertising weights with photographic or printed images under glass were made in Pittsburgh under William H. Maxwell's 1882 patent and Albert Graeser's 1892 process (Wikipedia) — rectangular, flat-topped "glass advertising paperweight".
- Look-alikes: glass doorknobs and newel-post caps, marbles/crystal balls, Chinese scrambled weights (often titled "antique"), resin snow-globe-type desk ornaments.

## Makers, brands and variants
See above. Highest values: Baccarat/Clichy/St. Louis antique, Stankard, Ayotte, Kaziun. Mid: Perthshire, Caithness limited editions, Lundberg, Orient & Flume, Ysart/Monart. Low: Chinese millefiori (1930s–present, heavy, crude canes, flat bases — huge supply), Murano souvenir, resin.
- **Baccarat**: "B" signature cane, sometimes with a date (1848 most often); many have sharp star-cut bases (Appraisily).
- **Saint-Louis**: "SL" monogram cane; star cuts more elaborate than Baccarat's (Appraisily).
- **Clichy**: the "Clichy rose" cane recurs in their designs; pontils often less polished (Appraisily).
- **New England Glass Co.** and **Boston & Sandwich**: American classic-period; NEGC used spaced millefiori, unfinished bases, rarely signed (Appraisily); Bacchus (UK) and NEGC made some weights rivalling the French (Wikipedia).
- **Charles Kaziun** (US) produced lampwork weights from 1940; **Paul Ysart** (Scotland) from the 1930s led to Manson, McDougall, Holmes and Deacons; **Francis Whittemore** and **Paul Stankard** from the late 1960s–70s rival classic work (Wikipedia).
- **US studios** from mid-20th century: Lundberg Studios, Orient & Flume, Correia, St. Clair, Lotton, Parabelle (Wikipedia).
- **Perthshire** (Crieff, Scotland): "P" signature cane; many are dated by a year cane or a letter cane (the PP14 design used a letter cane for the year, A = 1969, B = 1970 per collectors on Glass Messages); dealers list them with year codes like "1981D". Pattern numbers ("PP10", "PP14") identify designs.
- **Caithness** (Scotland, incl. Whitefriars-branded work): etched base signatures with title, edition and designer (e.g. Allan Scott), checkable against edition lists.
- **Chinese**: early (pre-1940), mid (1940–60) and later (1960–90) production; early pieces copied antique French and American designs for US importers and are "well made, though not well enough … to be confused with originals by experienced collectors"; often misattributed as "Bohemian" or antique French; may carry a "Made in China" label or a "China" insert, and some are sold through Murano with Murano labels (Paperweight Tests).

### Quick ID checklist
- Glass or metal/stone? Then size (diameter and height).
- Base: concave ground with polished pontil (classic), star-cut, flat ground, or rough/unfinished — photograph it.
- Look for a signature cane (B, SL, P, initials) or date cane; photograph close.
- Etched base signature (Caithness/studio) — read title and edition.
- Cane quality: crisp, even, well-centred vs smeared, lumpy, off-centre.
- Glass colour: water-clear vs greenish/greyish tint (cheap/Chinese).
- Labels: "Made in China", Murano foil, studio stickers.
- Dome: chips, bruises, scratches; ground-down height (flattened profile).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Glass: clarity, absence of bubbles (except designed), fine cane detail, signature cane or base etching. Chinese copies: greenish tint, sloppy canes, lumps. Metal apples: solid brass with enamel vs painted cast zinc; painted ones chip as in the example.
- French classic bases: ground and polished pontils, often with concentric circles; American classic bases usually unfinished (Appraisily).
- Reproduction warning signs (Appraisily): perfectly smooth base with no pontil mark, machine-uniform canes, overly bright synthetic colours, deliberately placed bubbles, light weight compared with dense lead crystal, modern cutting marks or glue.
- Studio weights: clean, intentional design, signed and often dated; limited editions with certificates.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Glass: chips/bruises on dome, regrinding (reduces height/value). Metal: paint chips.
- Scratches and abrasions from desk use are normal but lower value; internal fractures reduce value; previous repairs typically cut value by 50% or more (Appraisily).
- Regrinding/repolishing a dome to remove bruises lowers the profile and distorts the magnified view — compare height to diameter against known examples.
- Ask for a photo with a light shone through the dome from the side to show bruises.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese copies of French antique weights with fake date canes are common. Signatures on Caithness can be checked against edition lists.
- Some weights carry false dates or misleading signature canes; explicit signature canes are rare on genuine French weights (Appraisily).
- Leftover or stray date canes occur in genuine studio weights too (Perthshire PP10 with an "A" cane discussed on Glass Messages), so a date cane alone is not proof of design or year.

## Market
Large, steady glass-weight collector base; novelty metal weights sell slowly.
- Paperweight Collectors Association founded 1953 by Paul Jokelson (Wikipedia); specialist auctions (Bonhams, L.H. Selman) handle top French and studio pieces; eBay for mid-tier.

## What sellers usually miss
Perthshire/Caithness/Stankard signatures (cane initials) in "old glass ball" lots; French antique weights in estate drawers.
- Etched base titles/editions on Caithness and studio pieces that sellers don't turn over to read.
- Printed-image Victorian advertising weights (Pittsburgh) titled only "glass paperweight".

## Shipping / handling
Heavy for size (0.5–2 lb); $8–14. Glass needs dome protection.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Base photo, signature cane close-up, dimensions, chips.

### What to tell the buyer
Give diameter, height and weight; maker and how it was identified (signature cane, etched base text, label); design type (close-pack millefiori, lampwork, sulphide, overlay); and base finish (polished concave pontil, star-cut, flat). Describe the dome honestly: scratches, bruises, chips, and whether it appears reground. Do not call a weight "antique French" without a matching signature or specialist opinion; Chinese copies of French designs have been made since before 1940.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$10 − ~$10 − price ≥ max($50, price). Novelty: never. Studio glass with median $120: ≤ $35. French antique: verify with a specialist; often worth it if under $100.
