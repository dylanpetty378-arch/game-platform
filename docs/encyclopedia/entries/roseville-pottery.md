## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ohio art pottery, 1890s–1954. Matte, moulded floral or geometric relief on vases, jardinières and wall pockets.

Marks:
- 1930s pieces carry only a foil/paper triangle sticker ("Roseville Pottery") or an impressed shape number.
- Post-1936 pieces have a raised "Roseville U.S.A." plus the shape number (e.g. "634-7" = shape 634, 7" tall).

Money lines:
- **Wisteria** (1933): bark-textured body with purple wisteria clusters; blue ground > brown.
- **Pine Cone**: pine needles and cones; blue > brown > green.
- **Futura**: Art Deco geometric.
- **Sunflower**, **Blackberry**, **Falline**, **Ferella**, **Baneda**.

Common lines are everywhere: Zephyr Lily, Freesia, Magnolia, Clematis, Snowberry, Peony, Apple Blossom.

Reproductions from China (1990s–2000s) copy Wisteria, Pine Cone and Futura. Tells: a blurry or soft mould, a heavy glossy glaze, the wrong size, and a raised "Roseville" mark with crude letters.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Roseville Pottery Co. (Zanesville, OH)**: the only maker; the value is all in line, colour, shape and size.
- **Colour multipliers**: blue vs brown Wisteria is 2–3× (blue 630-6 $418.50, 631-6 $405, 635-8 $460 vs brown 634-7 median $158.75). Pine Cone blue > brown > green.
- **Size**: larger shapes in money lines step up a tier (Wisteria blue 8"+ $400–900; Pine Cone blue 8"+ $200–400).
- **Futura**: small shapes $200–400; tall or rare shapes are top tier.
- Earlier lines (pre-1920s Rozane, Della Robbia) are a separate, high-end market (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Genuine pieces are moulded earthenware with a soft matte glaze, crisp relief detail and colour pooling in the recesses.
- Marks are correct for the period: sticker or impressed on 1930s lines, raised "Roseville U.S.A." from ~1936.
- Shape number and height must agree (634-7 is 7").
- Repros feel heavier, with a glossier, thicker glaze and mushy detail (general knowledge on weight, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips and hairlines cut value 50–70%.
- Professional restorations show as glaze sheen differences, and under blacklight. Common on handles and rims.
- Ask for a photo of the base mark or sticker, the rims and handles in raking light, and whether the piece has a ring when tapped (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
1990s–2000s Chinese reproductions of Wisteria, Pine Cone, Futura and others. Tells: soft mould detail, heavy glossy glaze, wrong size for the shape number, crude raised "Roseville" letters. Fake foil stickers are possible, so don't rely on a sticker alone (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Large collector base with liquid eBay sales. For example, Wisteria 634-7 alone shows 62 recorded sales. Common lines are slow and soft. Money lines sell within weeks at fair prices. The collector base is ageing and mid-tier prices have softened since the 2000s peak (general knowledge, verify). No strong seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
The line name and colour (blue vs brown Wisteria is 2–3×). Many list as "vintage pottery vase". The sticker or mark photo decides authenticity.

## Shipping / handling
2–4 lb, double-box with 2" foam all round: $20–30 insured. Resell on eBay (13%). Chips and hairlines cut value 50–70%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: "Roseville [Line] [colour] [shape no.-size] [form] [year]", e.g. "Roseville Wisteria Blue 630-6 Vase 1933". State the height, colour, mark or sticker, and condition (no chips, cracks or restoration, checked under light). Photograph the base, the sticker and every side.

## Buy rule
Buy when: blue Wisteria/Pine Cone/Futura is under $120 with a clear mark photo; brown Wisteria is under $60; or any "unknown pottery vase" that matches a money line is under $40. Pass when: the seller names line + shape number and prices at eBay level, or there is any repro tell. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
