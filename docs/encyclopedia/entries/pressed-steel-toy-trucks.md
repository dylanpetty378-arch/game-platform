## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Toy trucks of stamped sheet steel with rubber or steel wheels. Modern décor replicas (Asia-made) imitate Model T trucks with spoke wheels, bolted body, distressed paint.

Detail for photos:
- **Size**: prewar Buddy L trucks are big — the 1920s tank/sprinkler truck is about 25 in long (ATCA). Postwar Tonka/Structo/Nylint are typically 10–22 in (general knowledge, verify). Décor replicas are usually 10–20 in with exaggerated spoke wheels.
- **Construction**: genuine toys are formed from heavy-gauge sheet with crimped tabs and rivets; décor replicas use thin sheet, visible modern nuts/bolts and Phillips screws, and painted-on "rust".
- **Where the maker shows**: Buddy L — decals and embossed names on doors/sides (e.g. "BL" seat-end decals and licence-plate decals on some 1930–32 trucks — ATCA); Tonka — oval "Tonka Toys" decal and "Mound, Minn." stampings on early pieces, embossed Tonka marks under cabs or on frame rails (Appraisily); Structo — name decal or stamping, postwar trucks on Goodyear-labelled rubber tyres (ATCA); Nylint, Wyandotte, Marx — stamped or lithographed names (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
Buddy L (1921–), Keystone, Structo, Tonka (1947–), Smith-Miller, Wyandotte, Marx, Nylint.

- **Buddy L** (Moline Pressed Steel Co., East Moline IL; begun 1921 by Fred Lundahl and named for his son "Buddy L" — general knowledge, verify; ATCA confirms the name came from the son of the man who made them). Described as the first heavy steel toys made in the US (ATCA). Example dating from ATCA for the tank/sprinkler line: 1924–29 doorless cab, black hood and fenders, short running boards, dark-green tank with brass faucet, red chassis, crimped-rod bumper, disc wheels with black hubs; 1929 change to open seat, front and rear fenders, full-length running boards, blue-grey-green tank; 1930–32 206-B Street Sprinkler with red embossed seven-spoke wheels and black Firestone tyres, Type II adding a nickel double-bar bumper, headlights and decal licence plate. Later Buddy L (postwar, then lighter 1960s–90s toys) is a much lower tier (general knowledge, verify).
- **Keystone** (Keystone Mfg. Co., Boston; 1920s–30s large pressed-steel trucks including "Packard" models — general knowledge, verify).
- **Sturditoy** (c. 1925 pressed-steel trucks — Antiques Roadshow appraisal title; details not read).
- **Structo** (Freeport IL): founded 1908 by Louis Strohacker and C.E. Thompson; construction sets until 1915; windup and push stamped-steel toys from the 1920s; cheaper push-type pressed-steel line from 1932; war work from 1942; postwar realistic trucks with Goodyear-labelled rubber tyres, opening hoods and detailed headlights; bought by ERTL in 1975 (ATCA).
- **Tonka** (Mound, MN): 1947–1954 Mound Metalcraft era — heavy all-steel, some metal wheels, oval "Tonka Toys" decals, "Mound, Minn." stampings; 1955–63 Tonka Toys, Inc. — steel with more detailed grilles/cabs and fleet door decals (Tonka Farms, City Service, Allied Van Lines, Texaco, Fire Dept.); 1964 "Mighty Tonka" large yellow construction trucks in thick steel; plastic windows/interiors from the late 1960s; plastic trim and wheels more common from the mid-1970s (Appraisily).
- **Smith-Miller** (Los Angeles; heavy cast-and-steel trucks from the mid-1940s to the mid-1950s, later revived — general knowledge, verify). Postwar, not prewar; high-end of the postwar market. Coca-Cola delivery trucks with wooden cases are a known model (Grey Flannel Auctions listing title).
- **Wyandotte** (All Metal Products Co., Wyandotte MI), **Marx** (lithographed pressed steel), **Nylint** (Rockford IL) — mid tier (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Maker name: decal, stamping or embossing on doors, sides, under the cab or frame rails.
- Fasteners: crimped tabs and rivets = original; Phillips screws, hex nuts or bolts = later repair or décor replica.
- Wheels/tyres: disc or spoke steel wheels, early rubber tyres (Firestone/Goodyear-labelled) vs plastic wheels (mid-1970s+ Tonka) vs oversized cast "spoke" wheels on décor replicas.
- Paint: worn through to bare steel on edges (play wear) with consistent age = original; flawless paint with aged hardware = repaint.
- Decals: fine cracking and mellowed sheen = original; bright, thick, perfect decals sitting on top of the paint = reproduction decals (Appraisily).
- Size: 20 in+ heavy steel = prewar/early postwar contender; 10–15 in thin tin with "rust" = décor.
- Completeness: ladders, buckets, tank caps, faucets, bumpers and headlights present.
- Original box adds substantially (2–4× for high-grade scarce models — Appraisily).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Heavy-gauge steel, decals, crimped tabs (old) vs thin tin, rivets, modern hardware (replica).

More: pre-1960s bodies feel heavier and more rigid; earliest Tonka cabs have little or no plastic (steel or absent windows and steering wheels); later repairs show Phillips screws or mismatched hardware; fresh peening and colour mismatches indicate replaced parts (Appraisily). Original Buddy L paint is enamel over heavy steel with factory decals; restored examples often have too-glossy paint and new decals (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint loss, repaints, missing parts.

Also: rust (surface vs rust-through on floors and fenders), bent fenders/bumpers, cracked or replaced tyres, missing small parts (faucets, filler caps, ladders, headlights), broken dump/hoist mechanisms, and replaced decals. Repaints generally lower value compared with honest unrestored originals in the same grade, and touch-ups near graphics are often obvious (Appraisily). Ask for underside photos (rivets, stampings) and close-ups of decals.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Repro Buddy L and decor replicas.

Also: reproduction decals and parts are sold for restorers, so a restored truck can look factory-new (Appraisily). Asian décor "antique" trucks (Model T pickups, fire trucks, tankers with spoke wheels and faux rust) are new — they have no maker name, use modern fasteners, and the "rust" is paint.

## Market
Strong for prewar. Buyers: toy collectors (Buddy L, Keystone, Smith-Miller), nostalgia buyers (1950s–60s Tonka), restorers buying parts trucks, and décor buyers. Q4 demand (gift season).

## What sellers usually miss
Prewar Buddy L in barns. Also: early Tonka "Mound, Minn." markings, which separate 1947–54 trucks from later ones, and small original parts that complete a set.

## Shipping / handling
$15–30.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, originality, size.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker and the evidence (decal/stamping location, with photo), give length, width and height, and describe originality plainly: "original paint and decals, play wear", "repainted", or "reproduction decals". List what is present and missing (ladders, faucet, caps, bumpers, headlights), the tyre type, and any rust-through or bent parts with photos of the underside. Give a date range only with reasons (e.g. "Mound, Minn. stamping — Mound Metalcraft era, 1947–54"). Mention an original box only if it is the matching box.

## Buy rule
Decor replicas never clear.
