## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Brass (copper-zinc) and bronze (copper-tin) objects made in China or for the Chinese-style export market. Tells:
- **Decorative wall plaques** (dragons, phoenixes): thin sand-cast or stamped brass, flat back with hangers, polished bright, 1960s–90s Taiwan/Korea/Hong Kong (example 7952631186).
- **Engraved bowls and trays**: thin spun brass with chased dragon/phoenix and key-fret borders, 20th-c. export (example 7934115711) – sometimes sold as "singing bowls"; Tibetan/Nepalese bowls are thicker hammered bell bronze.
- **Hand warmers (shou lu)**: oval or round lidded pots with pierced lids (often with flower/lattice/shou patterns), swing handle(s), 4–7" wide; Qing examples have hand-pierced lids, thick walls, a cast or engraved maker seal on the base, worn patina; 20th-c. and modern ones have machine-pierced lids and bright polish (example 7939880705 – polished, double swing handles with shield-shaped mounts, likely 20th c.).
- **Censers**: tripod or compressed globular with handles; "Xuande" six-character cast mark on 95%+ of later pieces.
- **Buddhas and figures**: gilt bronze (Tibeto-Chinese), heavily reproduced.

## Makers, brands and variants
No makers' names on most pieces. Marks: Xuande reign mark (almost always apocryphal), "Zhang Mingqi" seal (famous 17th-c. hand-warmer maker; mostly faked), "Made in China"/"Hong Kong"/"Taiwan" stamps (post-1950 export), "Korea" stamps. Japanese bronzes (Meiji) are a separate type with signed panels.

### Golden-egg tells
- Hu Wenming (Yunjian, late Ming) signed parcel-gilt censers — ~150–250× ($5,000–7,500 estimate) vs the $32 median — six-character "Yunjian Hu Wenming zhi" mark cast along the base, mythical-beast handles and gilt relief on a dark ground (https://live.revereauctions.com/online-auctions/revere-auctions/17th-c-chinese-hu-wenming-bronze-mythical-censer-6428164)
- 17th–18th c. Song-style censers with a long (20-character) Xuande mark and original mallow-leaf stand — £62,500 sold vs a £200–300 estimate — multi-line cast reign mark rather than the usual six characters, a fitted lobed bronze stand, thick heavy walls (https://learnantiques.com.au/wight-place-right-time-for-censer/)
- Late-Ming/Kangxi (c. 1690) bronze censers — ~150–250× ($5,000–8,000 at auction) vs the median — crisp cast detail, deep layered red-brown patina worn on handles and rim, heavy for size (https://dipsy.pbs.org/wgbh/roadshow/appraisals/chinese-bronze-censer-ca-1690)
- Republican (1912–49) white-bronze/paktong hand warmers — ~3–6× ($150–300 dealer) vs 1960s–90s export brass — silvery-grey alloy, incised butterflies/flowers, ~9 in wide with hand-pierced lid and swing handle; not mirror-polished (https://www.1stdibs.com/furniture/asian-art-furniture/metalwork/chinese-white-bronze-hand-warmer-censer-republican-period/id-f_43282972)
- Qing hand warmers with a cast base seal (Zhang Mingqi type) — ~6–45× ($200–1,500) vs the median; the seal is almost always faked — hand-pierced lid with slightly irregular lattice, thick walls, worn dark patina, seal cast not stamped (entry comp)
- Gilt-splash ("sprinkled gold") bronze censers — ~5–30× ($200–1,000+) vs plain censers — irregular gold splashes fused into a brown ground, not painted-on gold paint that sits proud (entry comp)
- "Made in China"/"Taiwan"/"Korea" stamped dragon plaques and spun bowls — no multiple; this is the $15–60 floor — stamped country name, bright lacquered polish, light weight (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight: real cast bronze/brass is heavy for its size; spun or stamped brass is light and rings.
- Casting: crisp, undercut detail and hand-finished chasing = better; soft, blurry detail with file marks or porosity = recent.
- Patina: genuine old patina is layered and worn on high points, often dark with green/red in recesses; artificial patina is uniform, powdery, and may rub off; mirror polish (example 7939880705) removes evidence and value.
- Base: well-worn, smooth bases on old pieces; sharp edges and bright metal on new.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, splits on spun bowls, broken or replaced handles, missing lids/liners on hand warmers, solder repairs (silver-grey lines), over-polishing, lacquer coatings, verdigris (active bronze disease – powdery light-green spots).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
This is one of the most faked categories in antiques: "Xuande" censers, "Zhang Mingqi" hand warmers, archaic-style ritual vessels with fake burial patina, gilt Buddhas. Any claim older than 19th century needs expert authentication – outside Dylan's rules. Treat everything as 20th-century décor unless proven otherwise.

## Market
Strong Chinese-buyer auction market for genuine antiques; decorative export brass is abundant and slow. No seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
Heavy, crisply cast censers or hand warmers with worn patina mixed in estate brass lots. Sellers usually over-estimate polished modern brass as "antique Qing".

## Shipping / handling
Plaques and bowls 1–4 lb $10–18; hand warmers 1–3 lb $12–18; censers heavy, $15–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** Chinese brass/bronze, form (censer, hand warmer, bowl, plaque), motif (dragon, lion), mark (Xuande, "China"/"Hong Kong"), size, "20th century". Junk: "Ming", "Qing", "antique" without provenance, "rare", "museum". `Chinese Brass Censer Incense Burner Xuande Mark 20th Century 6 in`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers; genuine antiques with provenance sell for far more at LiveAuctioneers than on eBay.
- **Price position:** export pieces at the sold median ($15–60; censer median $32), OBO, accept −25%, floor $15; 20th-c. hand warmers/censers at median $50–200, OBO; cut 20% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** full piece on plain background; base with mark close-up; interior; detail of casting crispness and patina wear on handles/rim; dents/repairs; ruler for scale; piece on a scale.
- **State:** "Chinese brass, 20th century" unless provenance supports older; dimensions, weight, mark photo, condition (dents, polish, solder repairs, missing lid/stand), flaws by photo number; no dynasty claims.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox, 1–4 lb $10–18 for plaques/bowls, 1–3 lb $12–18 hand warmers; heavy censers $15–25, double-box, wrap lid and stand separately.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [cast brass] [censer] with a [Xuande-style] mark, [heavy at X lb] with [worn patina]; comparable 20th-century pieces sell for $[50–200], and I've priced it as 20th century, not as an antique."
