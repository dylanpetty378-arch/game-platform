## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hard-paste porcelain from Russian/Soviet factories. Lomonosov Cobalt Net: white ground with a lattice of cobalt-blue lines and small 22k gold stars at the crossings (designed by Anna Yatskevich, 1940s; in production since ~1950). LFZ figurines: glossy, slip-cast animals (polar bears, cats, dogs, birds, seals) and folk/ballet figures with fine painting. Dulevo: bright, colourful folk-style figurines and "Agitka" tea sets. Kuznetsov: heavy, transfer-printed and hand-painted 19th–early-20th c. plates and tea ware with Cyrillic marks and imperial eagles. Gardner: 19th c. biscuit (unglazed) peasant figures, rich Imperial-style tea ware.

## Makers, brands and variants (marks)
- **Imperial Porcelain Factory, St Petersburg (1744)** → renamed State Porcelain Factory (GFZ) 1917 → **Lomonosov (LFZ)** 1925 → Imperial Porcelain (IFZ) 2005. Marks: Soviet LFZ – blue underglaze stylised "ЛФЗ" logo, often with "Made in USSR" / "СДЕЛАНО В СССР"; post-1991 "Lomonosov – Made in Russia"; post-2005 "IFZ"/"Imperial Porcelain 1744" with double-headed eagle. Soviet-era (USSR mark) is worth more than current production.
- **Kuznetsov (M.S. Kuznetsov partnership, 1889–1917)**: factories at Dulevo, Riga, Budy (Ukraine), Verbilki (after buying Gardner 1892), Tver, Kuznetsovo. Marks: "Т-ва М.С. Кузнецова" with double-headed eagle, factory name.
- **Gardner (Verbilki, 1766–1891)**: Latin "Gardner" or Cyrillic "Гарднеръ" with St George; post-Soviet Verbilki factory reuses look.
- **Dulevo (DFZ)**: Soviet marks with "Дулёво" and stylised logo; figurines and colourful tea sets.
- **Others**: Konakovo (faience figurines), Polonne (Ukraine), Baranovka, Gorodnitsa, Riga RPR (Latvian Soviet porcelain/figurines, "made in USSR"), Proletary, Kiev.
- **Agitprop (1918–1920s)**: GFZ propaganda plates, often over-painted on Imperial blanks with double marks (Imperial cipher + Soviet hammer-and-sickle). Auction-level and the most faked category.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- LFZ: hard-paste, thin, bright white, crisp underglaze cobalt; gold stars hand-applied; slight irregularities = hand-painting. Factory-second marks: a scratched-through mark or a "2" on the base means second grade.
- Cobalt Net: genuine has 22k gold that wears; cheap Chinese copies have printed decal "net" with no stars or flat printed stars and no underglaze blue mark.
- Kuznetsov: heavier, often creamy body, transfer prints with hand-colouring, imperial eagle marks.
- Soviet-era vs current: "Made in USSR" or Cyrillic only = Soviet; English "Made in Russia" = post-1991.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Gold wear on cobalt net, chips on spouts and lid rims, hairlines in teapots, broken ears/tails on animal figurines, restoration on figurines (check under UV). Teapots: check lid fit and spout tip.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Agitprop and Imperial: massive fake industry (new decoration on old blanks, fake marks). Do not buy.
- Gardner and Kuznetsov: fake marks on recent Chinese/Eastern European porcelain.
- Cobalt Net: Chinese knock-offs and "inspired by" patterns.
- LFZ is still in production; post-1991 pieces are worth less than USSR-marked ones.

## Market
Steady niche: Russian/Ukrainian diaspora and porcelain collectors; buyers in US, Germany, Israel. Post-2022 sanctions cut new Russian supply to the West, which tends to support prices for pieces already outside Russia. Figurines sell in 2–6 weeks; cobalt net in 1–4 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
- LFZ animal figurines look like generic "porcelain bear" or "white cat figurine" – the blue underglaze ЛФЗ mark tells the story.
- Cyrillic marks are unreadable to most US sellers, so Kuznetsov pieces get listed as "old china plate".
- USSR vs. "Made in Russia" difference.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile. Teapots and figurines need double-boxing with 2 in of padding; $10–18 Ground Advantage; lids wrapped separately.
