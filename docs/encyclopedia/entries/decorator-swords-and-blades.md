# Decorator swords, "antique" machetes and tourist blades (Indian/Pakistani decorators; real makers: Wilkinson, Ames, Emerson & Silver, Collins, Solingen, Klingenthal)

Summary: Decorator/"Made in India" swords and Pakistani "Damascus" bowies $20–50; numbered WWII Japanese NCO swords, Collins machetes $100–300; Civil War / Wilkinson / signed gunto $400–1,500; signed Japanese blades and presentation swords $2,000+ (outside scope).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Craigslist "antique cavalry sword / Indian sword / machete antique" is almost always a 1970s–2000s Indian or Pakistani decorator: **"MADE IN INDIA" stamped at the ricasso**, red/green velvet-covered scabbard, acid-etched scroll "engraving", wire-wrapped or dyed-leather grip, thin guard. Hand-forged "tribal" sickle blades with scratched runes are the same category.
Real military swords have: maker/arsenal stamps (Wilkinson, Ames, Solingen, Klingenthal, proof marks), steel/leather scabbards (never velvet), unit numbers on the guard, and a serial on Wilkinsons.
Look-alikes: modern replica makers (Windlass, Cold Steel, Hanwei) are sharp, well-made and marked; they are not antiques but have their own user market (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Indian/Pakistani decorators** (1970s–present): "Made in India/Pakistan" ricasso stamp, velvet scabbards, etched scrolls. Junk tier.
- **Pakistani "Damascus" bowies**: pattern-welded look, bone/wood handles, leather sheath; $20–50.
- **Ames / Emerson & Silver** (US Civil War makers): M1860 cavalry sabres, M1850 officer swords; collectible tier. "Ames-marked" US M1860 reproductions sit in the decent tier.
- **Wilkinson** (UK): officer swords with a serial on the spine that can be looked up in the Wilkinson proof books (general knowledge, verify).
- **British 1796 Light Cavalry** pattern: collectible tier.
- **Solingen / Klingenthal**: German and French blade-making centres; proof marks and maker logos at the ricasso.
- **Collins & Co.** (Connecticut) machetes: stamped "Collins & Co. Legitimus" with a crowned-hand logo (general knowledge, verify); $100–300.
- **Japanese WWII**: Type 95 NCO sword (cast-aluminium or copper handle, serial-numbered) decent tier; Type 94/98 gunto with signed tang collectible; signed blades in shirasaya top tier.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Decorator: thin, soft blades, acid-etched (not engraved) designs, brass-coloured cast guards, velvet over wood scabbards, glued or loose handles. Real: tempered steel with proof marks, fullers ground evenly, steel scabbards with drag and rings, shagreen or leather grips with twisted wire, blued/gilt etching on good officer blades. Old blades show even grey patina and honest wear at the grip; new "aged" pieces have uniform chemical darkening (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common: loose handle, rust pitting, bent blade, missing scabbard, replaced grip wire. On real swords, a matching original scabbard and intact etching matter most; heavy cleaning/polishing cuts value (general knowledge, verify). A loose handle on a decorator is just more evidence of junk.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian-made Civil War and Japanese "gunto" reproductions are common; fake Japanese tang signatures and fake Ames stamps exist (general knowledge, verify). Authenticating Japanese blades and Confederate swords needs an expert — outside Dylan's scope.

## Market
Real military swords sell steadily to militaria collectors on eBay and at specialist auctions; decorator swords sell slowly and cheaply, mostly locally (general knowledge, verify). Facebook Marketplace bans weapons, so eBay is the main outlet.

## What sellers usually miss
Nothing on the junk tier; on the real tier they miss the maker stamp. The velvet scabbard and "MADE IN INDIA" are instant passes.

## Shipping / handling
3–5 lb, long box ($25–40). eBay allows swords; Facebook Marketplace does not.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker/arsenal stamp, pattern/model, overall and blade length, blade condition, scabbard type and whether it matches, any unit or serial numbers. Photograph the ricasso stamps, tang (if removable), guard numbers and scabbard throat. Title keywords: maker, model year/pattern, "sabre"/"sword", country, era (general knowledge, verify).

## Buy rule
Buy when: arsenal/maker stamp legible in photo and price ≤ 25 % of comps. Pass when: velvet scabbard, "Made in India/Pakistan", loose handle, "collectible" with no maker. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat (long but flat).
