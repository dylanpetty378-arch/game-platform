Buy rule (short): Only buy 19th-century pieces (pearlware, unmarked early Staffordshire, big well-drainers/platters), Gaudy Willow, or rare forms; pass on teapots and dinnerware from 1950 on.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A chinoiserie transfer pattern (Thomas Minton design c.1790) showing a willow tree, a bridge with three figures, a pagoda/teahouse, a boat, a fence, and two birds. Made by hundreds of factories in England, the US and Japan from the 1790s to today. Variants: standard Willow, "Two Temples", Flow Blue Willow (blurred), Gaudy Willow (overpainted rust/gold), and red/pink/brown/green Willow.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Spode (1790s on, impressed SPODE), Minton, Davenport: early, pearlware, valuable.
- Booths "Real Old Willow" (1900s–1940s; blue with a gold rim), Royal Doulton "Booths" later.
- Buffalo Pottery (US 1905–1916; Gaudy Willow especially).
- Johnson Bros, Churchill, Wood & Sons, Alfred Meakin, Swinnertons, Myott, Allerton: 20th-c English.
- Sadler (Burslem; swirl-moulded teapots, 1940s–2000s; base marked "Sadler England").
- Homer Laughlin, Royal China, Jeannette (US mass market; Royal "Willow Ware" is a supermarket premium).
- Japan export (1920s–80s; "Made in Japan" ink stamp; thin porcelain, often cobalt).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Early (pre-1840) pearlware has a bluish glaze pooling in the footring, three-point stilt marks on the underside, a soft blurred transfer, and is light for its size. Late 19th/20th c: whiter body, crisp transfers, printed backstamps with "England" (after 1891) or "Made in England" (after ~1910s). Japanese: thinner porcelain, brighter cobalt, often cruder line work.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Crazing, staining, utensil marks, rim chips, hairlines. Teapots: chipped spout tips, lids broken or swapped, lid knob repairs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese "Willow" with fake old marks; Churchill and Johnson Bros still produce Willow. "Spode" Blue Willow is still made, and modern backstamps say Spode with a date.

## Market
Very large supply and soft demand. Teapots and 20th-c pieces sell slowly at low prices. eBay: hundreds sold per month. Days to sell: 20–60.

## What sellers usually miss
Unmarked early pearlware platters (bluish glaze, stilt marks) get priced like modern ones; Gaudy Willow gets called "Imari"; Booths gold-rim pieces get lumped in as "blue and white".

## Shipping / handling
A teapot needs a double box with the lid wrapped separately; $15–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Blue Willow", maker (Spode/Booths/Buffalo/Johnson Bros/Sadler), form (platter/teapot/well-and-tree), size, era ("antique" only pre-1900, "pearlware"), "Gaudy Willow", "England". Junk: "blue and white dishes", "Imari" for Gaudy, "rare". `Antique Staffordshire Blue Willow Platter 18" Pearlware Unmarked c.1830`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for 19th-c. platters, Booths, Buffalo Gaudy; Replacements buys complete 20th-c. patterns; FB local for modern dinnerware sets and Sadler teapots ($24 median).
- **Price position:** 20th-c. pieces at the sold median ($5–25, teapots $24), OBO, cut fast; 19th-c. platters at the sold median (Spode platter $61) to p75 by size, accept −15%; cut 10% at 45 days.
- **Photos:** first: face straight-on; then backstamp or unmarked footring with glaze pooling, stilt marks, rim all round, back under light, crazing/staining close-up, teapot lid fit and spout tip, ruler.
- **State:** maker, era (pearlware vs modern body), form, dimensions, backstamp wording, crazing/staining/chips/hairlines by photo number, teapot capacity and lid match.
- **Pack / haul:** plates box-in-box, $12–15; teapot double box, lid wrapped separately, $15–20; big platters 5+ lb, $20+.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Early Staffordshire] pearlware platter, [18"], bluish glaze pooling and stilt marks on the foot — the 20th-century Willow sells for $15; the pre-1840 platters are the ones at $100–400."

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$18 out − ~$18 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Only 19th-c pieces, Gaudy Willow or rare forms qualify, at ≤ ~30% of the median and shoebox-sized or smaller (big platters are OK if they fit a passenger seat). Never buy modern teapots or dinnerware for resale.
