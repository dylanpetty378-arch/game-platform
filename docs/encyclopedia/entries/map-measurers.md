## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A small wheel run along a route on a map; a dial or scale converts revolutions to miles/km at given map scales. Forms:
- Pen-style tube (Mile-O-Graph): plastic barrel with a printed scale, tiny wheel at tip; the 1950s "The New Precision Mile-O-Graph Mileage Measurer" came in a red/white box.
- Pocket-watch style: round dial with one or two hands, chrome or brass, wheel at top; Swiss/German made, often marked "Swiss Made", "Germany", "Derby", "Silva".
- Victorian/Edwardian: brass, sterling or gilt pen-shaped or charm-style "Chartometers", sometimes with compass; patent dates.
- Military (WWII German "Kartenmesser" with Wehrmacht marks; British Army) — fake risk.
- **Terms** (Wikipedia): opisometer = the instrument for measuring curved lines (also curvimeter, meilograph, map measurer); **chartometer** = a geared version whose dial shows the length directly.
- **Morris's Patent Chartometer** (Birmingham, patented 1873 by Edward Russell Morris): brass case with a hinged glass front opened by a side press-button, steel wheel at the bottom, interchangeable card scale discs; face reads "Morris's Patent"; casting under the front reads "THE MORRIS PATENTS ENGINEERING WORKS BIRMINGHAM"; serial numbers; some with a red-figure totaliser window. Later sold under the made-up name **"Wealemefna"**. Sizes from a 15 g "bijou" to ~80 g with 2 in dial (Three Points of the Compass).
- **Mile-O-Graph** (Three Points of the Compass): pen-shaped, ~162 mm long, ~21 g, mostly plastic with a brass tracking wheel; body marked with an arrow, "PAT'D NO. 2747287", "MILE-O-GRAPH ® N.Y.C." and "MADE IN U.S.A."; internal paper roll holds 30 scales. Body colours black, red and white (white yellows to cream).
- **Title words**: map measure, opisometer, curvimeter, chartometer, Wealemefna, mileage measurer, Kartenmesser, Kurvimeter.

## Makers, brands and variants
Mile-O-Graph (US, 1950s–60s, sold retail and by mail order, some printed for promotional gifting). Keuffel & Esser and Dietzgen (US drafting suppliers, chrome dial types). Derby / Coradi / Hafa (Swiss/German dial types). Silva (Sweden). Victorian English makers (e.g. Morris's Patent "Chartometer" and "Wealemefna", Birmingham, patented 1873).
- **Mile-O-Graph detail**: invented by Louis J. Petion of Garden City, NY; patent filed 1955, granted 29 May 1956 (US 2,747,287); advertised 1956–57 by Joseph Mennen Co., New York, at about $2 (three for $5), later $1.25–$1.98. Nautical version in a blue box and kilometre version with its own box; early boxes are sturdy lidded card; later ones slim vinyl slip cases; leather slip cases briefly. Later clones swapped the brass wheel for plastic.
- **Hamilton Watch Co. (USA)**: dial-type map measure, Model 331 (and "MODEL 33" marked examples), back stamped "HAMILTON WATCH CO. USA"; possibly WWII-era military use, unconfirmed (Collectors Weekly / NAWCC Bulletin).
- **Morris (Birmingham)**: Patent Chartometer variants with pointer only or pointer plus totaliser; sizes from bijou to large; often in leather-bound wooden cases with scale cards.

### Quick ID checklist
- Pen-shaped plastic with "MILE-O-GRAPH ® N.Y.C." = 1956-on Mile-O-Graph (low tier); check for a brass wheel (original) vs plastic (clone).
- Round dial with a hinged glass front and card scale discs, "Morris's Patent" on face = Victorian Morris chartometer.
- "Wealemefna" on the dial = Morris's later trade name.
- Look for hallmarks on silver cases (lion passant, date letter) — sterling raises the tier.
- Watch-maker names on the back (Hamilton, Swiss watch brands) = better than generic.
- Box or fitted case present? Photograph it; it adds value.
- Wheel turns and pointer moves; scale legible; glass intact.
- Military marks: be sceptical of eagles or unit markings; ask for close photos.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Morris's Patent Chartometer with totaliser, in fitted case with scale cards** — high tier $75–250 (existing ladder, verify) vs $8–25 plastic promo — brass dial with hinged glass front, red-figure totaliser window, leather-bound wooden case.
- **Sterling or gilt Victorian charm/pen chartometers** — $200–400+ (existing ladder, verify) vs $20–60 common brass — hallmarks, figural or engraved case.
- **Hamilton Watch Co. Model 331 map measure** — above the $20–60 mid tier (no sold comps fetched; est., verify) — "HAMILTON WATCH CO. USA" stamped on back.
- **Mile-O-Graph nautical or kilometre version in its original box** — top of the $8–25 promo tier (Terapeak median for the common one is $9.72) — blue nautical box or printed kilometre box.
- **Genuine WWII military measurers with verifiable markings** — $200–400+ top tier (existing ladder) vs fakes worth little — needs specialist verification; photos alone are not enough.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling or gilt-brass with hallmarks = high tier. Two-scale dial, jewelled movement, original fitted case add value. Plastic barrels are low.
- Mile-O-Graph accuracy is poor (about 10% over in one test); the paper scale roll is usually the first part to fail (Three Points of the Compass).
- Victorian instruments: cast maker's name inside the case, serial numbers, steel wheels, and replaceable card scales indicate genuine period work.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Wheel must turn and pointer move; scale legible; box adds 20–40% to cheap promo types.
- Missing scale cards on a Morris chartometer reduce value; cracked or replaced glass fronts are common.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Repro WWII military measurers with added eagles exist. Plastic promos not reproduced.
- Later plastic-wheeled clones of the Mile-O-Graph exist; they are not reproductions but cheaper copies.

## Market
Small, steady niche; a few dozen sales a month on eBay; slow except for Victorian pieces.
- Buyers: scientific-instrument collectors (Victorian chartometers), map and travel collectors, and mid-century advertising collectors (Mile-O-Graph).

## What sellers usually miss
Silver hallmarks on small Victorian chartometers listed as "old brass gadget".
- "Wealemefna" and "Morris's Patent" names are rarely put in titles.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size; first-class envelope/box $5–7.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Show the scale, wheel, box and any marks; state function tested.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the type (opisometer / chartometer) and maker as marked. For Mile-O-Graph: patented 1956 (US 2,747,287), made in New York, sold by mail order in the 1950s–60s; state body colour, scale type (miles, nautical, km), whether the scale roll turns and the box is present. For Morris: Birmingham, patented 1873; state pointer-only or with totaliser, number of scale cards, case, and serial number. Always say whether the wheel and pointer move.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$6 − ~$6 − price ≥ max($50, price). Low and mid tiers can never clear $50. Only Victorian silver/cased types (median $150+) at ≤ $40.
