## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **GWTW / parlor lamp:** a glass base/font (often painted to match) with a ball or tulip globe shade, kerosene burner with chimney; 1880–1910 peak.
- **Banquet lamp:** tall (24–36") slender stand, usually cast spelter or brass with an oil font and ball shade.
- **Electric repros (1950s–1990s):** same look but factory-wired with a socket hidden in the font, a three-way switch knob, often a cord through the base, modern decal or airbrush roses, fake "burner" fittings.
- Tell: original oil lamps have a working wick-winder knob, a font that holds oil (often removable), a collar screwed onto the font; wiring is retro-fitted through a drilled base.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bradley & Hubbard (Meriden CT):** cast-iron and brass bases, marked; high quality.
- **Pittsburgh Lamp Brass & Glass, Consolidated, Fostoria Glass Specialty, Plume & Atwood (burners), Miller (Edward Miller & Co.), Royal (burners):** stamped burners/bases.
- **Hand-painted milk-glass ball shades:** wildflowers, roses, cosmos, scenes; red/orange ombré fired paint; glossy.
- **Repro makers:** Fenton (1970s–90s, satin glass), L&L WMC, Accurate Casting, numerous unbranded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old glass: slight waves, bubbles, worn fired paint, ground rim on shade fitter.
- Old paint is fired into glass; new airbrushed decals are flat and uniform.
- Victorian iron/brass frames, wrought scrolls, spelter (zinc alloy; bends and breaks) bases.
- Burner names: P&A, Royal, B&H, Miller, Juno — signs of period.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Shade chips at fitter, cracks, mismatched shades, broken spelter, rust, rewiring (needed for electrified lamps — a repair).
- Replacement chimneys are fine; replacement shades kill value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Massive volume of 1960s–80s electric repros; most Craigslist "antique banquet lamps" are these. Painted roses in yellow/burgundy with cream ground are the classic repro look.
- Married pieces (old base, new shade) common.

## Market
- Market fell sharply after 2010; decorator demand weak. Most sell locally due to shipping fragility. eBay sell-through low for shipped lamps.

## What sellers usually miss
- Rarely undervalued — owners overprice. Occasionally a true Victorian lamp with intact original paint is listed as "old lamp".

## Shipping / handling
- Large (24–32"), fragile globe: ship globe and base in separate double boxes; total $40–70 shipped. Breakage claims common.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Disclose electrification, measure height, photograph burner markings, show globe in light to reveal cracks.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$45) − ship to Dylan (~$45) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- At typical $200 Victorian resale, max buy ≈ $35. Shipping two ways makes most lamps impossible. Must fit passenger seat (disassemble). Electric repro = always pass.
