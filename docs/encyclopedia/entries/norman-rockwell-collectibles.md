## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mass-produced 1970s–1990s collectibles licensed from Rockwell images: porcelain bells with wooden handles (Gorham "Christmas" and "Four Seasons" bells, 1975–), plates (Gorham, Knowles, Rockwell Society), figurines (Gorham 1974–, Dave Grossman 1973–), mugs, steins, ornaments; plus period printed matter.
- Gorham bells: ~6" porcelain, turned wood handle, gold trim, title and year inside or on rim ("Chilling Chore" 1977, "Sweet Song So Young", "Gay Blades").
- Saturday Evening Post covers (1916–1963): magazine covers, original or trimmed.
- Calendars: Boy Scouts of America (Brown & Bigelow, 1925–76), Brown & Bigelow "Four Seasons".
- Lithographs: hand-signed in pencil limited editions published 1970s (Rockwell died 1978); plate-signed (printed) reproductions are worth little.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Gorham (Providence RI): bells, plates, figurines, "Four Seasons" series.
- Dave Grossman Designs (St Louis): figurines 1973–; numbered NR-xx.
- Rockwell Museum (Philadelphia, commercial, not the Stockbridge museum), Lynell Studios, River Shore, Edwin M. Knowles (Rockwell Society of America plates), Franklin Mint (sterling plates: melt value), Danbury Mint, Bradford Exchange.
- Signed lithographs: Circle Fine Art (Chicago), Eleanor Ettinger (NY); editions of 200 with hand signature and number in pencil.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Porcelain items: decals, gold trim; boxes add little.
- Lithographs: check for pencil signature and number vs printed signature; embossed blindstamps.
- Franklin Mint sterling: stamped ".925 / Sterling" (value by weight).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on bell rims, loose or missing clappers, wood handle separated; figurine chips; magazine tears, missing mailing labels, trimmed covers.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake pencil signatures on offset lithographs; printed "signatures" mistaken for hand signing; reproduction Post covers (1970s) on heavy paper.

## Market
Extremely oversupplied; buyers are scarce for porcelain items. Printed ephemera and signed lithographs have a real market.

## What sellers usually miss
- Franklin Mint sterling Rockwell plates (silver weight).
- Hand-signed pencil lithographs in "Rockwell print" listings.
- They overvalue bells and figurines at original issue prices.

## Shipping / handling
Bells/figurines 1 lb; $10–12 each way.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give title, year and maker; photograph signatures closely; disclose chips; bundle porcelain items.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$10 to buyer − ~$10 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Porcelain bells, plates, figurines, mugs: pass at any price.
- Sterling plates: buy only if price ≤ melt × 0.6.
- Signed lithographs: need authentication (professional) → pass unless documented with COA from Circle/Ettinger and price ≤ $150.
