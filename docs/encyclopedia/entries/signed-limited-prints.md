## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Original print:** made by the artist (or under the artist's supervision) on a plate, stone, block or screen, and printed by hand in an edition. Pencil signature at lower right, edition number (e.g. 154/250) or "A.P." / "E.A." / "H.C." / "P.P." at lower left, title (if any) in the middle, all *below* the image in the margin.
- **Etching/drypoint/engraving/aquatint (intaglio):** look for a **plate mark**, a recessed rectangle pressed into the paper around the image with slightly bevelled corners; ink lines sit slightly raised (you can feel them); drypoint lines have a soft velvety burr; aquatint shows a fine grainy tone. Plate mark visible in a raking-light photo even through a mat if the margin is shown.
- **Lithograph:** flat, no plate mark; under a loupe an irregular, grainy or crayon-like texture (stone or plate) vs a **regular grid or rosette of dots** (offset reproduction = low value).
- **Serigraph/silkscreen:** thick, flat layers of opaque ink, each colour sitting on top of the last; edges of colours can be slightly raised.
- **Woodcut/linocut:** bold areas, wood grain sometimes printed, ink squashed toward the edges of shapes, often on thin Japanese paper.
- **Giclée/offset:** dot screen (offset) or fine random inkjet spray (giclée) under a 10× loupe → decor. "Signed in the plate" (signature printed as part of the image) is not hand-signed.
- Title words that matter: "original etching", "pencil signed", "plate mark", "AAA", "drypoint", "artist proof". Title words that usually mean decor: "giclée", "canvas transfer", "hand-embellished", "museum print", "COA".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Regional American etchers (1910–1950):** e.g., Walter Ronald Locke (Florida scenes; Anclote Light, Tarpon Springs), Chauncey Ryder, Luigi Kasimir (color etchings, $100–400), Lionel Barrymore, Kerr Eby, Levon West, Roi Partridge.
- **Walter Ronald Locke (1883–1949):** born Winchester MA, studied with Louis Kronberg and Alfred Hutty; studio on the Anclote River, Tarpon Springs FL from 1935 until death; summers in New England "hill country" (maple-sugaring and tree subjects). Florida titles: *Anclote Light, Fla.* (plate c. 8⅜ × 12⅜ in), *Mullet Fisherman, Fla.* (1937, 9 × 12), *The Net-Mender*, *Patio, Old Toledo House, St. Augustine* (1937), *Glorious Eucalyptus* (1938), *The Patriarch, Florida* (1942), *Pelican Key*, *Palmettos*, *Chinaberry Tree*, *Cormorant Rookery*. "Thousands" of his etchings were published by Associated American Artists (AAA), New York; works are pencil-signed, titled and often dated in the lower margin, and many keep the AAA label with a printed artist biography on the back. In Library of Congress and Nelson-Atkins collections; prizes St. Petersburg Art Club 1938, Florida Federation of Art 1943. (artistsofoldflorida.com; Leland Little)
- **Associated American Artists (AAA, New York, from 1934):** sold signed etchings and lithographs by American artists by mail and department store, in editions typically of about 250 (general knowledge, verify). AAA label or blind stamp is a good sign of a genuine pencil-signed original but also means the image is not rare. Big AAA names (Benton, Grant Wood, John Steuart Curry, Doris Lee) sell far higher than mid-list names (Locke, etc.).
- **French naive/decorator lithographers (1970s–90s):** Isabel Baillen, Yves Ganne, Michel Delacroix (his originals sell $200–800), Francine Van Hove, Guy Dessapt. Most sell $20–150.
- **Big names:** Chagall, Miró, Dalí (fraud heavy), Picasso (fraud heavy), Rockwell (signed offset lithos common), Erté.
- **Variants that move value:** early state or proof impressions (marked "trial proof", "1st state") > numbered edition > posthumous restrike (no pencil signature, often thinner modern paper, wider modern margins); small editions (<100) > large; hand-coloured by the artist vs later colouring; full margins vs trimmed.

### Quick ID checklist
- Is there a **plate mark** around the image? (Yes = intaglio original or restrike; check signature.)
- Is the signature in **pencil** in the margin (grey graphite sheen, pressure varies), or printed in the image?
- Edition number / A.P. at lower left? Title in pencil?
- Loupe or close-up photo: regular dot grid/rosette = offset reproduction; random fine spray = giclée.
- Paper: deckle edges, watermark (Arches, BFK Rives, Van Gelder), soft rag feel vs bright white wood-pulp.
- Back of frame: AAA label, gallery label, artist bio sheet, framer's date — photograph it.
- Margins full (not trimmed into the plate mark)? Any mat burn or foxing?
- Artist has **real auction records** (AskArt/Invaluable) at ≥ $150 for this kind of print?

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Rag paper with deckle edges, watermark, plate mark, pencil (not printed) signature.
- Foxing, mat burn and acidic mats reduce value.
- Quality tells: rich, slightly raised black ink with clean wiped highlights (intaglio); sharp fine lines without dot breakup; paper that feels heavy and soft; generous margins.
- Cheap production tells: glossy or coated paper, image printed right to the edge, "COA" from a decor company, numbered in the thousands, signature that looks identical across many listings (printed).
- Old vs new: period frames with wire hangers and paper dust covers, browned backing board, AAA labels and 1930s–40s dates in pencil point to period impressions; bright white mats and Plexiglas are later reframes (not bad, but no provenance).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Foxing, light staining, mat burn (yellow line), tears, trimmed margins (bad for etchings), sun fading.
- Glued-down (fully mounted) sheets lose value; tape stains on the back margins are common in old frames.
- Restoration (bleaching, tear repairs) shows as uneven paper tone or thin spots against light; ask for a photo with the mat lifted.
- Ask: is it out of the frame? any staining under the mat? is it mounted with tape or glued down?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Dalí, Chagall, Miró, Picasso and Erté prints are heavily faked; "signed in the plate" vs pencil; pre-signed blank paper fraud (Dalí). Out of scope for Dylan (authentication needed).
- Offset reproductions with printed signature sold as "signed".
- Posthumous restrikes from original plates (Rembrandt, Whistler, Zorn and others) have plate marks but no pencil signature and sell for a fraction.
- Pages cut from books and portfolios (e.g. Verve, Mourlot) sold as "original lithographs" — some are genuine lithographs, but unsigned and worth far less than signed editions.

## Market
- Decor prints: glut. Listed etchers: slow but steady; regional (Florida) buyers pay for local subjects.
- AAA-published mid-list artists exist in large numbers, so they rarely spike; buyers are regional collectors, decorators wanting "real" art, and Florida-history buyers for Locke.

## What sellers usually miss
- A plate mark and pencil signature on an old etching means an original print; sellers often price as "framed print".
- Conversely, sellers inflate numbered decorator lithos.
- The AAA label and artist-bio sheet on the back (adds credibility); the exact title in pencil (lets buyers search records).

## Shipping / handling
- Ship unframed in a flat mailer if possible ($10–15); framed with glass $25–40 and breakage risk. Dylan can remove glass and ship with plexi.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Medium (etching vs litho), plate size, sheet size, signature/edition photos, condition (foxing).
- Photograph: full image, pencil signature and title close-up, plate mark in raking light, back of frame with labels, any foxing or mat burn.
- Title pattern: "<Artist> Original Etching '<Title>' Pencil Signed <year> <subject/place> AAA".

### What to tell the buyer
State the medium and how you know it (plate mark visible, pencil signature and title in the margin), the plate and sheet size, and any label on the back (for Locke: "published by Associated American Artists, New York; label retained"). For Locke, the verifiable background is: Walter Ronald Locke (1883–1949), studied with Louis Kronberg and Alfred Hutty, worked from a studio on the Anclote River in Tarpon Springs from 1935, prize-winning etcher held by the Library of Congress and Nelson-Atkins Museum. Describe condition plainly (foxing, mat burn, toning) and say whether it was examined out of the frame. Do not call a print "rare" or give an edition size unless it is written on the sheet.
