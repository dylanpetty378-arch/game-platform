## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Bulk bins: value by weight (eBay sold ~$8–12/lb clean sorted, $5–7/lb mixed with junk) plus MINIFIGURES (the real value: licensed Star Wars/Marvel figs $3–30 each, some $100+). Sealed/retired sets: check set number on the box against BrickLink. Retired big sets (Modulars 10xxx, UCS Star Wars, Tower Bridge 10214) run $200–600 used complete, more sealed. Every genuine LEGO stud is stamped "LEGO" (look-alikes: Mega Bloks larger studs, K'nex rods, Chinese clones Lepin etc.). Sellers write "Lego lot", "tote of Legos", "Lego collection", "by the pound".

Reading a bulk photo:
- **Density**: typical used bulk runs about 300–600 pieces per pound (Brickset). Lots heavy with big Duplo, baseplates or large Technic beams have fewer, lower-value pieces per pound.
- **Composition**: look for minifig heads/legs, printed tiles, Technic gears/axles, specialised pieces (windscreens, wheels, printed slopes), and instruction booklets — all lift value. Lots that look like only basic 2×4 bricks in primary colours are the low end.
- **Era**: old light/dark grey and old brown mean pre-2004–06 parts; light bluish grey/dark bluish grey and reddish brown mean 2003/04 and later (see Materials). Yellow-faced minifigs only (no flesh tones) is consistent with older/non-licensed themes; "light nougat" flesh-tone figs started 2004 with licensed themes.
- **Clones mixed in**: Mega Bloks (taller-looking studs, often branded "MEGA" on studs), K'nex rods/connectors, and unbranded studs.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Licensed themes**: Star Wars (1999–), Marvel/DC, Harry Potter, LOTR – minifigs drive value.
- **Retired large sets**: Modular buildings (10xxx), UCS Star Wars (Millennium Falcon 10179/75192, Star Destroyer), Tower Bridge 10214, Cloud City 10123, Ideas, Creator Expert.
- **Vintage**: 1980s Classic Space and Castle boxed sets – collector premium.
- **Clones/non-LEGO**: Mega Bloks, K'nex, Lepin and other clones – zero/negative value in a LEGO lot.
- **Technic**: gears, motors (Power Functions, older 9V/4.5V) and pneumatics are sought separately by MOC builders (general knowledge, verify).
- **Duplo**: double-size toddler bricks; sells by weight but lower per pound than System bricks and bulks up lots (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Zoom on studs: "LEGO" logo on every stud; logo-free or "MEGA" studs = clone content.
- Count minifigures visible; note licensed ones (lightsabers, helmets, capes, Hogwarts robes) — these are the value.
- Look for instruction booklets and set boxes; read set numbers off them.
- Check colour era: old grey/brown = pre-2004–06 parts; bluish greys = 2003/04+.
- Estimate pounds from the container (seller weight preferred) and price per pound vs $3 target.
- Look for yellowed whites/greys, dust, food crumbs or pet hair — cleaning time and lower price.
- Count non-LEGO parts (K'nex, Mega Bloks, Duplo): more than a little lowers the per-pound value.
- For a "complete" retired set, ask for the set number and whether all minifigs and instructions are present.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
ABS plastic with "LEGO" on every stud; colours consistent; old grey vs "new" bluish grey (2004 change) dates parts (general knowledge, verify). Quality lots: clean, sorted, with instructions and figs. Cheap/low lots: dusty, mixed brands, Duplo-heavy, sticker-damaged, yellowed white/grey bricks.

Colour-dating detail (BrickMissing colour study): Light Gray and Dark Gray were replaced in 2004 by Light Bluish Gray and Dark Bluish Gray (treat 2003 as the earliest reliable bluish-grey date); old Dark Gray was last seen in 2006; old Brown was retired by 2006 in favour of Reddish Brown. Light Nougat (modern flesh-tone minifig skin) arrived in 2004; Medium Green, Earth Orange, Light Green and original Pink were last used about 2005. So a tub of old greys and old brown is largely pre-2004 parts — of interest to builders of classic sets — while bluish greys mean 2003+.

Genuine-part quality tells (Bam Good Bricks): firm, slightly grainy ABS with some heft; LEGO logo also on the top of the minifig torso neck post, inside heads and on stud undersides; a cross ("X") reinforcing pattern under the minifig torso; minifig feet closed at the back; hands that grip accessories with firm, precise clutch; crisp, aligned pad printing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Yellowing (sun), bite marks, cracked bricks (especially old ABS), missing pieces in "complete" sets, smoke/pet odour, stickers. Sealed sets: box dents, resealed tape. Ask for figs photographed separately, set numbers, instructions present.

Also: broken pieces lower a bulk lot's value and are part of how bulk buyers score lots (Brickset); old light grey and some old dark-red/brown parts can become brittle and crack at stress points (general knowledge, verify); torn or water-damaged instruction booklets; minifig torsos with worn printing; cracked minifig hips (general knowledge, verify). Picked-over lots (minifigs and good parts already removed) are a common bust — ask directly whether figs were removed.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Clone sets (Lepin, King, etc.) copy retired sets and minifigs – check "LEGO" on studs and minifig neck; counterfeit minifigs common in "lot" photos. LEGO reissues some sets (e.g. updated UCS Falcon) which caps older set prices. (general knowledge, verify)

Counterfeit minifig tells (Bam Good Bricks): missing, faint or misspelled logo on the neck post; waxy, light or chalky plastic; floppy or over-stiff limbs; heavy mould ridges and messy injection marks; missing or crude "X" under the torso; open-backed feet; blurry faces, misaligned front/back torso print, off-tone colours. Listing red flags: "compatible", "works with", "Lego-style", and rare licensed figures priced far below market (counterfeit figs typically sell under about $8).

## Market
Very liquid: eBay, BrickLink, FB Marketplace, Mercari. Bulk sells steadily by weight; minifigs and retired sets sell fast. Strong Nov–Dec peak. (general knowledge, verify)

Who buys: parents (bulk by weight), MOC builders (specific parts, Technic, old greys for classic rebuilds), minifig collectors (licensed figs singly), and set collectors (complete retired sets with instructions). Parting out a bulk lot into figs + bulk + sets maximises return but costs hours.

## What sellers usually miss
Minifigs in the tote (Star Wars especially); retired set numbers; "Lego" that is actually Mega Bloks. Also: instruction booklets in the bottom of the tub (proof of which sets are there), Technic motors, and that a bin of old-grey parts may contain partial 1980s–90s sets.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy — 20 lb tote ≈ $30–45 each way; only worth it if figs/sets visible. eBay/BrickLink.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State weight, cleaned or not, LEGO-only (no clones), minifig count and themes, set numbers and completeness, instructions/boxes. Photograph figs separately, set boxes, a scale shot. Title keywords: "LEGO 10 lb bulk lot clean minifigures Star Wars", "retired 10214 Tower Bridge complete instructions", "sealed".

### What to tell the buyer
"Genuine LEGO only — every piece checked for the LEGO stud logo" (only if done); weight on a scale; washed or unwashed; number of minifigures and which themes; whether broken or yellowed pieces were removed; set numbers recognised and whether they are complete (and how checked — e.g. against the BrickLink inventory); instructions included. For figs, say they were checked for the LEGO logo on the neck post. For sealed sets, state box condition and that the seals are factory-original, untaped.

## Buy rule
Buy when: price ≤ $3/lb with visible licensed minifigs, or retired set ≤ 40% of BrickLink used. Pass when: price ≥ $5/lb bulk, current retail sets, or mixed brands. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). A large tote fits a passenger seat.
