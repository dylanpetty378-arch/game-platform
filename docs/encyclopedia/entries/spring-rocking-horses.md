## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Wonder Horse** (Wonder Products, Collierville TN, 1950s–80s): moulded plastic horse on four big coil springs in a tubular steel frame, with "Wonder Horse" moulded on the body or neck.
- Same market: Radio Flyer and Hedstrom spring horses, Blazon.
- A separate, higher market: carved and painted wood dapple-grey horses on bow rockers or a "safety" stand (English Ayres and Collinson, $800–3,000), and 1950s Mobo (pressed steel, UK, $150–400).
- Seller words: "rocking horse", "Wonder Horse", "spring horse", "vintage toy horse".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Wonder Products Wonder Horse**: the classic 1950s–60s horse with the moulded name. "Palomino" and early versions in original paint are the best. 1980s–2000s versions are common.
- **Hedstrom / Radio Flyer / Blazon**: same use market, lower collector interest.
- **Mobo** (D. Sebel & Co., UK, 1947–70s): pressed-steel horses that "walk" when ridden. $150–400 (general knowledge, verify).
- **G. & J. Lines, F.H. Ayres, Collinson (UK)**: carved wood dapple greys with horsehair manes and tails, glass eyes and leather tack, on bow rockers or a safety stand. $800–3,000, more for early Lines and Ayres (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Plastic Wonder Horses: check that the moulded name is there, the paint is original, the springs are intact and the frame isn't rusted.
- Carved wood: gesso and paint dapple finish, real horsehair, glass eyes, leather saddle, maker's plaque or stamp on the stand. Original paint beats repaint, though old professional restorations are accepted in the UK market (general knowledge, verify).
- One older note says early Wonder Horses with glass eyes sell higher. Moulded-plastic horses usually have moulded or painted eyes, so treat "glass eyes" as a wood-horse tell (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Spring horses: cracked plastic, broken spring mounts, a rusty frame and missing stirrups or handles.
- Wood horses: missing mane or tail, overpaint, woodworm, cracked legs and replaced tack.
- These are bulky, so damage is visible in photos. Hidden risk is in the spring anchors and in old repairs to wooden legs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Modern decorative reproduction dapple-grey horses (often made in Asia, or new UK makers like Stevenson Brothers, which are genuinely valuable new pieces) can be confused with antiques. Look at the wear, construction and maker plaque (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Local pickup on Facebook Marketplace and craigslist. Buyers are nostalgic parents and grandparents and decorators. Busiest before Christmas. Carved antiques go to auction houses and specialist dealers (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- The age and model of a Wonder Horse.
- Carved wood horses listed as "rocking horse $50".

## Shipping / handling
- Frame 40"×20"×36", ~25 lb: $80+ each way, effectively local-only. Wood horses are larger and need freight.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker and moulded name, decade, height and length, whether all springs and handles are present, paint condition. For wood horses, the maker plaque and whether the mane, tail and tack are original.
- Title keywords: "Vintage 1960s Wonder Horse Spring Rocking Horse", "Antique Dapple Grey Rocking Horse Lines/Ayres".
