## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast-iron frame with swivel arm and guides holding a long backsaw (22–30 in); quadrant with angle stops; wood base; corrugated or flat fences.
- **Stanley**: "Stanley" cast on frame, No. 346/358/360; flat fences. The 358 quadrant is numbered by polygon sides: 0 at 90°, then 24, 12, 8, 6 (30°), 5, 4 (45°) (Scodan).
- **Millers Falls Langdon Acme**: corrugated fence and swivel, "Langdon" casting; model number = box size + saw length.
- **Goodell-Pratt**: similar, "GP" marks.
- **Small clamp-type / wooden-base boxes** (e.g. Stanley No. 150): small, light, a few pounds; the only shippable type.

## Makers, brands and variants
Stanley (New Britain CT), Millers Falls (Langdon), Goodell-Pratt, Sargent, Disston (saws).
- **Stanley No. 358** — cast iron, japanned, made 1905–1972; cuts a 2×10 at 90° and a 2×8 at 45° (just). **358A** is the aluminium version, much lighter (Scodan). Original equipment includes stock guides and length stops, usually missing.
- **Millers Falls Langdon Acme** — three box sizes (1, 2, 2½) × saw lengths give model numbers (Sawmill Creek, Joshua Clark):

| Model | Box | Saw |
|---|---|---|
| 68 | 1 | 22 in |
| 69 | 1 | 24 in |
| 70 | 1 | 26 in |
| 71 | 2 | 22 in |
| 72 | 2 | 24 in |
| 73 | 2 | 26 in |
| 74 | 2½ | 28 in |
| 75 | 2½ | 30 in |

  Box 1 cuts ~7 in at 45°/90°; boxes 2 and 2½ ~10 in. The 74 is the most common; the 75 is more popular and more expensive because it is the larger (Sawmill Creek). Accessories: frame stops, a "dog" to hold stock to the fence, height stops, length stops (almost always missing).
- **Goodell-Pratt, Sargent** — similar cast-iron boxes, less collected.
- **Nobex** (Sweden, modern) — aluminium and plastic, with its own saw; a user tool, not vintage.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the casting: "STANLEY" + number, "MILLERS FALLS" / "LANGDON ACME" + number, "GP".
- Model number tells the saw length (Langdon 74 = 28 in, 75 = 30 in).
- Saw present? Read the saw's medallion/etch (Disston, Stanley, Millers Falls) and measure the length.
- Count accessories: stock guides, length stops, dog, height stops.
- Look for cracks in the base casting and the swivel arm (Sawmill Creek: check base breaks first).
- Japanning intact vs rust; saw guides move smoothly up and down.
- Aluminium (358A) vs cast iron: grey cast vs bright alloy, and the seller's carrying photo.
- Measure overall length: 30 in boxes fail the shipping maths.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Stanley No. 358 complete with original Stanley/Disston saw, stops and guides** — Terapeak 358 p75 $145 vs median $25.50 for generic "vintage miter box" — "STANLEY No 358" cast in, polygon-numbered quadrant, length stops on rods.
- **Millers Falls Langdon Acme 75 (30 in saw)** — more sought than the 74 (Sawmill Creek); Terapeak Langdon p75 $100 vs $35 p25 — "75" cast in, 30 in saw.
- **Stanley 358A aluminium** — lighter, which is the selling point to site carpenters (Scodan); premium vs cast-iron in shipped sales (general knowledge, verify) — bright alloy frame, "358A".
- **Original medallion saw matching the box** — missing saws halve value (old entry) — saw etch/medallion of the same maker, correct length for the model.
- **Small Stanley No. 150 or Goodell-Pratt bench box** — the only ones that ship cheaply enough to profit at ≤ $15 — small, light, wooden or cast base, no long saw.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original Disston/Stanley saw with medallion; complete stops and guides; crisp castings. Japanned (black enamel) finish on Stanley castings; original nickel or bright parts on guides. Smooth guide bearings and tight swivel are the user test (Sawmill Creek). Repainted boxes in spray-black lose the japanning gloss.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing stops, cracked castings, rusted saws; missing saws halve value. Also broken bases (check first), welded or brazed repairs on the swivel arm, worn guide bearings and screws that cannot be judged from photos (Sawmill Creek advises in-person inspection), length stops almost always missing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None. Married saws (a different maker's saw in the box) are common; state it.

## Market
Woodworkers locally; eBay shipping prohibitive. Parts (stops, guides, saws) sell individually on eBay to people completing boxes.

## What sellers usually miss
Complete original saws; Stanley No. 358 premium; the model number cast in tells the saw length.

## Shipping / handling
30 × 10 × 8 in, 25–40 lb: $40–70.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model number, saw length/brand, completeness, cracks.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] No. [model] cast-iron miter box, [box size/capacity]. Saw: [maker] [length] in, [medallion/etch], [sharp/needs sharpening]. Includes [stock guides, length stops, dog, height stops]. No cracks in base or swivel; guides slide freely; locks at [detents]. Japanning [x]%. Local pickup preferred; shipping is heavy (~[weight] lb)." Photograph the casting number, saw etch, quadrant and the underside of the base.
