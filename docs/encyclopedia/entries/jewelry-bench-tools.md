## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Rolling mill: two hardened steel rollers in a cast frame with a top screw, hand crank; brand cast or plated on the frame. **Durston** (UK, green or grey, "Durston" on the side plate), **Pepe Tools**, **Cavallin** (Italy) = valuable; unbranded black/green import mini-mills (Vevor, Harbor Freight style, "Made in India") = cheap. Combination mills have flat rollers plus wire grooves. Disc cutter: blue steel block with punches (Pepe = $150–300; import $30–60). Arbor press "No. 1/No. 2" green = generic import ($40–80). Foredom flex shaft (hanging motor, flexible shaft, handpiece, foot pedal) = $80–150 used. Seller words: "jewelry equipment", "metalsmith tools", "rolling mill".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Durston** (UK) – C80/C100/C130 (roller width in mm), DRM series, Hobby 80 economy (Otto Frei retail ~$700 new), power mills. Holds resale ~60–70% of retail (Ganoksin consensus).
- **Cavallin** (Italy) – quality manual mills.
- **Pepe Tools** – disc cutters, dapping sets, mills (some Pepe items are imported/rebadged; general knowledge, verify).
- **Foredom** (USA) – SR, CC, TX motors; handpiece type (#30, #44T quick-change) matters (general knowledge, verify).
- **Bonny Doon** – hydraulic presses and dies; strong resale.
- **Generic imports** (India/China) – mini mills, No. 1/No. 2 arbor presses, disc cutters.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Quality mills: heavy cast-iron frame, hardened ground rollers with mirror finish, gear reduction, maker plate. Cheap mills: light castings, soft rollers that mark easily (Ganoksin), rough paint. Disc cutters: Pepe/Swanstrom are precision-ground with a tight punch fit; imports are loose. Old vs new: Durston paint colour/plates changed over time (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Roller pitting, rust, dents or engraved scratches (transfer onto every sheet – major value loss), stripped screws, missing handles; flex shaft motor brushes, worn sheath, dead foot pedal. Ask for roller closeups and whether the mill turns smoothly; for Foredom, a running test.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No true fakes, but import mills are painted green to resemble Durston; check for the cast/plated Durston name on the side plate. (general knowledge, verify)

## Market
Niche but steady: jewelry students, hobby metalsmiths, small studios; estate sales of retired jewelers produce lots. eBay plus benchpeg and Ganoksin classifieds, FB groups. Heavy items sell better locally. (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
Brand on the mill frame. "Jewelry equipment $1 – quality tools" lots sometimes hide a Durston. Need a photo of the side plate.

## Shipping / handling
Mills 20–60 lb cast iron – $40–90 freight-ish; arbor press 15 lb. eBay 13%. Bolt mills to a pallet/board in the box; protect rollers from moisture (oil + wrap).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State brand, model, roller width, flat/wire/combination, roller surface condition, gearing, weight. Photograph maker plate, rollers closeup, gears, any rust. For Foredom: motor model, handpiece type, running video. Title keywords: "Durston C100 rolling mill", "combination", "Pepe disc cutter", "Foredom SR".

## Buy rule
Buy when: Durston/Pepe/Cavallin visible and price ≤ 35% of retail. Pass when: unbranded green/black import (resale $80–120 minus shipping = nothing). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat.
