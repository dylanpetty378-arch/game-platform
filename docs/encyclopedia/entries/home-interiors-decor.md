## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Direct-sales home decor sold at home parties ("shows") by Home Interiors & Gifts, founded 1957 in Dallas by Mary Crowley. The company peaked in the 1970s–80s, had tens of thousands of representatives by the 1990s (appraisily cites "over 35,000"), and went into bankruptcy and was dissolved in 2008. What turns up:
- **Framed prints**: offset-litho prints in ornate gilt resin/composition or wood-look frames, often with oval or arched double mats. Subjects: lighthouses and coastal scenes, cottage gardens, florals, Victorian children (often in oval frames), Madonna and Child / Christ pictures, country farmhouse, the "window pane" pictures (a print behind a faux multi-pane mullion frame), and four-seasons sets.
- **Mirrors, brass-tone sconces, candle holders, shelves, plaques** (many of the plaques and frames are moulded resin by Burwood Products).
- **Homco figurines**: porcelain and bisque children, animals, birds, horses, Native American figures, religious and Victorian figures; the premium porcelain line is **Masterpiece Porcelain**; the 1985 **Denim Days** line is small figurines of children (Danny and Debbie) in blue denim clothes with stock numbers in the 88xx range.
Marks: frame backs carry a "Home Interiors" sticker or stamp and a stock number; prints and frames may read "© HOMCO" plus a year; figurines have a moulded or printed "Homco" or "© [year] Homco" and a four-digit stock number under the base (e.g. "(c) 1991 8799"), and often a country sticker (Taiwan, Mexico, U.S.A.). Title words: "Home Interiors", "Homco", "HI&G", "Masterpiece Porcelain", "Denim Days", "vintage 80s", "window pane picture".
Look-alikes: Syroco and Burwood resin wall decor sold through department stores (same look, no HI&G sticker); Enesco, Lefton and Avon figurines; Celebrating Home (the successor party company) pieces from after 2008.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Home Interiors & Gifts (1957–2008)**: the party-plan company; stickers and stock numbers on everything.
- **Homco**: HI&G's own manufacturing and import division; figurines, frames and accessories. Earlier figurines were made in Taiwan and Japan; some porcelain was made in the U.S. and the line later moved to Mexico (Masterpiece birds marked "Made in Mexico", 1985; a 1991 horse with a "Made in U.S.A." sticker).
- **Masterpiece Porcelain**: Homco's premium line (late 1970s–1990s): birds, horses (e.g. "The Champion" rearing horse, mare and foal), the Endangered Species series (American Bison with Calf), eagles, tigers, doves, swans. Bigger, better-painted and heavier than the bisque children.
- **Denim Days (1985)**: children in denim, numbered (e.g. #8821 "Helping Mom", #8827 "Playtime"), with matching plate and frame. Collectors try to complete the run, so big lots get attention.
- **Burwood Products**: resin wall plaques, frames and sconces sold through HI&G.
- **Interior Specialty items**: accessory line.
- **Gifts by Celebrating Home**: the successor party company.
- Print artists who are searched by name (per appraisily): B. Mitchell (window pane series, including a 12-panel version), Andres Orpinas, Barbara Mock, Catherine Simpson, Donna Richardson.

### Quick ID checklist
- Frame back: "Home Interiors" sticker or stamp with stock number, or "© HOMCO [year]"; brown or black backing paper, original hanging wire.
- Print under glass with halftone dots visible up close = offset print (all of them are); no originals.
- Figurine base: moulded "Homco" plus a 4-digit stock number and year; country sticker (Taiwan / Mexico / U.S.A.).
- Masterpiece Porcelain: glazed porcelain, larger (8"+), animal or bird subjects, often a date 1979–1990s on the base.
- Denim Days: small children in blue denim, 1985 date, 88xx stock number.
- Set completeness: four-seasons prints or a full figurine series in one photo.
- Condition: no fading, no water stains on mats, frame corners unchipped.

### Golden-egg tells
- Large Denim Days lot (30+ figurines with plate and frame) — whole lot listed at ~$280 asking (eBay.de 156525969474, unsold) vs $5–15 for a single figurine — count the figures in the photo and look for denim-blue children with an 85 date.
- Masterpiece Porcelain large animals (Endangered Species bison, rearing horse, mare and foal) — ~2–4× a bisque Homco child figurine (est., verify) — glossy porcelain, base marked "Masterpiece" or "Homco" with a 1979–1991 date, size 8"+.
- Complete four-seasons or matching print sets in original frames — appraisily says matching sets hold more value together; est. ~2× the sum of singles (verify) — four identical frames in one photo, consecutive stock numbers on stickers.
- B. Mitchell 12-panel window pane print — named by appraisily as the collectible version of the window pane pictures — multi-pane mullion frame, artist signature in the print.
- 1970s–80s pieces over 1990s ones — appraisily calls the 1970s–80s the "golden era" — earlier "© HOMCO 197x/198x" dates on the label.
- Single lighthouse print, sconce or mirror — no multiple; these are the common version ($5–30).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Offset-litho prints (not originals), resin or composition frames with gilt, glass glazing. Low quality uniform production; little variation. Frames are cast resin over a wood or fibreboard core; gilt is paint, so it rubs to the grey or white resin on corners. Brass-look sconces are brass-plated or lacquered steel and zinc, not solid brass. Figurines: the bisque children are unglazed matte porcelain with simple painting; Masterpiece Porcelain is glazed and better finished. Old vs newer: 1970s–80s prints have browned kraft backing paper and ageing stickers; 1990s pieces have bright white stickers, laminated surfaces and pastel or "country blue and mauve" palettes. Original paper tags and unbroken backing are worth noting (appraisily says the gap between good and excellent condition "can double the value", but no dollar figures).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Frame corner chips, faded prints, sun-yellowed mats; figurines chipped. Also: resin frames crack at the mitres, gilt rubbed, glass broken in transit, water stains (tide lines) on mats, foxing; sconce glass globes or candle cups missing; figurine fingers and ears chipped, glued breaks. Ask for photos of the frame back and the figurine base.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not applicable. No faking of this low-value line is known. The confusion is attribution: department-store Syroco and Burwood decor, and post-2008 Celebrating Home pieces, get listed as "Home Interiors". Only an HI&G sticker or Homco mark confirms it.

## Market
Nostalgia buyers; slow; local pickup best. Buyers are people refurnishing a 1980s look or replacing a parent's piece, plus Denim Days and Masterpiece collectors trying to complete runs. Single prints rarely sell shipped.

## What sellers usually miss
Nothing—overpriced more often than underpriced. The only exceptions: big Denim Days or Masterpiece Porcelain lots sold cheap as "figurines", and complete matching sets split up.

## Shipping / handling
Framed glass 16–24": $20–30, breakage risk. Figurines: small, ship $8–15 each, wrap fingers and ears; big lots are heavy (a 34-piece lot listed with $97 shipping).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title "Home Interiors", stock number, size, condition. Photograph the sticker or base mark and the stock number; give frame outside dimensions and image size; say whether the glass is original; for figurines give the line (Masterpiece Porcelain / Denim Days), year and the figure's name.

### What to tell the buyer
"Genuine Home Interiors & Gifts piece, sold through home parties (company 1957–2008). The back carries the original Home Interiors / © HOMCO label with stock number [####]. Offset print in the original gilt resin frame, [W × H] in outside. [For figurines:] Homco [Masterpiece Porcelain / Denim Days], marked '© [year] Homco [stock #]' on the base, made in [country per sticker]. No chips or repairs [or: describe]. Picked up locally or shipped double-boxed."

## Buy rule
Never meets profit ≥ max($50, price) after shipping. Pass. The only exception: a bulk Homco Denim Days or Masterpiece lot at ≤ $1 per piece where the lot's resale is ≥ $100.
