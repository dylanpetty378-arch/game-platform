## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Example 7954044942: (left) 3-tine steel table/serving fork with cylindrical bone (or celluloid "French ivory") handle and nickel/plated ferrule and cap, c. 1880–1930; (right) 2-tine carving/meat fork with stainless/steel tines and a hollow silverplate (or weighted sterling) handle in a Neoclassical/Art Deco shield pattern, c. 1920s–40s. Common.

## Makers, brands and variants
Sterling: Gorham (lion-anchor-G), Reed & Barton, Wallace, Towle, International, Whiting, Tiffany, Kirk (repoussé). Coin silver (pre-1868 US; marked "COIN", "PURE COIN", maker name). Silverplate: 1847 Rogers Bros., Holmes & Edwards, Oneida/Community, Wm Rogers. Sheffield cutlers (Joseph Rodgers, Mappin & Webb) for bone/ivory handled steel.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Marks decide: "STERLING" / ".925" vs "EP", "EPNS", "A1", "Silverplate", "XII". Hollow handles of carving forks are often "STERLING HANDLE" — weighted (filled with pitch), so silver weight is small. Bone shows small dark pores/lines; celluloid shows even grain lines and may smell of camphor; ivory shows Schreger lines (legal restrictions on ivory sales — avoid).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked or shrunk bone/celluloid handles, rust on steel tines, loose hollow handles, plating wear to copper/brass, bent tines.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little faking at this level; fake hallmarks on "silver" from Asia exist.

## Market
Silverplate oversupplied; sterling liquid at melt and pattern-matching buyers (Replacements, eBay).

## What sellers usually miss
Small STERLING stamps on the back of handles; coin-silver early American pieces sold as "old silverware".

## Shipping / handling
Light; flat mailer $5–6.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Marks photo, length, weight, pattern name (look up on Replacements.com).

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $6 − $6 − price ≥ max($50, price). Plate and bone-steel never qualify. Solid sterling (not "sterling handle") ≥ 60 g below ~50% of melt, or named patterns.
