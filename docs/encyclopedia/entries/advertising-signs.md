## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Porcelain enamel: glassy surface, chips show black steel underneath with a "shelf" edge, grommeted mounting holes, usually pre-1960. Tin litho: thinner, scratches show silver, rusts at edges. Plastic/acrylic: modern gas-station canopy inserts (1970s–2000s Shell, Texaco, Chevron) – thick, 3" deep, moulded, worth $50–150 only. Neon: glass tubing on a metal can; beer neons (Caffrey's, Budweiser) 1990s–2000s $100–250. Porcelain street signs (city blue/white or black/white, 20–24") $60–150. Joe Camel folding litho floor ashtray (1990s) $150–300. Sellers write "gas sign", "porcelain sign", "neon", "metal sign". Petroliana cans/globes: see `advertising-oil-cans`; neon detail: see `beer-neon-signs`.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Petroleum**: Shell, Texaco, Mobil (Pegasus), Sinclair, Musgo – pump plates and 4–6 ft signs are the top tier.
- **Motorcycle/auto dealers**: Harley-Davidson, Indian – top tier.
- **Soda/tobacco**: Coca-Cola, Marlboro, Camel (Joe Camel 1990s promo items) – tin litho originals 1930s–50s collectible; 1990s promo items decent.
- **Beer neons**: Caffrey's, Budweiser etc. 1990s–2000s, $100–250.
- **Street signs**: porcelain city signs; odd names and city marks bring more.
- **Farm/hardware** (e.g. Bridges Hardware tractor sign) – value depends on brand and originality (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Porcelain: heavy steel, multiple fired layers, chips show a stepped shelf down to black steel, brass grommets. Tin litho: thin, embossed edges on "tackers", printer's marks/maker names along the bottom edge on originals (general knowledge, verify). Plastic: moulded, thick, back-lit inserts. Original age shows as even weathering and fading; repros show printed-on "wear".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust-through and repro holes kill value. Chips at the mounting holes on porcelain are normal; chips in the field and fading reduce value. Touch-up paint on porcelain chips is a common hidden repair – ask for raking-light photos (general knowledge, verify). Neons: broken tubes and dead transformers.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Repros flood the market, especially porcelain Shell/Texaco/Harley signs. Reproduction tin signs are thin and printed; fake porcelain (often imported) has overly uniform colours, artificial chips without the layered shelf, and thin steel (general knowledge, verify). Authenticate anything priced as top tier.

## Market
Large decorator and "man cave" market plus serious petroliana collectors. Small signs sell on eBay; large originals through Morphy/Richmond auctions; big signs and neons are local-pickup items (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Porcelain vs tin vs plastic – ask for an edge/chip photo. Porcelain street signs are listed by the street name; collectors pay for odd names and city marks. Rust-through and repro holes kill value.

## Shipping / handling
Porcelain pump plates 5–8 lb, $20; large signs are freight/local only. Neons must be crated – local only.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State material (porcelain/tin/plastic), single or double-sided, exact dimensions, any maker's mark/date along the edge, chips and their location. Photograph front, back, edges, mounting holes, and chips close-up. Keywords: brand + "porcelain sign" or "tin sign" + size + "original" + era.

## Buy rule
Buy when: porcelain (not plastic/tin) pump plate or small sign ≤ $150 with clean gloss, or porcelain street sign ≤ $30. Pass when: plastic canopy signs, neons (freight/breakage), any Shell/Texaco over $150 without an authentication-grade photo. Signs bigger than a passenger seat (e.g. 4–6 ft) are excluded by the size rule. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
