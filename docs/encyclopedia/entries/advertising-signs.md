# Advertising signs (porcelain, tin, plastic, neon, street signs)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: craigslist chico listing body (5×5 ft Shell sign confirmed plastic); Joe Camel floor ashtray eBay listings ($150–300 asks, 2025); porcelain street-sign eBay sold knowledge ($60–150); Richmond Auctions / Morphy petroliana results for porcelain Shell signs ($800–5,000).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Porcelain enamel: glassy surface, chips show black steel underneath with a "shelf" edge, grommeted mounting holes, usually pre-1960. Tin litho: thinner, scratches show silver, rusts at edges. Plastic/acrylic: modern gas-station canopy inserts (1970s–2000s Shell, Texaco, Chevron) – thick, 3" deep, moulded, worth $50–150 only. Neon: glass tubing on a metal can; beer neons (Caffrey's, Budweiser) 1990s–2000s $100–250. Porcelain street signs (city blue/white or black/white, 20–24") $60–150. Joe Camel folding litho floor ashtray (1990s) $150–300. Sellers write "gas sign", "porcelain sign", "neon", "metal sign".

## What sellers usually miss
Porcelain vs tin vs plastic – ask for an edge/chip photo. Porcelain street signs are listed by the street name; collectors pay for odd names and city marks. Rust-through and repro holes kill value.

## Shipping / handling
Porcelain pump plates 5–8 lb, $20; large signs are freight/local only. Neons must be crated – local only.

## Buy rule
Buy when: porcelain (not plastic/tin) pump plate or small sign ≤ $150 with clean gloss, or porcelain street sign ≤ $30. Pass when: plastic canopy signs, neons (freight/breakage), any Shell/Texaco over $150 without an authentication-grade photo. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.
