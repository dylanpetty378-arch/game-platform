## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Embroidered silk from Chinese garments and accessories, mostly c.1850–1930: **sleeve bands** (long narrow strips, ~45–60 × 9–14 cm, often in pairs, sewn as cuffs on women's robes), **purse/pouch faces** (small shaped panels), **skirt panels**, **collars**, **rank badges** (squares ~28–30 cm with a bird or animal under a sun), **chair covers and table frontals**, and fragments of **dragon robes**. Westerners framed them in pairs for decades — hence Craigslist "framed embroidery pair", "Blindstitch embroidery" (a corruption of the "forbidden stitch" myth) or "Japanese embroidery".
Photo cues for good old work: dense, flat, glossy **satin stitch** that follows the curve of each petal; fields of tiny raised **Peking knots** (seed knots); **couched gold or silver-wrapped thread** outlining motifs; scenes of court ladies, scholars in gardens, boys, auspicious objects (bogu), birds, butterflies and peonies; background silk satin in deep blue, black, ivory or plum; slight fading and age soiling.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Han Chinese women's robe sleeve bands (late Qing, 1850–1911)** — figural garden/courtyard scenes or flowers; worked in satin stitch, Peking knot, or both; best in matched pairs (Lyon & Turnbull 2023 group of eleven).
- **Purse faces and small accessories** — shaped panels from purses, fan cases, spectacle cases; often small but finely knotted.
- **Rank badges (buzi, mandarin squares)** — civil ranks birds (crane, golden pheasant, peacock, wild goose, silver pheasant, egret, mandarin duck, quail, paradise flycatcher), military ranks animals (qilin, lion, leopard, tiger, bear, panther, rhinoceros, seahorse) (general knowledge, verify); Qing badges are pairs (front split in two halves).
- **Kesi (k'o-ssu)** — not embroidery but silk tapestry weave: look for tiny vertical slits where colours meet; higher value.
- **Dragon robe and theatrical fragments** — couched gold dragons, wave borders (lishui) at the bottom.
- **Canton export (shawls, panels for the West)** — large floral piano shawls with fringes; different market (general knowledge).
- **Republic (1912–49) and later** — coarser, more synthetic dyes; Suzhou embroidery institute pictures (1950s–90s): cats, goldfish, landscapes on fine gauze, often double-sided in rosewood stands — souvenir tier.
- **Machine embroidery (1980s–now)** — Cornely/Schiffli or computer embroidery on polyester satin, sold framed in Chinatown/tourist shops.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Matched pair of figural sleeve bands on intact silk** — ~$100–200 vs ~$30–50 for a single floral strip (2–4×; framed Qing median $145, pair $112 on Terapeak) — two long narrow panels mirroring each other, figures with faces in tiny stitches, in a double frame.
- **Peking-knot ("forbidden stitch") fields covering whole motifs** — ~1.5–2× satin-only work of the same size — textured, beaded look over flowers or figures; a raking-light photo shows bumps.
- **Qing rank badge (bird or animal under red sun, sea-and-mountain base)** — $200–1,500+ vs $40 (5–30×, verify) — square ~30 cm, central bird/beast, couched gold background, split in half for the front badge.
- **Kesi silk tapestry** — 2–5× embroidery of the same size (verify) — flat, woven surface with slits between colour blocks, no raised stitches.
- **Couched gold-thread dragon on deep blue or black (robe fragment)** — $100–400 (verify) — metallic gold coils outlining a five-clawed dragon over waves.
- **Fine silk ground with figures (court ladies, scholars, boys at play) vs flowers only** — ~1.5–2× — tiny faces with stitched eyes, layered garments.
- **Kill: machine work** — $10–40 — white bobbin thread showing on the back, stitches running straight across petals instead of following the shape, connected thread between dots, overly shiny, uniform thread (Needle'n Thread).

### Quick ID checklist
- Ask for a back-of-frame photo or the reverse of the textile: hand work has knotted ends and irregular carries; machine work shows white bobbin thread and systematic jumps.
- Ground: silk satin (lustrous, frays in fine threads) vs polyester.
- Stitch direction follows the shape of each petal/figure.
- Peking knots present? Couched gold?
- Size of each panel (sleeve bands are long and narrow; rank badges ~30 cm square).
- Matched pair? Mirror-image composition = from the same garment.
- Condition: splits in silk, missing knots, stains, fading, moth holes.
- Frame size — must fit a passenger seat (most sleeve-band frames are ~10×12 to 20×24 in).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Floss silk (untwisted) satin stitch on silk satin or damask grounds; Peking knots in tightly wound twisted silk; gold-leaf-on-paper-wrapped threads couched with fine silk. Natural dyes (pre-1870) are softer; aniline (later 19th c.) brighter and prone to fade unevenly. Quality: density, tiny figure faces, consistent stitch length, gradual colour shading.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Light fading, silk splitting in the satin ground (shattering), lost knots, moth damage, water stains, acidic mats burning the edges, glue on backs from old framing. Repairs and overstitching lower value; never wash. Framed under glass protects but may hide damage on the reverse.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Copies of rank badges and sleeve bands made 1930s–1980s for the tourist and export trade, sometimes artificially aged; machine-made "antique" panels; Suzhou institute pieces sold as "Qing". Japanese embroidery (kimono fragments, Meiji fukusa) is a separate market — Chinese pieces are often misattributed as Japanese.

## Market
Moderate, steady demand from textile collectors, Asian-art buyers and decorators; framed pairs sell in 2–6 weeks on eBay. Good unframed pieces sell to dealers who re-frame. Glass shipping is the main friction.

## What sellers usually miss
That "Blindstitch"/"Japanese" framed pieces are Chinese and often 100+ years old; that two small frames are a pair from one robe; that the pieces are worth more than the frame. Framed pairs list at $1–"make offer".

## Shipping / handling
Framed under glass: tape the glass, corner protectors, double-box, $15–25 (or swap to acrylic before shipping). Unframed: flat between acid-free tissue and board, $6–10.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give type (sleeve band, purse face, rank badge), technique (satin stitch, Peking knot, couched gold), ground, size (sight and frame), period estimate, condition (fading, splits, stains), and a reverse/back photo if possible.

### What to tell the buyer
"Antique Chinese hand-embroidered silk [sleeve band / purse panel], late Qing to early Republic, c. 1880–1920 (estimated). Worked in [satin stitch / Peking knot] with [couched gold] on [blue] silk satin; figures of [subject]. Sight size X × Y in, framed under glass at X × Y in. Some fading and [condition]. Hand-worked — not machine embroidery."
