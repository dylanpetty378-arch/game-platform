## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Oak magneto wall telephone (1895–1930):** vertical quarter-sawn oak box 18–24" tall with a sloped writing shelf, a crank on the right side (magneto), two nickel bells on top or a bell under the shelf, a black transmitter on an adjustable arm in the middle of the front, a receiver on a hook (switch) on the left, and a hinged front door hiding the magneto, induction coil and dry-cell batteries. Maker's name is on the transmitter face, inside the door (paper label or stamped), on the bell cups, or on a brass/nickel plate on the front.
- **Three-box ("long box") wall sets (1880s–1900):** three stacked boxes — bells top, transmitter middle, battery bottom — the oldest and most valuable; **cathedral-top** (arched or pointed top boxes) and **picture-frame front** sets are also early.
- **Wood "vanity"/cabinet desk phones (1890s–1910s):** telephone circuitry on a flat oak desktop, magneto and bells behind a glass door; hotel lobbies and offices; rare.
- **1930s wood/Bakelite art-deco desk sets:** Kellogg Masterphone 900/925 series (Bakelite, "one of the most beautiful art deco phones"), Stromberg-Carlson 1212 "Fatboy" (bulging Bakelite body), S-C 1197 (no dial)/1198 (dial), plus wooden-cased handset sets and intercom/extension sets with a handset on a cradle on top of a wooden radio-style cabinet with an oval or slotted grille over the ringer (our Phoenix example). Many of the wood ones are non-dial extension, hotel or intercom ("interphone") sets.
- **Candlestick and metal 1930s desk sets** are covered in vintage-telephones.md; this entry is for the wood-cased ones.
- Photo tells for real vs repro (see Fakes): quarter-sawn oak with ray fleck and a dark aged finish vs orange-stained pine; slotted brass screws vs Phillips; a real magneto (a heavy bar-magnet generator with a gear) inside vs a plastic keypad or electronic board; nickel or brass fittings with wear vs shiny lacquer.
- Title words: "antique wall phone", "crank phone", "oak telephone", "Kellogg", "Western Electric", "Stromberg Carlson", "magneto", "hand crank", "party line", "restoration project", "works".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Western Electric (Bell System):** 317 series (1907–1920s; the standard Bell oak wall set, "317 type" with the Bell logo on the transmitter, 2 bells on top), 301/315/293 earlier; 1317 (common-battery, no crank). Most-seen maker; value $100–300 (est) complete, more if dated early.
- **Kellogg Switchboard & Supply (Chicago, 1897–):** 1000-series oak wall sets (very common on farms), 1920s–30s "Redbar"/Masterphone Bakelite sets (900/925 Masterphone, 1930s, art deco, non-dial and dial versions, black). Kellogg identification label inside the door; "Kellogg" on the transmitter.
- **Stromberg-Carlson (Rochester NY, founded 1894):** oak wall sets 1900s–20s (often with a cathedral or "bridging" party-line design), 1930s Bakelite 1212 "Fatboy" (dial and non-dial), 1197 (non-dial)/1198 (dial) desk sets, 1211 "theater phone" (non-dial), wood vanity cabinet sets; S-C name cast on induction-coil/network blocks and ringer boxes, so an S-C-marked network inside a wooden box can be an S-C subset fitted to another maker's or a custom cabinet.
- **Northern Electric (Canada):** WE-licensed designs, same values in the US; "Northern Electric" on the transmitter.
- **American Electric, Leich (Genoa IL), Chicago Telephone Supply, Swedish-American, Eureka, Monarch, Sumter, Dean, Williams-Abbott, Ericsson (US-made), Automatic Electric** — small/independent makers; scarcer, collectors pay more for complete, labelled examples ($300–800+ est).
- **Wood intercom/extension sets (1920s–40s):** Western Electric and S-C "interphone" units, hotel room sets, and wooden art-deco cabinets housing a subset (network + ringer) with a handset on a cradle, non-dial. Decorative, sold as "radio-style telephone"; $75–250 (est) depending on looks and working condition.

### Quick ID checklist
1. Maker name: transmitter face, inside door label, bell cups, front plate, network/ringer castings.
2. Wood: quarter-sawn oak (ray fleck), walnut (early/premium), cherry; aged finish with alligatoring vs fresh stain.
3. Mechanism: crank + magneto inside (magneto set) vs no crank (common-battery) vs dial (1920s+).
4. Completeness: receiver and cord, transmitter and arm, crank, both bells, door and lock, shelf, battery shelf, induction coil, terminals.
5. Form: single box / three-box / cathedral top / picture frame / vanity cabinet / 1930s deco desk.
6. Originality: replaced parts show as newer nickel, different screws, mismatched wood colour; repro transmitters are smooth and light.
7. Size and weight for shipping (single-box oak wall set ≈ 9×20×12", 12–20 lb).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Three-box "long box" wall set (1880s–90s, bells/transmitter/battery boxes stacked, often walnut)** — $800–2,000+ vs $100–300 for a 1910s single-box oak set (~5–8×) — three separate stacked wooden boxes on a backboard, early "Blake" or "Hunnings" transmitter, exposed nickel bells on the top box.
- **Cathedral-top or picture-frame-front oak sets (1890s–1905)** — $400–1,000 vs $100–300 (~3×) — an arched/peaked top on the box or a raised moulded "picture frame" around the front door, instead of the plain rectangular 317-style box.
- **Small independent makers with a complete paper label (Leich, Chicago Telephone Supply, Swedish-American, Monarch, Eureka, Sumter, Williams-Abbott)** — $300–800 vs $100–300 WE/Kellogg (~2–3×) — the name on the transmitter face or inside-door label; collectors chase these for completeness.
- **Vanity/cabinet desk telephone (oak desk with glass door over magneto and bells, S-C/WE 1890s–1910s)** — $500–1,500 (est; 1stDibs dealer avg $995 for 1930s phones generally) vs $100–300 — a piece of furniture with the phone built into the desktop.
- **Fully original, working, dated Western Electric 317 with Bell-marked transmitter, original receiver and cords** — $250–450 refurbished dealer vs $100–150 incomplete (~3×) — "Western Electric" + patent dates on the transmitter, matching dark nickel on all fittings, original cloth cords.
- **1930s Kellogg Masterphone 900/925 or Stromberg-Carlson 1212 "Fatboy" in uncracked Bakelite with dial** — $300–700 restored (Old Phone Works asks $724 CAD for a restored S-C 1198) vs $75–150 raw non-dial (~3–4×) — bulging deco Bakelite body, dial present, no cracks at the handset cradle corners.
- **Colour (ivory/red/green) Bakelite deco desk sets** — 2–5× black — colour moulded through; look at chips.
- **Rail/hotel/theatre sets with original brass plaque or "no dial" ringdown with a key switch** — modest premium, $150–300 (est) — brass plaque, numbered rooms, key or push-button on the front.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Original oak wall sets are quarter-sawn white oak with a dark shellac/varnish that alligators; brass bells nickel-plated (wear shows brass at the edges); transmitters are hard rubber/Bakelite with a nickel or brass face; the magneto is a heavy bar-magnet generator (3–5 lb alone).
- A refinished set (sanded, modern poly, bright orange oak) loses 30–50% to collectors but sells fine to decorators.
- 1930s deco sets: Bakelite (dense, heavy, warm smell when rubbed, no seam flash) vs later plastic; wood cabinets are usually walnut veneer over pine with inlaid stringing or painted pinstripes (our example has incised/painted lines and an oval slotted grille).
- Quality/originality: slotted screws, cloth cords, patent dates, ink-stamped dates inside the box, consistent nickel wear; cheap production/repro: Phillips screws, bright lacquer, light weight, plywood back, modular jack only, pushbutton keypad in the transmitter.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Common: missing crank or receiver, missing batteries shelf, cracked/split door, veneer lifting, missing bells, replaced transmitter or receiver (repro parts widely sold), dead magneto, seized hook switch, mouse damage inside, cut/missing cords, converted to modern network (not a defect for users; a defect for purists).
- Kills value: replaced front door or box, missing magneto, a cut-down box, heavy refinishing with filled holes, non-period parts mixed in ("married" sets — common; a Kellogg phone with a Stromberg ringer box is noted on Collectors Weekly).
- Acceptable: alligatored finish, dark patina, one replaced cord, modern line-cord conversion if the original parts are retained.
- Repairs show as: brighter nickel on one part, new screws, fresh glue lines, mismatched oak colour, foam/felt added inside.
- Ask: "Does the crank turn and ring the bells? Is the magneto inside? Any cracks in the wood? Is the maker's label inside the door? Will you ship?"

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- 1970s–2000s **reproduction oak wall phones** (Crosley CR91, "Country Kitchen" style, Taiwan/China): pine or thin oak with orange stain, a working push-button keypad inside the transmitter or on the front, a fake crank, electronic ringer, modular cord — $20–60 sold. Tells: shiny finish, Phillips screws, no magneto weight, keypad, "Made in China/Taiwan" sticker on the back.
- **Repro parts** on real boxes: new transmitters, receivers, cranks and bells are sold by Old Phone Shop/Phoneco; a mix is normal on "restored" sets — original-part completeness is what collectors pay for.
- **Marriages**: a genuine box with a different maker's innards, or a 1930s subset in a home-built wooden cabinet (our Phoenix set may be this).
- 1930s deco Bakelite sets are not reproduced; Chinese "retro" plastic phones imitate the 302/candlestick, not these.

## Market
- Buyers: telephone collectors (TCI/ATCA members — want originality, maker, date, completeness), decorators/farmhouse (want the look, buy repros too), restorers (want projects cheap).
- eBay US: roughly 100–200 "antique wall phone" sales a month including repros; genuine complete sets sell in 2–4 weeks at $150–300; projects and local pickups sit. Heavy, so local pickup via Craigslist/Facebook is common and depresses prices; shipped listings command more from nationwide collectors.
- Seasonality: slight Q4 gift lift; farmhouse décor steady.
- Trend: flat to soft for common oak sets (lots of supply from farm estates); early/rare and well-restored sets holding.

## What sellers usually miss
- Maker and model (they say "antique crank phone"); the label inside the door; patent dates on the transmitter.
- Whether it is a three-box/cathedral (early) vs a plain 317-type.
- That it is a reproduction (orange pine, keypad) — or the reverse: that a dirty project is a complete original WE 317 worth $200+.
- The magneto: "crank spins" ≠ "bells ring"; a ringing magneto doubles saleability.
- 1930s Bakelite deco desk sets listed as "old black phone" for $20–40.

## Shipping / handling
- Oak wall set: ~9×20×12", 12–20 lb; remove the receiver, transmitter arm (if it unscrews) and batteries, wrap bells, double-box with 3" padding; $35–60 UPS/FedEx Ground; wood splits and bell cups bend in transit. 1930s desk set: ~8 lb boxed, $18–25. Wood intercom cabinet (our example ~10×10×8"): ~10 lb boxed, $25–35.
- Fragility 3/5 (Bakelite handsets crack; veneer chips; glass fronts on vanity sets break).
- Fits a passenger seat (single-box sets do; three-box and vanity cabinets are at the size limit).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Title: "Antique Western Electric 317 oak wall telephone magneto crank phone 1910s complete original Bell" — maker, model, wood, form, completeness, working status.
- Photograph: front, inside the door (label, magneto, coil), transmitter face with patents, bell cups, back, shelf, any damage; show the crank ringing in a short description ("crank rings both bells").
- State: dimensions, weight, wood, maker/model/date, what is original vs replaced, whether converted to modular/works on a landline (magneto sets need a modern network to talk), finish condition.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a genuine (quarter-sawn oak, slotted screws, magneto present) wall set with the maker identified and ALL parts (crank, both bells, receiver, transmitter, door) at ≤ $75 and the seller ships or it is local; a three-box/cathedral/small-maker set at ≤ $200; a 1930s Masterphone/Fatboy/S-C deco Bakelite set uncracked at ≤ $60 (resale $200–400 raw if dial and complete); a decorative wood deco intercom/handset cabinet only at ≤ $25 and only if clean and complete (resale $100–200 — thin).
Pass when: repro (keypad, pine, Phillips), "restoration project" missing crank/receiver/bells, split or replaced wood, married parts with no maker label, cracked Bakelite, untested magneto with the box priced over $100, or a pickup-only seller more than an hour away (shipping a 15–20 lb oak box eats $40–60 each way). No repairs, so skip projects. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
