## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Enamel-over-steel pots with a cross-shaped lid handle (lid doubles as trivet), 1956–80s; marked "DANSK DESIGNS FRANCE/DENMARK IHQ" (France = earliest, most valuable), later "Dansk Designs Ltd" and modern reissues marked "Dansk Kobenstyle" (made in Thailand/China). Colours: red, yellow, teal/turquoise, white, blue; rare: black, olive, orange, brown. Pieces: 2-qt/4-qt casseroles, paella pan, butter warmer, fondue set with teak handle and iron stand. Seller words: "Dansk enamel pot", "mid century enamelware", "Kobenstyle".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Dansk Designs** (founded 1954, US company; designer Jens Harald Quistgaard). Kobenstyle introduced 1956.
- Marks: "DANSK DESIGNS FRANCE IHQ" (earliest, made in France), "DENMARK", later "Dansk Designs Ltd" (Japan/other), modern reissues "Dansk Kobenstyle" (2012+, Thailand/China).
- Forms: 1-, 2-, 4-qt casseroles, saucepans, paella pans (largest, sought), butter warmers, tea kettles, fondue sets (teak handle, iron stand).
- Colours: red, yellow, teal/turquoise, white, blue common; black, olive, orange, brown rare (top tier).
- Related Quistgaard lines (Fjord teak utensils, Rare Woods) are separate categories (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Porcelain enamel over carbon steel, glossy colour outside and white (or speckled) inside, black enamel rims. Vintage pieces: heavier gauge steel, IHQ initials in the mark. Reissues: lighter, marked Kobenstyle with Asian origin. Teak handles on fondue sets should be original and not split.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Enamel chips (especially on the lid cross and rims), crazing, rust at chips, stained/burnt interiors, cracked or replaced teak handles, missing fondue forks/stand. Chips kill value. Ask for close-ups of the rim, lid cross, interior and base mark.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Dansk's own 2012+ reissues are the main confusion: marked "Dansk Kobenstyle" and made in Thailand/China, worth $15–40. No significant fakes (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Steady mid-century-modern kitchen market: eBay, Etsy, Chairish, 1stDibs. Buyers are MCM collectors and cooks who use it. Dealer asks on Chairish run high. Mild Q4 gift bump (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
"France" base mark (early) and colour rarity. They call it "enamel pot with cross lid".

## Shipping / handling
2–6 lb; enamel chips on impact – wrap lid separately; ~$15–20 in. eBay 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Form and size (qt / diameter), colour, base mark (France/Denmark IHQ), interior condition, chips (state none only if verified), teak condition on fondue sets, completeness. Photograph base mark, lid cross, rim all round, interior. Title: "Dansk Kobenstyle [colour] [form] France IHQ Quistgaard MCM".

## Buy rule
Buy when: vintage "France/Denmark" mark, no chips, ≤ $35 for casserole/paella or ≤ $60 for complete fondue set. Pass when: seller lists "Jens Quistgaard" (knows) or price ≥ $100. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule) – at the $50–180 sold range most of these caps only clear for rare colours or paella pans, so check the math. Must fit a passenger seat.
