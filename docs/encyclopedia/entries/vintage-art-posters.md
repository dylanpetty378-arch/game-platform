## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Printed paper posters advertising events, exhibitions, travel, products. Age tells: printer credit line at bottom ("Imp. Chaix Paris", "Printed in U.S.A. by..."), fold lines (posters were folded when issued until the 1970s), stone-lithograph crayon texture vs. halftone dots, linen backing. Modern posters: bright coated paper, rolled not folded, small copyright/ISBN-like credits.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Belle Époque** (Mucha, Chéret, Toulouse-Lautrec, Cappiello) — originals rare, reprints everywhere.
- **Travel posters** (1920s–60s): airlines (Pan Am, TWA, United), railways, steamship lines.
- **Rock posters**: Fillmore (BG numbers), Family Dog, Grande Ballroom — first printings vs. later.
- **Museum/gallery exhibition posters** (1960s–2000s): Picasso, Miró, Chagall lithograph posters by Mourlot (some valuable), most offset worth little.
- **Local festival posters** (1980s–2000s): signed and numbered by the designer, regional audience only.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lithograph (stone or plate): continuous tones, no regular dot pattern under 10× loupe. Screenprint: thick layered ink, crisp edges. Offset: rosette dot pattern. Signature in pencil with edition number (e.g. "45/500") adds modest value on modern posters.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tears, fold separation, fading (sun), staining, trimming of margins, dry-mounting onto board (kills value of originals), mat burn. Linen-backing is a positive restoration for vintage originals.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Huge: reprints of Mucha, travel posters, rock posters (second/third printings, bootlegs). Reprints often slightly smaller than originals and on bright paper.

## Market
Modern posters: oversupplied. Original vintage: strong collector market via Heritage, Swann, Poster Auctions International, and eBay.

## What sellers usually miss
Folded original travel posters in attic tubes; Mourlot lithograph exhibition posters; first-print rock posters mixed with reprints.

## Shipping / handling
Unframed: tube, $10–15. Framed with glass: large box, $40+, often too big for a passenger seat; usually sell framed modern posters locally only.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** artist/event (Mucha, Pan Am, Fillmore BG number, Picasso Mourlot), "original" only for a period printing, process (lithograph, screenprint, offset), printer credit, year, size, "signed numbered". Junk: "original" on a reprint, "rare", "antique". `Original 1960s Pan Am Travel Poster Lithograph Printer Credit 25x40 Folded`
- **Where:** modern/framed posters local only on FB; signed festival posters eBay BIN with offers; original travel, first-print rock and Mourlot lithos sell for far more at Heritage, Swann or Poster Auctions International.
- **Price position:** modern at the sold median ($15–60; decorative prints $12), Mourlot/later-print rock $75–300, original travel $200–1,500 (named 1930s airline lithos $3,000–33,000 at Swann); OBO, accept −20%, floor $10; cut after 30 days or consign.
- **Photos:** full front flat; printer credit and date line close-up; paper edge and margins; back; fold lines; signature/edition; loupe shot of the surface (stone litho vs dot screen); frame corners and glass.
- **State:** printing method, dimensions, printer credit, signature/edition, first vs later printing if known, folds, tears, pinholes, frame condition, glass included (local pickup only for large glass), flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** unframed rolled in a tube $10–15; framed with glass is a large box $40+ and often too big for a passenger seat (~10 lb) — local only.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a period [1960s] [Pan Am] printing with the [printer credit] and stone-litho texture, not the open-edition reprint; originals sell for $[200–1,500] and the reprints are $20."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Framed modern posters never qualify (resale low, freight high). Original vintage posters unframed at resale ≥ $200 for ≤ $50. Pass on anything needing authentication (Mucha, Lautrec, major movie posters).
