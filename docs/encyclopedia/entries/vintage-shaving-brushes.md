## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
US brushes from the 1910s–1960s with stamped handles ("EVER-READY", "RUBBERSET", "SET IN RUBBER STERILIZED", "MADE IN U.S.A.", model numbers like 100, 150, 200T). The knot is set in vulcanised rubber in the handle's cup.
- **"Sterilized" stamp**: belongs to the WWI-era anthrax scare. Outbreak summaries for 1915–24 counted 149 US military and 17 US civilian cases tied to brushes, and almost all medical case reports fall in 1917–23. Wartime makers had substituted horsehair shipped straight from Russia, China or Japan, without disinfection, for scarce badger. The first entry's "post-1915 regulations" is approximately right in period. The exact regulatory date is not confirmed by the sources fetched.
- **Handle material drives value**:
  - catalin: glossy, translucent at the edges, yellows to butterscotch, marbled swirls
  - bakelite: dark and opaque
  - early celluloid
  - later polystyrene: light and hollow
- Wet-shaving restorers buy old brushes for the handles and re-knot them, often with new badger knots that cost more than the handle did.
- **Title words**: "catalin", "butterscotch", "bakelite", "Ever-Ready", "Rubberset", "Made Rite", "sterilized", "badger", "NOS", "barber".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Ever-Ready** (American Safety Razor):
  - Model numbers stamped on the handle (100, 150, 200T, 300L, 304L, 400, 750, 1000, C40, 300cc travel). Collectors have never decoded a consistent scheme, and some think the numbers are lot codes.
  - Reported examples: 200T (20 mm knot, 55 mm loft); the 300 series often has gold or silver rings and badger hair; the 300cc travel brush has a butterscotch handle and an 18 mm knot; the 102D has a heavy burgundy rubber handle stamped "Sterilized Pure Badger"; one 150 has a red marbled handle; the 1000 is solid bakelite or celluloid.
  - Knots mostly run 17–23 mm.
- **Rubberset** (Newark NJ): its "SET IN RUBBER" stamp is the classic. The 400N is a butterscotch-handle model with finest-badger restorations.
- **Made Rite** (e.g. #18 butterscotch).
- **Erskine** (B200), **Opal** (#58 fan-shape), **Brushman**, **Baton**, **Fuller**.
- **Kent** and **Vulfix** (UK), **Simms** (Australia).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the handle stamp: maker, model number, "STERILIZED", "PURE BADGER", "MADE IN U.S.A."
- Handle material: translucent glow at the edges plus butterscotch or marbled colour suggests catalin, while opaque black or brown is bakelite or rubber. Light, hollow, with visible mould seams means polystyrene.
- Size: measure the knot diameter (mm) and the loft. Large knots (22 mm+) and big "400"-type handles are wanted.
- Knot type: badger (dark band, lighter tips), boar (stiff, white or tan), horsehair.
- Cracks in the handle, especially at the cup rim where the knot is set.
- Box or card: NOS in its original box multiplies the price.
- Is it a modern Asian "vintage-style" red/cream brush? Check for a stamp and the weight.

### Golden-egg tells
- **NOS boxed Ever-Ready / Rubberset brushes**: $60–200 vs $12–30 for a used plain one, about 4–6× (existing entry; verify). Photo tell: the original printed box or cellophane, a fluffy unused knot, crisp stamping.
- **Marbled or two-tone catalin handles** (red marble, green swirl, butterscotch with a black base): $30–80 vs $12–30, about 2–3× (existing ladder). Photo tell: translucent glowing colour with swirls, rather than an opaque solid.
- **Large-size handles ("400"-type, 22 mm+ knots)**: in the upper tier (existing summary; verify). Photo tell: a fat, tall handle with a big knot next to a ruler.
- **"Sterilized Pure Badger" and ringed 300-series Ever-Readys**: a premium over boar models (forum reports; no $ cited). Photo tell: a "PURE BADGER" stamp and a gold or silver ring band.
- **Butterscotch Made Rite, Erskine, Rubberset 400N and Opal fan handles**: restoration favourites (forum; no $ cited). Photo tell: an amber-gold catalin handle with the maker stamp.
- **Lots of 4+ catalin handles** priced as "old plastic brushes": the main buying edge (existing buy rule). Photo tell: several glowing yellow or red handles in a jumble.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Catalin**: heavy, with glowing colours, and may have hairline crazing. **Polystyrene**: lighter, with mould seams.
- **Knots**: badger (dark band, lighter tips), boar (stiff, white or tan), horsehair.
- Catalin darkens with age (white goes to butterscotch, clear to amber). A cleaned spot showing a lighter colour supports catalin.
- Rubber-coated and hollow bakelite-with-rubber-top handles exist; the rubber hardens and cracks.
- **Old vs new**: period stamping is pressed into the handle, often gold-filled. Modern repros have printed or no lettering and light hollow plastic.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Shedding knots, cracked handles, smell. Used brushes are cleaned and re-knotted by buyers, so the knot matters less than the handle.
- Split handles repaired with epoxy show as a clear seam and lower the value.
- **Health note**: CDC researchers judge the risk from used vintage brushes extremely low, and they do not recommend home disinfection (the steam, pressure and formaldehyde process is riskier than the benefit). Unused early-20th-century brushes carry the small residual anthrax question, so state facts and do not claim "sterilized/sanitized" unless done.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern "vintage-style" red/cream brushes sold on Amazon/Temu imitate Ever-Ready; check the stamp and the handle weight. Re-knotted vintage handles are honest restorations but should be sold as re-knotted, not original.

## Market
Niche but steady (wet-shaving hobby); lots move in 1–2 weeks. Restorers buy handles cheaply, often "a few dollars each" at flea markets per the forums, so single used brushes don't command much.

## What sellers usually miss
Catalin handles sold as "old plastic brushes"; NOS in boxes in barber estates; "PURE BADGER" stamps.

## Shipping / handling
Light, $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Stamp photo, handle material, knot type, loft height, used/NOS; say "sanitized" only if done.
### What to tell the buyer
Quote the stamp exactly (e.g. "EVER-READY 200T / STERILIZED / MADE IN U.S.A."). Give the handle material with the reasoning ("translucent butterscotch, consistent with catalin"), the colour, the handle height, the knot diameter and loft in mm, and the hair type (badger, boar, horse). Say used or NOS, original or re-knotted, and disclose cracks, shedding and odour. For restorers, say "sold as a handle for restoration" where the knot is poor.
