## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
US brushes 1910s–1960s with stamped handles ("EVER-READY", "RUBBERSET", "SET IN RUBBER STERILIZED", "MADE IN U.S.A.", model numbers like 100, 150, 200T). "Sterilized" stamp dates to post-1915 anthrax regulations. Handle material drives value: catalin (glossy, translucent at edges, yellows to butterscotch, marbled swirls), bakelite (dark, opaque), early celluloid, later polystyrene (light, hollow). Wet-shaving restorers buy old brushes for the handles and re-knot them.

## Makers, brands and variants
Ever-Ready (American Safety Razor), Rubberset (Newark NJ), Made Rite, Simms (Australia), Kent and Vulfix (UK), Fuller.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Catalin: heavy, glowing colours, may have hairline crazing; polystyrene: lighter, mold seams. Knots: badger (dark band, tips lighter), boar (stiff, white/tan), horsehair.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Shedding knots, cracked handles, smell; used brushes are cleaned/re-knotted by buyers, so knot condition matters less than handle.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern "vintage-style" red/cream brushes sold on Amazon/Temu imitate Ever-Ready — check stamp and handle weight.
