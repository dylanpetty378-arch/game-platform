## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Chrome-plated forged steel tools stamped CRAFTSMAN with size and "USA" or a maker code. Combination wrenches (open + 12-point box) are the commonest form. Identifying era from photos: font and markings in the raised panel on the shank, forging codes (single letters or symbols) near the box end, and whether the shank is raised-panel, flat, or I-beam.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Pre-WWII (1927–1940s): made by Herbrand ("-H-" style), Moore Drop Forging ("=V=" and "-V-" codes), J.P. Danielson; script "Craftsman" and "Craftsman Dura-Bright"; the "=V=" (double-line V) Moore wrenches 1940s–early 60s are the collector series.
- 1950s–60s: "-V-" Moore; New Britain Tool ("-N-"); Pexto.
- 1960s–80s: "-V-" Moore continued; Danaher "K" ("-K-" Kelly Hardware/Danaher), "W" Western Forge; full polish; metric introduced early 1970s; raised panel fonts.
- 1980s–2000s: Danaher (Western Forge, -K- and later "VA" codes), Apex/Easco ("-S-"?), still USA. Craftsman metric combination wrenches like the 7–16 mm set seen are ordinary 1980s–2000s USA production.
- 2010–present: China/Taiwan production ("Made in China"), Stanley Black & Decker after 2017; worth least.
- Craftsman Professional, Industrial and "Craftsman Pro" lines (better finish) carry modest premiums.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Chrome-vanadium steel, nickel-chrome plating. Full-polish vs. satin; older wrenches have deeper, more hand-finished forgings and often "Forged in USA". Flaking chrome = poor. Lifetime warranty (no longer honored like before) drove past owners to swap wrenches, so sets are often mixed eras.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust pitting, chrome flaking, rounded box ends, cheater-bar bends, grinding modifications. Mixed-era sets reduce collector appeal but not user appeal.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No faking at these prices. Note that modern Chinese "Craftsman" shares the name; check "USA" marking.

## Market
Very liquid (hundreds sold/month on eBay), but prices are low and buyers are mechanics shopping on value. 7–21 days to sell at fair price.

## What sellers usually miss
Early "=V=" and Herbrand-era wrenches mixed into "old tool box" lots; Craftsman specialty tools (crowfoot sets, tubing wrenches, early ratchets "Craftsman 44816" etc.).

## Shipping / handling
10 wrenches weigh ~4–6 lb; USPS flat rate medium/large box $18–25. Durable.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
List sizes, forging codes (-V-, -K-, -W-), "Made in USA", condition of chrome, and a photo of every mark.

## Buy rule
Profit formula: 0.87 × median − ship to buyer (~$20) − ship to Dylan (~$20) − price ≥ max($50, price). A typical USA set selling $40 nets ~$35 before price and shipping-in — always a pass shipped. Only buy early-mark lots (=V=, Herbrand, script) under ~$30 where sold median ≥ $120.
