## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pressed steel plates coated with tin and printed by offset lithography. Decorative types reproduce paintings (Gainsborough, Lawrence, Romney portraits; Currier & Ives; Norman Rockwell; Christmas scenes) with an embossed or printed scroll border, often in silver/gold. Fine screen dots under magnification and a rolled rim are giveaways. Advertising trays (beer, soda, cigars, insurance) carry a brand in the design and a maker credit around the rim ("Chas. W. Shonk Co. Litho Chicago", "American Art Works Coshocton O.", "H.D. Beach Co. Coshocton", "Kaufmann & Strauss NY").

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Ohio Art Company** (Bryan, Ohio, 1908–): best known for Etch A Sketch; made millions of tin litho decorative plates, trays and coasters 1960s–80s, many dated on the back ("Ohio Art 1974"), sometimes in boxes. Low value.
- **J. Chein & Co. / Cheinco** (NJ): tin trays, canisters, wastebaskets 1930s–80s.
- **Elite Trays (England)**, **Daher (Long Island)**, **Worcester Ware (England)**, **Bristolware (Bristol Ware)**: 1960s–80s decorative trays.
- **Advertising tray makers (pre-1930)**: American Art Works, H.D. Beach, Chas. W. Shonk, Kaufmann & Strauss, Meek & Beach — tip trays (4–6 in) and serving trays (12–13 in) are the valuable side of this category.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Modern decorative: bright, even colour, glossy lacquer, plain grey or printed back with company name and date, often a hanging hole.
- Pre-1930 advertising: heavier gauge, deeper colour, maker credit on rim, often wear showing bare metal, slight rust at rim.
- Halftone dot pattern = printed (all tin litho is printed; the question is age, not "hand painted").

## Condition, damage and repairs
Scratches and chips through the litho, rust bloom, dents, fading. Advertising trays are graded on a 1–10 scale; a scratch in the face drops value by half. Repaints and inpainting on rare trays are common — check with UV light.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction Coca-Cola, beer and Moxie trays since the 1970s (often marked "reproduction" small on the rim, or modern even lithography, light gauge). Modern "vintage style" tin signs and trays are everywhere. Decorative art-print plates are not faked because they have no value.

## Market
Decorative tin plates: oversupplied, slow, low. Advertising trays: active collector market (breweriana, Coca-Cola collectors) but reproduction-heavy.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers overvalue art-print plates ("1700s painting!"). The opportunity is the reverse: a small advertising tip tray in a box of costume jewellery or a junk drawer, priced as "old tin plate".

## Shipping / handling
Light (0.5–1 lb), flat; ship in a rigid mailer with cardboard, $6–10. Trays 12 in: $10–14.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give maker, date on back, diameter, and the painting reproduced (e.g. "after Sir Thomas Lawrence"). For advertising trays photograph the rim credit, give honest grade, mention any rust spots.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$10 ship to buyer − ~$10 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A decorative plate can never reach this. Only buy advertising trays with a pre-1930 maker credit and resale ≥ $120 at ≤ $30. Pass on high-value Coca-Cola/beer tray claims that need grading/authentication.
