## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Any porcelain or ceramic vase, basket or pitcher sold as decor. About 90% of Craigslist examples are 1980s–2010s Asian-made gift ware sold under a US importer's name. Tells for that tier:
- **Decal decoration**: identical flowers repeating, dot-matrix edges under magnification, no brush texture, colour sitting perfectly inside outlines.
- **Bright, even, slightly bluish white body** with a thick glassy glaze and a glazed or felt-padded base.
- **Gold "brushstroke" trim** on handles (Madison & Max, Andrea by Sadek), crowned/scalloped rims, chinoiserie peony scroll in a very even mid-blue (Seymour Mann "China Blue").
- **Back stamps**: "China Blue Fine Porcelain Exclusively for Seymour Mann", "Andrea by Sadek" (often with a paper label "Made in Taiwan/Japan/China"), "Madison & Max At Home" with "Judy Buswell" artist credit, "Bombay Company", "Maitland-Smith" (better), "Toyo", "Chinese Export Porcelain" printed in English (a modern import line, not period export).

Tells that something is better:
- **Hand-painted brushwork** that varies stroke to stroke, slight blurring of cobalt into the glaze ("heaping and piling" on genuine Ming/Qing blue), raised enamel or moriage slip beading (Nippon), tube-lined raised outlines (Moorcroft), crackled ivory glaze with dense gilt figures (Satsuma).
- **An unglazed foot rim** showing the clay: on genuine Chinese pieces it is often orange-toned ("burnt") where iron in the body oxidised, smooth from wear, not gritty-new.
- **Marks**: Moorcroft impressed "MOORCROFT MADE IN ENGLAND" + painted WM/initials; KPM blue sceptre; Royal Copenhagen three waves; Herend blue shield; Rosenthal crown "Studio-Linie"; Nippon "hand painted Nippon" wreath/M-in-wreath (1891–1921); Satsuma gold mon (cross-in-circle).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Seymour Mann (New York importer, 1960s–2000s)**: "China Blue" line of chinoiserie blue-and-white porcelain made in China/Taiwan, 1980s–90s, often dated (e.g. "1996"). Vases, ginger jars, temple jars, lamps. Decor value.
- **Andrea by Sadek (Sadek Imports, NJ, 1960s–2000s)**: wide line of Japanese-, then Taiwanese- and Chinese-made giftware: blue-and-white pitchers, cachepots, figural pieces. $10–40.
- **Madison & Max At Home (2000s)**: decal porcelain giftware; Judy Buswell "Roses and Birdhouses" pattern (pink cabbage roses, blue birdhouse) in basket vases, trinket boxes, watering cans. $10–25.
- **Bombay Company, Maitland-Smith, Mottahedeh**: Mottahedeh (reproductions of historic export patterns, marked) $30–150; Maitland-Smith $40–200.
- **Moorcroft (Burslem, England, 1897–present)**: tube-lined art pottery. Modern decorative vases £100–400 at auction, Walter Moorcroft era £300–900, early William Moorcroft £800–3,000+ (Potteries Auctions).
- **Chinese export / Qing (1644–1911)**: Kangxi, Qianlong, Guangxu blue-and-white, famille rose, Canton. Republic (1912–49) pieces often carry fake Qianlong marks. PRC (post-1949) pieces may be stamped "China, Jingdezhen" or carry a hand-painted reign mark.
- **Japanese**: Nippon (1891–1921, hand-painted, moriage, gold) $40–300; Satsuma (Meiji 1868–1912 = high; post-war "Satsuma" gift ware = $20–60); Imari (Arita, 19th c.) $80–400; Noritake (decal and hand painted, $15–80).
- **European**: KPM, Meissen, Herend, Royal Copenhagen, Bing & Grøndahl, Rosenthal Studio-Line, Limoges hand-painted (artist-signed pieces higher). Herend and Royal Copenhagen $40–1,500 per estimonia.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Hand painted vs decal**: look for brush strokes and pooling; decals show a faint film edge and identical repeats. Andrea by Sadek is a mix of both.
- **Old vs new**: wear on the foot rim, light scratches inside the mouth, toned crazing on earthenware; new pieces have pristine bases and a uniform modern white.
- **Cobalt tone**: genuine Qing cobalt is uneven, sometimes purplish or "heaped"; modern export blue is flat and even.
- **Gilding**: old gold is soft and rubs on high points; modern gold is bright and often decal "brushstroke".
- **Weight**: modern import porcelain is thick and heavy for its size; Japanese eggshell (Nippon, Noritake) is thin and translucent.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips to scalloped rims and handles (basket vases), hairline cracks from the rim (hold to light, flick for a dull "thunk" instead of a ring).
- Gold rub on handles; decal wear.
- Restoration: matte patches under UV, colour mismatch on a rim, a glued handle. Restored mass-market pieces are worthless; restored Qing pieces still sell but at 30–50%.
- Drilled bases (converted lamps) cut value 50–80%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Chinese reign marks (Kangxi, Qianlong, Xuande six-character marks) are on millions of 20th–21st c. pieces. A mark alone means nothing; judge form, glaze, foot and wear (Oriental Antiques guide).
- Modern "Chinese Export" lines printed in English, and Mottahedeh reproductions, are honest reproductions but get mis-titled "antique".
- Fake Moorcroft is rare; "Moorcroft-style" tube-lined pieces from other makers exist (Cobridge, Anita Harris, Old Tupton Ware = $15–60).
- Fake Nippon marks (modern "Nippon" back stamps on 1980s imports) are common; the real ones are under the glaze and match the hand-painted quality.

## Market
Mass-market import vases: high supply, slow. Many sit 60–180 days at $20–40. Moorcroft and Nippon sell steadily (weeks). Genuine Qing sells fast at auction but rarely surfaces at ≤ $200 from a private seller with good photos.

## What sellers usually miss
- The foot rim. Sellers photograph the pretty side. A burnt-orange unglazed foot with wear, or an impressed Moorcroft mark, is the money. Always ask for the base.
- Tube-lined raised outlines (Moorcroft) mistaken for "hand painted pottery vase".
- Moriage beading and "hand painted Nippon" green M-in-wreath on a "vintage Japanese vase".
- The reverse mistake is far more common: sellers think "China Blue" or "Chinese export" import ware is antique and price it at $25–100.

## Shipping / handling
2–5 lb packed. Double box, 2" of padding all round, fill the interior. $12–20 each way for a 10" vase; tall or basket handles add breakage risk. eBay 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the back stamp exactly, height and widest point, hand painted vs transfer, and photograph the base, rim and handle closely. For Chinese pieces, say "reign mark, later period" unless confirmed by a specialist. Disclose any crazing, gold wear or hairlines.

## Buy rule

- **Pass**: any piece with an importer stamp (Seymour Mann, Andrea by Sadek, Madison & Max, Bombay Co.) at any price – max resale ~$40 leaves no profit.

- Genuine-Qing claims over $150 need specialist confirmation – treat as "needs authentication", pass.
