## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Mid-century costume (1940s–60s):** prong-set (not glued) rhinestones, rhodium or gold plating, japanned (black) backs, foil-backed glass, aurora borealis (AB, iridescent coated) stones from 1955+, hook-and-eye or box clasps, screw-back or clip earrings.
- **1970s–80s:** goldtone chains, Monet/Napier/Trifari signed chains, Sarah Coventry home-party sets, chokers, wood and heishi beads, snake/herringbone chains.
- **Modern fashion (1990s–now):** cast zinc alloy, glued stones, lobster clasps, lightweight stamped findings, "statement" bib necklaces with faux pearls and spikes, gunmetal "vintage-look" cocktail rings, Disney Couture.
- **Precious content in lots:** sterling ("925", "Sterling", "Italy 925"), gold-filled ("1/20 12K GF"), and karat gold ("10K", "14K") stamps on clasps and ring shanks.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Top tier ($80–400+):** Miriam Haskell (hand-wired seed pearls/beads, signed oval plaque), Schreiner (inverted stones, unsigned often), Juliana/DeLizza & Elster (unsigned, five-link construction, hand-set stones), Eisenberg, Hobé, Boucher, Trifari Jelly Bellies, Coro Duettes.
- **Mid tier ($25–80):** Weiss (Weiss brooch median $34), Garne (1940s–60s NY, "Garne Jewelry" mark; $8–80, median ~$24), Kramer, Lisner, Sarah Coventry sets, Marvella, Vendome, Monet signed chains (a lot of 8 Monet collars sold $250).
- **Low tier ($5–25):** unsigned 1970s chains, AB bead sets (median $25), fashion rings, modern statement necklaces.
- **Disney Couture (2000s–2010s):** licensed fashion jewelry, mostly $7–40; large ornate Kingdom key pieces new with tags occasionally $75–129.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Prong-set stones and rhodium plating = better. Glued-in stones and pitted plating = lower.
- Marks: on clasp tags, backs of brooches, earring clips. Copyright © symbol after a mark = post-1955.
- Weight: older pot-metal and brass pieces are heavier; modern zinc alloy feels light and greyish where plating wears.
- Sterling and GF stamps make a "junk" lot worth its metal: sterling ~ spot value per gram minus refining; GF lots sell by weight.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Missing or dead stones (dark/foil-loss), green verdigris, plating loss, broken clasps, missing earrings from sets.
- Replaced stones and re-plating are common; disclose them.
- Faux pearls peel; bead strings stretch or break.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Fake signatures (added "Haskell", "Eisenberg", "Juliana" tags) and Chinese copies of Trifari Jelly Bellies and Eisenberg figurals.
- "DeLizza & Elster/Juliana" attributions on unsigned pieces are often wishful.
- Modern "vintage-inspired" fashion jewelry in mixed lots.

## Market
- Very liquid category on eBay (thousands of sales a month) but low prices; signed sets and named designers sell within days to weeks. Mixed lots sell to crafters and resellers by weight.
- Sterling/gold content tracks metal prices.

## What sellers usually miss
- Marks: sellers don't flip pieces over – a signed Haskell, Juliana-construction or Trifari piece hides in "grandma's jewelry box" lots.
- Metal stamps on chains and rings (925, GF, 10K/14K) in "junk" lots.
- Unsigned high-quality construction (Schreiner/Juliana) recognisable by hand-set prongs, layered stones and open backs.
- Conversely: sellers overprice modern fashion pieces and single unsigned items from Lens matches.

## Shipping / handling
- Light: padded mailer or small box, $5–9 Ground Advantage. Wrap pieces separately to protect stones.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Photograph marks, clasps, backs and every stone; give length, weight, closure type; disclose missing/darkened stones.
- Group signed sets; sell metal-content pieces separately by weight with stamp photos.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; fits anywhere. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$6 shipping to buyer − ~$8 shipping to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Single costume pieces almost never clear it (would need a ~$100+ median at a $10 price).
- Lots: buy only when the photo shows identifiable signed or metal-stamped pieces, or the seller confirms stamps; target price ≤ 30% of the sum of identifiable comps.
- Real precious jewelry claims (diamond, fine gold with stones, designer fine jewelry) need professional testing – pass unless it's a plain stamped-metal lot.
