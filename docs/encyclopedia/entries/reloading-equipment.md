## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tools for hand-loading ammunition:
- **Dies**: threaded 7/8-14 steel cylinders in sets of 2–3 (sizer, seater, expander), in a plastic box (RCBS green box, Redding blue/red box, Lyman red, Lee red/white). The caliber is printed on the box label and stamped on the die.
- **Presses**: cast-iron "O" frame (RCBS Rock Chucker – green; Lyman Orange Crusher, Spar-T turret – orange/red), C-frame (RCBS Jr., Lee), progressive (Dillon – blue, Hornady LNL).
- **Scales**: beam scales (RCBS 5-0-5, 10-10, Lyman/Ohaus M5).
- **Powder measures**, trimmers, primer tools.
- **Tong tools**: pliers-shaped all-in-one reloaders – **Ideal No. 3/No. 4/No. 6** (pre-1925, Ideal Mfg. New Haven) and **Lyman 310** (post-1925, with separate interchangeable 310 dies). Often also cast a bullet in the handle (early Ideal).
- **Bullet moulds**: Ideal/Lyman, RCBS, Lee, Saeco; iron or aluminum blocks with wooden handles.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **RCBS** (Rock Chuck Bullet Swage, Fred Huntington, Oroville CA, 1943). Green paint. Rock Chucker press (from ~1967), Jr. press, 5-0-5 scale, Uniflow measure. Older "Oroville" stamped dies preferred.
- **Lyman** (Middlefield CT, 1878) – bought **Ideal Manufacturing Co.** (New Haven) in 1925. Ideal tong tools and moulds pre-1925 are collectible; Lyman 310 tools and "Ideal" branded moulds continued long after.
- **Lee Precision** (Hartford WI, 1958) – cheap, high-volume; low resale except Lee Loader kits in odd calibers.
- **Redding** (Cortland NY) – premium dies; competition dies hold value.
- **Hornady / Pacific** (Pacific Gun Sight bought by Hornady 1970s), **C-H**, **Bonanza/Forster**, **Herter's** (collectible, Waseca MN), **Dillon** (progressive, strong resale), **Hollywood** presses (collector demand, heavy).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Value hinges on **caliber** and **completeness**, not looks. Obsolete calibers (.25-20, .32-20, .38-55, .45-70 tong tools, .348 Win, .219 Zipper, 8mm Lebel, 7.62×54R, .303 Savage, 6.5×55 is common now) and wildcats sell much higher than .223/.308/9mm. Complete sets in original boxes with shell holder; tong tools with all parts (mould, dies) and good bluing. Rust and pitting on die interiors are a deal-breaker for shooters.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rusted die bores, stuck case in a sizer die (common – look for a case in the die), cracked press frames (rare), missing ram/shell holder, scales missing pan or poise, Uniflow missing drum. No repairs per Dylan's rules.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No fakes. Lyman reissued 310 tools; modern 310 tools are worth less than pre-war Ideal. Collectible Ideal pieces carry "Ideal Mfg Co New Haven Conn" and patent dates.

## Market
Strong and steady, especially after the 2020–2023 ammunition shortages (demand cooled since). Dies and tools in odd calibers sell within days. Buyers: hunters, precision shooters, cowboy action shooters, collectors of Ideal tools. eBay allows reloading presses, dies and tools; it prohibits ammunition, primers and powder (verify current policy). GunBroker is an alternative channel.

## What sellers usually miss
Estate sellers list a box of "old reloading stuff" as one lot; the money is in odd-caliber dies, Ideal tong tools and moulds hidden in the box. Green RCBS boxes with obsolete calibers are worth 2–4× a common set.

## Shipping / handling
Dies: small, heavy, cheap to ship ($8–15). Presses: 15–25 lb, $25–40. Make sure no powder, primers or live rounds are in a lot (cannot ship them; hazmat). Passenger seat: everything fits.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + tool + exact caliber ("RCBS .257 Roberts die set", "Ideal No. 4 tong tool .38-55", "Lyman 310", "Rock Chucker press") + "with shell holder" + "Oroville" if stamped; Junk: "reloading lot", "old gun stuff", "ammo" (never). Example: `RCBS .257 Roberts 2 Die Set Oroville Calif Green Box Shell Holder Vintage`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (dies and tools in odd calibers sell within days; presses, dies, tools allowed — no primers, powder or ammunition); GunBroker alternative. List each odd-caliber set separately, common calibers as a lot or local.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (common sets $10–35; obsolete-caliber dies/Ideal tong tools $40–150; Rock Chucker $90–200; Dillon $300–500), accept −15%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo the set in its box with the caliber label; then die bodies (caliber stamps), bores (clean/no rust, no stuck case), shell holder, box ends, tong-tool maker marks and patent dates, mould cavities.
- **State:** maker, exact caliber, number of dies, shell holder included, bore condition, box; tong tools: maker marks, patent dates, included dies/mould; no powder/primers/live rounds in the lot.
- **Pack / haul:** dies small and heavy, small box ($8–15); presses 15–25 lb, remove the ram and handle, pad ($25–40); everything fits a passenger seat.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "RCBS [.257 Roberts] set, [Oroville]-stamped — the older green-box dies in a caliber they barely make now; a new set is [$X] if you can find it and the common calibers go for a third of this."

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship (~$12) − ship to Dylan (~$12) − price ≥ max($50, price). Single common die sets never work. Targets: obsolete-caliber sets/tong tools with $80–150 medians bought ≤ ~$25–40, or mixed estate lots ≤ $60 containing several odd calibers, split into separate listings. Rock Chucker press: only ≤ $40 with local pickup. Reject lots containing powder/primers/ammo unless the seller removes them.
