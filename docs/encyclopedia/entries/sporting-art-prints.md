## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Framed images of British fox hunting (red coats, hounds, top hats), racing, coaching and shooting scenes; plus American waterfowl/wildlife prints.
- **Period aquatints/engravings (1800–1880)**: plate mark (rectangular indentation around image), hand-colouring, publisher line ("Pub'd by Ackermann", "Fores"), title in engraved script below.
- **Signed early 20th c. prints** (Snaffles, Aldin, Edwards): chromolithographs/collotypes with pencil signature and sometimes a pencil "remarque" sketch in the margin.
- **Decorator reproductions (1960s–2000s)**: offset lithographs with dot screen, printed title in script below image, cream mats with gilt fillet in dark wood/gold frames; often from Royal hunt paintings by Heywood Hardy, George Wright, Thomas Blinks, John Sanderson-Wells.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Magnify: offset prints show a rosette dot screen; aquatints show granular tone; engravings/etchings show lines; collotypes show reticulated grain. Plate mark and deckled or wide margins indicate fine prints. Pencil signatures (graphite sheen) vs. printed facsimile signatures.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foxing, toning, mat burn, water stains, trimmed margins, faded colour, frames with glass damage.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Most "antique hunting prints" are reproductions; restrikes of Alken plates (later 19th–20th c.); facsimile signatures on Snaffles reproductions; prints cut from books and framed as "antique".

## Market
Reproductions: very slow, local pickup preferred due to glass/size. Signed prints: collector market steady in UK/US.

## What sellers usually miss
Pencil signatures and remarques on Snaffles/Aldin prints sold as "old horse picture"; plate marks on genuine aquatints.

## Shipping / handling
Large framed glass ~24x20: $25–40 with risk; often not worth shipping — sell local. Barely fits passenger seat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State "reproduction offset print" honestly; frame size, image size, condition. For fine prints: process, plate mark, signature, publisher line.
