## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A long tube (brush holder, 6–9 in) with an attached ink pot (sumi cotton-pad well) with hinged lid at one end; carried in the obi. Often misidentified as an opium or tobacco pipe because of the bowl-and-stem shape. Smoking-pipe-shaped yatate exist.

## Makers, brands and variants
Mostly unsigned Edo (pre-1868) to Taisho (to 1926) workshop pieces. Better ones: shakudo/shibuichi inlay, engraved dragons/bamboo, signed by metalworkers; wooden/bamboo with lacquer.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Brass/copper with patina; fine engraving and inlay = better; crude cast decoration = later/export or Chinese copy.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken hinge, missing lid, dents, cleaned/polished (reduces value).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese-made modern copies, often with crude castings and artificial patina.

Thin but steady; Japanese dealers flood eBay with $20–40 yatate, capping prices.

## What sellers usually miss
They call them opium pipes (and overprice them) — the mislabel rarely means undervaluation.
