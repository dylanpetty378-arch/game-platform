## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Vertical rectangular signs (9–39 in.) or round 12 in. dial thermometers with a glass tube or dial, printed with brand advertising. Originals: lithographed or embossed steel, porcelain enamel (glossy, chipping to black/blue steel), or glass-faced dials with metal rims; tube held by metal clips; often a small maker's line at the bottom edge ("Stout Sign Co. St. Louis", "Robertshaw", "Taylor").
- History: advertising thermometers appeared in the 1900s and were widespread in the US by the 1920s; the earliest were tin and wood, later ones porcelain like porcelain signs; they were made to hang outside (Collectors Weekly).
- Forms: round dials with clock-style hands; vertical rectangular or die-cut (bottle-shaped) signs with a tube in the middle (Collectors Weekly).
- Very large porcelain examples exist (e.g. a Mail Pouch porcelain thermometer 19 × 73 with wood frame, RM Sotheby's); anything over about passenger-seat length is excluded by the size rule. The common Mail Pouch is the 39 in. size.
- Where the marks are: maker's line along the bottom or side edge (tin), small text on the dial face (round dials), date or "printed in USA" line on the face, and stamped marks on the back.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Stout Sign Co. (St. Louis)**, **Robertshaw-Fulton**, **Taylor Instrument**, **Springfield**: tin vertical thermometers 1940s–70s.
- **Pam Clock Co.**, **Ohio Thermometer Co. (Springfield OH)**: 12 in. round glass-dome dials (1950s–60s) — high value for Coke, Pepsi, Dr Pepper, 7Up.
- **Porcelain**: Prestone, Marvel Mystery Oil, Mail Pouch, Hills Bros (1915–50) — the big money. Prestone made both a 9 × 36 in. vertical porcelain and a 10 in. round porcelain dial with chrome bezel (RM Sotheby's).
- **Nostalgia repros**: Ande Rooney (porcelain-look repros 1990s+), unnamed 1980s–2000s plastic/thin-tin "gasoline filling station" types, Coca-Cola licensed 1990s.
- Value drivers: porcelain over tin; round glass-dome dial over flat vertical; die-cut bottle shapes; early brand logos and slogans; strong colour and gloss; working original tube.

### Quick ID checklist
- Material: porcelain (glassy, layered, chips show dark steel), lithographed or embossed tin, or plastic (pass).
- Maker's line on the bottom edge or dial face (Stout, Robertshaw, Taylor, Pam, Ohio Thermometer); photograph it.
- Back: old steel with even age, original mounting holes; modern bright steel or plastic = suspect.
- Wear matches outdoor use: chips at mounting holes and edges, fading on the sun side, rust at holes.
- Slogan and graphics match the brand's period (no "Clean Rest Rooms"/"Route 66" novelty text).
- Tube intact with liquid continuous; clips original.
- Round dial: glass dome uncracked, rim and hands original.
- Size in inches; over about passenger-seat length = excluded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original: heavy steel, natural rust at holes/edges, fading consistent with outdoor use, period phone numbers/addresses, maker imprint. Repro: plastic or thin stamped tin with perfect print, molded raised frames, "Made in USA/China" with tiny modern printing, generic slogans ("Clean Rest Rooms", "Gasoline Filling Station"), fake distress. Porcelain repros are thinner, too bright, with even fake chips.
- Porcelain is fused glass in layers; colours sit at different levels and chips are conchoidal. Painted reproductions have flat, single-layer colour.
- Inpainting (touch-up on chips) is common even on auction originals and should be disclosed (RM Sotheby's lot 1396 notes "a few isolated areas have been inpainted").

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or separated mercury/spirit tubes (replaceable but buyers discount), rust-through, porcelain chips (especially at mounting holes), dent/bend, faded print. Repainted touch-ups lower value.
- Typical original porcelain wear: chipping along the sides, mostly around mounting holes, and on the bottom edge (RM Sotheby's).
- Ask for photos under raking light to show touch-up and for the back.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Very high repro risk — petroliana and Coke are the most faked advertising categories. Ande Rooney and "Old Time Signs" style repros flood the market.
- Licensed Coca-Cola reissues from the 1980s–90s are dated in small print; they are legitimate but low value.
- Fake-aged porcelain: chips evenly spaced or on flat areas rather than at mounting holes and edges.

## Market
Big, active collector category (petroliana + soda). Originals sell in days to weeks; repros sit. Car-collection and petroliana auctions (RM Sotheby's, Morphy, Matthews) set the top prices; eBay for mid and low tiers.

## What sellers usually miss
Small-town dealer/feed-store thermometers have regional collectors; round dial thermometers are much more valuable than vertical tin.
- The maker's line on the edge, which separates a period original from a reproduction.

## Shipping / handling
Vertical tin 1–3 lb, long box; dial thermometers need double box (glass dome). $10–20 to ship. Porcelain chips at edges in transit: corner protectors and rigid backing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State original vs reproduction plainly, dimensions, maker imprint, tube intact and working.

### What to tell the buyer
Say whether it is a period original or a reproduction and why (maker's line, material, wear pattern), with photos of the maker's imprint and the back. Give exact dimensions, material (porcelain, tin or plastic) and whether the tube or dial works. Describe chips, rust, fading and any touch-up or replaced tube honestly; chips around mounting holes are normal on outdoor originals and should be shown, not hidden.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship − ship − price. Repros: pass at any price over $5. Originals: buy if comp median ≥ $150 and price ≤ $50 (tin) or ≤ 1/4 comps (porcelain/dial). Ask for close-ups of the bottom edge imprint and mounting holes. Must fit a passenger seat (the 6-ft Mail Pouch size does not).
