## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mixed watches sold singly or as lots. Ninety percent are cheap fashion quartz (gold-tone plated alloy, mineral glass, Japanese Miyota movements) worth a few dollars each. The money is in **sleepers**: solid gold cases hidden among "gold-tone" watches, vintage mechanical Swiss or Japanese watches, and collectible references (Seiko 6139/6105, Bulova Accutron).
Photo tells:
- **Dial text**: "21 Jewels" or "17 Jewels" plus "Swiss" = mechanical (not quartz, whatever the seller says). "Quartz" on the dial = battery.
- **Case-back stamps**: "14K", "18K", "0.585", "0.750" with no "GF", "RGP", "Gold Filled", "Plated" or "Base metal back" = solid gold. "10K RGP", "10K GF", "Gold Electroplate" and "Stainless steel back" = not solid.
- **Wear**: brass showing through on lugs and edges = plated or filled. Solid gold doesn't brass.
- **Service award watches** (Jostens, Balfour, O.C. Tanner) have a company logo on the dial. Some have solid 10K or 14K cases (and some carry diamonds); many are gold-filled.
- **Fashion tells**: big branded logos (Skechers, Kathy Ireland, Geneva), clip-in bracelets, sticker case backs, plastic straps, blister packs.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Fashion quartz (near-zero)**: Skechers, Kathy Ireland, Geneva, Armitron, Anne Klein, Guess, Fossil (some vintage Fossil character tins do $20–40), Relic, Timex quartz, Disney character watches, cherub/angel bangle watches (QVC-type, 1990s).
- **ESQ** (founded 1992 in Switzerland; part of Movado Group): Swiss quartz steel watches and chronographs (e.g., E5290), typically $30–80 used.
- **Bulova, Seiko, Citizen, Hamilton, Elgin, Gruen, Benrus, Waltham**: vintage mechanicals $30–200. Gold-filled common; solid 14K ladies' cases $200–450 (melt-driven).
- **Jostens** (Owatonna/Minneapolis, MN; best known for class rings) supplied corporate service-award watches with company emblems on the dial, often with Swiss jeweled movements and karat-gold or gold-filled cases. Value depends almost entirely on the case metal.
- **Collectible Seiko** (vintage Seiko 5 special dials, Speed-Timer 6139, SKX divers $250–575, King/Grand Seiko): see underpriced.app figures. Watch for them in lots.
- **Luxury** (Rolex, Omega, Cartier, Patek): require professional authentication. Outside Dylan's rules.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Solid gold: crisp karat stamp inside the case back (often with a maker hallmark), no brassing, rich colour, heavier than it looks. Snap or hinged backs (screw backs usually mean filled cases, per Pocket Watch Database).
- Gold-filled/rolled gold plate: "10K RGP", "GF", "1/20 10K", plus a stainless "base metal back". Wear shows brass.
- Mechanical vs quartz: jewel count on the dial; a crown that winds with resistance; a sweeping vs ticking seconds hand.
- Mesh "Hadley" and similar bands are usually gold-filled or plated, so they don't add gold value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Dead batteries (normal for quartz), leaked batteries (corroded movements), scratched crystals, broken bracelets, missing crowns, non-running mechanicals (service costs $80–200, so don't buy runners as non-runners).
- Gold cases: dents, worn engraving and a mismatched movement have little effect on melt value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Fake luxury watches (Rolex, Omega, Cartier, Michael Kors copies) appear in lots constantly: never buy or list a watch as a luxury brand without authentication.
- "14K" stamps on plated Asian watches exist. Test with an acid kit or XRF before buying in quantity. The tell is a "14K" stamp plus "plated" or "HGE" elsewhere.
- Redials and fantasy dials on vintage Seikos and Swiss watches.

## Market
A huge, fast market for good watches; fashion lots are oversupplied. Gold-cased watches are liquid (scrap or eBay) and track the gold price. With gold above $4,000/oz in 2026, solid 14K vintage ladies' watches sell readily for $200–450. Collectible mechanicals sell in days. Fashion quartz lots sit for months.

## What sellers usually miss
- They call any small gold-coloured watch "gold tone quartz" without reading the dial ("21 Jewels Swiss" = mechanical) or the case back ("14K").
- Service-award watches with corporate logos look like promo junk but can be solid gold.
- Vintage Seikos and Citizens hidden in lots of fashion watches.
- Conversely, Lens and sellers overvalue ESQ ("Swiss!") and character watches.

## Shipping / handling
Small and light: a padded box or bubble mailer in a small box, $5–8 USPS Ground Advantage. Insure gold watches; use signature confirmation over $250. Inbound from sellers is also about $6–10.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the case material and stamp (photo of the inside case back), karat and weight in grams (watch head without strap), movement (jewels, Swiss/Japan, running or not), case size in mm and band material.
- For fashion watches, sell in themed lots (brand or style) with "new battery" if replaced.
- Never use luxury brand names unless authenticated.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price. Pass unless profit ≥ max($50, price). Tier A: profit ≥ 2× price.
- Watches ship cheaply (~$6–8 each way), so a gold ladies' watch with a $250 median bought at ≤ $100 clears the bar easily. Fashion lots never clear it.
- Buy only when photos show solid-karat marks (or the seller confirms them), or a collectible reference you can name.
- Quartz watches are battery electronics: test, or price them as non-working. Mechanicals must be wound and seen running if possible.
- No luxury brands (Rolex/Omega/Cartier): professional authentication required.
- Fits in a pocket.
