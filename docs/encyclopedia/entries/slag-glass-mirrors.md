## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mirror surrounded by panels of opaque streaky "slag" or cathedral glass joined by lead came or copper foil and solder. 1970s craft pieces: wide flat soldered seams, zinc outer channel, amber/white streaky Kokomo-type opalescent glass, octagon or arched shapes. Period (1900–1925) pieces: slag glass set in cast or stamped metal (bronze/copper-plated) frames with filigree overlays; often table lamps rather than mirrors.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hobbyist/craft studio (1970s–80s)**: unmarked; huge supply after the stained-glass hobby boom.
- **Bradley & Hubbard**, **Miller**, **Handel**, **Pittsburgh Lamp**: metal-overlay slag glass (mostly lamps; some mirrors/frames).
- **Kokomo**, **Wissmach**, **Youghiogheny**: glass makers whose opalescent sheet glass was used.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Even bead solder, patina (black/copper), tight fit, no cracked pieces = better craft work. Period pieces: metal frames with filigree, mellow patina, felt backs, older mirror glass with desilvering spots.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked glass pieces (hard to see in photos), loose came, bowed frames, desilvered mirror. Repairs require re-leading.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese Tiffany-style mirrors exist; craft mirrors are not fakes but often oversold as "antique".

## What sellers usually miss
Little — this category is usually over-priced by sellers, not under-priced.

## Shipping / handling
Typically 20–30 in., 8–15 lb, glass + lead — needs crate/foam; $40–80. Often too big for the passenger-seat rule.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Dimensions, weight, light-through photos to reveal cracks, local pickup recommended.

## Buy rule
Pass. Craft median ~$60 cannot cover shipping. Period metal-overlay pieces: only if ≤ 24 in., marked, comp ≥ $300 and price ≤ $75.
