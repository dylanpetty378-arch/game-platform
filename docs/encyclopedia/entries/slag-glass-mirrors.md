## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mirror surrounded by panels of opaque streaky "slag" or cathedral glass joined by lead came or copper foil and solder. 1970s craft pieces: wide flat soldered seams, zinc outer channel, amber/white streaky Kokomo-type opalescent glass, octagon or arched shapes. Period (1900–1925) pieces: slag glass set in cast or stamped metal (bronze/copper-plated) frames with filigree overlays; often table lamps rather than mirrors.
- **What "slag" means**: in Arts & Crafts lighting, "slag" is the trade name for opaque, marbled sheets of two or more colours (opalescent sheet glass) set in metal fixtures; the name probably does not come from iron-smelting slag (Arts & Crafts Homes). Kovels uses "slag glass" mainly for pressed Victorian marbled glass (purple, blue, pink, red, chocolate), made from about 1880 — a different collecting field.
- **Two forms that sell well**: small **jewellery boxes and vanity boxes** with leaded slag panels and a mirror lid (the Terapeak "leaded slag glass mirror" hits were mostly these), and **period bronze desk and frame pieces** with slag behind a pierced overlay (Tiffany Studios Grapevine pattern; Apollo Studios and others "in the manner of" Tiffany).
- **Construction tells in photos**: copper-foil work shows narrow, rounded solder beads; lead-came work shows flat, wider H-section lead; overlay pieces show a cut metal pattern (vines, scrolls) laid over a solid sheet of glass.
- **Title words**: slag glass, caramel slag, leaded, stained glass, Tiffany style, Arts & Crafts, Mission, overlay, filigree, Grapevine, Bradley & Hubbard, Handel, Miller.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hobbyist/craft studio (1970s–80s)**: unmarked; huge supply after the stained-glass hobby boom.
- **Bradley & Hubbard**, **Miller**, **Handel**, **Pittsburgh Lamp**: metal-overlay slag glass (mostly lamps; some mirrors/frames).
- **Kokomo**, **Wissmach**, **Youghiogheny**: glass makers whose opalescent sheet glass was used.
- **Tiffany Studios (New York, c.1900–1930)**: desk-set pattern lines, the best known being **Grapevine** — bronze (often gilt or green-patinated) pierced vine overlay over green/white slag glass — in picture frames, calendar frames, pen trays, letter racks and inkwells; genuine pieces are usually stamped "TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK" with a model number on the back or underside (1stDibs listings note "signed to underside"/"signed to back"). 1stDibs shows Grapevine pieces offered from ~$509 to $15,000 (asking).
- **Apollo Studios (New York)** and similar firms made Grapevine-pattern bronze frames "in the manner of" Tiffany at much lower values.

### Quick ID checklist
- Measure first: anything over passenger-seat size is excluded.
- Metal overlay on a solid glass sheet = period overlay piece; soldered seams between many glass pieces = craft leaded work.
- Turn it over: look for a stamped name (TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK + number, B&H, Handel, Apollo) on the back or underside.
- Patina: mellow dark-brown or green bronze with gilt traces = period; shiny bright copper or black-painted solder = later craft.
- Mirror glass: desilvering spots and a wavy reflection suggest old; flat bright mirror = modern.
- Back: felt, paper or wooden backing nailed in = older; staples and cardboard = craft.
- Hold to light (ask for a backlit photo): cracks in glass pieces show as dark lines.
- Small leaded jewellery boxes with mirror lids: count cracked panels and check the hinge.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Tiffany Studios Grapevine picture frame or desk piece, signed** — 1stDibs asking ~$509–15,000 vs $30–90 craft mirrors — pierced bronze vine overlay over green/white slag, "TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK" stamp with number.
- **Period metal-overlay slag mirror or frame (Bradley & Hubbard, Miller, Handel era)** — $200–1,200 (existing ladder, verify) vs $30–90 — cast/stamped metal filigree frame over slag panels, maker stamp on the back, felt or wooden backing.
- **Apollo Studios or other period bronze Grapevine-pattern frames** — period tier but well below Tiffany (no comps; est., verify) — "Apollo Studios" mark; same vine pattern, lighter casting.
- **Large, complex, signed studio leaded mirrors** — $80–200 (existing ladder) vs $30–90 plain octagons — signed glass piece or label, many small pieces, even beads.
- **Small leaded slag jewellery box with mirror lid** — Terapeak median $25 (existing comp) — the cheap end; useful only as a cheap lot add-on.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Even bead solder, patina (black/copper), tight fit, no cracked pieces = better craft work. Period pieces: metal frames with filigree, mellow patina, felt backs, older mirror glass with desilvering spots.
- "Stained" historically meant painted-and-fired glass; today it means any coloured glass, so the word in a title does not indicate age (Arts & Crafts Homes).
- Prairie-style period panels often used zinc came for a crisper look (Arts & Crafts Homes).
- Tiffany Studios bronzes are heavy castings with crisp detail; spelter (zinc alloy) copies are lighter and show grey metal where the finish wears.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked glass pieces (hard to see in photos), loose came, bowed frames, desilvered mirror. Repairs require re-leading.
- On overlay pieces, replaced glass shows as a colour that does not match the other panels; ask for a backlit photo of every panel.
- Re-patinated bronze looks uniformly dark with no wear at high points.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese Tiffany-style mirrors exist; craft mirrors are not fakes but often oversold as "antique".
- Tiffany Studios desk pieces are faked and married (a genuine frame with replaced glass, or an added stamp). A stamp alone is not proof; casting quality and patina must agree. Treat any claimed Tiffany as needing specialist review.
- Kovels notes reproductions of pressed slag (chocolate and coloured slag).

## Market
Local, slow; shipping glass panels is expensive and risky so eBay sells poorly.
- Period overlay and Tiffany Studios desk pieces sell through specialist auction houses and 1stDibs, not local.

## What sellers usually miss
Little — this category is usually over-priced by sellers, not under-priced.
- The exception: small period bronze-and-slag frames and desk pieces mixed into estate lots without the back photographed. Always ask for the back.

## Shipping / handling
Typically 20–30 in., 8–15 lb, glass + lead — needs crate/foam; $40–80. Often too big for the passenger-seat rule.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Dimensions, weight, light-through photos to reveal cracks, local pickup recommended.

### What to tell the buyer
Say what it is in plain terms: hand-made leaded (or copper-foil) stained glass mirror, probably 1970s–80s hobby work, unsigned; or, for period pieces, metal-overlay slag glass with the exact mark from the back. Give overall dimensions and weight, glass colours, construction (lead came or copper foil), and the result of a backlit check for cracks, panel by panel. State mirror condition (desilvering) and that local pickup is preferred for large pieces.

## Buy rule
Pass. Craft median ~$60 cannot cover shipping. Period metal-overlay pieces: only if ≤ 24 in., marked, comp ≥ $300 and price ≤ $75.
