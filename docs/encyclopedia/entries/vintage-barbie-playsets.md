## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Furniture, play environments, carrying cases and tiny accessories made for Barbie and her family (Ken, Midge, Skipper, Francie, Tutti) since 1959. Photo tells by era:
- **1959–1967 ("vintage" to collectors):** pastel cardboard playsets (Barbie's Dream House 1962, Fashion Shop 1962, Cafe Today), Suzy Goose wooden-look plastic furniture (1962–64: wardrobe, canopy bed, vanity, piano), black or colored vinyl cases with printed art of bubble-cut/ponytail Barbie, "© MATTEL INC" with year. Accessories are tiny, crisp, molded in hard plastic, often gold-painted details.
- **1965–1972 ("mod"):** Francie, Twist 'N Turn Barbie, Skipper, Tutti; vinyl trunks by SPP (Standard Plastic Products) with mod graphics; Barbie Family House, Country Living House, Barbie's Dream Kitchen-Dinette (1964).
- **1973–1989 ("superstar"/80s):** molded pink/peach plastic: Townhouse (1974), Dream House (1979), Beauty Salon #4839 (1983), Dream Store, Glamour Home, Arco brand playsets (Arco was Mattel-owned, 1980s). Large, bulky, common.
- **1990s–present:** mass-market, low collector value except NRFB rarities.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Mattel** — official sets; the year on a case or part is the *copyright* year of the design, not proof of manufacture year.
- **Suzy Goose (Kusan)** — 1960s licensed Barbie furniture (wardrobe with hangers, bed, vanity), "Suzy Goose" molded mark.
- **SPP (Standard Plastic Products, NJ)** — made the 1960s–70s vinyl cases and trunks; "SPP" logo printed on case.
- **Arco (Mattel subsidiary, 1980s)** — budget fashion-doll playsets often confused with Barbie sets.
- **Accessories** come from boxed outfits: e.g., "Barbie Q" (#962), "Picnic Set" (#967), "Friday Nite Date" (#979), "Movie Date," Ken outfits; and from Go Granny Go-type 1970s outfits (turntable + transistor radio). Many non-Mattel lookalikes (Ideal Tammy, Tressy, generic Hong Kong "Barbie-size" accessories) exist — markings ("MATTEL" or nothing) matter.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Cases:** 1960s vinyl over cardboard with metal hardware; check for "© 1965 Mattel Inc" and SPP logo; interior may have hanger rods, drawers, and doll compartments.
- **Accessories:** genuine early Mattel pieces are sharp-edged and well painted; reproductions (sold by repro sellers since the 1990s) are often glossier, slightly different colors, unmarked or marked with tiny "R."
- **1980s plastics:** peach/pink polypropylene with sticker decals; mirror stickers fade and lift.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cases: split seams, torn vinyl, rusted latches, broken handles, mildew odor, missing paper inserts.
- Playsets: missing small parts (rollers, combs, drawers, sink pump), sun-faded or yellowed plastic, peeling stickers, broken hinges.
- Accessories: paint wear, chewed edges (children/pets), warped soft plastic.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Mattel's own "reproduction" vintage dolls and outfits (1990s–2000s collector issues) look vintage — boxes say "reproduction" or have later dates.
- Aftermarket reproduction accessories and cases are common; loose pieces in lots may be repro.
- Arco and Tara Toy playsets resemble Barbie sets but sell lower.

## Market
- Very active Barbie collector market, but it is driven by 1959–1972 dolls and outfits. 1980s playsets sell slowly and mostly locally because of size and shipping.
- Q4 (holiday) and nostalgia buyers lift 1980s–90s sets slightly.

## What sellers usually miss
- What's inside the case: a "vintage Barbie case $20" may hold 1960s dolls, Francie mod outfits and tiny accessories worth hundreds. Always ask to see inside.
- Tiny accessories (shoes, purses, phones, radios) from 1959–66 outfits sell individually; a baggie of "Barbie stuff" can be the best buy in the lot.
- Conversely, sellers price 1980s playsets as "vintage" at 2–3× their real value.

## Shipping / handling
- Cases: 13×10×7", ~2 lb, $12–18. Playsets like the 1983 Beauty Salon: big box, 3–5 lb, $25–65 — shipping often kills the sale. Accessories: envelope or small box, $5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Identify the set name/number and year from the base or box; list every included and missing piece against the original contents.
- For cases, show inside and outside, note odor and seams.
- For accessories, show the "MATTEL" mark when present; never call a piece "vintage Mattel" without evidence.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Pass on 1980s–2000s playsets (sold $20–70, heavy shipping).
- Empty 1960s cases: pass unless ≤ $5; ask what's inside every case.
- Buy when photos/answers show 1959–67 dolls (bubble cut, ponytail, Francie, Skipper, Midge), named outfits or complete boxed 1960s playsets and the sold median clears the rule.
- Must fit the passenger seat; small and flat is ideal.
