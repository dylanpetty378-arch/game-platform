## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Box with a steel disc ~4.5" (Thorens AD30, Swiss 1940s–70s) or 11"–27" (antique Regina/Symphonion/Polyphon, 1890s–1910s) sitting on a comb movement. Thorens: small inlaid/marquetry box (often Sorrento "Made in Italy" inlay cases, or Thorens-branded walnut), a stack of 4.5" black discs with a yellow Thorens label, hand crank underneath. Antique Regina: large oak/mahogany case, big punched discs, serial plate. Seller words: "music box with discs", "interchangeable discs", "vintage music box".
Look-alikes: Sankyo/Reuge cylinder movements (pinned cylinder, no disc) in the same Sorrento boxes are the cheap tier.
- **AD30 disc detail**: the 4.5" (11.5 cm) disc is edge-driven with sawtooth projections, and the tune title is printed/embossed on the disc (MMD 1995, 2009). A swing arm holds the disc on the spindle; a lever or switch starts it.
- **Later AD30 cases**: about 10 × 10 in boxes with a domed lid, movement on the left and upright disc slots on the right, sold through the San Francisco Music Box Company in the 1990s (MMD 1995). These are late Reuge-era production, not 1940s–70s.
- **Antique tabletop**: a Regina 15½" double-comb box in cherry measures about 13" H × 21" W × 19" D with the Regina logo printed under the lid (Renaissance Antiques) — far bigger than an AD30 and the photo shows a large flat disc lying over a long comb.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Thorens AD30** (Sainte-Croix, Switzerland, 1940s–70s): 4.5" discs, hand crank; sold in Thorens walnut cases and in Italian Sorrento inlaid boxes. Value scales with disc count. Correction/extension: Reuge bought Thorens in 1986 and kept making essentially the same AD30 mechanism until the end of 2009 (MMD 2009), so AD30 production runs from the 1940s to 2009; later discs say "Reuge" and play the same as Thorens-marked discs (MMD 1995).
- **Helvetia** (Sainte-Croix): made a very similar 11.5 cm disc player; Thorens and Helvetia discs are basically interchangeable (MMD 2009).
- Over 1,700 tune discs were made for these machines; a tune list is at thorensad30.com (MMD 2009). Symphonion discs and German Junghans clock discs share the 11.5 cm size and may play on an AD30 (MMD 2009).
- **Thorens gramophone-style** variants (horn-gramophone-shaped case) – one sold at $388 in 2013.
- **Reuge / Porter**: two separate makers, not one company. Reuge (Sainte-Croix) is the Swiss maker that absorbed Thorens. **Porter Music Box Co.** was founded in 1974 by Dwight Porter in Randolph, Vermont. Its large disc boxes were made almost entirely in-house (comb, spring, discs) at about 120 a year. In 1995 they retailed at $6,000–20,000, a twin-disc model was its signature, and replacement discs were $60 each (Seven Days VT). Modern high-quality 10"+ disc boxes sell for $800–2,500.
- **Regina** (Rahway NJ, 1890s–1910s): 15.5" tabletop $1,500–4,000; 27" upright/changer $8,000–25,000. The 15½" is the most widely available Regina size; single-comb and double-comb versions exist, and double comb strikes twice as many notes (Renaissance Antiques). Case woods include oak, mahogany and cherry; the logo is under the lid. The "Reginaphone" is a Regina combined with a phonograph (Leland Little listing title).
- **Symphonion / Polyphon** (Leipzig, Germany, 1890s–1910s): similar antique disc machines; value by disc size, case and comb condition (general knowledge, verify).
- **Sankyo** (Japan): 18-note cylinder boxes, junk tier.

### Quick ID checklist
- Disc or cylinder? A flat steel disc on top = disc box; a pinned brass cylinder = Sankyo/Reuge cylinder (cheap tier).
- Disc diameter: ~4.5" = Thorens/Helvetia/Reuge AD30; 11"+ = antique Regina/Polyphon/Symphonion or Porter.
- Movement plate: "Thorens", "Reuge" or "Helvetia" stamp; Sorrento inlay boxes carry "Made in Italy" on the case, not the movement.
- Count the discs and read the titles; disc count is the main price driver on an AD30.
- Comb: ask for a close photo along the comb under good light; every tooth present, no rust on the underside.
- Case: Sorrento inlay (olive wood) vs Thorens walnut vs late 10 × 10 domed SF Music Box Co. case.
- Antique: logo under lid (Regina), single vs double comb, case wood, disc size, number of original discs.
- Video of a full disc playing is the only real test.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Steel comb with tuned teeth; damper wires under teeth on antique machines; steel punched discs. Thorens discs carry a yellow Thorens label and are interchangeable across AD30 boxes. Antique cases: solid oak/mahogany, often carved or with a lithograph lid print. Sorrento boxes: olive-wood/marquetry inlay, "Made in Italy". Quality tell: full, clean tone with every note sounding (general knowledge, verify).
- Disc compatibility nuance: not every Thorens disc sits properly on every machine because the design, disc thickness and material changed; newer nickel-plated discs often play poorly on older machines; a technician can adjust any AD30 to play almost all discs; moving the swing arm 3/4–1" from the spindle can help a problem disc (MMD 2009).
- Age: judged by close comparison of small construction details on the mechanism; there is no simple serial chart (MMD 2009).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or missing comb teeth (kills value; re-tipping is specialist work), rusted discs, bent disc projections, broken springs/governors, cracked inlay or veneer lifting. Ask for a video of a full disc playing. Regina restoration costs run into hundreds of dollars (general knowledge, verify).
- AD30 specifics (MMD 2009): rust on the top of a disc is cosmetic, but rust on the underside teeth/projections causes missed notes and can break comb teeth; bent teeth jam the mechanism; a warped star-wheel block causes popping, clicking or jamming; a click followed by total failure usually means a cracked nylon pinion or release gear (2009 repair ~$125, gear ~$30). A fully reconditioned AD30 mechanism was ~$275 in 2009. Do not use Evapo-Rust on old discs — it strips the printing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes; modern reproduction discs exist for Regina/Polyphon (lower value than originals), and married cases/movements occur (general knowledge, verify).
- AD30 movements were sold into many third-party cases (Sorrento, gift-shop boxes); a Reuge-marked AD30 is late (1986–2009) production, not a vintage Thorens.

## Market
Steady eBay demand from music-box collectors and gift buyers; the Thorens market has softened since 2013. Antique Regina/Symphonion sell best through specialist auctions and the Musical Box Society community (general knowledge, verify). Mild Nov–Dec gift peak. The Mechanical Music Digest (mmdigest.com) is the main collector forum for repair and ID questions.

## What sellers usually miss
Thorens movement inside a Sorrento inlay box – they list the box maker, not the movement. Disc count drives price (each disc $4–7). Antique Regina owners usually know; the Thorens ones don't. Also: Helvetia discs and some Symphonion 11.5 cm discs fit AD30s, so mixed stacks still count.

## Shipping / handling
Thorens box ~5 lb + discs; wrap discs separately, ~$20–25 in. Regina tabletop 40+ lb, crate – local only. eBay 13%. Remove the disc from the spindle and lock the swing arm before packing so it cannot bend comb teeth in transit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State movement maker (Thorens AD30), disc count and titles, whether it plays every note, box/instructions present, case maker and condition, dimensions. Photograph the movement label, a disc label, the comb (all teeth) and include a playing video link. Title: "Thorens AD30 disc music box + N discs, Sorrento inlay, works".

### What to tell the buyer
The Thorens AD30 is a Swiss disc music box from Sainte-Croix. It plays interchangeable 4.5-inch (11.5 cm) edge-driven steel discs, and the tune title is printed on each disc. Thorens made it from the 1940s, and after Reuge bought Thorens in 1986 Reuge made essentially the same mechanism until 2009. Name the brand stamped on the movement plate (Thorens or Reuge) and describe the case separately (for example an Italian Sorrento inlaid box). List every disc title, state whether every note sounds and whether any discs skip, and include a video of one full disc playing. Mention any rust on the underside of the discs. For an antique Regina, give the disc size, single or double comb, the case wood, the location of the logo, and the number of original discs.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Thorens AD30 + 15+ discs ≤ $100 (2026 Terapeak data says it clears comfortably only at ≤ $75), or any 10"+ disc box with discs ≤ $200 (confirm it plays). Pass when: broken comb teeth visible, no discs, or "needs work". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Regina tabletops (40+ lb) are local-only; 27" uprights are bigger than a passenger seat and excluded.
