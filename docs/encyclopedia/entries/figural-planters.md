## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ceramic planters shaped as people, animals or objects, 1940s–70s. The value tiers are clear in photos:
- **Lady head vases**: glossy porcelain woman's head/bust, painted face, applied pearl necklace/earrings, real-looking hair-curl moulding, often stiff black lashes, gloved hand at the cheek. Paper label or stamped "Napco", "Inarco E-xxxx", "Relpo", "Lefton", "Japan".
- **American art pottery figurals**: McCoy, Shawnee, Hull, Royal Copley — even airbrushed glazes, unglazed dry foot with moulded mark (McCoy "USA", Shawnee "USA 7xx").
- **Hobby/ceramics-class**: thick, chalky bisque with matte acrylic or "stain" paint, sometimes scratched initials and date underneath — 1970s–80s home ceramics (Holland Mold, Atlantic Mold). Low value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Napco, Inarco, Relpo, Lefton, Caffco, Parma, Ruebens** (Japan importers): lady, teen, baby, nun, clown head vases.
- **Shawnee (Zanesville OH, 1937–61)**: figural planters, cookie jars; **McCoy (Roseville OH)**: animal planters 1940s–60s; **Hull**: Red Riding Hood, Novelty line; **Royal Copley (Spaulding China)**: birds, Oriental figures.
- **Kitsch "granny" / Mother Hubbard utensil holders**: 1970s–80s, often hobby or Japanese/Taiwan imports.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Glossy fired glaze and crisp detail = factory; matte paint over bisque = hobby, worth little. Japanese head vases: thin porcelain, label remnants, numbered moulds.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on hair curls, lost lashes, missing earrings/necklace, paint rubs (cold paint flakes), hairline cracks from soil watering, efflorescence stains inside.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction head vases from China since 2000s (heavier, poorer facial paint, no wear). McCoy marks faked on Chinese planters.
