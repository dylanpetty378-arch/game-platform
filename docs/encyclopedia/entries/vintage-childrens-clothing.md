## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pre-1990 kids' clothing. Era tells: union labels, single-stitch hems on tees, metal snaps/zippers (Talon), bold 1970s colors, smocking.
More era tells (general knowledge, verify): no care label or a separate small care tag (care labels became US law in 1971/72); "Made in USA" with an RN number; fibre-content tag in older fonts; metal zippers (Talon, Scovill, Conmar) in 1950s–60s, nylon coil zippers from the late 1960s; polyester double-knit in the 1970s; Sears "Winnie-the-Pooh" line labels (1960s–90s; a 1980s Sears Pooh toddler sweater with tags is listed on eBay). OshKosh adopted the name "OshKosh B'Gosh" in 1937 and its children's line took off from about 1960 (≈15% of the company by 1979), so kids' OshKosh is overwhelmingly 1960s or later; 1940s–50s adult overalls had a green-outlined label and shamrock buttons; a blue "100 years" label dates to 1995 (VFG forum, citing Wikipedia).

Photo routine: neck/waist label close-up, care/content tag, zipper pull, hem stitching, full flat-lay with measurements (chest pit-to-pit and length), any stains under light.

## Makers, brands and variants
Florence Eiseman (Milwaukee, couture kids, $40–150); Feltman Brothers (heirloom); Polly Flinders (smocked $20–50); Health-tex/Carter's ($10–30); OshKosh Vestbak ($30–80); handmade ($8–25).
- **Florence Eiseman** (Milwaukee, founded 1945; first big order from Marshall Field's; Neiman Marcus Fashion Award 1955; peak mid-1980s ~$7M sales; later outsourced to El Salvador). Hallmarks: A-line shapes, pinafores buttoning to the dress front, bold appliqué (simplified later in the 1980s), coordinated sibling sets, fine European cottons and linens (later blends). Worn by the Kennedy children, Carrie Fisher, Monaco princesses; held by the Met Costume Institute, MFA Boston and Mount Mary University (Wikipedia). Older US-made Eiseman with appliqué and European cotton beats later blended imports.
- **Polly Flinders**: everyday and special-occasion smocked dresses, popular in the 1960s (Museum of Florida History); hand-smocked bodices (general knowledge, verify).
- **Feltman Brothers**: heirloom baby gowns, christening and layette (general knowledge, verify).
- **OshKosh B'Gosh**: Vestbak was the kids' bib-overall label (general knowledge, verify); kids' line grows from ~1960.
- **Health-tex, Carter's, Sears (Winnie-the-Pooh, Toughskins), JCPenney, Garanimals**: mass-market; value only in good graphics, NWT, or nostalgic lines (general knowledge, verify).
- **Kids' Levi's**: "Big E" red tab (capital E, pre-1971) on kids' jeans and jackets is the denim-collector tell (Grazia, general Levi's dating).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the label: Florence Eiseman, Feltman Brothers, Polly Flinders, OshKosh "Vestbak", Levi's Big E = look further; generic store brand = pass.
- "Made in USA" + union label (ILGWU/ACWA) = pre-1990s domestic production.
- Care tag absent = likely before early-1970s.
- Metal Talon/Scovill zipper, single-stitch tee hems, hand smocking = older.
- Appliqué, A-line, pinafore-buttoned dress in fine cotton/linen = Eiseman style even if label is hidden.
- Character prints: identify the licence and date line on the print or label (©Walt Disney Productions, Sears Pooh).
- Condition under light: yellowing at collars, fold-line fading, holes, stains.
- Lots: count labelled pieces; value is per labelled piece.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Florence Eiseman appliqué dress, US-made, natural fibre** — ~3–8× a generic vintage kids' item ($40–150 vs ~$7–13, Terapeak generic p50–p75) — Florence Eiseman Milwaukee label, bold appliqué, A-line, pinafore buttons.
- **Victorian/Edwardian white-work christening gown or Feltman Brothers heirloom gown** — ~4–10× ($40–200 tier) — long white cotton/lawn gown, lace insertions, pin-tucks, hand-stitched seams.
- **OshKosh B'Gosh "Vestbak" kids' overalls (1950s–60s)** — ~3–5× a 1980s OshKosh ($30–80 vs the $10–30 mass-brand tier) — Vestbak label, union label, metal buckles, Made in USA.
- **Kids' Levi's with Big E red tab** — multiple of later kids' Levi's (magnitude unverified) — capital "LEVI'S" on the red tab, selvedge/single-stitch details.
- **1970s licensed character graphic (Disney, Sesame Street, Star Wars, Sears Winnie-the-Pooh) NWT or crisp print** — ~2–4× ($25–80 vs ~$7–13, Terapeak generic p50–p75) — © date line on the print, single-stitch hems, original tags.
- **Polly Flinders hand-smocked dress in excellent condition** — ~2–3× ($20–50 vs ~$7–13, Terapeak generic p50–p75) — Polly Flinders label, hand-smocked bodice.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cotton/wool natural fibers; acrylic yarn = 1970s–80s handmade. Quality: hand smocking with even pleating, bound seams, covered buttons, appliqué stitched (not fused), European cotton/linen (Eiseman). Cheap: thin poly-cotton, fused appliqué, serged-only seams, printed "smocking". Old vs new: older fibre-content labels and fonts, Made in USA/union labels, metal zips; newer brand reissues and "vintage-style" reproductions carry current care labels and overseas origin.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Stains, yellowing, pilling, holes. Also: fold-line fading, rust spots from storage pins, moth holes in wool/knits, shrinkage, mended seams. Buyers of heirloom pieces tolerate faint age spotting if disclosed; character tees and play clothes need clean, uncracked prints. Ask for photos in daylight and for smells (smoke, mildew).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not significant. Brands still in business (OshKosh, Carter's, Florence Eiseman) sell new clothes in classic styles; date by label and origin, not by look.

## Market
Large supply, low prices; Etsy better for heirloom. Eiseman has a collector/gift market because of its museum and celebrity profile.

## What sellers usually miss
Eiseman labels; Vestbak tags. Also Big E tabs on kids' Levi's and © date lines on character prints.

## Shipping / handling
Poly mailer $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Measurements, label, flaws.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the label exactly as written (photograph it), tagged size and actual flat measurements (chest pit-to-pit, length, waist and inseam for bottoms), fibre content, country of manufacture, closures (metal zipper, snaps, buttons), and era with the reason ("Made in USA, no care label, metal Talon zipper"). For Eiseman, mention the appliqué and fabric; for heirloom gowns, length and lace. List every stain, hole or repair and whether it has been washed. Say "handmade" rather than inventing a maker.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$6 out − ~$6 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Singles never qualify.
