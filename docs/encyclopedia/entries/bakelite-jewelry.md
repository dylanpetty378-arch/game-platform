## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast phenolic resin (Catalin, Marblette, Prystal — "Bakelite" in trade usage) jewelry of the 1925-1945 era: bangles (plain, spacer, carved, hinged, "clamper"), brooches (figural, geometric, fruit dangles on chains), dress clips, earrings, rings, necklaces, buckles and buttons. Colours were cast bright and have oxidised: white → cream/custard, clear → amber "apple juice", pale yellow → butterscotch, pale green → "creamed spinach"/olive, blue → grey-green, translucent red → deep cherry, pink → salmon. The surface has a deep, waxy, slightly cloudy depth; carving is hand-tooled with crisp inner corners and tool chatter; edges are rounded by polishing; the inside of a bangle is often slightly rough from the lathe. No seams or mould lines (cast rods were turned and carved). Heavy for size: a 1/2-inch bangle weighs 25-40 g and clacks with a dull "clunk". Brooches have a rolled-steel or brass safety pin riveted into the back, often with a brass "C" catch. Title words: Bakelite, Catalin, apple juice, butterscotch, carved, tested, "Simichrome tested", "hot water tested", dangle, cherries, art deco. Look-alikes: Lucite (acrylic; crystal-clear, light, no patina, often bright primary colours or glitter), celluloid (very thin, light, camphor smell, cracks/crazing, ivory-grained "French ivory"), Galalith (casein; matte, chalky, often marbled, 1920s-30s European), resin/"Fakelite" (1990s-2020s reproductions, uniformly bright, moulded, light), and modern Indian/Chinese "Bakelite style" bangles.

## Makers, brands and variants
Bakelite jewelry is almost never signed; value lives in form, colour, carving depth and construction. The "makers" that matter:

**Period (1925-1945) production.** Catalin Corporation (NY), Marblette, Bakelite Corp. supplied rod/sheet; jewelry was carved by anonymous shops and sold through dime stores and department stores; some retailer-labelled pieces (Saks, Bonwit Teller) and designer-attributed (Belle Kogan, Martha Sleeper figurals — Sleeper's whimsical pins $300-1,500). Spacer bangles (1/8-1/4 in thin bangles sold in sets) are the commonest form: $10-30 each, $8-15 for cream/custard, $15-40 for apple juice and marbled, up to $50 for translucent red/cherry (estimates). Plain wide bangles $25-60; carved bangles $50-200 by depth (shallow "pinwheel" vs deep "pineapple", "rope", "floral" carving); hinged (clamper) bangles $100-400; "gum drop" and "dot" bangles (inlaid contrasting dots) $300-1,500; laminated two/three-colour bangles $150-800; reverse-carved and painted apple-juice bangles/pins $150-600; "Philadelphia" bracelet (a wide, multi-coloured geometric-inlay hinged bangle, so named after a 1980s find) $1,500-6,000; "end-of-day" (swirled multicolour from the last pour) $200-1,000; cobalt blue, true bright red (not oxidised), lavender and black-and-white laminates at the top. Figural brooches: Scotties, horse heads, hands, hearts, Bakelite "dangle" pins (a bar with cherries, fruit, dice, hearts or acorns on brass chain) $150-800, with cherry dangles the most faked. Fruit necklaces (cherries/bananas/grapes on celluloid chain) $400-1,500. Buttons and buckles $5-40.

**Modern makers (1990s-2020s).** Shultz (Ron and Ester Shultz) — new laminated/dot/carved pieces from old Bakelite stock, signed "Shultz" and dated; $300-2,000 and rising, legitimate and collected. "Fakelite" (Chinese/Indian resin, 1990s-2020s): bright, uniform, light, moulded, smells of plastic when hot; $3-15. Modern Indian "Bakelite" bangles sold at bead shows: resin.

**Other early plastics (so you don't overpay).** Celluloid (1870s-1940s): thin, light, often with rhinestones; collectible but $10-60. Galalith (casein, 1900-1940): matte, European Deco; $20-150. Lucite (1937-): clear/confetti/"moonglow" 1950s-60s; $5-40, more for Lea Stein-style (actually cellulose acetate, $60-150) or Alexis Bittar signed ($80-300).

### Quick ID checklist
1. Colour family: oxidised custard/butterscotch/apple-juice/olive/salmon tones, never crystal-clear or neon.
2. Hot-water test: run under hot tap 20-30 s, smell — formaldehyde/"sick-sweet" chemical = phenolic (Wikipedia: acrid, sweet or fishy odour when rubbed/heated); camphor/mothballs = celluloid; no smell/plastic = Lucite/resin.
3. Simichrome (or 409/Scrubbing Bubbles) on a cotton swab turns yellow on Bakelite (black pieces may fail).
4. Weight and sound: dense, clunks; two bangles knocked together give a dull "clack", not a glassy "tick".
5. No mould seams; tool marks inside carving; lathe lines inside the bangle; pin backs riveted, not glued.
6. Patina: tiny scratches with softened edges, an amber "skin" where a chip reveals a lighter interior.
7. For dangles: brass chain, period "C" catch or rollover, jump rings hand-closed; cherries in oxidised cherry-red, slightly translucent, not glassy.

### Golden-egg tells
- Cherry/fruit dangle brooch with 5-8 carved Bakelite cherries on brass chain from a carved bar or log — $150-800 vs $10-25 for a one-piece Lucite/resin cherry pin — each cherry is a separate turned ball with a drilled hole, hanging on chain; a flat moulded cluster is not it.
- "Philadelphia" bracelet (wide hinged bangle with multicolour geometric inlays) — $1,500-6,000 vs $40 for a plain hinged bangle — stepped inlaid blocks of 3-5 colours with a hinge and clasp.
- Dot ("gum drop"/"polka-dot") and laminated bangles — $300-1,500 vs $25-60 plain — contrasting round dots drilled and plugged through the bangle wall, or visible lamination bands of two or three colours along the edge.
- Deep "pineapple"/"rope"/floral carved bangles ≥ 1 in wide — $100-400 vs $25-60 shallow pinwheel — carving depth that casts shadows, crisp hand-cut facets.
- Reverse-carved and painted apple-juice (clear amber) pieces — $150-600 vs $15-40 plain apple-juice — flowers/fish carved into the back and painted, seen through the amber front.
- End-of-day multicolour swirl (mixed colours in one piece, not marbling of one colour) — $200-1,000 — random blocks/veins of 3+ distinct colours.
- Cobalt blue, true bright red, lavender/purple, black-and-white laminates — 3-10× the same form in butterscotch — colour alone; cobalt and lavender hardly oxidise and are rare.
- "Shultz" signed (scratched signature + date on the inside) modern laminated pieces — $300-2,000 — look inside the bangle for the engraved name.
- Martha Sleeper figural pins (whimsical animals/objects, often with wood or chain), and 1930s Art Deco geometric clips with chrome — $300-1,500 vs $30-80 anonymous figurals — complex multi-part figurals with metal findings and painted details.
- A full stack of 8-12 matched spacer bangles in apple-juice/butterscotch/custard — $60-150 for the set vs $10-15 each — sold as a stack ("stacking set") they fetch more than singly.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Phenolic (Bakelite/Catalin): cast in rods and sheets, dense (1.3 g/cm³), takes a high polish, oxidises from the surface in over decades (so the colour on a chip's fresh face is paler than the surface), will not scratch with a fingernail, sinks in water, and has no "cold" feel — it warms in the hand. Moulded Bakelite (filled, dark brown/black) is for radios and handles, rarely jewelry. Catalin was the lighter-colour cast version; Prystal the clear. Celluloid: thin, flexible, light, camphor smell, prone to crazing and "celluloid rot". Galalith: casein, matte, often has a fibrous look, swells in water. Lucite: crystal clear, light, modern; "moonglow" Lucite has a glowing layered look. Resin (modern): light, often with tiny bubbles, bright uniform colour, moulded seams, glued findings, smells of styrene when hot. Quality tells for period work: hand carving with depth and variation; polished (not buffed-soft) edges; brass or steel findings with age; no glue. Cheap/new: moulded relief with soft detail, seams, glued pins, unnaturally even colour.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common: chips at the edges (visible as paler fresh resin), cracks (phenolic cracks through on impact), heat damage (whitish bloom or warping), heavy scratches, dulled surface from dishwasher/cleaners, loose or replaced pin backs, missing dangles or replaced chain (new brass is bright), repaired hinges on clampers, shrunken/sprung hinges. Value: a chip on a plain bangle −30-50%; cracks −70%; replaced dangles −50%; a re-polished surface is accepted if the carving is intact. Acceptable: light surface scratches, oxidation (that is the patina buyers want). Ask: has it been tested and how; any chips/cracks; inside diameter (2.5 in standard, under 2.4 in is "child/small" and sells for less); for dangles whether all drops are original.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
"Fakelite" reproductions of cherries, dangles, dot bangles, Scottie pins and Philadelphia bracelets have been made since the 1990s in resin and even in new-cast phenolic (which passes Simichrome — the test is not definitive for new phenolic). Tells for repros: bright, un-oxidised colour (white is white, not custard), uniform machine carving, moulded relief, light weight, new findings with modern safety catches, glue, chains that are too shiny, and cherry pins that are a single moulded piece. Married pieces: old bangles re-carved (legitimate if disclosed), old bars with new dangles. Lucite copies of spacer bangles: crystal-clear with bright colour and no weight. Rule: untested "Bakelite?" is Lucite until proven; the seller's hot-water or Simichrome test (with a described result) is required before paying phenolic prices.

## Market
Large and steady: hundreds of "tested Bakelite" pieces sell on eBay US weekly; sell-through moderate for plain bangles (many actives, 2-4 weeks), fast (days) for carved, dot, figural and dangle pieces; Etsy and Ruby Lane are the retail venues; specialist dealers and the Bakelite collector community (Facebook groups) pay the most for rare colours. Buyers: collectors (colour/forms), vintage-fashion wearers, Deco decorators. Seasonality: cherries and red/green pieces spike November-December; summer for fruit. Trend: stable since the 2000s peak; top pieces still rising, plain bangles flat.

## What sellers usually miss
That it is Bakelite at all ("vintage plastic bracelets"), the test, the colour names (apple juice, butterscotch, creamed spinach) that drive searches, carving depth, whether a bangle is laminated or dotted, the inside diameter, and that a pile of spacers sells better as a matched stack. Conversely sellers over-call Lucite and resin "Bakelite" — the price asked tells you which mistake you are looking at.

## Shipping / handling
Light (bangles 1-2 oz each, a stack under 1 lb); USPS First Class/Ground Advantage $5-8 in a small box with each piece wrapped (bangles chip each other). Phenolic cracks if dropped on a hard surface and blooms if left in a hot car — pack well, avoid heat. Dangle pins: wrap each dangle, pin the catch. Fits in a pocket.

## Buy rule
Buy when: the seller confirms a hot-water or Simichrome test with the expected result (or the photos show deep hand carving, oxidised colour and period findings beyond doubt), and the form is carved/dot/laminated/hinged/dangle/figural or a matched stack of ≥ 8 spacers, at ≤ 30% of the conservative sold value, with profit ≥ max($50, price) after 13% fees and ~$8 shipping each way. Pass when: untested "Bakelite?" bangles above $10 total, anything crystal-clear or neon, single plain bangles (resale $10-30 cannot clear $50 profit), chipped/cracked pieces, cherry dangles without a test and detailed back photos, and repro-risk forms (Philadelphia, dots) above $100 without a dealer's provenance. Size never an issue.
