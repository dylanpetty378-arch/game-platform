## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Portable wick lamps with a glass globe inside a wire guard, a fuel fount (base) and air tubes (tubular lanterns) on each side. **Cold blast**: an air chamber atop the globe feeds fresh air down the tubes; **hot blast**: recycles some exhaust; **dead flame**: older, no tubes. **Railroad lanterns**: tall bell-bottom or "fixed globe" types by Adlake, Handlan, Dressel, Armspear, with RR initials stamped into the top or frame (e.g. "N.Y.C.R.R.", "B&O", "PRR"). Globes may be clear, red, green, amber, blue, cast or etched with RR name.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Dietz (R.E. Dietz, NY, 1840–present; China production since ~1990s)**: models No. 2 Blizzard, Monarch, Little Wizard, D-Lite, Junior, Air Pilot, Vesta, Comet. Patent dates on the tubes/top date them; "Made in USA" vs "Made in China" or "Hong Kong" is the key old/new tell.
- **Embury (Warsaw NY, 1908–1953)**: Air Pilot, Supreme, Little Air Pilot; often brass founts on early ones.
- **C.T. Ham (Rochester NY)**: barn & street lanterns.
- **Adlake / Adams & Westlake, Handlan, Dressel, Armspear**: railroad, marine, switch lamps.
- **Feuerhand (Germany)**, **Hurricane / Paull's**, **Defiance**, **Perkins Marine**: also collected.
- **Post-1960 imports** (Taiwan, Hong Kong, China, "Dietz" new): $10–30.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Tin-plated/painted steel; brass on better models and early founts. Embossed maker names on globes ("DIETZ", "EMBURY") help ID. Original paint (red, blue, green, nickel) adds over rust. Electric conversions (bulb socket in the burner) lose most collector value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust-through founts, dented tubes, cracked/replaced globes (replacement globes widely sold), missing burner, electric conversion, repaints. Mismatched globe is common: check the globe's embossed name matches.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction railroad globes and lanterns with fake RR markings are common (especially "Santa Fe", "Union Pacific" casts). New Dietz from China. Fake brass-plating. RR names stamped crisply on a clean top on an otherwise weak lantern are suspect.

## Market
Large and steady; hundreds sell monthly. Plain barn lanterns are commodity decor. Railroad lanterns have a dedicated, informed collector base.

## What sellers usually miss
RR initials stamped into the top or bell-bottom are easy to miss; small Embury/Dietz models in brass; colored globes. Sellers overvalue ordinary barn lanterns ("antique").

## Shipping / handling
Globe is fragile: wrap separately. Box 12×12×18; $20–30 each. Remove fuel; ship dry.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph globe markings, patent dates on tubes, base stampings, burner. State "not tested with fuel" unless tested and whether electrified.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$25 out − ~$20 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Common barn lantern ($50 resale) never qualifies. Railroad-marked at $200 resale: buy at ≤ $65 (Tier A ≤ $40). Pass on electrified.
