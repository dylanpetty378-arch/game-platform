## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand lanterns powered by a 6-volt square battery or D cells, with a spotlight reflector and sometimes a top dome light.
Railroad versions (brakeman/conductor/trainman) have a bail handle, a domed top lamp plus front headlight, and are often
stamped with a railroad name (e.g. "PRR", "Santa Fe", "UP", "N&W") on the base or handle. Traditional trainman lanterns
light one bulb at a time: one for hand signals and one with a focusing reflector for spot use (RYPN). Electric railroad
lanterns appeared in the early 1900s, while kerosene lanterns lingered into the 1970s (RYPN), so "railroad lantern" in a
title may mean either kind. Delta Electric (Marion, IN) made Powerlite and Buddy lanterns, often painted in
blue/green/red enamel; Delta pieces may carry a maker's sticker rather than a stamp (EBTH). A typical Delta tin lantern
measures about 7" W x 8.5" H x 4.5" D (EBTH).

Where marks are: maker name stamped/embossed on the body, base or switch plate, or a paper/foil sticker; railroad
initials stamped on the base, body or handle; patent numbers stamped on later Congers (RYPN); battery-compartment
instructions printed inside the door (Winchester, per Artisera).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Delta Electric Co. (Marion IN)**: formed 1913 in Marion after moving from an existing Chicago business; bought by
  Novo Industrial (1964 or 1965), part of United Filtration from 1968, acquired by Halle Industries 1977, production shut
  down March 31, 1978 (Wikipedia). Made lanterns, flashlights, bicycle and auto lighting. Lantern models: Powerlite,
  Buddy, Hot Shot, Railroad Special, Juniorlite (two-light lamp with handle, c.1930s–40s, Laurel Leaf Farm). Tin bodies
  with clear and red plastic lenses and multi-position switches (EBTH). (Earlier entry text gave "1906–1950s"; corrected
  to 1913–1978 per Wikipedia.)
- **Star Headlight & Lantern Co. (Rochester NY)**: railroad electric lanterns, "Star" switch with chrome headlight; later
  Lexan-bodied lanterns — orange model 222 (screw-base bulbs like early Congers), yellow 292 (flange bulbs); latest
  models use LEDs (RYPN). Plastic Star lanterns are working tools, low collector value (general knowledge, verify).
- **Conger Lantern Co.**: early single-bulb Conger with wooden slide switch (later replaced with plastic); a
  "patent pending" version with different stamped font and shorter guard struts is earlier; stainless redesigned models
  stamped with a patent number after 1953; Star Headlight is believed to have bought Conger (RYPN, collector opinion).
- **Justrite (Chicago)**: best known for mining lamps; also made a railroad model (RYPN).
- **Adlake**: electric versions vary mostly in handle shape; patent-pending model differs in guard and reflector (RYPN).
- **Economy Electric ("Eco-Lite")**: railroad lantern with a wood handle (RYPN).
- **Bright Star, Eveready, Burgess, Winchester (1920s flashlights/lanterns)**: Winchester-marked are collector items.
  Winchester also sold a "Twin-Service Headlight Lantern" (metal and glass, 4 x 5 x 6.5", chrome reflector, head strap,
  logo buckle on the rear door, five D cells, boxed in blue and red) that one dealer dates to the 1950s (Artisera).
- **Modern look-alikes**: Rail-Tek all-LED lanterns (RYPN) and Barebones Living LED "railroad lanterns" (appear in the
  Terapeak results below) — not vintage.

Value movers: railroad marking (and which railroad), maker (Winchester, early Delta tin litho, early Conger), metal vs
plastic body, original glass lens with maker name, original box, working condition, no corrosion.

### Quick ID checklist
- Body: steel/tin or nickel-plated brass (older) vs Lexan/plastic (later Star 222/292) vs LED modern.
- Railroad initials stamped on base, body or handle? Photograph them; check the font looks period-stamped, not engraved.
- Maker mark: embossed/stamped name, sticker (Delta), printed battery-door instructions (Winchester).
- Switch type: wooden slide switch (early Conger) vs plastic switch.
- Patent stamp vs "patent pending" (Conger: patent-pending = earlier).
- Lenses: glass with maker name (better) vs plastic; red top dome lens present?
- Inside battery compartment: corrosion (green/white crust), original springs and contacts.
- Handle: wooden grip, bail, gooseneck; owner initials scratched on the body are common (RYPN) and not railroad marks.
- Battery format: 6-volt square lantern battery vs D cells.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Railroad initials, heavy nickel-plated brass bodies, glass lenses with maker names = collectible. Thin steel, plastic
lenses, 1970s Rayovac/Eveready = low. Painted enamel finishes (blue, green, red) on Delta lanterns: original paint with
even wear beats repaint (general knowledge, verify). All the classic trainman lanterns run on a standard 6-volt
square-base lantern battery (≈$4 at big-box stores), and adapters allow D cells (RYPN) — so a working test is cheap.
Owner modifications are common: replacement nuts on handle bolts, initials scratched or written on the body, taped or
bent handles (RYPN).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Battery acid corrosion inside (common, seen as green/white crust), missing reflectors, cracked lenses, broken switches,
paint loss. Also replaced switches (wood to plastic on Congers), missing guard struts, missing red dome lens, rewired
bulb sockets. Ask for: a photo inside the battery compartment, a photo lit with a fresh battery, and close-ups of any
stamping.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake railroad stampings added to common lanterns occur — look for crisp, period-appropriate stamping fonts. Not widely
reproduced otherwise. Modern LED "railroad lanterns" (Barebones Living, Rail-Tek) are sold alongside vintage ones and
pollute sold searches. Scratched owner initials are not railroad markings.

## Market
Railroadiana collectors are steady; common lanterns are decor buys; 30–60 days. Railroadiana collectors pay for the
railroad name; general buyers pay for looks (colour, chrome) and working condition.

## What sellers usually miss
RR markings stamped under the base; Winchester branding. Also: early Conger features (wooden switch, patent-pending
stamp); original boxes; a working light (most sellers list untested).

## Shipping / handling
2–4 lb each, bulky; $12–18 each. Never ship with a battery installed; pad the lens and dome.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Markings, whether it lights (with battery), lens and reflector condition, interior corrosion.

### What to tell the buyer
"Vintage [maker] [model] battery lantern, [6-volt lantern battery / D-cell], c.[era; Delta Electric of Marion, Indiana
operated 1913–1978]. Markings: [stamp/sticker text; railroad initials if present, photographed]. Tested: [lights on
both settings with a fresh battery / untested]. Battery compartment: [clean / light corrosion]. Lenses: [glass/plastic,
cracks]. [W x H x D]. Battery not included." Only say "railroad" if a railroad marking is visible; otherwise use
"railroad-style".

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 ship to buyer − ~$12 ship to Dylan per lantern − price ≥ max($50, price). Common lanterns fail. Railroad-marked lanterns only at ≤ $20 each.
