## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand lanterns powered by a 6-volt square battery or D cells, with a spotlight reflector and sometimes a top dome light. Railroad versions (brakeman/conductor) have a bail handle, a domed top lamp plus front headlight, and are often stamped with a railroad name (e.g. "PRR", "Santa Fe", "UP", "N&W") on the base or handle. Delta Electric (Marion, IN) made Powerlite and Buddy lanterns, often painted in blue/green/red enamel.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Delta Electric Co. (Marion IN, 1906–1950s)**: Powerlite, Buddy, Hot Shot, Railroad Special.
- **Star Headlight & Lantern Co. (Rochester NY)**: railroad electric lanterns, "Star" switch with chrome headlight.
- **Conger Lantern Co., Justrite (Chicago)**: railroad and mining lanterns.
- **Bright Star, Eveready, Burgess, Winchester (1920s flashlights/lanterns)**: Winchester-marked are collector items.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Railroad initials, heavy nickel-plated brass bodies, glass lenses with maker names = collectible. Thin steel, plastic lenses, 1970s Rayovac/Eveready = low.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Battery acid corrosion inside (common, seen as green/white crust), missing reflectors, cracked lenses, broken switches, paint loss.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake railroad stampings added to common lanterns occur — look for crisp, period-appropriate stamping fonts. Not widely reproduced otherwise.
