## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Dial and caseback are the whole story. Small (30–36 mm) gold-filled or steel cases, manual wind or automatic. Brand on
dial; the movement and case-back stamps ("10K RGP", "14K", "Swiss", movement calibre) decide value. Craigslist sellers
price from the brand name alone. Tells:
- **American mid-century (Elgin, Bulova, Hamilton, Gruen, Benrus, Waltham, Wittnauer)** 1940s–60s: 10K gold-filled or
  rolled-gold-plate cases, 17–23 jewels, small (31–34 mm). Running men's examples **$40–120**; "diamond dial"
  Elgin/Bulova (tiny chips at the markers) $60–150; **Hamilton Electric** (Ventura, Pacer, Altair – asymmetric cases)
  $300–1,500; **Bulova Accutron** tuning fork (Spaceview skeleton dial) $150–500; **Hamilton 992B/railroad pocket
  watches** $250–500. Solid **14K gold** cases: melt value (~$300–600) sets the floor – look for "14K" not "14K GF/RGP".
- **Swiss**: Tissot PR50/PR100 quartz $50–100; Tissot/Omega/Longines mechanical 1950s–60s $150–600 (Omega
  Seamaster/Constellation $500–2,000); **Rolex/Tudor** at any CL price = authenticate (dial, caseback, reference, serial
  between lugs).
- **Japanese**: Seiko 6139/6138 chronographs ($250–600), 6105/6309 divers ($400–1,500), Seiko 5 ($30–80), Grand
  Seiko/King Seiko ($500–2,000); Citizen Eco-Drive Skyhawk/Promaster $120–250; Orient $50–120.
- **Fashion/junk**: Invicta (any model) $20–60 regardless of "retail $495"; Fossil, Guess, MK, Nixon $15–60; Swatch
  standard $15–40 (early 1980s rare models and Pop Swatch $40–100; Kiki Picasso/art specials $500+); Phillip Stein
  $60–150; "Railmaster homage" / Steeldive / Pagani $40–80.
- **Military**: WWII A-11 (Elgin/Bulova/Waltham) $150–400; Vietnam-era Benrus/Hamilton GG-W-113 $300–600;
  Rolex/Panerai/Omega military – auction.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bulova**: dress watches (common), Accutron 214/218 tuning fork, Spaceview (skeleton dial, collectible), A-11 military.
  Bulova date codes on the caseback/movement (letter + digit) date the watch (general knowledge, verify).
- **Elgin, Waltham, Gruen, Benrus, Wittnauer**: mostly GF dress watches; military A-11 and GG-W-113 versions collectible.
- **Hamilton**: Electric (Ventura, Pacer, Altair), Thinline, 987A WWII military, railroad 992B pocket watches.
- **Omega**: Seamaster, Constellation (pie-pan dials), Speedmaster (top). **Longines**, **Zenith**, **Universal Genève**,
  **Heuer** (Autavia, Carrera), **Patek** (top).
- **Rolex/Tudor**: Oyster Precision (collectible), Submariner/GMT (top).
- **Seiko**: 5 (decent), 6139/6138 chrono (chronograph pushers are the tell), 6105/6309 divers, Grand/King Seiko.
- **Citizen**, **Orient**, **Tissot** quartz — decent at best.
- **Fashion**: Invicta, Fossil, Guess, MK, Nixon, Phillip Stein, Swatch (Pop Swatch and early/art specials excepted).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid gold (14K/18K stamped inside caseback, no "GF/RGP/plated") vs gold-filled/rolled-gold-plate (10K GF, 10K RGP) vs
base metal/steel. Original dials (no refinish — refinished dials have crisp, sometimes misspelled or misaligned print)
matter most to collectors. Jewel count and movement calibre (photo of movement) identify grade. Wear-through on GF cases
at the lugs and edges is common (general knowledge, verify on dial-refinish tells).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Non-running = pass (Dylan: no repair; a service costs $150–300). Common issues: broken mainspring/balance, water damage,
refinished or "redial" dials, replaced crowns and crystals, worn GF plating, aftermarket bands, missing Accutron
batteries (needs a special cell). Ask for a video of it running, photos of the caseback inside and the movement.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Rolex, Omega, Heuer and Seiko fakes and "Frankenwatches" (mixed parts, redialed Seikos) are widespread; homage brands
(Steeldive, Pagani) copy famous designs. Authenticate reference/serial, movement and dial before any premium purchase
(general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Very liquid on eBay; Chrono24 for Collectible+. Buyers are vintage-watch enthusiasts (mechanical, military, Seiko
modders) and gift buyers. GF dress watches are oversupplied; Seiko chronographs/divers and Omega have strong demand.
Q4 gift season lift (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Solid gold vs gold-filled (14K stamp inside caseback), Accutron tuning-fork movement in a plain Bulova, Hamilton Electric
asymmetric cases listed as "old Hamilton", Seiko chronograph references (chronograph pushers on Seikos). Model names
(Accutron, Spaceview, Seamaster, Electric) and military dial markings. "Vintage men's watch – see pictures" with no brand
is where the finds are. Conversely, sellers of Invicta/Phillip Stein always overprice vs retail.

## Shipping / handling
Tiny: ounces, $6–10 each way ($8 insured). eBay 13% (or Chrono24 for Collectible+). eBay authenticity guarantee kicks in
over $2,000. Non-running = pass (Dylan: no repair).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model/reference, year (date code/serial), case material and stamps (14K vs 10K GF), case size without
crown, movement calibre and jewels, running status and accuracy, service history, dial originality, band. Photograph dial,
caseback inside and out, movement, crown, and lugs. Title: maker, model, reference, calibre, year, case material,
"running", "serviced".

## Buy rule
Merged from both write-ups:
- Buy when: Accutron/Hamilton Electric/Seiko 6139/Omega/Longines mechanical under $75 and described as running; solid
  14K case under melt; WWII A-11 under $60; or Accutron/Seiko chrono/Omega/Longines/military-marked at ≤ 30% of tier,
  running.
- Pass when: gold-filled Elgin/Bulova/Hamilton over $40 (second write-up: generic 1950s Bulova/Elgin over $60), anything
  "not running", Invicta/Phillip Stein/Nixon/Citizen/Tissot quartz at any price (no spread), "law enforcement" or quartz
  novelty watches.
- Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat.
