## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Cotton, linen or silk squares, 10–16 in. Collectible eras: 1930s–1960s printed cottons and linens, plus earlier (pre-1930) fine whitework and lace.
- **Signed designer prints:** the designer's name is printed (screen-printed, part of the design) in one corner, usually small script near the hem: "Tammis Keefe", "Peg Thomas", "Pat Prichard", "Burmel", "Faith Austin", "Carol Stanley", "Franshaw", "Jeanne Hamm". Photograph and zoom all four corners; sellers often fold the hanky so the signature is hidden.
- Tammis Keefe squares are usually 14–15 in., silkscreened, with hand-rolled edges (dieworkwear.com).
- **Souvenir hankies** print fair buildings, logos and dates ("A Century of Progress 1933–34 Chicago"; 1939 New York World's Fair Trylon and Perisphere), state maps, cities, tourist sites and museums (e.g. Henry Ford Museum souvenir hanky, 1952–56).
- **WWII sweetheart hankies:** military insignia, "Remember me" / "Mother" / "Sweetheart" verses, camp names (general knowledge, verify).
- **Whitework/lace:** white or ecru linen with drawn-thread work, embroidered initials, needle or bobbin lace borders (Brussels/Belgian, Irish, Appenzell from Switzerland), often a wedding hanky.
- **Marghab:** very fine hand-embroidered Irish linen or "Margandie" (Swiss organdy) worked on Madeira, 1933–1980 (Wikipedia). Look for minute, dense satin stitch and shadow-work; Marghab pieces are often found with paper labels or stamped tags (marking method not confirmed by sources, verify).
- Title words sellers use: "hanky", "hankie", "MCM", "signed", "lot", "souvenir", "wedding", "lace", "Madeira", "hand rolled".
- Look-alikes: modern printed bandanas (square, cotton, but bandana paisley prints and machine-hemmed), dinner napkins (larger, heavier), scarves (silk/rayon, 20 in.+).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Tammis Keefe (1913–1960)**: Los Angeles-born textile designer (Chouinard, Disney, then Dorothy Liebes Studio by 1948). Made hundreds of handkerchief designs between 1944 and 1960; manufactured for J.H. Kimball, first sold through Lord & Taylor. Bright, whimsical prints: animals, cities, maps, holidays, food, roosters. Signed "Tammis Keefe" in a corner; some signed **"Peg Thomas"**, a line Keefe made for Kimball to sell to stores that could not use her name (dieworkwear.com; Wikipedia). $10–40 each. Her work is held by the Met, Cooper Hewitt and FIT. Michael Miller Fabrics reissued some of her prints as yardage in 2013 (not hankies; a lookalike risk in craft items).
- **Kimball (J.H. Kimball)**: a manufacturer/converter, not a designer; it commissioned Keefe and ran the "Peg Thomas" line. Old entry listed Kimball among the designers; treat a "Kimball" corner label as a brand, not a signature (correction).
- **Burmel, Pat Prichard, Carol Stanley, Faith Austin, Franshaw, Jeanne Hamm**: signed 1940s–60s printed designer hankies, $8–25 (general knowledge, verify).
- **"Jeanne Lanvin-inspired"**: listed in the old entry title; no source found; treat as unverified.
- **World's Fair**: 1933–34 Chicago Century of Progress and 1939–40 New York $15–50; 1904 St. Louis (earlier, often linen or silk) $40–120 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Lace/whitework**: Appenzell (Swiss, extremely fine hand embroidery), Brussels lace, Marghab (Madeira) $30–150.
- **Marghab Linens (1933–1980)**: founded by Emile and Vera Marghab; hand-embroidered on Madeira on Irish linen and Swiss "Margandie"; embroiderers paid per stitch. The South Dakota Art Museum holds 300+ patterns.

### Quick ID checklist
- Zoom every corner for a printed signature (Tammis Keefe / Peg Thomas / Pat Prichard / Burmel / Franshaw / Faith Austin). No signature = mixed-lot value.
- Edge: hand-rolled hem (soft rolled edge, tiny slanted stitches) vs machine-overlocked or straight-stitched hem.
- Size: designer hankies 14–15 in.; wedding/lace hankies often 10–12 in.
- Look for a date or event name printed in the design (1933, 1934, 1939, 1940, 1904).
- Lace: is it a real needle or bobbin lace border (irregular, raised) or flat machine lace?
- Embroidery: is it hand satin stitch (raised, dense, slight irregularity) or machine embroidery (flat, uniform, thread jumps on the back)? Ask for a back-side photo.
- Paper tags, foil labels or a "Madeira", "Hand made", "Swiss", "Irish Linen" sticker add credibility.
- Count the lot and look for any matching/duplicated designs (duplicates suggest dealer stock).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Tammis Keefe signed print** — ~3–6× a common printed hanky ($10–40 vs $2–6) — "Tammis Keefe" script printed in a corner; bright, flat-colour whimsical screen print.
- **"Peg Thomas" signed print** — Keefe-designed, same family of value (verify; usually a little under Keefe-signed) — "Peg Thomas" printed in a corner; sellers rarely know it is Keefe.
- **1933–34 Century of Progress / 1939–40 NY World's Fair souvenir** — ~5–10× a plain hanky ($15–50) — fair name, Trylon & Perisphere or Hall of Science printed, dates in the border.
- **1904 St. Louis World's Fair (Louisiana Purchase Exposition)** — ~$40–120, 10×+ a plain hanky — date 1904, often linen/silk, sepia or monochrome printing.
- **Marghab (Madeira) hand-embroidered linen/organdy** — ~$30–150 vs $2–6 — extremely fine white or pastel satin stitch on sheer organdy or fine linen, often with a paper label.
- **Appenzell hand embroidery / Brussels needle lace** — ~$30–150 — near-invisible stitches, tiny raised florals, lace border with irregular hand-made mesh.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Hand-rolled hems on quality; machine overlocked hems on cheap. Signatures and corner labels. Colour fastness.
- Fabrics: fine linen (crisp, slubby, slightly lustrous), cotton lawn (soft, fine), silk (sheen, often souvenir/WWII), rayon (1940s–50s, slightly shiny, can shrink).
- Printing: screen print (flat solid colour areas, slight registration offset) is period; digital print (photo-like gradients, very even colour) is modern.
- Old vs new: period hankies show fold-line ageing, slight yellowing and softness; modern reprints are crisp, bright white and often polyester-blend.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Stains, foxing (brown spots), holes, fold-line yellowing; washing can remove.
- Colour bleeding on reds after washing kills a print's value.
- Lace tears and pulled threads in drawn-thread work are hard to fix; mended lace is visible as uneven thread.
- Ask: smoke or musty smell, any holes, have they been washed, are the stains on fold lines only.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Few; modern Chinese reprints of 1950s designs exist.
- Keefe prints were reissued as fabric yardage (Michael Miller, 2013); items cut and hemmed from yardage are not period hankies (machine hems, no aged fold lines).
- Machine "Battenberg" and Chinese embroidered "wedding hankies" are sold as vintage lace; they show machine tape lace and uniform embroidery.

## Market
- Steady, low-value craft and collector market; many quilters buy lots.
- Menswear buyers also buy Keefe squares as pocket squares (dieworkwear.com).
- Designer and souvenir hankies sell individually on eBay/Etsy; mixed lots sell to crafters.

## What sellers usually miss
- Signatures in corners; World's Fair dates.
- "Peg Thomas" = Tammis Keefe.
- Hand-rolled hems and Madeira/Marghab embroidery in "old white hankies" lots.

## Shipping / handling
Flat envelope $4–6.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Each hanky photographed flat, dimensions, signature, stains.
- Photograph the signature corner close-up, the hem, and the back of any embroidery.
- Title keywords: designer name, "signed", "hand rolled", "linen", subject (rooster, map, Christmas), event and year.

### What to tell the buyer
"Vintage signed [designer] handkerchief, about [14 × 14] in., [cotton/linen], screen-printed with the designer's signature in the corner and a hand-rolled hem. Tammis Keefe (1913–1960) designed hundreds of handkerchiefs between 1944 and 1960, made by J.H. Kimball; some carry her 'Peg Thomas' signature instead. Condition: [no holes / light fold-line toning / small spot at corner — shown in photos]. Not washed by me." For souvenirs: name the event and year as printed. For Marghab/Madeira: "hand-embroidered on Madeira; Marghab Linens worked 1933–1980 on Irish linen and Swiss organdy."
