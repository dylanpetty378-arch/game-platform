## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
American pressed and mould-blown art glass from Williamstown, WV (1905–2011; small-scale 2011–2017). Typical shapes: ruffled/crimped vases, baskets with applied handles, fairy lamps, candle bowls, epergnes, animal figures, lamps.
Photo tells:
- **Marks**: oval "Fenton" moulded in the base from 1970 (with decade digit added: "8" under the logo 1980s, "9" 1990s, "0" 2000s); script "F" in an oval for pieces made from Verlys/other moulds (1983+). Paper oval labels "Fenton Handpainted in USA" with script F (1980s–2000s).
- **Hand-painted**: decorator's signature on the base (e.g., "S. Kibbe", "D. Barbour") plus a Fenton family signature on some special issues (Bill, Frank, Shelley, George, Don Fenton) which adds little.
- **Hobnail**: raised dots; opalescent (white-tipped) and cranberry hobnail are wanted; amber, colonial blue, colonial green 1960s–70s hobnail is common.
- **Crest**: Silver Crest (white with clear edge, very common), Peach Crest, Aqua Crest, Emerald Crest.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Fenton Art Glass Co.** (1905–2011). Lines: carnival glass (1907–1920s and reissued 1970+), stretch glass (1920s), Karnak/Mosaic/Hanging Hearts off-hand art glass (1925–26, rare), opalescent hobnail (1939+), crests (1940s–), milk glass (1950s–80s, common), Burmese (1970+), Rosalene (1976+), Custard (painted), Connoisseur Collection (1979–2000s, limited editions), QVC exclusives (1990s–2000s), Mary Gregory style enamelled.
- **Look-alikes**: Mosser (Cambridge OH, made some pieces for Fenton late), Boyd (Cambridge OH, figurines), Imperial, Westmoreland, L.G. Wright (used Fenton as a contractor; unmarked, often confused), Northwood carnival, Dugan.
- **Fenton for Fenton/Fenton 2011–2017**: very small output, beads and small items.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Cranberry: gold-ruby (gold in the glass), pinkish red; Burmese: uranium-yellow shading to pink (glows under UV); Rosalene: pink-white slag-like; Vaseline/topaz opalescent glows green under UV.
- Hand-painted: crisp brushwork, signed and sometimes numbered; mass "gift line" decoration 1990s–2000s is common.
- Older (pre-1970) pieces are unmarked: identify by shape/catalogue numbers; ground and polished pontil on art glass.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips to crimped/ruffled rims (look along the rim in photos), cracks at applied handles, cloudiness ("sick glass") inside vases, worn paint, ground rims (repairs that reduce height), lamp wiring issues. Fairy lamps often missing the base or the candle cup.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fenton reissued its own carnival and hobnail shapes many times; the moulded logo with a decade number dates them. Chinese copies of Fenton fairy lamps and hobnail exist (lighter, thinner, no mark). L.G. Wright and Mosser pieces are often mislabelled "Fenton" by sellers.

## Market
Oversupplied: the collector base is ageing and estate sales are full of Fenton. Common pieces sit for months. Cranberry, Burmese and Connoisseur items still sell in 1–4 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
- Unmarked pre-1970 opalescent hobnail and early carnival get lumped with common milk glass.
- Owners overprice common pieces because "Fenton" sounds collectible (both examples in this sweep).
- UV-reactive Burmese/vaseline pieces aren't flagged as such.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile, ruffled rims: double-box, 2" of padding all round; $12–20 ground for vases, $25+ for lamps.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give height, colour name, line name, decade from the base mark, decorator signature, UV reaction, and rim/handle condition with close-ups.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Only pieces selling ≥ $100 can qualify. Buy cranberry/Burmese/Connoisseur/Mary Gregory/early carnival at ≤ $25 when photos show the rim intact. Pass on everything common.
