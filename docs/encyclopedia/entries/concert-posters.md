## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Originals are printed for the show: 1960s Fillmore/Family Dog numbered posters, 1970s–80s "boxing style" letterpress and silk-screened telephone-pole posters on thin card (often 11x17 or 15x22 with pinholes/staple holes at corners), 1990s Hatch Show Print and art-print gig posters. Reprints are glossy, larger, or have "reproduction" in the margin. Seattle/Portland poster shops (e.g. Art Chantry era) silk-screened on cardboard – holes in corners are normal and prove it hung outside.

Families to recognise from a photo:
- **San Francisco psychedelic (1966–71)**: hand-lettered, hard-to-read swirling type, vivid clashing colours, often split-fountain colour fades. Bill Graham posters carry a **BG number** (e.g. "BG-62" / "#9") and Family Dog/Avalon posters an **FD number** (e.g. "No 33"). Typical sizes are about 14" x 20" to 14" x 24" on thin card/cover stock. The number, the printer credit and the corner text are what identify the printing.
- **Boxing style (1950s–80s)**: big bold wood and metal type, block colours (often a fluorescent or split-fountain background band), photo of the act, venue, date, ticket price and outlets. Printed on heavy cardboard; sizes of 14" x 22", 17" x 22" and 22" x 28" are documented (Classic Posters). Printer's name usually small along the bottom edge (Globe Poster Baltimore, Tribune, Colby, Hatch Show Print Nashville, Tilghman Press Oakland, Murray Poster NYC).
- **Telephone-pole silkscreens (1970s–90s)**: flat matte ink, few colours, card stock, staple/pin holes.
- **1990s art gig posters**: signed/numbered limited silkscreens (Kozik, Coop, Emek, Hess) – the edition number and signature in the margin matter.

Words in titles: "original", "1st printing", "BG-", "FD-", "pre-concert", "boxing style", "window card", "silkscreen", "telephone pole". Look-alikes: commemorative/anniversary posters, framed "art prints", bootleg re-creations of famous shows, offset tour merch posters sold at the venue (legit but lower value than show posters).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bill Graham Presents (Fillmore, BG numbers)** and **Family Dog (FD numbers)**, 1966–71: first printings vs later printings are the whole game; artists Wes Wilson, Victor Moscoso, Rick Griffin, Stanley Mouse/Alton Kelley, Bonnie MacLean (general knowledge, verify). Wes Wilson designed for both the Avalon and Fillmore from Feb 1966 to May 1967 (Wolfgang's). FD posters in the early run were printed by **The Bindweed Press, San Francisco** (credit in the lower right on FD-33 1st and 2nd printings; Wolfgang's).
- **How printings differ (documented examples)**:
  - FD-33 (Grateful Dead, Mouse/Kelley): 1st = drab olive green, Bindweed credit, ~8.5-pt stock, pre-concert; 2nd = kelly green with black split-fountain, still Bindweed credit; 3rd = uniform kelly green on uncoated index, Bindweed credit removed, "No 33-3" lower right and "Family Dog Productions 1725 Washington Street" lower left; 4th = "33-4" and "639 Gough St." address with 1967 ©. Later printings sold for about $76 (3rd, mint) at Wolfgang's.
  - BG-62 (Grateful Dead, Wes Wilson, May 1967): several pre-concert 1st variants (split-fountain green-to-pink; one with yellow), 2nd post-concert in brown/yellow/silver; modern 3rd (2014, glossy, "Wolfgang's Vault" in margin, 20 3/8" x 34 1/8") and 4th (2023, "W 2023" in margin) reissues retailed at $73.
  - BG-9 (Dead/Mothers/Quicksilver, June 1966): original has union logo #72 lower left; 2nd printing drops the union logo and adds "#9" next to "San Francisco" (Classic Posters).
  - Rule of thumb from these: later printings add a printing suffix ("-2", "-3"), a new address, a © line, or a reissue mark, and often change the stock or colour. Always look the number up (Classic Posters, Wolfgang's, Grushkin) before paying.
- **Boxing-style letterpress** (Colby, Tribune, Globe Poster of Baltimore, Hatch Show Print Nashville) – 1950s–80s R&B, country, rock (general knowledge, verify). Globe (Baltimore) printed e.g. the 1958 Buddy Holly Paramount and 1963 Motortown Revue Toledo posters; Murray Poster Printing (NYC) the 1958 Alan Freed Akron Armory; Tilghman Press (Oakland) 1957 Little Richard Portland. Runs were typically one or two hundred and leftovers were thrown away (Classic Posters). Globe's wood type archive went to MICA (Baltimore) after the firm closed (Collectors Weekly); Hatch Show Print still operates in Nashville and prints new posters, so a Hatch credit alone does not mean old.
- **Pacific Northwest silkscreens** – Art Chantry era Seattle/Portland 1980s–90s.
- **1990s art gig posters** – Frank Kozik, Coop, Emek, Derek Hess (general knowledge, verify).
- Value multipliers: band (Zeppelin, Dead, Nirvana, Pearl Jam early), first printing, artist, venue history. 1950s rock'n'roll boxing-style originals are a separate top tier: Classic Posters advertises buying Alan Freed and Buddy Holly posters at $5,000+, Motortown Revue / Biggest Show of Stars at $3,000 and 1957–59 Little Richard at $2,000 (dealer buy offers, not sold comps).

### Quick ID checklist
- Is there a BG / FD number, and is there a printing suffix (-2, -3, "33-4") or reissue mark ("Wolfgang's Vault", "W 2023", "Bill Graham Archives")? Suffix or reissue mark = later printing.
- Printer credit present and period-correct (Bindweed Press on early FD; Globe/Tribune/Colby/Hatch on boxing style)?
- Stock: thin matte card or heavy cardboard (good) vs glossy coated paper (usually reprint).
- Size against the reference (e.g. ~14" x 20"–24" for SF posters; 14x22 / 17x22 / 22x28 boxing style); oversize = reissue.
- Under a close-up: flat or slightly raised ink with small registration offsets (letterpress/silkscreen) vs a dot screen (offset, usually reprint — though many original 1960s SF posters were offset litho, so check the reference).
- Pin/staple holes and sun toning consistent with having been hung.
- Date, venue and ticket price printed on it (show poster) vs generic tour art (merch).
- Ask for front, back, all four corners and a tape measure in frame.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original pole posters: thin card or uncoated paper, flat matte silkscreen or letterpress ink (feel the raised ink / see the slight registration offsets), fluorescent day-glo inks common. Reprints: glossy coated stock, offset dot pattern visible under a loupe, oversized formats, "reproduction"/copyright line in the margin. Fillmore first printings have specific printer credits and sizes (general knowledge, verify).

More detail:
- SF psychedelic posters were mostly printed by offset lithography on cover/index stock, so "offset dots" alone does not condemn a 1960s Fillmore poster; the stock, colour and credits do. FD-33's printings are separated by paper (8.5-pt vs 9–11-pt textured vs uncoated index) and colour (olive vs kelly green) (Wolfgang's).
- Split-fountain colour fades are an original-period technique; a reprint may reproduce the fade but with a dot screen and different hue.
- Boxing-style posters are letterpress from wood and metal type on heavy board; the impression often shows as a slight emboss on the back. Day-glo backgrounds fade where exposed.
- Old vs new: real age shows as even toning of the card, rust halos round staples, soft corners; artificial ageing (tea staining, uniform brown) and crisp bright white backs on "1960s" posters are warning signs.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Corner holes do not hurt value much; water stains, tape, tears into the image, fading and trimming do. Restoration (linen backing, infill) is common on valuable posters and should be disclosed. Ask for front and back photos and the dimensions.

Further: check the back for tape residue and old glue (posters were often taped to walls); trimmed margins remove the printer credit or printing number and can turn a first printing into an unidentifiable one; fluorescent ink fades fast in sunlight; folds and crushed corners from storage are common on card stock. Linen-backing shows as a canvas texture on the reverse; infill and inpainting show as colour mismatches under raking light.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fillmore/Family Dog posters had many later printings and outright counterfeits; Nirvana and other 1990s posters are heavily reproduced (general knowledge, verify). Check paper, size and printing method against reference catalogs (e.g. Classic Posters, Grushkin's guide).

Documented examples: a convincing counterfeit of BG-9 exists that turned up in several large BG collections; the only reliable tell is image size — measured from above-left of the "B" in "Bill" to below-right of the "O" in "San Francisco", the original is 22 29/64" and the counterfeit 22 15/64" (Classic Posters). Authorised modern reissues (Wolfgang's/Bill Graham Archives 2014 and 2023 printings, marked in the margin) are legitimate but sell in the tens of dollars, not hundreds. Hatch Show Print and other letterpress shops still print new boxing-style posters, so check dates and paper ageing.

## Market
Active collector market on eBay, Heritage and specialist dealers; buyers are band fans and rock-art collectors. Prices track band popularity and anniversaries; top posters go to Heritage/specialist auctions (general knowledge, verify). Specialist dealers (Classic Posters, Wolfgang's, Concert Poster Gallery) actively buy 1950s–60s originals in any condition, which is an exit for top finds.

## What sellers usually miss
That it's an original (they say "old poster"), the artist/printer, and the Zeppelin connection in the title (The Firm = Jimmy Page and Paul Rodgers). Corner holes do not hurt value much; water stains and tape do. Also: the BG/FD number and printing (sellers call later printings "original" and miss real first printings), the printer credit on boxing-style posters, and opening acts that later became famous.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, in a rigid mailer, $8–12. eBay (13%). Photograph front and back to show the card stock. Large posters: ship flat between boards or rolled in a heavy tube. Do not roll heavy boxing-style card; it cracks.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Band, venue, city, date, printer/artist, printing method (silkscreen/letterpress), size, paper stock, "original from the show" with the evidence, every flaw (holes, stains, tears). Photograph front, back, corners and printer credit.

### What to tell the buyer
State the poster number and printing with the evidence ("BG-9, union logo #72 lower left, no '#9' added — matches the first printing as described by Classic Posters"), the exact measured size, the stock (thin card / heavy board / index), the printer credit as printed, and the printing method. Say plainly whether it is a show original, a later printing or an authorised reissue. Disclose every hole, tear, stain, tape mark, trimming and any backing. For telephone-pole posters, say the corner holes are from being hung for the show. Do not claim "1st printing" unless a reference catalogue confirms the tells.

## Buy rule
Buy when: an original 1980s or earlier poster for a name act is under $25. Pass when: reprint, or heavily damaged. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
