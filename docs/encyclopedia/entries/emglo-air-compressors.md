## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Emglo (Johnstown PA, pre-DeWalt) twin-stack "AirMate" AM78/AM780 – red tanks, cast-iron oil-lubed pump, ~75 lb; Rol-Air (blue), Thomas (black oilless), Makita MAC2400/5200 (teal). Contractors want them because the pump is rebuildable and quiet. Big-box oilless pancakes (Porter-Cable, Craftsman, Husky) are $30–60 used and not worth touching.

Colour notes to verify (2026-10-13): Fine Homebuilding contractors describe the classic Emglos as **blue** (vs later yellow DeWalt-era units) and describe Rol-Air colour-coding as **orange = all US-made, green = US-made with some offshore parts, dark grey = all offshore** — which conflicts with the "red tanks" and "Rol-Air (blue)" notes above. Identify by the brand decal and data plate, not colour.

How to read a photo: two horizontal tanks stacked one above the other in a tubular frame with a carry handle ("twin stack" / "hand carry", ~4 gallon), electric motor on top with a belt or direct drive to a cast-iron pump; an oil sight glass or dipstick on the pump crankcase = oil-lubed; two gauges (tank and regulated) and a regulator knob; drain valves under each tank. Wheelbarrow units have a single large tank, a gas engine (often Honda) and a wheel — too big for the size rule.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Emglo** (Johnstown PA; bought by DeWalt/Black & Decker – the later DeWalt-branded twin stacks share the design, general knowledge, verify): AM78/AM780 electric twin-stack; K5HGA-8P gas wheelbarrow.
  Detail (Fine Homebuilding): Emglo and Jenny Products began as sister companies; DeWalt bought the Emglo name and for a time Emglo built DeWalt compressors; Jenny later bought back the commercial/industrial Emglo line and its portable units use the Emglo motors and pumps (still blue). Several contractors say the yellow DeWalt-era units did not last as long as the old blue Emglos; later DeWalt units are reportedly made in China, Taiwan and Mexico. Old Emglos are still in daily use on framing crews.
- **DeWalt twin stacks** (e.g. D55151 / D55155): D55151 is 1.1 hp, 4-gallon twin stack, oil-lubricated with a cast-iron cylinder, 3.2 CFM at 100 psi (DeWalt). Users report the small DeWalt oil units are "cold blooded" and trip breakers on cold mornings (Fine Homebuilding).
- **Rol-Air** (blue): JC10 electric; 5.5 hp gas wheelbarrow. Contractors rate Rol-Air highly for its cast-iron pump (vs aluminium pumps with a cast sleeve); three quality levels marked by colour (see above) (Fine Homebuilding).
- **Thomas** (black): oilless, lighter, less desirable. Contractors call Thomas "very good" but one reported drain-cock and service problems (Fine Homebuilding).
- **Makita MAC2400 / MAC5200** (teal): "Big Bore" oil-lubed cast-iron pump. MAC2400 specs: 2.5 hp, 4.2 SCFM at 90 psi, 4.2-gallon twin stack, 130 psi max, 79 dB, 12.3 A at 120 V, oil sight glass and drain, ball-valve tank drain, roll-cage handle (eIndustrial Solutions).
- **Big-box oilless pancakes** (Porter-Cable, Craftsman, Husky, Campbell Hausfeld): junk tier.
- Others contractors name: Hitachi EC12 (poor reports), Jenny (current Emglo-pump units).

### Quick ID checklist
- Brand decal and data plate: Emglo / Jenny / Rol-Air / Makita MAC / Thomas (good) vs Porter-Cable, Craftsman, Husky, Campbell Hausfeld pancakes (junk).
- Oil sight glass or dipstick on the pump = oil-lubed contractor pump.
- Cast-iron pump (dark, finned, heavy) rather than plastic shroud over an aluminium pump.
- Twin-stack frame and handle intact; wheels/engine = wheelbarrow (too big).
- Tanks: look under them for rust, weeping and pitting; drain valves present.
- Both gauges and the regulator present; cord not spliced.
- Seller video: cold start, fills to cut-out (~120–130 psi), cuts back in, holds pressure overnight.
- Rol-Air colour: orange (US-made) > green > dark grey (offshore), per contractors.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast-iron, oil-lubricated pump with sight glass or dipstick = contractor grade; aluminium oilless pump and plastic shroud = consumer. Look for pressure gauges, regulator, drain valves, and tank date/rating plates (general knowledge, verify).
Further: copper finned discharge tubes, automotive-style intake filters, ball-valve drains and thermal-overload resets are contractor features (Makita spec list). Older US-made Emglo/Rol-Air units are heavier and rebuildable (rings, valves, check valve available); offshore later units are lighter. Upgraded quarter-turn drain valves are a sign of a cared-for unit (Fine Homebuilding).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rusted tanks (drain valve never opened) are a safety issue – pitted or leaking tanks kill value; worn rings (slow to fill, oil blow-by), bad check valve or unloader, frayed cord, broken gauges. Ask for a video of it filling to cut-out pressure and holding air (general knowledge, verify).
Further: oil in the tanks or the air line means worn rings (the Hitachi EC12 failure mode reported on Fine Homebuilding); trouble starting in the cold and tripping breakers is common on small oil units and is not necessarily a fault — ask whether it starts at room temperature; overdue oil changes (dark, low oil in the sight glass) shorten pump life.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None known; brand rebadges (Emglo → DeWalt) only. Note the Emglo name later appeared on DeWalt-era and Jenny-era products; old US-made Emglos are the ones contractors want.

## Market
Steady local demand from contractors, trim carpenters and roofers; sells within days on Craigslist/Facebook when priced right. Spring–summer construction season is strongest (general knowledge, verify). In cold climates buyers ask whether oil units start in winter.

## What sellers usually miss
"Emglo" (they write "air compressor"). Age doesn't matter – working pump does. Also: that an old US-made Emglo is preferred over newer DeWalt-badged units, and the Rol-Air colour (orange = US-made).

## Shipping / handling
70–85 lb – $80–120 to ship. Local-only item; profit must be computed on local resale. Drain the oil before any transport on its side; carry upright.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State brand/model, hp, tank size, oil-lubed cast-iron pump, fill time, max PSI, holds pressure, last oil change. Photograph the data plate, gauges, pump and tanks. Title: "Emglo AirMate twin-stack oil compressor, cast iron pump, works".

### What to tell the buyer
Give brand and model from the data plate, horsepower, tank size, voltage/amps and the oil-lubricated cast-iron pump. Say it was tested: time to fill from empty, cut-out pressure, that it cuts back in and holds pressure (overnight if checked), and that both gauges and the regulator work. Describe tank condition honestly (drained, no pitting or leaks), when the oil was last changed, and cord condition. For an Emglo, note it is the original pre-DeWalt contractor design that framing crews still use.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Emglo/Rol-Air/Makita under $60 and within driving distance. Pass when: must ship. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and costs (local resale, no shipping). Twin-stack electric units fit a passenger seat (heavy); gas wheelbarrow units are bigger and excluded.
