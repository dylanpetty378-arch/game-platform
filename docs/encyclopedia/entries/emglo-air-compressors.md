## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Emglo (Johnstown PA, pre-DeWalt) twin-stack "AirMate" AM78/AM780 – red tanks, cast-iron oil-lubed pump, ~75 lb; Rol-Air (blue), Thomas (black oilless), Makita MAC2400/5200 (teal). Contractors want them because the pump is rebuildable and quiet. Big-box oilless pancakes (Porter-Cable, Craftsman, Husky) are $30–60 used and not worth touching.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Emglo** (Johnstown PA; bought by DeWalt/Black & Decker – the later DeWalt-branded twin stacks share the design, general knowledge, verify): AM78/AM780 electric twin-stack; K5HGA-8P gas wheelbarrow.
- **Rol-Air** (blue): JC10 electric; 5.5 hp gas wheelbarrow.
- **Thomas** (black): oilless, lighter, less desirable.
- **Makita MAC2400 / MAC5200** (teal): "Big Bore" oil-lubed cast-iron pump.
- **Big-box oilless pancakes** (Porter-Cable, Craftsman, Husky, Campbell Hausfeld): junk tier.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast-iron, oil-lubricated pump with sight glass or dipstick = contractor grade; aluminium oilless pump and plastic shroud = consumer. Look for pressure gauges, regulator, drain valves, and tank date/rating plates (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rusted tanks (drain valve never opened) are a safety issue – pitted or leaking tanks kill value; worn rings (slow to fill, oil blow-by), bad check valve or unloader, frayed cord, broken gauges. Ask for a video of it filling to cut-out pressure and holding air (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None known; brand rebadges (Emglo → DeWalt) only.
