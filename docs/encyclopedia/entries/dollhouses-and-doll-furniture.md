## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Toy houses for dolls (1:12, 1:16, 1:24 scale) and the furniture for them, plus play-size doll beds and cribs for 12–22" dolls. Photo tells:
- **Bliss (R. Bliss Mfg., Pawtucket RI, 1890s–1914):** wooden houses covered in chromolithographed paper (brick, clapboard, windows), turned porch posts, "R. BLISS" printed on the door or gable.
- **Schoenhut (Philadelphia, 1917–1930s):** wood houses with embossed fiberboard "stone/brick," celluloid windows, decal labels.
- **Converse (Winchendon MA), Wolverine, Keystone of Boston, Rich Toys (1930s–50s):** printed fiberboard or Masonite houses with lithographed details — moderate value.
- **Marx (1950s–60s):** tin-litho houses with plastic furniture; very recognizable printed interior walls.
- **Tynietoy (Providence, 1920–1940s):** colonial-revival hand-painted wood furniture, black stamp "TYNIETOY" (paper label 1920s, rubber stamp ~1925–30s, impressed later); houses rare ($20–30k).
- **Real Good Toys (Vermont, 1970s–present):** kit or factory-built wooden houses (Colonial, Farmhouse, Victorian), plywood with cedar shingles, often assembled and painted by owners.
- **Homemade / U-Bild:** U-Bild sold full-size woodworking patterns; houses built from them (and from Popular Mechanics plans) are one-of-a-kind and mostly decor value.
- **Doll-size cribs/beds:** spindle cribs, sleigh beds, iron doll beds (1890s–1930s), wicker doll carriages. Value is in age, original paint, and any antique doll included.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Furniture:** Tynietoy (stamped, hand-painted), Schoenhut, Tootsietoy (Dowst, die-cast 1920s–40s, flocked upholstery), Strombecker (wood, 1930s–50s), Renwal (1946–60s plastic, marked "RENWAL" with number), Ideal (plastic 1950s; Petite Princess 1964–65 with gold-trim boxed sets), Plasco, Marx, Kage, Wolverine, Lundby (Sweden, 1960s–80s), Caroline's Home (Lundby/Brio).
- **Houses:** Bliss, Schoenhut, Converse, Rich, Keystone, Gottschalk (German "blue roof," 1880s–1930s, top value), Tri-ang (UK), Marx, Wolverine, Real Good Toys, Greenleaf (kits, 1970s–present), Dura-Craft (kits), Kaybee/Plan Toys (modern).
- **Doll beds/cribs:** mostly unmarked; Gendron (wicker/metal), Heywood-Wakefield and Lloyd Loom (wicker), cast-iron makers (Hubley, Kenton for small beds), folk-made wood.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Old vs new:** cut nails, chromolithographed paper, original paint with crazing, hand-cut window frames → pre-1930. Plywood with uniform veneer, Phillips screws, craft-store hinges, lace-trim curtains and cedar shingles → 1970s+ kit or homemade.
- **Shingles:** individually laid cedar shingles = kit/hobby house (Real Good Toys, Greenleaf). Printed roofs = factory toy.
- **Scale:** 1:12 (1" = 1') adult hobby; 3/4" (1:16) for Renwal/Ideal toy furniture; small sets 1:24.
- **Furniture marks:** look under seats/tables for stamps (TYNIETOY), molded marks (RENWAL, IDEAL, Petite Princess), "Made in USA" decals.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Houses: repainting (kills antique value), missing doors/chimneys/stair rails, water damage, warping, broken hinges, paper lithography peeling.
- Furniture: missing knobs/drawers, broken legs, sun-faded plastic, cracked styrene.
- Cribs: loose joints, missing spindles, wobbly rockers.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction Bliss-style lithographed houses (1970s–90s) and "antique-style" Chinese wood houses.
- Reproduction Tynietoy-style hand-painted furniture and German copies (noted by the Roadshow appraiser).
- Ideal Petite Princess has been reproduced in resin by hobbyists.

## Market
- Houses: slow, regional, pickup-dominated (Facebook Marketplace/Craigslist); eBay buyers won't pay freight. Holiday season (Nov–Dec) lifts local demand.
- Furniture: active national market on eBay; vintage plastic (Renwal, Petite Princess) and Tynietoy sell quickly.
- Doll cribs: decor market; value usually rides on the doll.

## What sellers usually miss
- A box of "dollhouse furniture" often holds Renwal, Tootsietoy, Strombecker or even Tynietoy (look for the stamp) — worth more than the house.
- Antique china/bisque dolls tucked into old cribs/beds — the doll is often the real value.
- Original boxes for Petite Princess and Tootsietoy sets.
- Sellers over-value homemade houses because of the hours spent building them.

## Shipping / handling
- Houses: oversized, 15–40 lb; $60–150 via Ground with large box charges, frequent damage. Treat as local-only.
- Furniture lots: shoebox, $10–15; wrap pieces individually.
- Doll crib ~20–24" long: large box, $25–45.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give dimensions, scale, maker evidence (stamps, labels), and whether furniture/lighting is included.
- Photograph interiors, roof, back, and every mark; disclose repainting.
- For cribs, measure the mattress area (buyers want to know what doll size fits).

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price).
- **Houses: pass** — they fail the passenger-seat rule or the shipping math almost every time.
- **Furniture:** buy marked Tynietoy, Tootsietoy boxed, Petite Princess boxed, Schoenhut, or big Renwal lots when the sold median clears the rule.
- **Cribs/beds with dolls:** judge the doll (see composition-dolls; china/bisque heads separately); ask for the doll's back-of-shoulder marks before buying.
- No repairs (no regluing, repainting, restringing).
