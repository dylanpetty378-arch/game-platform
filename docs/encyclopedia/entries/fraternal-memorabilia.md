## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Souvenirs and regalia from fraternal orders. The classic is the Shrine (AAONMS) Imperial Council Session souvenir mug, produced yearly c.1895–1917, often by German/Austrian makers (Tirschenreuth, Rudolstadt, Pickard decoration, Jos. Rieber), with the host temple's building, an Arabic scimitar-and-crescent emblem, and the year and city on the reverse. Knights Templar Triennial Conclave goblets and shell-cup souvenirs (1890s–1910s) are glass or porcelain with a cross-and-crown logo. Shrine fezzes (red felt with a jewelled emblem), Masonic aprons (painted silk/leather, 18th–19th c.), swords, and pins.

## Makers, brands and variants
Shrine mugs: Lulu Temple (Philadelphia; Moorish building on Spring Garden St), Syria Temple (Pittsburgh), Mecca (New York), Medinah (Chicago), Al Malaikah (LA). 1904 was the Atlantic City session hosted by Lulu. Makers: Tirschenreuth, Rudolstadt, "Bavaria"; there is also lustreware and figural (fish/horn handles). KT: Libbey/Pairpoint-cut goblets.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Porcelain transfer-printed with a lustre or matte brown body; fine detailed engraving-style transfers. Figural handles (horns, fish, camels) are worth more. Later 1950s+ items are mass-produced.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Hairlines and chips at the rim and handle; worn gold. Fezzes: moth damage, missing stones. Aprons: tears, fading.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few. Some modern "Masonic" reproduction swords and aprons exist.

## Market
Steady but niche, with an aging collector base. eBay: 200+ Shrine mugs/pieces per month. Days to sell: 20–60.

## What sellers usually miss
Figural handles; scarce host-temple/city combinations; folk-art aprons.

## Shipping / handling
A mug ships in a double box for $14–18.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give the year, city, host temple and maker mark from the base, and photograph both sides.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$16 out − ~$16 in − price ≥ max($50, price). A common $40–60 mug never qualifies. Buy only rare-form/early regalia at ≤ ~30% of the median.
