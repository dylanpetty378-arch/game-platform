## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Framed lithographs, usually double-matted in oak/cherry frames, 1985–2005. Redlin: twilight farm/lake scenes, warm lit
windows. Tells for the valuable version: pencil signature **and** edition number (e.g. 1234/9500) in the lower margin,
often a remarque. Open-edition / "licensed" prints have a printed signature only. Seller words: "Terry Redlin print",
"framed Redlin", "signed". Certificates of authenticity (COA) on the back from the publisher (Hadley House for Redlin;
Greenwich Workshop for Doolittle) support edition prints (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Terry Redlin** (Hadley House): twilight rural scenes; 141 tracked auction lots, all under $1,000, median ~$80.
- **Bev Doolittle** (Greenwich Workshop): camouflage art ("Pintos", "The Forest Has Eyes"); some early titles collectible
  (general knowledge, verify).
- **Thomas Kinkade**: open editions and canvas "S/N" giclées — oversupplied, low resale (general knowledge, verify).
- **Frank Massa** (F. Massa): bird prints, decor tier (seen in sweep).
- Others in this genre: Robert Bateman, Lee Kromschroeder, duck-stamp prints with stamp attached (general knowledge,
  verify values).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Offset lithographs on paper (most), some canvas transfers/giclées. Pencil signature (graphite, slight sheen, indent) vs
printed signature (flat, part of the image). Edition number fraction, "AP" (artist's proof) or "remarque" (small hand
sketch) increase value. Frames are oak/cherry with double mats; frame quality adds little.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fading/sun damage, foxing, water stains, mat burn (acid mats), broken glass, frame dings. Unframed sheets stored flat
are easier to ship. Ask for a close-up of the lower margin (signature and number) and the back (COA/labels).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Open-edition reprints and posters of the same images are everywhere; licensed products (plates, calendars, puzzles)
carry the images too. Only the pencil signature + number distinguishes the edition print.

## Market
Midwest/rural buyers, cabin and lodge decorators; mostly local sales (Facebook Marketplace, auctions). The market has
softened as the original 1980s–2000s buyers downsize, creating heavy supply (general knowledge, verify). Shipping framed
glass is the main barrier to online sales.

## What sellers usually miss
Whether the pencil number exists. Many sellers just say "Redlin print". Check the lower margin photo.

## Shipping / handling
Framed under glass 30x40" – $60–100 to ship, glass breakage. Only worth it local or if S/N and under $40. Pulling the
print from the frame and shipping it flat/rolled cuts cost sharply (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State artist, title, year, edition number and size, signature type, COA present, image and frame dimensions, condition
(fading, foxing, glass). Photograph the full piece, the signature/number close-up, and the back with labels. Title:
artist, title, year, "signed and numbered", edition, "framed", size.

## Buy rule
Buy when: pencil-signed & numbered confirmed in photo, price ≤ $40, local or driveable. Pass when: open edition, or
seller quotes eBay asking prices (knows). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must
fit a passenger seat (30x40" frames usually do).
