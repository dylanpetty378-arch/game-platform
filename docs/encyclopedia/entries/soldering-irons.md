## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A copper tip on a steel barrel with a heating element, a wood or phenolic handle, and a cloth-covered cord (pre-1970 cloth cords; the example has a braided cloth cord).
- **American Beauty** (Detroit, since 1894): big industrial irons, wood handles, logo stamped on the barrel, model numbers by wattage (e.g. 3138).
- **Hexacon** (NJ): hex-section barrels, red/black handles.
- **Weller**: pistol-grip "soldering guns" (common).
- **Jackson, Vulcan, Wall, Ungar, Craftsman**: smaller hobby irons.
- **Soldering coppers**: pointed copper heads on iron rods with wood handles, heated in a fire or furnace, 1800s–1940s.

## Makers, brands and variants
American Beauty, Hexacon, Weller, Ungar, Jackson, Vulcan Electric, Wall Manufacturing, Craftsman (Sears). Stained-glass and tinsmith markets keep big irons in demand.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Tip condition (unpitted), intact cord insulation (cloth cords fray dangerously), wattage plate legible, original box.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Burned-out elements, frayed cords (fire risk), pitted tips, cracked handles. Rewiring is a repair Dylan does not do.
