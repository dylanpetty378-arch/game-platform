## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Grey or tan stoneware, cylindrical crocks and shoulder jugs, with a gallon number and often a cobalt-blue mark. The money is in the mark and the decoration.
- **Red Wing (MN)**: red "wing" stamp plus an oval "Red Wing Union Stoneware" ink stamp. Earlier pieces have a hand-drawn cobalt "birch leaf" or "elephant ear" and a "Minnesota Stoneware" stamp. Wing size (small 4", 6", large wing) changes value.
- **Greensboro / New Geneva PA** (Hamilton & Jones, Jas. Hamilton & Co., T.F. Reppert, Williams & Reppert): stencilled cobalt lettering, often with stencilled flowers or eagles and freehand stripes. Sellers copy the name off the pot ("TR Ruppert", "Jas Hamilton") without knowing the market.
- **Freehand cobalt birds, deer and flowers** on salt-glazed crocks (NY/PA/OH, e.g. Cowden & Wilcox, White's Utica, Fort Edward, Remmey) are the top tier.
- **Western Stoneware** (maple-leaf mark, "Monmouth Ill."), Ruckels, Uhl, Robinson-Ransbottom (RRP, blue crown), unmarked Bristol-glaze crocks: mostly decor.
- **Forms**:
  - crocks, with a numbered gallon size
  - shoulder jugs: brown Albany-slip top and white bottom = 1880s–1920s "whiskey jug"
  - hanging salt crocks: 6", blue-sponged or embossed "SALT"
  - blue-banded stoneware/yellowware bowls and pitchers, 1920s–40s
- Value tells: cobalt freehand decoration (birds, flowers, numbers, slip-trailed script), maker stamps, large sizes (6–30 gal), and rare merchant advertising text stencilled on a jug.
- Seller words: "antique crock", "whiskey jug", "stoneware", "moonshine jug", "salt glaze".

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Salt glaze: an orange-peel surface texture from salt thrown into the kiln. Older (19th c.) and usually grey with cobalt.
- Bristol glaze: a smooth, opaque white glaze. Later (1890s–1930s), usually stamped.
- Albany slip: a dark brown glaze, inside or on top.
- Freehand cobalt vs stencilled vs stamped: freehand brushwork is worth the most. Stencils (Greensboro) are a collected category of their own. Stamped ovals (Red Wing) are valued by mark and size.
- Old vs new: kiln burns, salt drips, "kiln kisses" and a worn, unglazed base suggest age. Crisp, even glaze and paper labels suggest modern craft or reproductions (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Hairlines, through-cracks, rim chips, glaze flakes, base chips and missing lids. Cracks cut value 50%+. Hairlines are tolerated more on rare decorated pieces.
- Restorations: filled and painted-over chips and cracks. Ask for a raking-light photo and a "ring test" (a sound piece rings, a cracked one thuds) (general knowledge, verify).
- Ask for a photo of the whole pot all the way round: a stencil on the back is the money.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Modern "primitive" reproduction crocks and jugs with fake cobalt birds and artificial ageing are sold widely. Look for too-regular decoration, a light weight, and a clean base (general knowledge, verify).
- Red Wing has been reissued with modern commemorative stamps (Red Wing Collectors Society annual pieces); these are dated and not antique (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
- Steady collector market. Decorated pieces sell at Crocker Farm, Pook & Pook and similar specialist auctions.
- Marked Red Wing sells to Midwest collectors (with an annual Red Wing convention in July).
- Plain crocks sell locally to decorators and farmhouse-style buyers.
- Heavy weight limits eBay to small or decorated pieces. Prices for plain stoneware have softened over the past 15 years (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- They copy the stencil text but don't know that "Greensboro, PA" stoneware is a collected category.
- Freehand cobalt decoration (bird, flower) vs stencil; large vs small wing on Red Wing; early "Minnesota Stoneware" marks.
- Cobalt decoration and maker marks generally ("old jug $40").
- Advertising text on a jug (a merchant's name and town) is the biggest multiplier. The maker stamp often sits near the base.

## Shipping / handling
- 1 gal ~5 lb ($15 to ship); 2–3 gal 8–20 lb ($25–45); 5 gal ~20 lb ($40); big crocks 15–40 lb ($40–80, double-boxed). Fragile and chip-prone.
- Big pieces are almost always local-only unless decorated. Resell on eBay, or consign decorated pieces to Crocker Farm.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker and mark as read on the pot, gallon size, height and diameter, glaze type (salt/Bristol/Albany), decoration (freehand/stencil/stamp, with a description), every chip, crack and hairline, and whether there's a lid.
- Photograph all four sides, the mark close up, the base and the inside, and use raking light for hairlines.
- Title keywords: "Antique Salt Glaze Stoneware Crock Cobalt Bird", "Greensboro PA Hamilton & Jones Stencilled", "Red Wing 5 Gallon Large Wing Crock", "Advertising Whiskey Jug".

## Buy rule
Three earlier write-ups gave different thresholds. They are reconciled here, and all three conditions are kept.
Buy when:
- a Greensboro/New Geneva stencilled piece is under $100 with no crack;
- any freehand-decorated crock is under $150 (Oct 2026 write-up);
- a cobalt-decorated or advertising-marked piece is ≤ $40 with no cracks (2025 write-up);
- a decorated, advertising or large-wing piece is under $60 and local (blue-band/Albany write-up).
Pass when: cracked; pickup-only; Red Wing under 10 gal at > $40; plain or unmarked; lidless salt crocks; blue-band at any price above free; anything over 5 gal that must ship.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat; 10+ gal crocks generally don't.
