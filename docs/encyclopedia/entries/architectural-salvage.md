## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Components removed from buildings. Passenger-seat-sized and shippable categories are what matter:
- **Door hardware**: knobs (cast bronze/brass with patterns – Eastlake, Aesthetic, Japanesque), backplates/escutcheons, hinges with decorative leaves and ball/steeple tips, mortise locks. Pattern names catalogued (e.g., Russell & Erwin "Windsor", Sargent "Lorraine", Yale "Gothic").
- **Glazed architectural terra cotta**: egg-and-dart, bead-and-reel, dentil cornice blocks, crazed white or cream glaze over buff clay, hollow cellular backs (example 7942248064) – very heavy (15–40 lb per block).
- **Turned wood**: balusters/spindles, newel posts, finials. Retail reproduction barley-twist spindles (example 7942251853 photo is a retailer's stock photo with "THESE SOLD OUT") are new.
- **Finials**: turned wood, cast iron, or zinc fence/post finials (example 7975276227 – a turned, weathered cone finial, metal or wood, origin unknown).
- **Stained/leaded glass**: transoms and panels, size drives shipping risk.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Hardware: Russell & Erwin, P&F Corbin, Sargent, Yale & Towne, Reading Hardware, Branford, Mallory Wheeler – ornate bronze sets $50–300; signed Arts & Crafts hardware (Stickley, Roycroft) higher.
- Terra cotta: Gladding McBean (CA), Northwestern Terra Cotta (Chicago), Atlantic Terra Cotta (NY), New York Architectural Terra Cotta – value mostly décor, $50–300 per block, local sale.
- Woodwork: period newels and balusters $15–150; reproductions low.
- Glass: Victorian/Arts & Crafts leaded panels $100–1,000+.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast bronze is heavy, golden-brown under patina; brass-plated steel shows rust at wear; pressed steel backplates are thin. Real period woodwork shows paint layers, tool marks, old nail holes; reproductions are clean, uniform, machine-carved.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken/cut hardware, missing spindles on knobs, stripped threads, paint-encrusted hardware (can be cleaned but labour heavy – counts as repair), cracked glass and lead fatigue, terra cotta chips and spalling, wood rot and lead paint.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large volumes of reproduction cast-iron and brass Victorian hardware (Asian imports) with fake rust; resin corbels; new barley-twist spindles; reproduction "salvaged" stained glass. Real hardware has makers' marks on the back or the spindle and wear patterns.

## Market
Strong local demand (renovators, designers); shipping limits online sales of heavy items. Hardware sells well online. Spring/summer renovation season bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Named ornate bronze hardware patterns sold as "old door knob"; Arts & Crafts hardware. Sellers often over-estimate terra cotta, plain spindles and finials.

## Shipping / handling
Hardware 1–5 lb $10–18. Terra cotta 15–40 lb per piece, $40–80 and breakage – don't ship. Glass panels need crating.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + pattern + item + metal + "antique" + era: "Russell & Erwin bronze doorknob backplate set", "Sargent entry set", "Victorian cast iron register grate 12x14", "leaded stained glass window 25x36"; Junk: "old door knob", "salvage lot", "rustic", "reclaimed". Example: `Antique Russell & Erwin Bronze Doorknob Backplate Set Victorian Aesthetic Pattern Marked`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for hardware (sells well, 1–5 lb) and small leaded panels; terra cotta, newels and large glass local to renovators/designers (spring–summer bump). Local beats eBay for anything heavy.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (Sargent bronze $50, p75 $85; ornate Victorian knob+backplate sets $50–150; stained glass $187, p75 $415; terra cotta $50–150 local), accept −15%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo the set laid out on neutral cloth; then maker mark on the back/spindle, pattern detail, wear, spindle and threads, paint residue, glass panels backlit and the lead lines, chips/spalling on terra cotta, tape for dimensions.
- **State:** maker/pattern name, metal, dimensions, set complete (knobs, plates, spindle, screws), original patina vs cleaned, stripped threads/cracks/lead fatigue by photo number, repro check (fake rust, no marks).
- **Pack / haul:** hardware 1–5 lb, wrapped, small box ($10–18); glass crated; terra cotta 15–40 lb — don't ship; passenger-seat size for all but blocks.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Russell & Erwin] marked on the back, [Aesthetic] pattern bronze with the original patina — the period hardware, not the Asian repro; complete set with spindle; named ornate sets bring [$50–150] and the figural ones far more."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$15) − ship to Dylan (~$15) − price ≥ max($50, price). Buy only compact ornate bronze/brass hardware in named patterns or small fine stained-glass panels (≤ 20") at ≤ 1/3 of median. Pass terra cotta, stone, long woodwork, reproductions, oversize glass, anything requiring stripping/cleaning.
