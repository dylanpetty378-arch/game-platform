## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Thick book-form albums with gilt-edged card pages cut to hold cabinet cards and CDVs.
- Covers:
  - plush velvet with brass corners and a clasp
  - celluloid (an early plastic) with lithographed Victorian ladies or flowers
- Some have a wind-up music box in the base, with a tune card on the bottom ("Blaze Away", "Irish Bells March" = c. 1900–10).
- Value drivers: the celluloid cover art's condition (no cracks or lifting), a working music box, an intact clasp, and above all the photos still inside. Tintypes, identified people, occupational images, post-mortem, Black Americana and Civil War soldiers add $5–100 per photo.
- Seller words: "antique photo album", "Victorian album", "velvet album", "music box album".

## Makers, brands and variants
- Most albums are unmarked German or American production. The music-box movements are often Swiss or German (general knowledge, verify).
- Variants:
  - plain leather albums, 1860s–80s CDV era; earlier and usually less decorative
  - velvet/plush with a brass clasp, 1880s–1900s
  - celluloid-covered, 1890s–1910
  - musical base
  - stand-top musical albums on a built-in easel (top tier)
  - albums with a heart- or round-shaped mirror inset

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Celluloid covers show embossed, lithographed scenes. Cracks, lifting and yellowing reduce value.
- Plush: colour (teal, burgundy, wine), wear on the edges.
- Brass clasps: intact and working.
- Page cut-outs sized for CDVs (2.5×4") and cabinet cards (4.25×6.5").
- Photos inside: tintypes (iron, dark), CDVs, cabinet cards with photographers' imprints on the back.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Detached covers, broken clasps, torn or loose pages, brittle celluloid, dead music box springs, worn plush.
- Repaired spines (glue or tape) show as stiffness and residue.
- Ask: does the music play, how many photos are inside, are any identified on the back, and what condition are the pages in.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Few reproduction albums. Modern prints of tintypes and post-mortem photos are inserted into old albums to raise prices. Check for paper stock, dot-screen printing and magnetic iron in tintypes (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Steady eBay and Etsy demand from photo collectors, genealogists and Victorian decor buyers. The photos sell individually to niche collectors (occupational, military, Black history, post-mortem). Q4 has a mild gift peak (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- The photos. Sellers price the album; collectors pay for the contents.
- A working music box adds $40–80.

## Shipping / handling
