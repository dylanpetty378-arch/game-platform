## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-painted earthenware ("Majolika", really tin/lead-glazed faience-type earthenware) from Schramberg in Germany's Black Forest. The money period is the **late 1920s–early 1930s**, when the Meyer brothers hired modern designers and the factory produced Bauhaus-adjacent geometric decorations on simple cylinders, spheres, cones and stepped forms — vases, pitchers, tea/coffee services, bowls, ashtrays, candleholders. Decoration is painted by hand in bold blocks, bands, grids, dots and spirals in red, black, grey, blue, green, yellow and white; some pieces have finely lined "textile" surfaces (the "Gobelin" family).
Back marks: a stamped (usually black) **shield with interlaced "SMF"** over "SCHRAMBERG", with "Handgemalt"/"handpainted", often "Majolika" and "Germany", and often a **decoration name** ("Dec. Mondrian", "Malta", "Plume", "Adlon", "Anemone", "Ceylon", "Grinzing") and painted numbers (decor and painter numbers) plus an impressed shape number (Porcelain Marks & More; Cooper Hewitt).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Faist / Uechtritz & Faist (1820–1883)** — impressed "Schramberg"; creamware and majolica (Porcelain Marks & More).
- **Villeroy & Boch Schramberg branch (1883–1912)** — majolica and earthenware, 19th-c. leaf plates, barbotine; V&B-style marks with "Schramberg", later "Made in Germany".
- **Schramberger Majolikafabrik, Moritz & Leopold Meyer (1912–1938)** — SMF shield marks; 1912–18 mark includes "Dec. Grinzing"; mark registered 1918. **Eva Zeisel (née Stricker) designed there from autumn 1928 to summer 1930; most of her designs appeared from 1930 on** (Porcelain Marks & More). Other in-house and freelance designers produced similar geometric lines.
- **Aryanised 1938–1949, Meyer family again 1949–1989** — post-war hand-painted florals, stylised 1950s decors, "West Germany"/"Germany" marks; factory closed 1989.
- **Named decors** (from marks): Mondrian (often on shapes Zeisel did not design — Porcelain Marks & More), Gobelin (numbered, e.g. "Gobelin 13", Zeisel, Cooper Hewitt), Malta, Plume, Adlon, Anemone, Ceylon, Grinzing, plus florals like Roma/Madrid-era 1950s lines (general knowledge, verify).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Eva Zeisel shapes/decors 1928–30 (e.g. Gobelin series)** — ~$150–400 per piece vs $20–40 for a floral SMF vase (5–10×; triage $100–300, verify) — Cooper Hewitt-type "MAJOLIKA / SMF / SCHRAMBERG / Handgemalt" mark; stark geometric forms (cones, stepped cylinders, spheres with flat lids) with fine parallel painted lines or rhythmic stripes.
- **"Dec. Mondrian" mark** — ~$100–250 vs $30 (verify) — decor name stamped in the mark; rectangles of primary colours with black lines.
- **Complete 1930 tea/coffee/mocha services in a single geometric decor** — $400–1,500+ (general knowledge, verify) — matching pot, sugar, creamer, cups in bold bands; sets sell on design platforms.
- **Bold Art Deco abstracts (Malta, Plume, Adlon) on vases 6"+** — $60–150 vs $25 (verify) — decor name in the mark; spirals, dots, crossed bands, airbrushed backgrounds.
- **Early 1912–18 "Dec. Grinzing" Wiener Werkstätte-influenced pieces** — premium over generic (verify) — mark with fir-tree shield and "Dec. Grinzing".
- **19th-c. Schramberg majolica (leaf plates, barbotine, cake stands)** — $40–200 by form (1stdibs sold examples, prices not shown) — impressed "Schramberg", coloured lead glazes, moulded leaves.
- **Kill: post-war florals, Black Forest landscapes, souvenir bud vases** — $15–40.

### Quick ID checklist
- Base photo: SMF shield mark present? Read every word — decor name, "Majolika", "Handgemalt"/"handpainted", "Germany".
- Painted decor/painter numbers and impressed shape number (ask; the triage asks for the painted 4-digit decor number).
- Decoration type: geometric/abstract (good) vs floral/landscape (low).
- Shape: severe geometric (cone, stepped cylinder, sphere) suggests 1928–32 design.
- Rim and foot chips (soft earthenware chips easily), glaze crazing, hairlines.
- Size (most vases 4–9").
- Set completeness for tea/coffee services.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Soft buff/red earthenware body with opaque glaze, overpainted by hand; earthenware chips to show the clay body (pale or reddish). Period Deco pieces show confident brush-painted lines with slight irregularity; post-war pieces are glossier, thinner-painted florals. Matte and semi-matte glazes are common on the 1930 lines.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips and flakes (very common), crazing, hairlines, worn paint on handles and rims, restored chips filled and overpainted (check under UV or for colour mismatch). Damage takes 50–80% off design pieces.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes; the risk is misattribution — sellers label any geometric SMF as "Eva Zeisel". Mondrian decor appears on non-Zeisel shapes (Porcelain Marks & More). Attribute only by documented shape/decor (Cooper Hewitt objects, Zeisel literature).

## Market
Small, design-led market: mid-century/Bauhaus collectors and Zeisel collectors (US and Germany). Generic SMF sells slowly on eBay; documented Zeisel/Mondrian sells better on design platforms (1stdibs, Pamono, Chairish) and German eBay. Expect 30–90 days.

## What sellers usually miss
That the decor name in the mark (Mondrian, Gobelin, Malta) matters; that a stark geometric form may be a Zeisel shape. Most sellers call everything "vintage German vase" at $30 — which is fair for florals, cheap for 1930 design pieces.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile earthenware; double-box with 2" padding, $12–18. Lids and handles wrapped separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the full mark; transcribe decor name and numbers; give height, condition (chips, crazing) and the factory history. Attribute to Zeisel only with a documented shape/decor reference.

### What to tell the buyer
"Schramberger Majolikafabrik (SMF), Schramberg, Germany, hand-painted earthenware, c. 1928–1935, stamped SMF shield mark 'Schramberg Handgemalt' with decoration name '[name]' and painted numbers. Height X in. Condition: [chips/crazing]. [Design attributed to Eva Zeisel, who worked at Schramberg 1928–1930, by comparison with documented examples — or: designer unattributed.]"

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$15 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A geometric Deco piece at ~$120 sold → buy ≤ $25; a Zeisel-documented piece at ~$250 → buy ≤ $85. Florals/landscapes never clear. Require a base photo with the mark and no chips. Vases fit a passenger seat.
