## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Decorative faux fruit. Three families:
- **1960s–70s pinned/sequin fruit**: Styrofoam forms covered with sequins and seed beads held by straight pins — home craft kits and Hong Kong/Japan imports. Visible pin heads in rows; sequin sheen. Popular c. 1940s to mid-1970s; hexagonal beads pinned with steel pins, some capped with gem-style push pins (Apartment Therapy). Kits such as Walco's "Do-It-Yourself Beaded Fruit" (early 1970s; Walco was a New York bead company) supplied forms, beads, sequins and pins.
- **Wired glass-bead fruit/grapes** (Czech, Italian, Japanese, 1920s–60s): grapes and berries of blown or pressed glass beads on wire stems; heavier; true collector item.
- **Modern sugared / microbead fruit** (1990s–now, Pottery Barn, Hobby Lobby, Kirkland's): foam fruit rolled in tiny glass or plastic microbeads giving a frosted, granular "sugared" look; no pins, uniform granular coating, plastic stems.
Note: vintage "sugared/frosted" fruit also existed mid-century — small smooth beads glued on rather than pinned, more translucent (Apartment Therapy). Age then rests on the forms (Styrofoam, real-looking plastic leaves, wire stems wrapped in floral tape) and wear rather than the coating alone.

Styles: realistic (a yellow banana with brown "ripe" spots) and deliberately fanciful (purple pears, metallic colours) both exist.

## Makers, brands and variants
Mostly unbranded. Glass-bead fruit: Czech (Bohemian) and Italian (Venetian) workshops; Japanese export. Modern: Pottery Barn, Williams-Sonoma, Hobby Lobby, generic Chinese import.
- **Walco** (New York bead company; "Do-It-Yourself Beaded Fruit" kits by the early 1970s; business passed to the Holiday Company c. 1976): kits in original boxes are collected in their own right (Terapeak: "Vintage 1973 Walco Beaded Fruit Craft" $33.99).
- **Push-pin sequin fruit**: home-made from kits; quality varies from tight, even rows to sloppy.
- **Czech glass fruit beads**: small moulded glass fruit beads (used in jewellery and fruit sprays) — different from large pinned fruit.

### Quick ID checklist
- Zoom on the surface: rows of pin heads and overlapping sequins = MCM push-pin; individual glass beads strung on wire = glass-bead fruit; even granular frost with no pins = sugared (usually modern).
- Weight: glass-bead grapes are noticeably heavy and cold; foam fruit is feather-light.
- Stems/leaves: wire wrapped in floral tape and felt or velvet leaves (older) vs moulded plastic stems (modern).
- Count pieces and sizes; matched sets of 10–20+ pieces sell better than odd lots.
- Kit box present? Note the brand (Walco) and year.
- Bead loss: bare patches of foam show in photos.
- Colour: fanciful metallic colours and realistic ripe-spot detailing both point to MCM pin work.

### Golden-egg tells
- Large set of MCM push-pin sequin fruit (20+ pieces, intact) — ~2–3× a small mixed lot (Terapeak lots median $22; general knowledge, verify) — many matching pieces with full pin rows and no bare foam.
- Wired glass-bead grape clusters (Czech/Italian) — ~$20–60 each vs $2–6 per modern piece (entry tiers) — individual glass beads on wire, heavy, cold, some bead colour variation.
- Unused boxed beaded-fruit kit (Walco, 1970s) — ~$30+ sold vs ~$15 for a loose lot (Terapeak examples) — original box, sealed bags, instructions.
- Mid-century sequin fruit in fanciful metallic colours (gold, silver, purple) — decorator premium (verify) — metallic sequins with pin heads.
- Modern sugared microbead fruit — the common version, $15–35 a lot — uniform granular coating, plastic stems.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Glass beads cold to touch, heavier, individual beads on wire = old/valuable. Sequins pinned in overlapping rows = 1960s craft (modest). Even granular frosting with no individual beads/pins = modern (low).
- Old pins oxidise and may leave rust stains; yellowed Styrofoam shows at bare spots.
- Old sequins are often faceted cup sequins; modern ones are flat and very shiny plastic.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Shed beads, crushed foam, sun fading, missing leaves/stems, dust. Glass beads chip. Rusted pins stain foam; regluing loose beads shows glue shine.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern sugared fruit is often listed as "vintage". New Chinese glass-bead grapes exist (lighter, uniform plastic-coated wire).

## Market
Seasonal décor; moderate volume, low prices. Order of magnitude 50–150 sold/month. Trend interest from MCM/"grandmillennial" decorators (Apartment Therapy calls it a resurging trend); widely available on Etsy, Mercari and eBay, which caps prices.

## What sellers usually miss
Real wired glass-bead grapes mixed into faux-fruit lots.

## Shipping / handling
Light but bulky; box padded, $10–15.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Count and name the pieces; specify sequin/pin, glass bead or modern sugared; note bead loss.

### What to tell the buyer
State the construction plainly ("hand-pinned sequins and beads on foam forms, mid-century" or "glass beads on wire" or "glued sugared bead coating"). Count and name every piece and give the size of the largest. Disclose bare spots, missing leaves and rusted pins with close-ups. If a kit, give the brand, year and what is included. Do not call sugared fruit "vintage" without evidence such as Styrofoam forms and taped wire stems.

## Buy rule
Needs median ≥ ~$95 at a $10 price after ~$25 shipping total — only large vintage glass-bead lots qualify. Pass on modern sugared fruit.
