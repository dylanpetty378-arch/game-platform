# Japanese bronze vases (Meiji / Taisho / Showa archaistic & mixed-metal)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: appraisily Meiji bronze vase guide (tiers + repro tells), 1stdibs/catawiki listings, eBay sold memory 2024-25

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Dark brown/black patinated bronze, 8–18" tall. Two families: (a) **archaistic** – Chinese-bronze-style taotie/key-fret bands, bird- or elephant-head lug handles, pear or baluster body (what sellers call "champleve", "brass urn", "oriental vase"); (b) **mixed-metal / relief** – cranes, dragons, irises, Mt Fuji in high relief, sometimes gold/silver inlay (zogan). Signature (if any) is a cartouche of 2–4 kanji on the base. Seller words: "brass vase", "bronze urn", "Japan", "Chinese", "champleve".

## What sellers usually miss
Call it "brass" or "Chinese". Don't photograph the base (signature) or the inside (coarse sand = repro). Weight: real Meiji castings are heavy and have nearly invisible seams; cheap repros have crude interior seams and bright uniform gold.

## Shipping / handling
8–15 lb, sturdy – double box, ~$25–40 in. Resell on eBay (13%) with base/signature photos; collectors want interior shots.

## Buy rule
Buy when: crisp casting + bird/elephant handles + 12"+ tall and price ≤ $60, or any visible kanji signature with price ≤ $150. Pass when: thin, uniform shiny patina, under 8", or seller already says "Meiji signed". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).
