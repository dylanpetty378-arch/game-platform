## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small turned or boxed sycamore wood souvenirs made in and around Mauchline, Ayrshire, Scotland, c. 1820s–1933 (factory fire 1933 ended Smiths' production). Types:
- **Tartan ware**: covered with tartan pattern — first hand-painted (pre-1840s, rare), then machine-printed paper tartan glued on and heavily varnished (1840s–1900). Usually a **clan tartan name** is printed in small gold/black letters on a seam or band ("McBeth", "Stuart", "Prince Charlie", "McDonald"). Edges have a thin black paper/painted band.
- **Transfer ware**: plain varnished sycamore with a black transfer print of a tourist view (seaside towns, churches) — the most common.
- **Fern ware** (fern-leaf stencil), **photographic ware**, **Black lacquer** with views, **Tunbridge-like** pieces.
Forms: needle cases, spectacle cases (elongated oval hinged with brass pins), thread and pin boxes, snuff boxes with "invisible" wooden hinges, stamp boxes, egg cups, napkin rings, bookmarks, card cases, tape measures, darning eggs, sewing companions.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **W. & A. Smith, Mauchline** (c. 1820s–1933) — principal maker; tartan names usually printed.
- **Archibald Brown & Co.**, **Davidson, Wilson & Amphlet** (Mauchline), **Clark of Lanark**, **Charles Stiven, Laurencekirk** (snuff boxes, hidden hinges).
- Later imitations: Continental/French tartan-print boxes, 20th-c. Japanese/German tartan souvenirs, modern plastic-coated tartan.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Real: sycamore (pale, close-grained) visible inside or at worn edges; many layers of varnish with a yellowed glow; paper tartan with fine printed lines, matched (or slightly mismatched) at seams; tartan name printed; brass pin hinges on cases.
- Interiors of needle/spectacle cases are often plain wood or tartan-lined.
- Later/cheap: plastic film, printed tin, uniform glossy finish, no clan name, staples, modern glue.
- Hand-painted early tartan (no paper, softer lines) commands a premium.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Varnish crazing and wear at edges (expected), lifted or torn paper tartan, black edging rubbed, splits along grain, missing/loose hinge pins, overvarnished restorations, modern touch-ups with marker. Sold prices drop 30–60% for torn paper.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern reproductions are rare but tartan "style" boxes from the 1950s–80s (Japan, Hong Kong, UK souvenir) and decoupage DIY pieces exist. Tell: no clan name, fabric rather than paper, plastic film surface, screw hinges.

## Market
Small, steady collector market (UK strongest; US eBay tens of sales per month). Good pieces sell in 1–4 weeks; worn pieces linger. Sewing-tool collectors overlap.

## What sellers usually miss
- That paper tartan + clan name = 19th-century Scottish Mauchline ware, not "plaid" decor.
- Transfer-printed views name the town — collectors buy by location.
- Sewing tools inside cases (tape measure, needles) add value.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size, a few ounces. Bubble-wrap in a small box (~$6–9). Low transit risk unless crushed.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the tartan (read the printed name), form, size, maker if known (Smith of Mauchline), and photograph edges, seams, interior and every tear. State "19th century, paper tartan over sycamore".
