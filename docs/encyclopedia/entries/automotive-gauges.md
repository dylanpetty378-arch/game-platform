## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Round dial gauges in steel or chrome cups. Motorcycle OEM tachs (Nippon Seiki = Japanese OEM instrument maker; supplied Honda especially) have "x1000 rpm", a red zone, model-specific redline and often indicator lamps. Car aftermarket tachs (Sun, AutoMeter, Stewart-Warner) are electronic (wired) or mechanical.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Nippon Seiki: Honda CB/CL 1960s–70s (CB160, CB350, CL350, CB450, CB500/550/750 fours), Kawasaki triples, Yamaha twins. Redline position and face text identify the model; part numbers on the back (e.g., 37250-xxx-xxx for Honda).
- Sun Electric "Super Tach" (1960s muscle cars) – very collectible; Stewart-Warner "Wings" and "Green Line"; AutoMeter "Pro-Comp" (1970s–80s, Monster tach); Smiths (British cars/bikes), VDO (German), Jaeger (French/Italian).
- Mechanical (cable) vs electronic (wire leads) — mechanical can be tested by spinning the input with a drill.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original faces, unfaded red zones, clear lenses, intact rubber cushions and chrome cups. Aftermarket repro faces exist. Back case should show part numbers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fogged/yellowed lenses, faded faces, bouncing needles (dried damping), broken cable input, odometer gears (speedo), dented cups. Professional gauge restoration typically costs $100–200 (general knowledge, verify), which caps resale of unrestored units.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large volume of aftermarket reproduction Honda gauges (made in Taiwan/China) with near-identical faces; tells: lighter case, generic back without part number, different font, cheaper chrome.

## Market
Strong buyer base (restorers) but very fitment-specific; untested units sell at a discount; ~20–60 sales per model/month.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers rarely know which bike it came from; red-zone position + scale + face details give the model; NOS boxed units hiding in garage parts piles.

## Shipping / handling
Small, ~1 lb, $8–12. Pack lens carefully.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give part number, model fitment, mechanical/electronic, tested (show video spinning cable input), lens/face condition.

## Buy rule
This is a "tested" problem: Dylan avoids untested electronics; a mechanical tach can be spin-tested but the seller must show it. At $60 asking, needs sold median ≥ ~$150 to make $50: only CB750/rare-model or NOS boxed units qualify.
