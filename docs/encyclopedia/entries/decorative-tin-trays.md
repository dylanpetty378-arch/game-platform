## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thin steel sheet printed with lithographed pictures and pressed into trays, bowls, coasters and canisters. Daher Decorated Ware (Long Island City NY, importing from England, marked "Made in England", often with a year like 1971) dominates US listings. Patterns: Watteau-style courting couples in panels, roses, Dutch scenes, Christmas holly, florals on black.
- **Daher:** marked on the underside "Daher Decorated Ware, Long Island City, NY, Made in England" plus a year (1950s–80s). Scalloped or round bowls, 8–13" across.
- **Elite (England), Worcester Ware, Ballonoff, Chein (USA), Ohio Art:** similar decorative litho tin.
- **Antique toleware:** hand-painted tin, often black ground with gold stencilling, 1800s. Brush strokes visible, no print dots.
- **Advertising serving trays:** printed with a brand (Coca-Cola, beer, soda), made by American Art Works, Chas. W. Shonk, Kaufmann & Strauss. These are a different, collectible class.

## Makers, brands and variants
Daher (1950s–80s), Elite Ware, Worcester Ware (England, 1960s–80s), Ballonoff, Chein, Ohio Art, Baret Ware. Antique: Pontypool and American toleware workshops. Advertising: American Art Works (Coshocton OH), Shonk, Electro Chemical Engraving.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Litho tin: print screen dots under a loupe, glossy varnish, crisp registration lines. Mass-produced.
- Hand-painted toleware: brush strokes, raised paint, irregular gilding, and hand-crimped edges.
- Advertising trays: maker's name and city printed on the rim edge, often a copyright date.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust spots, scratches on the print, dents in the rim, and faded varnish. Repainting or "inpainting" lowers the value of toleware and advertising trays a lot.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
**Coca-Cola and beer trays are heavily reproduced** (1970s–2000s reprints of early designs). Reprints are thinner and have bright, flat colour and often a modern copyright line. Daher-type decorative tin is not faked; it is just common.

## Market
Daher-type bowls are everywhere at thrift stores and sell slowly. Buyers are cottagecore/vintage decor shoppers.

## What sellers usually miss
"Victorian" in the title usually just means the Watteau-style picture. The picture is Victorian style; the bowl is 1960s–70s. The real money is in old advertising trays, which owners sometimes lump in with decor tins.

## Shipping / handling
Light (under 1 lb), flat-ish. $6–9 in a padded box (general knowledge, verify). Protect the rim from denting.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Quote the underside mark and year, diameter, pattern name, and any rust or scratches.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$8 to buyer − ~$8 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A Daher bowl at $16 nets a loss before the price. Pass all decorative litho tin. Advertising trays need care with reproductions; buy only when the maker's rim mark is legible in photos and the price is ≤ 25% of verified sold.
