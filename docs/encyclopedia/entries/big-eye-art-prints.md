## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- 1950s–70s "big eyes" kitsch: children (waifs in rags, clowns, ballerinas, harlequins), women, cats, dogs and owls with enormous, often tear-filled eyes. Huge mail-order and department-store decorator category 1960–1975; revived by the 2014 film *Big Eyes*.
- The overwhelming majority are **offset lithographs**: smooth, uniform surface, rosette dot pattern under a loupe, printed signature in the lower corner. Many were glued to **textured "canvas-look" board** with fake brush ridges, then put in walnut/oak or gold-lip frames (e.g., pid 7949360137).
- **Pencil-signed limited editions**: signature in graphite and an edition number (e.g., 87/295) in the margin; Keane later editions come with Keane Eyes Gallery certificates.
- **Original paintings**: raised impasto you can see in raking light, canvas weave at the edges, brush hairs, signature painted in the paint layer (not over it), stretcher bars and canvas tacks/staples on the back, often an artist or gallery label. These are what is worth hunting.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Margaret Keane (1927–2022)**: the originator of the style; signed "KEANE" in block capitals for her husband Walter in the 1950s–60s; after the 1965 split and the 1986 court case she signed "MDH Keane" (Margaret D. H. Keane) (Wikipedia). Vintage 1960s offset prints of her children ("The Stray", "Waiting", "Lost", "Ballerina", "Our Children") are the commonest sellers: $6–40 (Terapeak comps $6–20 for loose lithos). Later pencil-signed lithographs/giclées and serigraphs sold via Keane Eyes Gallery: $250–1,500 (general knowledge, verify). Original oils: five to six figures at auction (general knowledge, verify).
- **Walter Keane**: claimed the work; "Walter Keane" or "Keane" signed prints exist; same print value as Margaret's; anything claimed as a Walter original needs authentication.
- **Lee** ("LEE" signature, 1960s–70s): very common big-eye children/women prints, often in pairs; prints $10–35; originals on canvas exist and sell a few hundred dollars (general knowledge, verify).
- **Gig** ("GIG", 1960s–70s): big-eye children, cats and dogs; prints $10–30; originals $150–600 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Eden** ("EDEN", 1960s): children and harlequins; prints $10–30; originals $200–800 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Maio**, **Franca/Ozz Franca** (Brazilian-born, 1960s–80s big-eye women, soft-focus), **Dallas Simpson**, **Lisa**, **Hal**, **Elena**: similar print values; Ozz Franca original paintings $300–2,000 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Igor Pantuhoff (1911–1972)**: Russian-born society portraitist who painted big-eyed girls in the 1950s–60s; associated with Margaret Keane's early years (Wikipedia). Originals $1,000–10,000+ (general knowledge, verify); prints low.
- **Medeiros** (printed signature "Medeiros"; Lens names Myrle Medeiros, not independently confirmed): 1960s–70s big-eye boys and girls in patched/ragged clothes, often in tall narrow formats and sold as boy/girl pairs; prints on textured board in wood frames $15–35 single, $35–70 pair (general knowledge, verify; Lens median $34).
- **Unsigned / store brands**: Turner Mfg., Windsor Art, Donald Art Co., Holly & Ivy-type publisher labels on the back; big-eye animals (cats, poodles, owls), velvet versions. $5–25.
- **Japanese/Hong Kong paint-by-number and velvet big-eyes**: low ($10–30) unless exceptional.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Print vs original**: printed signature sits under the varnish and is identical on every copy; a reverse search showing many identical examples = print. Textured board shows a regular, repeated "brush" pattern that does not follow the image.
- **Original tells from photos**: brush strokes follow the forms, paint ridges catch light, edges of the canvas wrap around the stretcher with unpainted margins, signature in wet-looking paint, often a pencil/ink title or price on the stretcher.
- **Pencil-signed editions**: graphite sheen on the signature, number fraction, deckled or wide white margins.
- **Age**: 1960s textured-board prints have brown kraft paper dust covers, sawtooth hangers, wire on screw eyes, and often a publisher/frame shop sticker; 1990s–2010s reprints have staples, MDF and bright, sharp colour.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Fading (reds go orange/pink), yellowed varnish, water stains, textured-board delamination and cracking, frame corner separation, broken glass where glazed.
- Originals: craquelure, flaking, punctures, overpainting. Restoration needs a professional – pass on anything needing it.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Keane prints are reprinted constantly; modern posters and giclées sold as "vintage" are common.
- Forged "KEANE" signatures on generic big-eye paintings are a known problem; any Keane "original" or signed edition needs Keane Eyes Gallery paperwork or an expert opinion. That is professional authentication: outside Dylan's rules.
- Hand-embellished prints (a few brush strokes over a print) are sold as "paintings" – look for the dot pattern under the strokes.

## Market
- Prints: steady but cheap; many listings, low sell-through, buyers want pairs and good original frames. Shipping framed pieces eats most of the margin.
- Originals and signed Keane editions: thin, collector-driven market; buyers are kitsch/MCM collectors and Keane fans; 2014 *Big Eyes* film and Margaret Keane's 2022 death raised interest.
- Not seasonal.

## What sellers usually miss
- Sellers over-ask on prints (they assume "Keane" = valuable). The real miss is the reverse: estate sellers listing an actual **painting** by Lee, Gig, Eden, Franca or Pantuhoff as "big eye print" or "60s picture". Check every listing for canvas texture, stretcher bars on the back, and an uneven, painted signature.
- Pairs split up lose value; a seller offering both halves together is worth more than double a single.

## Shipping / handling
- Framed pieces 12x24" to 24x36": box with corner protectors and foam board; tape an X on any glass or remove glass. $15–30 shipped within the US, more for large frames. Loose prints ship flat in rigid mailers for ~$6–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- State clearly whether it is a print (offset lithograph) or an original, and how you know (loupe photo, back photo).
- Photograph the signature close-up, the back with labels/dust cover, dimensions of image and frame, and any fading or frame wear.
- Name the artist only as the signature reads; for Keane do not claim originality or signed editions without paperwork.
- For pairs, title both subjects and sell together.

## Buy rule
- Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price; pass unless profit ≥ max($50, price). With a ~$25 median and $15–25 shipping each way, **no single big-eye print ever qualifies**; even a good pair at $80 resale nets ~$0–30 at a $10 buy price. Pass.
- Buy only an apparent original painting by a named big-eye painter (Lee, Gig, Eden, Maio, Franca, Pantuhoff) that shows paint texture, canvas edges and stretcher bars in photos, priced ≤ $150 and ≤ 30% of estimated value, fitting the passenger seat. Ask for a raking-light close-up and a photo of the back before paying.
- Pass on anything sold as a Margaret or Walter Keane original or signed edition without Keane Eyes Gallery paperwork – it needs professional authentication.
- Pass on damaged originals needing restoration.
