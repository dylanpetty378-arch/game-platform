## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Pre-1920 gas/electric combination**: arms pointing up (gas) and down (electric), cast brass, often with acid-etched or art-glass shades on 2¼-in fitters.
  - Combination fixtures were used from the 1880s into the early 20th century so electric light could be used with gas as a backup (Ingenium). Gas tells: a small decorative gas key (often openwork) on each gas arm, a gas burner tip or candle-slip socket, and a hollow pipe arm. Electric tells: a brass socket with a key switch; early sockets may be marked "EDISON PATENTS" and keys "G.E. Co." (Ingenium example dated 1882–1910).
- **1910s–30s Arts & Crafts / slip-shade**: cast-iron or brass with polychrome paint; shades slip into cups; schoolhouse globes; "Virden", "Lincoln", "Moe Bridges", "Riddle" fixtures.
- **Mid-century Spanish / Florentine brass** (1950s–70s): dark-patinated cast brass scrolls with acanthus leaves, 6–15 candle arms, hanging cut-glass prisms; made in Spain for export. Very common in estate sales.
  - Look underneath for a cast or stamped origin mark; one example is stamped "MADE IN ESPAIN" (Chairish). Heavy cast (not stamped) arms and tiered crystal drops are typical.
- **Modern brass coach lanterns** (1970s–90s): polished brass hexagonal lantern with clear/bevelled glass, eagle finial, candelabra socket on a wall plate, swag chain. Builder-grade; from Progress, Thomas, Nulco, Sea Gull.
- Photo tells for any era: socket type and markings, wire (cloth vs plastic), canopy size (gas-era canopies cover a threaded gas pipe), and whether fittings are cast or stamped sheet.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Pre-1930 makers: Mitchell Vance, Cornelius & Baker, Gill Brothers, Handel (signed shades), Jefferson, Pittsburgh, Lightolier (early), Beardslee, Virden, Moe Bridges.
- Mid-century: unsigned Spanish brass; Italian tole (painted metal flowers); Lightolier and Moe modern.
- Coach lanterns: Progress, Sea Gull, Thomas, Nulco — low value.
- Shades that multiply value: Quezal (Queens NY 1902–1924, wheel-engraved "Quezal" on the polished fitter rim per CMOG), Steuben Aurene, Lustre Art, Handel. See the antique-lamp-shades entry.
- Value nuances: matched pairs and sets of sconces > singles; original gas keys and shade holders present; original finish (not stripped and lacquered); signed shades that belong to the period of the fixture.

### Quick ID checklist
- Gas keys or burner tips on some arms → gas/electric combination (pre-1920).
- Socket: brass shell with key switch, maker name (Bryant, Hubbell, Perkins, GE/"Edison Patents") vs plastic socket.
- Wire: cloth-covered vs modern plastic; UL tag (modern or rewired).
- Arms and body: heavy cast brass vs thin stamped brass or brass-plated steel.
- Underside: "Made in Spain/Espain" (mid-century Spanish), "Taiwan", or a maker label.
- Shades: fitter size (2¼" common), signature inside the fitter rim.
- Finish: dark original patina, polychrome paint, or bright lacquer (new or stripped).
- Overall size versus a passenger seat (most chandeliers fail).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old: heavy cast brass with thin cloth-covered or old rubber wire, paper-shell sockets with stamped maker names (e.g. "Hubbell", "Bryant", "Benjamin"), screw holes for gas pipes.
- New: lightweight stamped brass or brass-plated steel, lacquer coating that peels, modern UL tags, plastic sockets.
- Glass: hand-blown with bubbles/acid-etched patterns vs moulded.
- Socket dating (Appraisily, verify): brass shells with porcelain insulation from the 1880s, typical through the 1920s; key switches c.1890s–1920s; pull chains c.1900–1915 onward, standard after c.1910; Bakelite parts 1920s onward; maker ranges Bryant 1888–1940s, Perkins 1890s–1930s, Hubbell 1888–present, GE 1890s–present (early ones with Edison patent dates), Eagle Electric 1920s–1960s. Cloth cords suggest pre-1950s. Early sockets used mica and resin-impregnated cardboard insulation (Ingenium).
- Modern UL markings and overly perfect finishes suggest a reproduction or a rewire (Appraisily).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Almost all antique fixtures need rewiring (UL code), which is a repair; missing prisms; broken arms; bent canopies; mismatched shades.
- Missing gas keys or burner tips on combination fixtures; capped gas arms.
- Stripped and re-lacquered brass (loses period patina); replaced canopy or chain.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Rejuvenation and others reproduce period fixtures; Chinese "antique style" crystal chandeliers flood the market. Shades are often married to fixtures.
- Reproductions use modern sockets and UL-listed wiring from the factory; check casting weight and screw types.

## Market
Local market (Facebook Marketplace, lighting dealers) dominates because shipping chandeliers is costly and risky. eBay sells sconces and shades.
- Restored and rewired pieces sell through dealers (Chairish, 1stDibs) at retail; as-found pieces sell to restorers at a fraction.

## What sellers usually miss
Signed shades (look for "Handel", "Steuben", "Quezal", "Lustre Art" etched inside the fitter rim) and early gas/electric fixtures sold as "old light".
- Maker-marked early sockets and gas keys that date a fixture pre-1920.
- Matched pairs of sconces split across listings.

## Shipping / handling
Chandeliers: oversize, $60–150 with crating. Sconces: $15–25 per pair. Prisms must be removed and wrapped.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State whether rewired, socket type, dimensions, number of arms, glass condition; recommend an electrician installs it.

### What to tell the buyer
Describe the type and era with the evidence ("gas/electric combination fixture, c.1890–1910: gas keys on the upturned arms, brass key-switch sockets marked 'Edison Patents'"; "mid-century Spanish cast brass, stamped 'Made in Spain' underneath"). Give overall height, width/spread, canopy diameter, number of arms/sockets, socket type and fitter size. State plainly whether it has been rewired; if not, say it is sold as-found for display or restoration and should be rewired and installed by a licensed electrician. List missing prisms, keys or shades and any signatures on shades.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price.
- **Chandeliers fail the size rule and almost always need rewiring: pass.**
- Coach-lantern pairs (~$40) never clear $50: pass.
- Small pre-1930 sconce pairs or signed shades (median ~$250) at ≤ $60: 217 − 40 − 60 = $117 (Tier A). Only if no rewiring is needed to sell (sold "as found").
