## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Silver certificates**: blue seal and blue serial numbers. $1 (1935, 1957 series), $5 (1934, 1953), $10 (1934, 1953). Star notes (★ in the serial) and the 1928/1934 $1 "Funnyback" are the premium ones.
- **Morgan dollars**: 1878–1921, 0.7734 oz silver. Value = date/mint (CC mint, 1893-S, 1889-CC) + grade. Slabs graded by PCGS/NGC are trusted; ANACS and others are discounted.
- **Mexico 1947–48 5 pesos Cuauhtemoc**: 30 g .900 = 0.868 oz ASW.
- **US Mint proof sets** 1980s–90s: $5–15 each (1989 ~$6).

Seller words: "silver dollar", "old money", "blue seal dollar". Look-alikes: red-seal US notes, brown-seal and yellow-seal WWII notes, and green-seal Federal Reserve notes. These are different series; 1934 Hawaii and North Africa notes are a premium category (general knowledge, verify). Morgans vs Peace dollars (1921–35) and Eisenhower "silver" dollars, which are mostly clad (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **$1 silver certificates**: 1935 (A–H series letters) and 1957 (A, B) are the common ones. 1928 series and 1934 $1 Funnybacks carry a premium. Star notes in CU are $20–60.
- **$5 and $10 certificates**: 1934 and 1953 series. Circulated $5 is $8–15; $10 carries a modest premium (general knowledge, verify).
- **Morgan dollars**: mint marks are under the eagle on the reverse (CC, S, O, D, none = Philadelphia) (general knowledge, verify). Keys include 1893-S, 1889-CC and 1895 proof-only. CC mints in MS are top tier.
- **Mexican 5 pesos 1947–48**: bullion value.
- **Proof sets**: 1980s–90s clad sets are $5–15. Silver proof sets (1992+ silver, pre-1965) are worth more (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Morgans are 90% silver. Natural toning and even wear on high points (hair above the ear, eagle's breast) are normal.
- Cleaned coins (hairlines, unnatural shine) sell at a discount.
- Notes: crispness, folds, tears, stains and pinholes drive grade. Circulated notes are worth little over face value premium.
- Slabs: check the PCGS/NGC certification number on their websites.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Coins: cleaning, polishing, damage, ex-jewellery solder marks and holes cut value to melt.
- Notes: tears, tape, writing, pinholes and heavy folds. Pressed or "doctored" notes are a risk at high grades.
- Ask for both sides in good light, the slab cert number, and close-ups of the date and mint mark.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit Morgans are rampant, especially Chinese-made fakes of CC and key dates. They have the wrong weight (26.73 g is correct), wrong ring and wrong diameter (38.1 mm) (general knowledge on specs, verify). Fake slabs exist, so verify cert numbers. Altered mint marks are added to common dates. Must authenticate before paying.

## Market
Very liquid. eBay, coin shops and dealer grey-sheet set prices, and bullion items track spot silver daily. Raw common Morgans sell quickly. Notes are slow for low grades. Coin sellers almost always overprice. No seasonality except silver-spot swings (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Nothing; coin sellers almost always overprice. The only arbitrage is key dates or star notes that are mislabelled, or bulk "old coins" lots sold by weight.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, cheap, insured. eBay 13% (coins category ~13%). Must authenticate before paying; counterfeit Morgans are rampant.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: "[Year]-[Mint] Morgan Silver Dollar [grade/slab]" or "Series 1935[letter] $1 Silver Certificate [star/grade]". State the date and mint, weight (coins), slab cert number, serial and series (notes), and any cleaning or damage. Photograph both sides at high resolution, with no stock photos.

## Buy rule
Buy when: silver is priced at or below melt (lots sold by weight), or a key-date/CC Morgan in a slabbed holder is under grey-sheet. Pass when: a single common silver cert is priced over $5, or a Morgan over $45 raw. The $45 cut-off was set at ~$35/oz silver; re-peg to current spot (Morgan melt = 0.7734 × spot). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
