## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tells: nib shape (hooded "51", inlaid Sheaffer "Triumph", white dot on Sheaffer clip = Lifetime), filling system (lever on side, button under end cap, plunger/Snorkel tube, cartridge), barrel material (celluloid striations, pearl/jade). The example: green plastic barrel, brushed steel cap with Sheaffer clip — 1960s–70s Sheaffer School/Cartridge (Skripsert-type) pen, low value.

Filling-system clues in photos: a **lever** in a slot on the barrel side (sac pen, 1910s–50s); a **blind cap** at the barrel end hiding a button or a **Vacumatic** plunger; an **Aerometric** 51 shows a ribbed silver sac protector when the barrel is unscrewed with "PRESS 4 TIMES"-type wording; a **Snorkel** has a thin tube that twists out under the nib; **cartridge** pens have no filler at all.

## Makers, brands and variants
Parker (Duofold 1920s, Vacumatic 1933–48 striped, 51 1941–72, 61), Sheaffer (Lifetime white dot from 1924, Balance 1929–40s, Snorkel 1952–59, PFM, Targa), Waterman (52, Ideal), Wahl-Eversharp (Doric, Skyline), Esterbrook (J series, renew-point nibs), Conway Stewart (UK celluloid), Montblanc (Meisterstück 146/149), Pelikan 400.
- **Parker 51** — developed 1939, test-marketed 1940, launched January 1941, US production to ~1972 (South America into the 1980s); 2001 reissue differs. **Vacumatic filler** (earlier; diaphragm usually needs replacing) and **Aerometric** (from ~1948; more reliable, often priced higher). Caps: Lustraloy brushed steel (most common), gold-filled, sterling, solid gold. **Blue Diamond** on the clip until mid-1947 (FTC ruling). First-year pens: aluminium cap jewels, "Parker 51" imprint on the blind cap. **Double jewel** (jewels at both ends) sells 2–3× single-jewel. Special "51" from 1950 with Octanium steel nib (~$20 unrestored). Pre-production colours that entered production: Yellowstone Yellow, Nassau Green, Buckskin Beige (Collectors Weekly). Collector consensus on valuable colours: Vac — Buckskin, Mustard, Nassau Green; Aero — Plum, Cocoa, Forest Green, Plum highest; single-jewel Buckskin/Yellowstone/Nassau rarer than double-jewel; many "Nassau Green" pens are really Navy Gray (FPN).
- **Sheaffer** — white dot (Lifetime guarantee) introduced 1924. **Balance** from 1929: oversize with the long humped clip was phased out in 1931; Marine Green (green/black marble, introduced 1930), Jade, Pearl and Black, Carmine, Roseglow are the celluloid colours (restored oversize Marine Green $250 at Peyton Street). **Snorkel** 1952–59 (Australia to ~1962): twist-out tube; Triumph conical inlaid nib on top models; 16 nib grades offered; clear demonstrators exist for salespeople; boxed sets with manuals prized (Inked Happiness). **PFM** (Pen For Men, 1959–63) — big inlaid nib, collectible. **School/cartridge/Skripsert** pens — low.
- **Esterbrook** — J family (J, LJ, SJ sizes), lever fillers with screw-in **Renew-Point** nibs numbered on the nib (2xxx, 9xxx; 9000 series iridium-tipped). Restored LJ in copper with a 9555 Gregg nib: $50 (Peyton Street). Copper, Pastels and rare colours > black/blue.
- **Wahl-Eversharp** — Doric (1930s, faceted celluloid, Kashmir and other colours), Skyline (1940s; Dubonnet red and sterling caps rare; flexible 14k nibs common, a plus).
- **Waterman** — 52 (hard rubber, lever), Ideal; red ripple hard rubber > black.
- **Conway Stewart** (UK celluloid), **Pelikan 400**, **Montblanc** (authentication required).

### Quick ID checklist
- Nib: hooded tube (Parker 51), inlaid conical (Sheaffer Triumph/PFM), screw-in with a 4-digit number (Esterbrook), open nib marked "14K" vs steel.
- Clip: white dot (Sheaffer Lifetime), Blue Diamond (Parker pre-mid-1947), arrow clip (Parker).
- Barrel imprint: "PARKER 51 MADE IN USA" (and date code), "SHEAFFER'S", "Esterbrook".
- Filler: lever / blind cap / twist-out snorkel / cartridge.
- Material: striated or marbled celluloid, red-black ripple hard rubber, jade/pearl → better; plain injection plastic → later.
- Cap: Lustraloy, gold-filled, sterling (marked), solid gold (marked 14K) — check for dents.
- Jewels: one or two (Parker 51 double jewel).
- Restored? New sac/diaphragm mention, writing sample photo.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Parker 51 with solid-gold cap or rare colour (Buckskin, Mustard, Nassau Green Vac; Plum Aero)** — "can reach thousands" vs "most 51s well under $100" (Collectors Weekly) — colour of barrel, cap imprint "14K", Vac blind cap.
- **Parker 51 double jewel** — 2–3× single jewel (Collectors Weekly) — a jewel at the barrel end as well as the cap.
- **First-year / Blue Diamond Parker 51 (1941–47)** — premium over later Aeros (FPN) — blue diamond on the clip, aluminium jewel, blind-cap imprint.
- **Sheaffer Balance oversize Lifetime in Marine Green/Jade, long humped clip (1929–31)** — $250 restored (Peyton Street) vs $12 Sheaffer cartridge median — torpedo shape, fat barrel, white dot, humped clip.
- **Eversharp Skyline in Dubonnet red with sterling or grooved cap; flexible 14k nib** — dealer "rare" tier — wine-red barrel, sterling cap mark, Wahl imprint.
- **Sheaffer Snorkel Triumph / Valiant / Statesman, boxed or demonstrator** — premium over loose Snorkels (Inked Happiness: boxed sets highly prized) — conical inlaid nib, twist-out tube, clear barrel for demonstrators.
- **Sheaffer PFM** — well above school Sheaffers (general knowledge, verify) — large inlaid nib, "PFM" model, snorkel-like filler.
- **Esterbrook J in copper or pastel with a flex/specialty nib (e.g. 9128 extra-flexible fine)** — ~$50 restored vs ~$20 common black (Peyton Street; general knowledge) — copper-brown barrel, nib number on the nib.
- **Waterman 52 in red ripple hard rubber** — premium over black (general knowledge, verify) — red-and-black swirled hard rubber, lever.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Celluloid and hard rubber = earlier, better; injection plastic = later. Gold nib marked 14K vs steel. Gold-filled caps worth more than chrome. Celluloid can discolour (ambering of Vac stripes, darkened barrels) and gives off a camphor smell; hard rubber fades to brown-green with light exposure. Crisp barrel imprints add value; worn imprints lower it. Original nibs matter: many 51 nibs were replaced over the years (Collectors Weekly).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dried ink sacs (needs resac — a repair; restored pens sell at 2× unrestored), cracked barrels, bent nibs, brassing on plating, discolored celluloid. Also: Vacumatic diaphragms usually need replacing; Aerometric breather tubes clog and corrode; dents in gold-filled/sterling/gold 51 caps; engraved names lower value; cap-lip cracks on Balance pens (Peyton Street example); mismatched parts from dealer/repair swaps (Collectors Weekly). Unrestored pens need service before use.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Montblanc fakes common; Chinese 51 clones (Hero 616) — check "PARKER 51 MADE IN USA" on barrel. 2001 Parker 51 Special Edition is a legitimate reissue that differs from originals. Married caps and barrels are common.

## Market
Strong collector community; Parker 51 sells in days. Cheap school pens slow. Buyers: collectors and users; restored, writing-tested pens sell best.

## What sellers usually miss
Parker 51s in drawer lots; gold nibs; Sheaffer white dot. Double jewels, Blue Diamond clips, rare colours and specialty nib grades rarely named in titles.

## Shipping / handling
Tiny, ~$5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, model, filling system, nib grade/material, tested/untested filling, restoration status.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker model], [year range], [colour/material]. Filling: [lever/Vacumatic/Aerometric/Snorkel] — [restored with new sac/diaphragm on date / unrestored, sold as-is]. Nib: [14K gold/steel], [grade], [original/replacement]. Cap [Lustraloy/gold-filled/sterling], [dents?]. Imprint [crisp/worn], [date code]. [Single/double jewel]. No cracks [checked at cap lip and barrel threads]." Photograph the nib (both sides), the imprint, the clip, the filler and the cap lip.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): only mid/high-tier pens bought cheaply qualify; unrestored sac pens sell as-is (no repair). Fits pocket.
