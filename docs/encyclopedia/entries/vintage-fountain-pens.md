Buy rule (short): Buy Parker 51/Vacumatic, Sheaffer Snorkel/Balance, Wahl Doric and gold/celluloid pens under ~$15–30; pass cartridge pens; Montblanc requires authentication (excluded).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tells: nib shape (hooded "51", inlaid Sheaffer "Triumph", white dot on Sheaffer clip = Lifetime), filling system (lever on side, button under end cap, plunger/Snorkel tube, cartridge), barrel material (celluloid striations, pearl/jade). The example: green plastic barrel, brushed steel cap with Sheaffer clip — 1960s–70s Sheaffer School/Cartridge (Skripsert-type) pen, low value.

## Makers, brands and variants
Parker (Duofold 1920s, Vacumatic 1933–48 striped, 51 1941–72, 61), Sheaffer (Lifetime white dot, Balance 1929–40s, Snorkel 1952–59, PFM, Targa), Waterman (52, Ideal), Wahl-Eversharp (Doric, Skyline), Esterbrook (J series, renew-point nibs), Conway Stewart (UK celluloid), Montblanc (Meisterstück 146/149), Pelikan 400.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Celluloid and hard rubber = earlier, better; injection plastic = later. Gold nib marked 14K vs steel. Gold-filled caps worth more than chrome.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dried ink sacs (needs resac — a repair; restored pens sell at 2× unrestored), cracked barrels, bent nibs, brassing on plating, discolored celluloid.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Montblanc fakes common; Chinese 51 clones (Hero 616) — check "PARKER 51 MADE IN USA" on barrel.

## Market
Strong collector community; Parker 51 sells in days. Cheap school pens slow.

## What sellers usually miss
Parker 51s in drawer lots; gold nibs; Sheaffer white dot.

## Shipping / handling
Tiny, ~$5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, model, filling system, nib grade/material, tested/untested filling, restoration status.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): only mid/high-tier pens bought cheaply qualify; unrestored sac pens sell as-is (no repair). Fits pocket.
