## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hard-paste porcelain from dozens of factories in and around Limoges, France (1770s–present; boom 1870–1930 driven by US export). Thin, bright white, translucent body; gilt rims. Two marks are common on one piece:
- **Underglaze factory (blank) mark**, usually green, under the glaze: e.g. "H&Co" (Haviland, 1876–89), "HAVILAND FRANCE" (1894+), "T&V" with "France" or a bell/"DEPOSE" (Tressemann & Vogt 1891–1919, "T&V Limoges France" 1892–1907, bell mark 1907–19), "JPL" (Jean Pouyat 1890–1932), "D&Co" (Délinières), "CFH/GDM" (Haviland & Abbot / Gérard Dufraisseix & Morel), "AL" (Lanternier), "W.G.&Co" (Guérin), "Elite L France" (Bawo & Dotter), "LS&S" (Straus importers).
- **Overglaze decorator mark**, red/blue/brown/purple, on top: factory decorating marks (Haviland red script with date code, "T&V Limoges France" in a circle, "Coronet Limoges France" crown for Borgfeldt), or **American studio marks** (Pickard Chicago – "Pickard" with maple leaf or wreath, "Hand Painted Pickard"; Stouffer; Rogers; Osborne; White's Art Co.), or **nothing** with an amateur artist's signature and date on the front or back (e.g. "M. Smith 1902").
"Limoges France" alone (without a factory name) = post-1891. "Made in France" = c.1920s+. "Limoges China Co. – Sebring, Ohio" is **American** semi-vitreous dinnerware (see american-dinnerware), not French.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Haviland & Co.** (David Haviland 1842; Charles Field Haviland): dinnerware in thousands of patterns; Schleiger numbers identify patterns. Most singles sell $5–25 (Replacements sets the ceiling). Art pottery (Auteuil studio, barbotine 1870s–80s) is top tier.
- **Theodore Haviland** (1892–): dinnerware; "Theodore Haviland Limoges France" marks; **"Theodore Haviland New York"** marks = made in the USA (Shenango, 1936–58) – lower value.
- **Tressemann & Vogt (T&V)** (1891–1919, then Raynaud): huge supplier of **white blanks** sold to American hobby painters and studios; also factory-decorated Art Nouveau pieces. Most T&V vases/jardinieres on Craigslist are amateur-decorated.
- **Jean Pouyat (JPL)**: blanks and factory pieces; also widely painted by amateurs.
- **Coronet (Borgfeldt importer) / B&H / Flambeau / Lazeyras Rosenfeld & Lehman (LRL)**: factory-decorated game/fish/oyster plates and plaques signed "Dubois", "Max", "B. Albert", "René", "Muville", "Vignaud" – collectible.
- **Pickard (Chicago, 1898–)**: professional studio decorators (Yeschek, Campana, Aulich, Loh, Challinor, Marker, Vokral) on Limoges/Bavarian blanks until 1930 – the high-value American layer.
- **Stouffer (Chicago)**, Osborne, Ruth Rogers, White's Art Co. (Chicago), Atlan Ceramic Club – studio decorators.
- **Limoges boxes** (1960s–present): "Limoges France – Peint Main" (hand painted) with artist initials, makers Rochard, Chamart, Artoria, Parry Vieille, Dubarry, La Gloriette, Beauté, Tiffany for retail. $20–150; rare/large themes more.
- **Modern ornaments and giftware** (example 7931656864 ballet slipper): often not Limoges at all (Japanese/Chinese bisque with a "Limoges" sticker), or Limoges Castel/Haviland gift lines – $5–20.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Amateur vs. professional**: The single biggest value driver. Amateur (1880–1930 "china painting" craze, ~20,000 women in the US): heavier enamel, muddy shading, uneven gold, signature + full date ("1902") or initials on the front; designs copied from Keramic Studio magazine – "conventional" stylized flowers in flat colour outlined with gold (e.g. the iris vase 7954897115) and naturalistic roses/asters/daisies on a blended ground (7956847094, 7939879293). Professional/factory: crisp layered shading, raised paste-gold or etched gold borders, uniform gilding, decorator backstamp, artist surname only (Dubois, Max, Yeschek).
- **Transfer vs hand-painted**: look under magnification for printed dots (transfer) vs brush strokes; many factory "hand painted" pieces are transfer outlines with hand fill.
- **Gold**: worn gold on rims/handles is normal; rubbed or stained gold cuts 20–40%.
- **Old vs new**: old pieces have kiln grit on the foot, slight warping, underglaze green mark sunk in the glaze; modern boxes have crisp brass hinges and "Peint Main" stamps.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips to rims and feet, hairline cracks (tap test rings dull), gold wear, flaking enamel, firing flaws (common on amateur pieces – kiln specks), restored rims (matte patch under UV light), missing lids. Jardinieres/cachepots often have water staining and crazing inside from use as planters; vases may be drilled for lamps (cuts value 50%+). Limoges boxes: broken hinges, chipped clasps, missing inner charm.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Chinese "Limoges" boxes and figurines with "Limoges France" but no "Peint Main" or a misspelled mark; sold in gift shops 1990s–2010s for $10–30.
- Japanese/Chinese porcelain with "Limoges" stickers or decals.
- Fake Pickard marks added to amateur pieces (look for a mark in different ink, not fired in, or too crisp).
- Re-decorated blanks: modern china painters still paint old T&V blanks; a recent date or acrylic paint (scratches off) gives it away.
- Modern Haviland reissues (Haviland Parlon) of old patterns sell lower than original.

## Market
Soft for dinnerware and amateur work (supply far exceeds demand – every estate has some), steady for game/fish plates, strong and specialised for Pickard (Woody Auction, Pickard collectors). eBay hundreds of Limoges sales per month; median single piece ~$30. Little seasonality; Mother's Day and wedding season slight bumps for boxes.

## What sellers usually miss
- Sellers over-value "signed by artist 1902" – a dated signature is usually an amateur, which is the low tier.
- They under-value **Pickard/Stouffer** studio marks when listed as "Limoges vase" (the edge) – a red/gold "Pickard" or "Stouffer" mark on the base of a "Limoges" piece is the find; also "Coronet" game plates signed "Dubois" sold as "old plates".
- T&V/JPL white blanks themselves have modest value; the decoration determines price.
- "Limoges" from Sebring, Ohio (Limoges China Co.) is American and cheap.

## Shipping / handling
Vases/jardinieres 2–5 lb, double-box with 2 in of padding: $15–25 each way. Plates in plate-boxes $12–18. Breakage risk is real; insure over $100. eBay 13%; Pickard top pieces → regional auction (Woody, Strawser, Jeffrey S. Evans).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph both marks (blank and decorator) in focus; state "hand painted (amateur/studio) on a T&V Limoges blank" rather than implying factory work; give height, diameter, signature and date; disclose crazing, kiln specks, gold wear and any lamp-drilling. Don't write "Pickard" unless the Pickard mark is present.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$20) − ship to Dylan (~$20) − price ≥ max($50, price). Buy: any piece with a Pickard, Stouffer, or Haviland Auteuil mark, or a set of signed Coronet/LRL game/fish plates, at ≤ $60; large well-painted factory pieces at ≤ $40. Ask seller for a base photo when a "Limoges" vase/jardiniere with conventional gold-outlined design or professional-quality painting is priced ≤ $100 – the base decides. Pass: amateur-signed pieces over $25, dinnerware, ornaments, any Limoges box without "Peint Main", drilled lamps, cracked pieces. All fit a passenger seat. Not an authentication category, but Pickard over $300 should be checked against known artist signatures.
