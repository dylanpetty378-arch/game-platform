## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tin-glazed earthenware from Quimper, Brittany (France), made since 1690. Signature decor: a Breton peasant man (blue baggy breeches "bragou braz", wide-brimmed black hat) and woman (coiffe headdress, apron), flanked by stylised flowers, with blue-and-yellow sponged or banded rims, "dot-and-dash" borders, crests of Brittany. Mark painted on the base in blue/black: "HB Quimper", "HenRiot Quimper France", "HB-Henriot", "Porquier" (AP monogram), sometimes with painter's initials or number.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Grande Maison HB** (Hubaudière-Bousquet), 1700s–1983; "HB" and "HB Quimper" marks.
- **Henriot** (Jules Henriot, 1886–1984), "HR" then "HenRiot Quimper".
- **Porquier** (Adolphe Porquier, to 1904) — "AP" monogram, early and valuable.
- **HB-Henriot** merger 1984; **Henriot-Quimper** continues today.
- **Keraluc** (1946–1984), modern Breton studio pottery.
- **Odetta** (HB stoneware, 1920s–30s): art-deco grey/brown stoneware — valuable.
- **Look-alikes**: Italian hand-painted peasant pottery (1950s–70s), Japanese "Quimper style", Desvres (northern France) copies with fake marks, and "Dutch boy and girl" pottery from Italy/Czechoslovakia — none have Quimper value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real: soft buff/pinkish earthenware, thick milky tin glaze, fast confident brush strokes, figure faces with few strokes, characteristic blue and yellow band rims. Mark painted by hand, not stamped. Pre-1922 pieces have "HB" without "France"; "France" added for export after 1891 (McKinley Act) but often omitted. Modern ones are brighter, thinner glaze, "France" plus artist initials.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Glaze flakes on rims (faience is soft), chips, crazing, hairlines, overpainted repairs. Rim flaking is normal on old pieces and lowers price ~30–50%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Desvres made copies with HB-like marks; Asian copies. Henriot reissues old designs today. Many "Quimper-style" bowls from Italy and Japan show Dutch-style couples with fruit — clearly different costume (Dutch wooden shoes, pointed caps).

## Market
Quimper peaked in the 1990s; now moderate and slower. Marked HB/Henriot pieces sell in weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
Odetta stoneware (doesn't look like "Quimper"); Porquier "AP" marks; named-artist figurines (René-Yves Creston, Mathurin Méheut designs).

## Shipping / handling
