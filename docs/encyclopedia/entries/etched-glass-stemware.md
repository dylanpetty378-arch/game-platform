## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Clear stemware and pitchers decorated by:
- **Plate (acid) etching**: a lace-like design printed with wax resist and etched with acid — fine, detailed, even depth; the hallmark of 1920s–50s "Elegant Glass" (Fostoria, Cambridge, Heisey, Tiffin, Duncan, Central, Morgantown).
- **Wheel cutting / grey cutting**: simple daisies, leaves and stems ground with a copper wheel, left matte grey ("gray cut") — 1920s–70s, many small factories, unmarked (as in both sweep examples).
- **Needle etching**: machine-traced thin parallel lines in bands (1920s–50s).
- **Rock crystal** (Libbey Rock Sharpe, Hawkes) — polished deep cuts.
- **Sandblast / modern** (1970s–2000s): Toscany (Romania), Princess House Heritage, Lenox; heavy bases, thicker glass.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Fostoria** (Moundsville WV, 1887–1986): etchings Navarre, Chintz, Meadow Rose, Romance, June, Versailles; cuts Holly, Buttercup, Heather. Stems $10–35.
- **Cambridge** (Ohio, 1873–1958): Rose Point (most collected), Chantilly, Wildflower, Elaine, Diane, Apple Blossom. Rose Point stems $20–60; pitchers $150–400.
- **Heisey** (1896–1957): Orchid, Rose, Minuet; marked H in diamond on some stems. $15–50.
- **Tiffin** (1889–1980): Cherokee Rose, June Night, Fuchsia, Persian Pheasant. $12–30.
- **Duncan & Miller**, **Central Glass**, **Morgantown** (coloured stems, Golf Ball), **Seneca**, **Libbey**, **US Glass/Tiffin**.
- **Modern**: Toscany, Princess House, Mikasa, Lenox, Gorham — $3–15 each.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Elegant era: thin blown bowls, a ground and polished pontil or plain mould foot, very fine plate etching with border bands; rings when tapped.
- Pattern ID: compare etching and stem shape to Replacements.com; stem shape matters (same etching on several blanks).
- Coupe vs sherbet: coupes 5–6" tall, wider bowl; low sherbets lower value.
- Wheel-cut daisy-and-leaf on thin glass = generic 1930s–50s, unmarked.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips and flea bites (run a finger), cloudy dishwasher haze ("sick glass"), worn gold rims, cracks in stems, ground-down rims, mismatched heights in "sets".

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little faking of elegant patterns; Fostoria made some later reissues (Lenox era 1980s). Mixed "sets" assembled from similar patterns are common — check that the etching and stem shape match.

## Market
Champagne coupes have a renewed wedding/cocktail-culture demand; full sets sell; singles are slow. Replacements.com sets retail ceilings; eBay sold often 30–50% of Replacements retail. Holiday bump.

## What sellers usually miss
- Cambridge Rose Point and other premium etchings listed as "etched floral glasses".
- Matching pitchers/decanters that complete sets.
- Reverse: unmarked wheel-cut glass priced as "crystal".

## Shipping / handling
Very fragile; stems double-boxed with dividers. Set of 6 stems 4–5 lb $18–28; pitchers 3–4 lb $16–22.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Pattern name (Replacements ID), maker, height and rim diameter, count, condition (no chips, no haze), photos against a dark background to show etching.

## Buy rule
Shipping ~$20 each way means a set must sell for ~$140+ at a $25 buy. Buy only identifiable premium patterns in sets of 8+ at ≤ $5 per stem. Pass on unmarked wheel-cut and modern pitchers. All fit in a seat.
