## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Signed mid-century and 1970s designer pieces hiding in "costume jewelry" lots. Tells on the back: "RAFAEL CANADA" (Rafael Alfandary, Toronto 1970s – hammered brass/copper with coloured glass cabochons, kinetic fringe, often large), "AARIKKA Finland" (lathe-turned wood beads, 1970s–80s), "TRIFARI" (crown-T = 1950s–60s, plain = later), "Miriam Haskell", "Eisenberg", "Boucher", "Schiaparelli", "Hobé", "Juliana/DeLizza & Elster" (unsigned, 5-link construction), Scandinavian silver (David-Andersen, Lapponia), Mexican silver (Taxco, "TM-xx" marks). Lia Sophia, Premier Designs, Park Lane, Avon, Monet (plain) = $5–20. Sellers' words: "vintage necklace", "costume jewelry lot", "brass necklace", sometimes the maker name copied from the back.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Rafael of Canada** (Rafael Alfandary, Toronto, 1970s): hammered brass/copper with coloured or Murano glass cabochons, kinetic fringe; small pendants $25–80, large kinetic necklaces $80–250, lockets rarer; dealer asks $250–500 on 1stdibs.
- **Aarikka** (Finland, 1970s–80s): lathe-turned wooden beads, $30–80.
- **Trifari**: crown-T mark = 1950s–60s, collectible sets; plain mark later, decent tier.
- **Monet, Napier**: signed pieces, decent tier; plain Monet low.
- **Juliana / DeLizza & Elster**: unsigned rhinestone sets identified by 5-link construction; collectible.
- **Miriam Haskell, Schiaparelli, Eisenberg Original, Boucher (figurals), Hobé**: top tier.
- **Scandinavian silver** — Lapponia (Finland), David-Andersen (Norway, enamel): top tier sets.
- **Mexican silver** — Taxco, "TM-xx" assay/registration marks: cuffs in collectible tier.
- **Junk tier**: Lia Sophia, Premier Designs, Park Lane, Avon, unsigned mall jewelry.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Signature on clasp, hang tag or back plate (photograph it). Quality tells (general knowledge, verify): prong-set (not glued) stones on better rhinestone pieces; Haskell's hand-wired seed beads and filigree backs; Eisenberg's heavy rhodium plating and large Swarovski stones; Rafael's hand-hammered brass with glass set in bezels; sterling marks "925"/"Sterling" on Scandinavian and Taxco pieces. Old vs new: patina on brass, older clasp types (box clasps, fishhooks) vs modern lobster clasps.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing or dead (cloudy/foiled-off) rhinestones, plating wear down to base metal, green corrosion (verdigris) on brass and copper, broken fringe links on kinetic pieces, cracked wood beads, replaced clasps. Re-glued stones show glue haze; replaced stones mismatch colour (general knowledge, verify). Ask for a photo of the back/clasp and close-ups of stones.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Faked signatures exist for Haskell, Eisenberg, Schiaparelli and Chanel-style pieces; Juliana attributions are often wishful (general knowledge, verify). Some makers reissued designs (e.g. later Eisenberg Ice, modern Trifari) — mark style dates the piece. Mexican silver: plated "Alpaca" pieces sold as sterling Taxco.

## Market
Very high volume on eBay and Etsy; buyers are vintage-jewelry collectors, resellers and wearers (general knowledge, verify). Signed pieces sell steadily; unsigned lots move slowly unless priced cheaply. Modernist brass (Rafael) has crossover demand from mid-century-modern decor buyers. Gift seasons (Nov–Dec, Feb) help (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Signatures on the back – they rarely photograph it. A "brass and glass necklace" lot at $10 can hold a $150 Rafael. Ask for a photo of the clasp/back.

## Shipping / handling
Ounces; $5–8. eBay ~13%. Pad in a small box or bubble mailer; bag each piece so fringe doesn't tangle.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)

Photograph front on neutral background, back with mark, clasp, worn on a bust for scale.
