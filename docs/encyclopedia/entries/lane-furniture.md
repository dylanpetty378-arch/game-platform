## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
The Lane Company (Altavista, Virginia; founded 1912 as a cedar-chest maker) made mass-market mid-century modern lines in walnut and walnut veneer. **Acclaim** (1958–60s, André Bus): walnut tables and case pieces with distinctive inlaid "dovetail" key joints across the top. **Rhythm** (1960s): rounded, sculpted walnut forms. **Perception** (1960s): glass-topped walnut tables with wood frames. Lane also made "Mosaic", "Tuxedo", "Copenhagen" and Brutalist/1970s lines (general knowledge, verify). Serial/stamp inside a drawer or under the piece (per earlier summary). Lane cedar chests carry a stamped serial number on the underside that reads in reverse as the manufacture date (general knowledge, verify). Seller words: "Lane MCM", "Lane Acclaim end table", "dovetail table", "walnut mid century".

More detail:
- **Company**: founded 1912 in Altavista, VA, by John Lane as a cedar-chest maker (ModCityMad; his son Ed Lane is also often credited, verify). It moved into bedroom and dining suites by the 1940s–50s and made walnut its main material. The mid-century peak was about 1955–1975, and Heritage Home Group acquired the company in 1987, after which solid-walnut construction declined (ModCityMad).
- **Acclaim**: launched late 1958 and discontinued around 1972. It grew to at least 48 pieces, mostly tables. The signature is the row of contrasting dovetail-shaped inlays bordering the top and other surfaces; they are decorative inlay, not structural joinery. The earliest pieces were walnut veneer over elm, later ones used ash, and the later **Acclaim II** used laminate instead of veneer (AOL/House Digest). Typical Acclaim forms are the two-tier step table, the square or rectangular "cube" end table with a lower shelf, wedge corner tables, and 50" and 56" coffee tables.
- **Designer attribution**: most sources and dealer listings credit Acclaim to the Dutch designer André Bus (AOL; 1stdibs). ModCityMad's guide credits Merton Gershun and describes star-shaped brass pulls. That looks like a mix-up with another line, so use "André Bus" in listings and treat the Gershun attribution as unconfirmed.
- **Where marks are**: a rectangular metal "LANE" tag (sometimes "ALTAVISTA, VA." and a date code) on the back of case pieces or inside a drawer. Paper labels sometimes name the line. On tables, the stamp and serial number are usually burned or ink-stamped on the underside of the top or the shelf (ModCityMad; general knowledge, verify).
- **Look-alikes**: Virginia Maid (a Lane brand) made a similar table with bowtie inlays at the corners instead of a dovetail border; one sold around $300 (AOL). Broyhill Brasilia, American of Martinsville and Drexel also made walnut mid-century pieces.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Acclaim** – most recognised; step tables, end tables, coffee tables, cube tables.
- **Rhythm**, **Perception**, **Copenhagen**, **Tuxedo**, **Mosaic** and 1970s Brutalist "Pueblo"/"Staccato" lines (general knowledge, verify names).
- **Cedar chests** – very common, low-to-mid value except MCM-styled ones.
- Look-alikes: Broyhill Brasilia, American of Martinsville, Drexel – similar walnut MCM.

Line notes from collector sources:
- **Staccato** (1960s): bold geometric grain with an angular, architectural look. It is less common and sells at the upper end; a Staccato two-piece china cabinet was listed at $2,775 (ModCityMad; asking). Pueblo is a separate named line with its own dealer collection page (ModCityMad).
- **Perception** (1960s–70s): refined and minimal, with clean drawer fronts and recessed hardware (ModCityMad). The tables are glass-topped (earlier summary).
- **Altavista** (1960s–70s): a prestige line with bookmatched veneers and higher-grade hardware (ModCityMad).
- **Acclaim II**: later laminate version, worth less than veneered Acclaim (AOL).
- **Value by form** (passenger-seat sizes first): two-tier step tables and cube end tables are the buyable sizes. Pairs sell for clearly more than two singles. Coffee tables, credenzas and bedroom suites are bigger than a passenger seat and excluded.

### Quick ID checklist
- Look for a row of contrasting dovetail-shaped inlays along the top edge. If present, it is Acclaim (or Acclaim II if the surface looks like printed laminate).
- Corner bowtie inlays instead of a dovetail border mean Virginia Maid (a Lane brand), worth about $300 (AOL).
- Ask for a photo of the underside or drawer showing the Lane stamp, tag or serial number.
- Check the surface in the close-up: real wood grain with depth means veneer; flat repeating grain means laminate (later or Acclaim II).
- Judge the size: step and cube end tables fit the car; coffee tables (50–56") and case pieces do not.
- Look for a matching second table in the photos or the listing text. A pair is the best buy.
- Check the top in raking light for water rings, sun fading, lifted veneer at the edges and chipped inlays.
- For glass-top Perception tables, check that the glass is original and unchipped.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Walnut veneer over solid/engineered core with solid walnut edges; Acclaim's contrasting inlaid dovetail keys; Lane stamp with model number and serial. Quality tells: original lacquer with even patina, intact veneer. Refinishing is common and acceptable to many buyers if well done. (general knowledge, verify)
- **Construction** (ModCityMad): solid walnut on visible surfaces, solid poplar or birch for drawer boxes and inner frames, dovetailed drawers, and no particle board in genuine period pieces. Cedar chests have a strongly aromatic cedar interior. Acclaim used walnut veneer over elm early and ash later (AOL).
- **Old vs new**: period pieces have nitrocellulose lacquer that ambers and fine-crazes. A plastic-looking, very even, thick finish suggests a polyurethane refinish. Laminate tops and particle-board cores suggest post-1987 production or Acclaim II.
- **Dating by serial**: commonly cited guides say to read the stamped serial number backwards to get the date. Hunker's worked example (248290 = September 28, 1924) does not follow a consistent rule. Lane itself has said it used more than one serial system, so treat any decoded date as approximate (Hunker). Date codes on later pieces show week and year (ModCityMad).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Veneer chips and lifting at edges, water rings, sun-faded tops, scratched glass (Perception), refinish quality, wobbly legs. Ask for top surface photos, edges, the stamp, and dimensions.
- **Inlay damage**: missing or lifted dovetail inlays on Acclaim are hard to repair invisibly and noticeably lower value.
- **Refinish tells**: stamps and labels missing (sanded or lost), an orange or overly dark colour, filled grain that looks plastic, and inlays that have lost their contrast.
- **Structural**: loose leg joints and racked shelves. Step tables often have chipped edges on the lower tier.
- **Cedar chests**: many older Lane chests were recalled because the automatic lock could trap children. Lane will supply a replacement latch even for very old chests (Hunker). Check whether the lock has been replaced.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Lane-style reproductions and modern "mid-century" pieces imitate the dovetail inlay; no Lane stamp. (general knowledge, verify)
Also, post-1987 Lane production uses more engineered material and is worth less than the 1955–1975 lines (ModCityMad). Acclaim II laminate tables are period Lane but not veneered Acclaim; price them lower (AOL).

## Market
Strong mid-century demand; sells best locally (FB Marketplace, Craigslist) and on Chairish; pairs sell faster and higher than singles. (general knowledge, verify)
Dealer asking prices run about twice the sold prices: unsold step tables averaged about $1,100 asking against sales near $500 (AOL). Price from sold data, not dealer listings.

## What sellers usually miss
Line name (Acclaim vs generic "Lane"), the stamp/serial inside the drawer, matched pairs.
Also the designer name "André Bus" (a strong search keyword) and the difference between veneered Acclaim and laminate Acclaim II.

## Shipping / handling
End tables 25–45 lb; freight/Greyhound or local pickup; shipping $80–150 per table makes eBay shipping uneconomic. (general knowledge, verify)

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State line, model/serial, dimensions, original vs refinished finish, condition of top/veneer. Photograph the stamp, dovetail inlays, top surface, legs. Title keywords: "Lane Acclaim walnut end table dovetail", "mid century modern", "pair".

### What to tell the buyer
Name the line and form ("Lane Acclaim two-tier step table") and credit the design to André Bus, launched 1958. Give the stamp and serial number exactly as stamped, with a photo. Give width, depth and height. Say the visible surfaces are walnut veneer with contrasting dovetail inlays and that the drawers (if any) are dovetailed. Say whether the finish is original lacquer or refinished, and list every inlay chip, lifted veneer edge, water ring and fade line, with photos in raking light. If it is a pair, say the two tables were bought together and give both serials. Do not call a laminate piece "walnut veneer"; call it Acclaim II if the stamp or surface supports that.

## Buy rule
Buy when: cube/end tables under $60 that fit the passenger seat. Pass when: case pieces, cedar chests and coffee tables – bigger than a passenger seat and **excluded** by the size rule; damaged veneer. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule).
