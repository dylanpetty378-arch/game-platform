## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Beech bodies: coffin-shaped smoothers (7–9"), jack (14–16"), jointers (22–30") with a front "horn" or tote; iron wedged
in. Maker stamps on the front end (toe): Ohio Tool, Sandusky, Auburn, Greenfield, Union Factory/Chapin, Scioto,
Marples/Mathieson (UK). Moulding planes: narrow (~9.5" × 1–3") with profiled soles, stamped on the toe; sets of hollows &
rounds. Transitional planes (wood body, Stanley metal frog) = cheap. Plow planes have a fence on two arms (wood screw
arms or wedged arms) and take a set of irons. Sellers write "antique wood plane", "old wooden planer", "primitive tools".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **American 19th-c. factories**: Ohio Tool Co., Sandusky Tool Co. (centre-wheel plows collectible), Auburn Tool,
  Greenfield Tool, Union Factory/H. Chapin, Scioto Works — bench planes decent tier when marked and clean.
- **18th-c. American makers**: Francis Nicholson, Cesar Chelor (top), others — collectible to top.
- **British**: Marples, Mathieson (Scotland), Varvill, Griffiths — Marples ebony/boxwood collectible.
- **Stanley transitional** (Liberty Bell / No. 2x series) — cheap.
- **Types**: smoothers, jacks, jointers, moulding planes (single and complete H&R sets of 18), plows, fillisters,
  sash planes, rounders.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Beech is standard; boxwood, rosewood, ebony and ivory tips mark premium plows. Laminated cast-steel irons stamped by the
maker (e.g. "Moulson Bros", "I. Sorby") add value (general knowledge, verify). Older (18th-c.) moulders are longer
(~10") with wide chamfers and flat (not rounded) chamfers; 19th-c. are 9.5" (general knowledge, verify). Original patina vs
scrubbed or varnished surfaces.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked bodies (especially at the throat and mouth), worm holes, worn/damaged sole profiles, missing wedges or irons,
replaced totes, broken screw-arm threads, missing plow irons. Sanding/varnishing erases toe stamps and lowers value.
Ask for photos of the toe stamp, the sole, the iron and any cracks.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern decor reproductions and "fake-aged" planes exist; faked 18th-c. maker stamps are a risk at the top end — stamp
style should match reference books (Pollak's "A Guide to the Makers of American Wooden Planes") (general knowledge,
verify).

## Market
Hand-tool woodworkers (users want H&R sets and plows), tool collectors (Brown Tool Auctions, Martin J. Donnelly/MJD) and
decor/primitive buyers for bench planes. Bench planes are oversupplied; plows and complete sets sell well
(general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Toe stamps; plow planes (fence + screw arms) mistaken for "weird old tool"; boxwood/rosewood screws; complete iron sets.

## Shipping / handling
Jointers 26–30" are oversize, 5–8 lb, $25–35 each way. eBay. Moulders are small (1–2 lb) and cheap to ship.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker from the toe stamp (Sandusky, Ohio Tool, Auburn, Marples), type (plow plane/moulding plane/jointer/smoother), number on the heel, profile (hollow/round/ogee), wood (beech/boxwood/rosewood), length, iron maker, "antique". Junk: "primitive decor", "old tool", "farmhouse", "rare". `Sandusky 132 Screw Arm Plow Plane Boxwood Arms Complete Irons Antique`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for plows and H&R sets (users and collectors); Brown Tool Auctions/MJD for 18th-c. makers and ivory-tipped plows; bench planes local to decor buyers.
- **Price position:** marked bench planes at the sold median ($40–90), OBO; Sandusky plows at $200–300, complete H&R sets at $200–800, accept −10%; unmarked jointers $25–40 local. Cut 10% at 45 days.
- **Photos:** first: plane in profile on a plain cloth; then toe stamp close-up, heel number, sole and profile end-on, iron stamp, wedge, throat/mouth for cracks, worm holes, fence and screw-arm threads, irons laid out for plows.
- **State:** maker from toe stamp, type, length, profile/number, iron maker, wood, cracks/worm/sole wear by photo number, completeness (wedge, irons, fence), unsanded/unvarnished or not.
- **Pack / haul:** moulders 1–2 lb, small box; jointers 26–30" oversize, 5–8 lb, $25–35; plows boxed with arms immobilized.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Sandusky 132] with boxwood arms, all [8] irons and the original fence — the unmarked beech jointers are $30 decor; a complete centre-wheel or screw-arm plow is the $200–800 tool."
