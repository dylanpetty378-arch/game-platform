Summary: Plastic/foam hunting decoys $5–15 each ($50–120/dozen) and decorative imports $5–30; unmarked working wood decoys $30–80 repainted, $60–250 original paint (Terapeak median $80); Mason standard, Herter's cork, Evans $150–800; Mason premier and named carvers $1,000–$80,000+.
Buy rule (short): Wood body, glass eyes, keel/weight and original crazed paint (or a belly stamp) at ≤ $40 each; pass plastic/foam lots, glossy decoratives, repaints.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Old hunting ("working") decoys vs. modern decorative carvings. Working-decoy tells: weathered/crazed original paint, glass or tack eyes, a lead or iron keel/weight strip screwed to the belly, a tie-line staple or ring at the breast, shot scars, hollow two-piece bodies (seam line along the side), a head that is a separate dowelled piece. Decorative/import tells: glossy varnish, sanded-smooth "duck shape", painted eyes, no keel, flat felt base, "Made in Philippines/Taiwan" sticker, brass "Ducks Unlimited" plaque. Species matters: canvasback, redhead, bluebill, black duck common; shorebirds (plover, yellowlegs) and oversized/ sleeping forms much rarer. Sellers' words: "wooden duck", "old decoy", "carved duck", "know nothing about it".
- **Modern hunting decoys** (GHG/Avian-X, Flambeau, Herter's foam/plastic): hollow plastic or foam, moulded feather detail, keel with line tie. $5–15 each used. 90 % of Craigslist "duck decoys".
- **Wood vs plastic**: a moulded seam line with a hollow moulded underside = plastic. Note the conflict between the two earlier write-ups: one lists a side seam (hollow two-piece wood body) as a working-decoy tell, the other says factory wood has "no seam line" and treats a seam as a plastic tell. Both can be true: a glued two-piece hollow **wood** body shows a wood joint line, while plastic shows a moulded mould-parting seam with a hollow open or plugged underside. Check the underside and material, not just the seam.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Mason Decoy Factory** (Detroit, c. 1896–1924): glass eyes, swirl paint; standard grade $150–800; premier/challenge grade $300–3,000 and far higher at auction.
- **Dodge, Stevens, Peterson** (19th-c. factories): collectible factory wood.
- **Evans** (Wisconsin): collectible tier.
- **Victor / Animal Trap Co.** (1930s–50s): stamped on belly; decent to collectible, $40–300.
- **Herter's** (Waseca MN): cork 1936–39 ($200–500), hollow wood/balsa 1946–50s ($100–200), Model 72 oversize balsa (collectible, $75–150; pairs worth more), Durlon foam and Tenite plastic 1950s–60s ($25–100 for nice ones), "Herter's Inc. 1893" bottom stamp on 1960s decoratives. Later Herter's (and the modern Cabela's-revived brand) foam/plastic is junk tier.
- **Named/regional carvers** (Crowell, Wheeler, Ward Bros., Wilson, Lincoln, Bowman; Illinois River, Chesapeake, Long Island, Maine, Louisiana styles): auction only, $1,000–100,000+.
- **Shorebirds** (plover, yellowlegs) and oversize/sleeper forms: much rarer and higher.
- **Modern decorative / Ducks Unlimited editions**: junk tier.

### Golden-egg tells
- Mason Premier grade — ~4× ($325 median; $700–1,200 with original paint) vs the $80 unmarked median — hollow-carved body, flat bottom with a recessed weight area, glass eyes, detailed swirl paint; later ones stamped "Mason Decoy Factory" (https://appraisily.com/most-wanted-antique-duck-decoy-identification/)
- Mason Premier wood duck drake — $690,000 (2014) — rarest species in the top grade; wood duck crest and white face stripes in factory paint (https://wisducks.org/mason-decoys/)
- Mason Challenge grade — ~3× vs unmarked — solid body, round bottom, glass eyes, detailed paint (https://appraisily.com/most-wanted-antique-duck-decoy-identification/)
- Elmer Crowell (East Harwich MA) signed/branded — ~30–1,000× ($2,500 for a black duck; $16,000–79,000 for preening/sleeping forms) — "A.E. Crowell" or oval brand on the underside, raised carved wing tips, feather-stippled paint (https://www.invaluable.com/blog/inside-the-archives-duck-decoy-prices/)
- Ward Brothers (Crisfield MD) — $45,000 for a pinch-breasted pintail — "L.T. Ward & Bro." stamp or signature on the bottom, pinched breast carving (https://www.invaluable.com/blog/inside-the-archives-duck-decoy-prices/)
- Shorebirds (yellowlegs, plover, turnstone) by any period carver — $30,000–85,000 — slender body on a stick-hole, long bill, Long Island/Cape Cod style (https://www.invaluable.com/blog/inside-the-archives-duck-decoy-prices/)
- Species: wood duck, harlequin, pintail, canvasback, redhead — ~1.5–3× vs mallards in the same grade — species-specific head shape and paint pattern (https://appraisily.com/most-wanted-antique-duck-decoy-identification/)
- Herter's 1936–39 cork bodies — ~1.1–2× ($90 median, $157 p75; $200–500 for clean ones) vs unmarked wood — pressed cork body with wooden bottom board and head, "Herter's" stamp on the board (entry comp)
- Victor / Animal Trap Co. stamped — ~1–4× ($40–300) vs unmarked — oval "VICTOR / ANIMAL TRAP CO" ink stamp or brand on the belly (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cedar, white pine, balsa or cork bodies; glass eyes (old) vs tack or painted eyes; lead or iron keel weights; staples/rings for tie lines. Original paint shows crazing, even wear at high points, and oxidised edges; repaint looks fresh, glossy, or covers old shot scars and wear. Separate dowelled head; check for replaced heads (different wood/colour, newer paint at the neck joint) (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
~90% of decoys have been repainted or have replaced heads (Invaluable/Guyette); original paint is the main driver. Common damage: bill chips, cracked necks, checks/splits in the body, missing eyes, flaking paint. Repairs show as filler at the bill or neck, mismatched paint, new screws. Ask for close photos of the underside, neck joint and bill.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Artificially aged "antique" decoys (faked crazing, distressed paint, new glass eyes), fake Mason/Ward stamps, and decorative carvings sold as working decoys exist; named-carver attributions need an auction house (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Steady collector market on eBay for singles; decoy auctions (Guyette & Deeter, Copley) for anything with a maker. Plastic/foam lots sell locally to hunters, peaking before duck season (Aug–Nov) (general knowledge, verify). Decorators also buy rustic old decoys. The low-end eBay data is thin (6 sold in the Terapeak search).

## What sellers usually miss
Anything beyond "wooden duck". Original paint + glass eyes + keel + species identification; a stamped belly (Mason/Victor/Evans/Herter's); regional style (Illinois River, Chesapeake, Long Island, Maine). Pairs (drake + hen) sell for more together. Wood vs plastic (look for a seam line and hollow moulded underside = plastic). "Old decoys from grandpa's shed" lots occasionally hide a Mason.

## Shipping / handling
2–5 lb each packed (a bare decoy is 1–2 lb), pack to protect bill and tail; $15–25 in. A dozen is 20–30 lb and bulky ($50–80). Sell singles on eBay (collectors), lots locally; send to a decoy auction (Guyette & Deeter, Copley) if a maker is suspected. Fees ~13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** species and sex (mallard drake), "wood decoy", maker/stamp (Mason, Herter's, Victor, Evans), "original paint", "glass eyes", region/style, length, "working". Junk: "folk art" on factory birds, "antique" unproven, "rare". `Vintage Mason Standard Grade Mallard Drake Wood Duck Decoy Glass Eyes Original Paint`
- **Where:** singles on eBay, 7-day auction for stamped/original-paint birds (collectors bid), BIN with offers for unmarked; anything with a maker sells for more at a decoy auction (Guyette & Deeter, Copley); plastic/foam lots on FB local before duck season (Aug–Nov).
- **Price position:** unmarked working birds at the sold median ($80; $60–250 original paint), Mason premier median $325; OBO, accept −15%, floor $30; cut 15% after 30 days; dozens locally $50–120.
- **Photos:** full side profile; underside with stamps, weight and keel; head/eye close-up; paint surface close-up (original vs repaint); bill and tail tips; any cracks or replaced head; ruler for length.
- **State:** species and sex, wood/cork/balsa, eye type, original vs repainted, keel/weight and tie-line hardware, stamp, region/style if known, length, cracks, repairs and replaced parts by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** carry-on, 2–5 lb packed, bill and tail protected, $15–25; a dozen is 20–30 lb and bulky ($50–80) — sell dozens locally.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [Mason standard grade] [mallard drake] with [glass eyes] and [original paint], [belly stamp shown]; original-paint factory birds sell for $[150–800], and ~90% of decoys out there have been repainted."

## Buy rule
Buy when: wood body with glass eyes and keel/weight and original crazed paint (old working decoy), or a bottom stamp (Mason, Evans, Herter's cork/balsa), at ≤ $40 each – expected sale $100–250 per the earlier write-up, but the Oct 2026 Terapeak median for unmarked glass-eye decoys is only $80, so at $35 profit is ~$40 and fails the rule; aim nearer $20–25 for unmarked singles. Pass when: plastic/foam lots, modern brands, anything "flocked", glossy decorative carving, painted eyes, no rigging, or heavily repainted. Stop and research when: a stamp is visible on the belly or the form is a shorebird/sleeper. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
