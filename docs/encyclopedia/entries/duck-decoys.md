# Duck decoys (working decoys, wood)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: Invaluable "What makes duck decoys valuable" (Guyette & Deeter auction data), Appraisily decoy ID guides, eBay sold listings (unmarked glass-eye decoys)

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Old hunting ("working") decoys vs. modern decorative carvings. Working-decoy tells: weathered/crazed original paint, glass or tack eyes, a lead or iron keel/weight strip screwed to the belly, a tie-line staple or ring at the breast, shot scars, hollow two-piece bodies (seam line along the side), a head that is a separate dowelled piece. Decorative/import tells: glossy varnish, sanded-smooth "duck shape", painted eyes, no keel, flat felt base, "Made in Philippines/Taiwan" sticker, brass "Ducks Unlimited" plaque. Species matters: canvasback, redhead, bluebill, black duck common; shorebirds (plover, yellowlegs) and oversized/ sleeping forms much rarer. Factory decoys: Mason (Detroit, glass eyes, swirl paint, premier/standard grades), Dodge, Stevens, Peterson, Evans, Animal Trap/Victor (stamped on belly), Herter's (cork/balsa, later). Sellers' words: "wooden duck", "old decoy", "carved duck", "know nothing about it".

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2007–2020 – named-carver "black duck" examples at Guyette/Christie's: $200–1,770 (Bowman $200, Lincoln $300, Crowell $1,500, Wheeler $1,770)
- 2026 (eBay sold, general) – unmarked hand-carved glass-eye decoys with original paint: $60–250 each depending on form/species
- Rule of thumb from Invaluable/Guyette: ~90% of decoys have been repainted or have replaced heads; original paint is the main driver

## What sellers usually miss
Anything beyond "wooden duck". Original paint + glass eyes + keel + species identification; a stamped belly (Mason/Victor/Evans); regional style (Illinois River, Chesapeake, Long Island, Maine). Pairs (drake + hen) sell for more together.

## Shipping / handling
2–5 lb each, pack to protect bill and tail; $15–25 in. Resell on eBay (collectors) or to a decoy auction (Guyette & Deeter, Copley) if a maker is suspected. Fees ~13%.

## Buy rule
Buy when: price ≤ $40 each and the photo shows original crazed paint + glass eyes + keel/weight (old working decoy) – expected sale $100–250. Pass when: glossy decorative carving, painted eyes, no rigging, or heavily repainted. Stop and research when: a stamp is visible on the belly or the form is a shorebird/sleeper. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.

## Additional research (earlier entry: Duck decoys (hunting and carved))
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: wisducks.org Herter's history (Warman's price guide values), duckboats.net forum, agent knowledge

### What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Modern hunting decoys** (GHG/Avian-X, Flambeau, Herter's foam/plastic): hollow plastic or foam, moulded feather detail, keel with line tie. $5–15 each used. 90 % of Craigslist "duck decoys".
- **Herter's (Waseca MN)**: cork 1936–39 ($200–500), hollow wood/balsa 1946–50s ($100–200), Model 72 oversize balsa (collectible, $75–150), Durlon foam and Tenite plastic 1950s–60s ($25–100 for nice ones), "Herter's Inc. 1893" bottom stamp on 1960s decoratives.
- **Factory wood**: Mason (Detroit, glass eyes, premier grade $300–3,000), Evans, Dodge, Stevens – look for painted or glass eyes, worn wood, lead keel weight, no seam line.
- **Hand-carved regional** (Ward Bros, Crowell, Illinois River, Louisiana): auction only, $1,000–100,000+.

### What sellers usually miss
Wood vs plastic (look for a seam line and hollow moulded underside = plastic). Glass eyes + ballast weight + brand stamp on bottom. "Old decoys from grandpa's shed" lots occasionally hide a Mason.

### Shipping / handling
Each decoy 1–2 lb; a dozen is 20–30 lb and bulky ($50–80). Sell singles on eBay; lots locally.

### Buy rule
Buy when: wood body with glass eyes and a bottom stamp (Mason, Evans, Herter's cork/balsa) at ≤ $40 each. Pass when: plastic/foam lots, modern brands, anything "flocked". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).
