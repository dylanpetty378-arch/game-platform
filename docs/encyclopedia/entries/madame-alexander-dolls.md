## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Alexander-kins / Wendy (1953–1965)**: 8" hard plastic, sleep eyes with moulded lashes and painted lower lashes, back marked "ALEX." (1953–76), straight-leg walker 1955–56, bent-knee walker 1956–64; outfits with "Madame Alexander" or "Alexander-kins" cloth tags; mohair or saran wigs. The money tier.
- **1965–1990s Alexander 8"**: straight legs, "ALEX" mark; international and storybook dolls in boxes; plentiful.
- **Vogue Ginny (1948–)**: 1950–53 painted lashes, strung; 1954 walkers; 1955–57 moulded lashes; later vinyl Ginny (1960s–90s, Lesney/Dakin/Vogue Dolls Inc. revivals); 2000s Vogue Ginny Disney/Mouseketeer collector editions with "Ginny" display stands.
- **McDonald's Madame Alexander (2002–2010s)**: 5" plastic Happy Meal dolls; vinyl heads, moulded shoes.
- **Larger**: Cissy (20", 1955–59), Elise (16", 1957–), Cissette (10", 1957–63), Lissy (12").

## Makers, brands and variants
- Alexander Doll Co. (NY, founded 1923 by Beatrice Alexander). Wrist tags (gold foil "Madame Alexander"), boxes with model numbers.
- Vogue Dolls (Medford MA, Jennie Graves); later owners Tonka/Lesney/Dakin, Vogue Dolls Inc. (1995–).
- Nancy Ann Storybook (5–6" bisque/plastic), Effanbee, Arranbee Littlest Angel, Cosmopolitan Ginger (Ginny clones).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- 1950s hard plastic: rigid, heavier, seams on limbs; sleep eyes; soft facial blush; tagged clothing with snaps (not Velcro); marks on back of torso.
- Later vinyl: softer, rotated heads, Velcro closures, polyester fabrics.
- Original outfit and wrist tag/box raise value 2–4x; hair untouched (set curls) matters.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Faded or missing face colour, green ear (from metal earrings), hair cut/played-with, missing shoes, loose strings/walker mechanism, cracked limbs, re-dressed in non-original clothes. Smoke odour and stains.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Alexander reissued 1950s designs; Ginny clones (Ginger, Muffie, Pam) and unmarked look-alikes; mismatched outfits put on genuine bodies.

## Market
Doll collecting is aging and prices have fallen sharply since 2000; only 1950s hard plastic in original condition sells well.

## What sellers usually miss
- 1950s hard-plastic Alexander-kins mixed with modern dolls ("old doll lot"); tells are moulded lashes, walker legs and tagged clothes.
- Original tagged 1950s outfits sold separately are worth $30–150 each.
- They overprice modern dolls using original retail.

## Shipping / handling
Small, light: $8–12 each way; protect hair and eyes.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph back marks, clothing tags, eyes open/closed, legs (bent-knee), hair condition; describe outfit originality; include box and wrist tag.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$10 to buyer − ~$10 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Post-1970 dolls, McDonald's minis, modern exclusives: pass.
- 1950s hard-plastic 8" in tagged original outfit: buy at ≤ $30 if resale ≥ $100 (ask back-mark and tag photos).
