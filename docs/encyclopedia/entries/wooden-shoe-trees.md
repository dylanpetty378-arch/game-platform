## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wooden forms put inside shoes to keep their shape. Types seen:
- **Split-toe spring trees** (1930s–present): toe block split down the middle, a coiled spring or metal tube to a heel knob. Cedar (reddish, aromatic) or beech/maple (pale).
- **Lasted / one-piece trees** (1900s–60s, and bespoke makers today): a solid carved forefoot with a hinged or separate heel block and turned knob, shaped like the shoe's last; often stamped with a size ("7½") and sometimes a maker.
- **Stretchers**: a forefoot form with a screw handle that widens it (metal T-handle, like the single one in our sweep).
- **Victorian boot trees**: tall three-piece (foot, leg, wedge) trees for riding boots; often with brass pulls. 
- **Cobbler's / factory lasts**: solid maple forms with metal heel plates, numbered sizes, hinged ("Vulcan", "Kozy"), $5–20 each as décor.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Rochester Shoe Tree Co.** (New Hampshire, 1921–present): cedar trees; stamped "Rochester".
- **Woodlore** (cedar, 1990s–present): common, $8–15 used.
- **Retailer/maker trees**: Florsheim, Allen Edmonds (stamped "Allen Edmonds"), Alden (lasted trees, $40–60 new), Johnston & Murphy, Hanover, Nettleton, Bostonian, Brooks Brothers: used $10–30 a pair.
- **English bespoke**: John Lobb (London), Lobb Paris, Edward Green, Gaziano & Girling, Foster & Son, Church's — lasted trees with brass name plates or stamped names, made for a specific customer's last; $100–400 a pair (general knowledge, verify).
- **Cobbler lasts**: US factory lasts (United Shoe Machinery), cast-iron stands (Spalding, Simmons).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Bespoke lasted trees: dense beech or hornbeam, hand-shaped to a narrow elegant last, brass plates or a customer's name/number inked inside, three-piece with wedge.
- Cheap: lacquered pale wood, plastic knobs, steel springs, mass-produced split toe.
- Cedar smells aromatic and is valued for moisture control (modern buyers).
- Old: shrinkage, patina, square-cut nails, hand-written sizes.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks along the grain, split heels, missing heel blocks or knobs, rusted springs, mismatched pairs (sizes differ — check numbers on each), worm holes, paint spatter.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked meaningfully. Beware "antique" decorative lasts made in India for décor shops (2000s–present), with cast handles and aged finish.

## Market
Steady demand from menswear buyers (#menswear Reddit/StyleForum crowd) for named-maker and bespoke trees in specific sizes; decorators buy old forms and lasts. Generic trees move slowly at low prices.

## What sellers usually miss
- Brass plates or stamped names (Lobb, Edward Green, Peal & Co., Alden) on old-looking lasted trees — the high-value variety looks like "old wooden shoe forms".
- Size and width stamps that match popular sizes (US 9–11) sell faster.

## Shipping / handling
Pairs ~1.5–2 lb, $10–14. Lots of 5–6 pairs 8–10 lb, $20–28. Durable.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker/stamp, size and width, wood type (cedar or beech), heel-to-toe length in inches, split-toe vs lasted, condition of springs/knobs.

## Buy rule
Generic lot selling for $45 nets ~$39 before ~$20 shipping each way: a loss. Buy only bespoke/named lasted trees, Victorian boot trees, at ≤ $15 per pair where sold ≥ $100 per pair (Tier A rarely). Passenger-seat size, no testing needed.
