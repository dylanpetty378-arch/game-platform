## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Wrenches supplied with a plow, planter, mower, binder or tractor to fit its nuts. Usually **cast (malleable) iron** or drop-forged steel, flat, 4–14" long, with open ends, box ends, square sockets or several openings ("multi-end"). The **maker's name and part number are cast in raised letters** on the handle (e.g., "IHC" + number, "McCORMICK", "DEERING", "JD 52", "OLIVER", "MOLINE PLOW CO", "J.I.C." for Case, "SUPERIOR DRILL CO", "AVERY", "E-B", "P&O").
- **Cut-out wrenches** have the maker's name cut right through the handle (letters are holes) — top tier.
- **Combination tools**: wrench + hammer + screwdriver + pliers + thread die, often patent-dated (Chamberlain 1912, etc.).
- **Hub/axle wrenches** (for wagon or buggy hubs), **S-wrenches** (curved), **alligator wrenches** (V-jaw with teeth), wood-handled **monkey wrenches**.
- Lots on craigslist are usually rusty piles photographed on the ground; legibility of the cast names is the whole game.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **International Harvester (1902+) and its predecessors McCormick and Deering** — the most common named wrenches; "IHC" with part numbers; McCormick and Deering pre-1902/early marks a bit more.
- **John Deere** — "JD" + number, "JOHN DEERE" or Deere & Co.; demand from Deere collectors keeps even common ones $10–30; the **John Deere cut-out wrench** is the holy grail (only three known per Farm Collector).
- **Oliver / Oliver Chilled Plow Works**, **Moline Plow Co. / Moline Drill**, **J.I. Case / Case Plow Works**, **Emerson / Emerson-Brantingham**, **Parlin & Orendorff (P&O)**, **Avery (Peoria)**, **Superior Drill Co. (Springfield OH)**, **Hoosier Drill**, **Osborne** (D.M. Osborne), **Tague & Kirlin**, **Sandwich**, **Keystone**, **New Idea**, **Massey-Harris** — scarcer names fetch more.
- **Ford / Fordson** — very common S-wrenches and open-ends, $5–20 each.
- **Tool makers** (Winchester, Keen Kutter, Billings & Spencer, Williams) overlap with hand-tool collectors (see craftsman-hand-tools and hand-tool-lots).

### Quick ID checklist
1. Can you read cast letters? Ask for a close-up of each named piece (chalk or a wire brush shows them).
2. Cut-out letters? Multi-end? Patent date? Combination tool?
3. Maker scarcity: Deere, Avery, Osborne, Superior, small plow companies > IHC/McCormick/Ford.
4. Breaks and repairs (cast iron cracks at the jaws).
5. Weight of the lot (shipping): ~1 lb per 2–3 wrenches.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Cut-out letter wrenches (John Deere, Osborne, IHC hub)** — ~20–100× a named common wrench ($325–410 for Osborne/IHC at auction; John Deere cut-out "several thousand dollars") — the maker's name pierced through the handle so light shows through the letters.
- **Patent-dated combination tools (wrench + pliers + hammer + die)** — ~20–40× ($395 for a 1912 Chamberlain at the Henry auction) — multiple tool functions in one piece, patent date cast or stamped.
- **Hub wrenches with original hub cap** — ~15–30× ($325, IHC cut-out hub wrench with cap) — round socket head sized for a wagon/implement hub, sometimes with the cap still attached.
- **Named John Deere implement wrenches ("JD" numbers, "JOHN DEERE")** — ~3–6× a generic named IHC ($20–60, estimate) — raised "JD" and a part number on the handle, often with a Moline IL mark.
- **Multi-end malleable wrenches from small makers (Paris, Challenge, Superior Drill, Avery, Tague & Kirlin)** — ~3–8× ($40–55 at the Henry auction; $12–30 asking in 1998) — several stepped openings along one handle, raised maker name.
- **Wood-handled monkey and alligator wrenches with patent marks** — ~2–5× ($20–75, estimate) — wooden grip with a ferrule, adjustable jaw, patent date on the bar.
- No premium: Ford/Fordson S-wrenches ($12–20), car-kit open-ends (Cadillac, Westline), unmarked forged wrenches, modern Allen and pipe wrenches — $1–10.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast malleable iron: grainy surface, rounded edges, raised letters, flash lines. Drop-forged steel: smoother, sharper edges, stamped (sunk) letters. Original paint traces (red IH, green/yellow Deere) add a little. Light surface rust is normal and accepted; deep pitting that erases letters kills value. Collectors dislike wire-wheeled/painted wrenches but accept gentle cleaning.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked or broken jaws (cast iron), welded repairs, heavy pitting, letters worn smooth, ground-down ends. Ask for close-ups of the cast names on each wrench.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Some reproduction cast-iron John Deere and IH "wrenches" and cut-outs exist (souvenir castings): crisp, sandy cast texture, too-perfect letters, wrong weight. Rare cut-outs should be bought from photos only at plain-wrench prices unless verified.

## Market
A Midwest farm-collector niche (Missouri Valley Wrench Club, tractor shows, farm-toy and Deere/IH collectors). Steady but price-sensitive; rarities sell fast to collectors, commons sit. eBay for named singles and small lots; farm auctions for big collections. Mild summer peak (show season).

## What sellers usually miss
The cast maker names and part numbers (rust hides them); cut-outs mixed into rusty buckets; patent combination tools. They also overvalue heavy anonymous lots by weight.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy: a 30-piece lot weighs ~20–25 lb. USPS Large Flat Rate box (~$25–30) can carry up to 70 lb domestically, which makes lots shippable to Dylan; selling singles costs ~$6–10 each. Wrap ends so they don't punch through the box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "antique" + maker (John Deere / IHC / McCormick / Oliver / Osborne) + part number + "implement wrench" + "cast" + feature (cut-out / hub / multi-end / patent) + "plow tractor". Junk: "rusty wrench lot", "old tools", "barn find". `Antique John Deere JD 52 Cast Implement Wrench Plow Tractor Moline IL`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for named singles and small lots (summer show-season peak); farm auctions and the Missouri Valley Wrench Club crowd for big collections and cut-outs.
- **Price position:** at the sold median per piece ($5–25 named; $25–75 Deere/multi-end; $300+ cut-outs/hub/patent), accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days; anonymous lots priced by the lot, firm.
- **Photos:** wrench flat on plain background; cast lettering in raking light both sides; each opening; cut-out/patent feature close up; breaks or welds; lot laid out with count; next to a ruler.
- **State:** maker, part number, length, opening sizes, cut-out/multi-end/patent features, rust level, breaks or repairs, by photo number; lots: count and which names are legible.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox; singles $6–10; 30-piece lot 20–25 lb in a USPS Large Flat Rate box $25–30; wrap the ends so they don't punch through, no double-box.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Maker] [part number] implement wrench, cast [era] with the lettering fully legible; generic rusty wrenches go for [$] and named [Deere/cut-out] pieces bring [$] at the collector auctions."

## Buy rule
Buy when: a lot shows ≥ 5 legible named implement wrenches or one cut-out/multi-end/patent combination piece, at ≤ $2 per pound and profit = parted-out resale × 0.87 − per-sale shipping − ~$25–30 flat-rate to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Pass when: anonymous rusty lots, car-kit/Ford wrenches, modern tools, or lots that need >2 hours of cleaning and sorting for <$100 total. Fits a passenger seat (a bucket or box).
