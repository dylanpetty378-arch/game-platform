## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Beige/brown keyboard-computers (1977–1990), in or out of original box. The model number is printed on the box and on a
label under the unit (Radio Shack catalogue no. 26-xxxx; Commodore / Apple / Atari model names on the case badge). Sellers
title them "Tandy computer", "old Radio Shack computer", "Commodore", "Apple computer", often with no model. Tell
models apart by case: C64 "breadbin" (brown-grey, rounded) vs C64C (flat, cream); Apple II/II+ (beige, separate lid,
keyboard without arrow keys up/down) vs IIe (adds open/closed-apple keys); IIc is a compact off-white unit with a handle;
MC-10 is a tiny white wedge with chiclet keys; TI-99/4A has a silver/black case and cartridge slot on the right.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Apple**: Apple II (1977) / II+ (1979) top tier; IIe (1983–93) common but solid; IIc (1984) and IIc+ collectible
  boxed; IIgs mid; Lisa and Apple I museum-level. Disk II drives, Apple monitors and cards add value.
- **Commodore**: PET (1977) collectible; VIC-20 common; C64 (breadbin 1982–86, C64C later) the volume seller; SX-64
  portable collectible; C128 mid; Amiga 500/1000/2000 strong (Amiga 1000 with signatures inside case is a tell).
- **Radio Shack / Tandy**: TRS-80 Model I (1977) collectible; Model III/4 mid; Color Computer (CoCo) 1–3 mid-low;
  MC-10 (26-3004) bottom tier — the 16K RAM module (26-3013) roughly doubles boxed value; Model 100 laptop (26-3801)
  collectible.
- **Atari**: 400 low; 800 boxed collectible; XL/XE common.
- **Texas Instruments**: TI-99/4A common; peripheral expansion box adds.
- **Others**: NeXT (anything), early IBM 5150 complete, Altair 8800, Osborne/Kaypro portables (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
ABS plastic cases (yellowing from UV is normal for 1980s beige; retr0brite whitening is common and some collectors
dislike it). Original boxes with styrofoam inserts, manuals and the matching power supply are the value multipliers.
Serial/model label on the bottom identifies revision. Signs of quality: original keycaps all present, no cracked
case corners, clean battery area (Apple IIgs / Amiga 2000 / Model 100 batteries leak) (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Untested is the norm and Dylan passes on it. Common faults: failed power supplies (C64 PSU can damage the machine —
collectors warn not to use original bricks), leaking batteries corroding boards, dead RAM/chips, sticky keyboards,
cracked cases, missing power supplies. Recapped boards and replacement PSUs are acceptable to most buyers if disclosed.
Ask for a photo/video of power-on showing the boot screen (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few outright fakes for common machines. Modern reissues/remakes exist (TheC64, Commodore 64 Ultimate-style
reproductions, Apple-1 replicas) — check model labels and date codes. Married boxes (a box from a different unit) are
common in lots (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Steady eBay demand from retro-computing hobbyists and nostalgia buyers (40–60 year olds); tested/working and boxed units
sell fast, untested loose units sit or sell as "for parts". Apple and Amiga have the deepest collector base. Local sales
are slow; eBay is the main venue. Mild Q4 holiday lift (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
"Tandy computer" / "old Radio Shack computer" with no model; the 16K RAM module (26-3013) in the box roughly doubles MC-10
value. Apple II-series and Amiga are the ones to stop for. Bundled software, disk drives, cartridges and manuals often
outsell the computer.

## Shipping / handling
Boxed MC-10 ~4 lb ($15); C64/Apple II with PSU 10–20 lb ($30–50). CRT monitors are heavy and fragile — ship separately or
sell locally. Untested electronics break Dylan's rule unless the seller demonstrates power-on.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State exact model and revision (from the bottom label), tested status with a boot-screen photo, what is included (PSU,
cables, manuals, box, software), cosmetic yellowing and any recapping/repairs. Photograph the bottom label, the box,
the keyboard and the boot screen. Title keywords: maker, model, "tested working", "boxed", "complete", "CIB", year.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Apple II family, Amiga, SX-64, Model 100 shown working, under $100. Pass when: MC-10, TI-99, Atari 400/800XL,
VIC-20, anything untested. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger
seat (CRT monitors alone may not — computer unit only).
