## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
NCR "National" registers. Valuable ones are the brass-cased 1890s–1920s models: ornate cast bronze/brass scrollwork, marble change shelf, glass-topped pop-up price flags, model numbers 3xx–4xx (313, 317, 442) and the small candy-store 313/310/311 (the "nickel-plated" and "brass" finish). 1930s–50s models (Class 100/125/700/1000-series, e.g. "125(4)-2", "2000") are painted steel or chrome with push keys – working office machines, not decorator pieces. Tell in the photo: ornate cast scrollwork and a marble shelf = pre-1925 brass; smooth painted/chrome body = post-1935.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **NCR – National Cash Register Co., Dayton, Ohio** (founded 1884): dominates the antique market. Brass candy-store models 310/311/313 (small, one drawer); 317; large multi-drawer 442/452 (top tier). Serial-number plate lets collectors date the machine (general knowledge, verify via NCR serial charts).
- Patterns of cast case design have names (e.g. "Renaissance", "Empire", "Dolphin") that collectors use (general knowledge, verify).
- Other makers (Hallwood, Boston, Michigan) exist and are less common (general knowledge, verify).
- 1930s–70s steel/chrome NCR Class machines – low value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original cases are cast bronze/brass (or nickel/copper plated over cast iron on some), heavy and crisply detailed; marble or glass change shelf; enamel or celluloid key tops; pop-up flags behind glass. Replated/polished brass is common – original patina or well-done plating both sell, but crude polish shows blurred detail. Reproductions: thin castings, no serial plate (per entry).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing keys, broken flags/glass, missing marble, jammed mechanism, missing drawer, missing crank. Restored and working is worth much more than "as found". Ask: does it ring, open and print/flag; photo of serial plate; all keys present.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction brass registers (1970s Taiwan, "Model 313" copies) exist: thin castings, no serial plate. Also decorative toy/bank copies.

## Market
Decorator and general-store collector market; brass models sell steadily but mostly local/regional because of weight. Freight-shipped eBay sales and auction houses (Morphy, regional) for the top. Steel models barely sell (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Model number plate (under the drawer / front edge) – a 313 listed as "old brass cash register" at $100 is a buy. Reproduction brass registers (1970s Taiwan, "Model 313" copies) exist: thin castings, no serial plate.

## Shipping / handling
Brass 313: ~50 lb, $60–90 freight each way. Steel 1950s: 60–100 lb, worse. Only local/regional buys make sense; profit rule means ≥ $500 sale for a $150 purchase shipped.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model and serial number (with build year from serial), finish, working status (keys, flags, drawer, bell), what's original vs replaced, dimensions and weight. Photograph the serial plate, flags up, case details, marble, inside of drawer. Offer local pickup.

## Buy rule
Buy when: brass candy-store model with serial plate under $150. Pass when: any painted/chrome post-1935 model (6755 type) regardless of price. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat – small candy-store models do; large multi-drawer machines are excluded.
