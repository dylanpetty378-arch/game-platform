## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand tool (c. 1880–1930) for beating dust out of rugs hung on a line: wooden handle with a wire or rattan beating head. Common: 2–4 twisted wire loops (the example: three long twisted-wire loops, ~30 in, painted wood handle, crimped ferrule). Better: wire bent into hearts, clovers, scrolls, double-spirals; rattan woven "pretzel" or trefoil heads (European, also new imports).

## Makers, brands and variants
Most unmarked. Some patented US designs (1890s–1920s patent dates stamped on ferrule). Wicker/rattan versions both antique (US, German) and modern Asian/European production.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-twisted spring steel, rust patina, worn painted handles (red/black/green), tin ferrules, square nails. New: shiny galvanized or uniform machine-twisted wire, varnished new handles. Rattan: old is dark, brittle, hand-woven irregularity.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent/crushed loops, broken wire strands, loose handles, missing paint. Light rust is fine/expected.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
New decorative rattan beaters (IKEA-style, Polish/German import $10–20 new) and "primitive" decor wire beaters from Asia with artificially aged finishes.

## Market
Farmhouse décor demand; moderate. 20–60 days.

## What sellers usually miss
Patent dates on the ferrule; complex wire designs.

## Shipping / handling
Long (28–36 in) and flimsy; box ~36×14×4; $12–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Length, wire pattern, handle paint, any marks; note primitive age wear.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): almost no rug beater clears this. Pass unless a patented/figural piece at ≤ $20.
