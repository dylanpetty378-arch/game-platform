## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand tool (c. 1880–1930) for beating dust out of rugs hung on a line: wooden handle with a wire or rattan beating head. Common: 2–4 twisted wire loops (the example: three long twisted-wire loops, ~30 in, painted wood handle, crimped ferrule). Better: wire bent into hearts, clovers, scrolls, double-spirals; rattan woven "pretzel" or trefoil heads (European, also new imports).
More: rugs, bedding and cushions were hung on a line or railing and beaten; the tool fell out of use as carpet sweepers and then vacuum cleaners spread (largely by the 1950s), though wire and rattan beaters are still sold new in Europe. Typical length 28–36 in (the heart-shaped Chairish example is 31 in × 9.5 in wide). Construction: one or two long spring-steel wires twisted together, bent into a head shape, with the two tails running down into a turned wooden handle and held by a tin or iron ferrule, a crimp, or wire wrapping. Some have a wire hanging loop at the handle end. Also made: all-wire beaters with a wire-wrapped handle, flat wooden paddles and lattices, and woven cane heads.
Words in titles: "primitive", "farmhouse", "antique rug beater", "carpet beater", "wicker", "heart", "Maltese cross".
Look-alikes: wire plant hangers and bale handles, kitchen whisks and egg beaters (short), modern decorative wall wire hearts.

## Makers, brands and variants
Most unmarked. Some patented US designs (1890s–1920s patent dates stamped on ferrule). Wicker/rattan versions both antique (US, German) and modern Asian/European production. More:
- US carpet-beater patents go back at least to 1876 (US176191, "Improvement in carpet-beaters") and continued into the 1900s (US726025, 1903, "Carpet or rug beater"); a stamped "PAT" date on the ferrule or handle ties a beater to a design.
- Design families: (1) plain loops, 2–4 parallel twisted loops (most common); (2) teardrop and racket shapes; (3) heart; (4) double-heart, clover, trefoil and scroll; (5) cross or star designs, including the double-wire "Maltese or German cross" type seen from Germany; (6) spring-coil heads (a flat spiral of wire); (7) rattan/cane "pretzel" and trefoil heads (European, especially German and Polish "Teppichklopfer"); (8) wooden paddle or lattice beaters.
- Note: an earlier version of this entry named a "Batchelder" maker; no such maker could be confirmed, so it is removed.

### Quick ID checklist
- Head shape: plain loops (common) vs heart, scroll, cross, clover, spiral (better).
- Ferrule or handle: any stamped "PAT", patent date or maker name.
- Wire: hand-twisted, uneven, dark rust patina vs shiny, uniform machine twist (new).
- Handle: original worn paint (red, black, green) or old turned wood with grime in the grooves vs fresh varnish.
- Fastening: tin ferrule, crimp, or wire wrap with age; no modern staples or glue.
- Rattan: dark, dry, irregular weave (old) vs pale, even, varnished (new import).
- Length shown against a ruler; loops intact, not crushed.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-twisted spring steel, rust patina, worn painted handles (red/black/green), tin ferrules, square nails. New: shiny galvanized or uniform machine-twisted wire, varnished new handles. Rattan: old is dark, brittle, hand-woven irregularity. More: old spring steel holds its shape when pressed; mild steel decor copies bend and stay bent. Old handles are turned hardwood with worn grip areas, dirt in the turnings and sometimes a hole for hanging; new handles are pale, sharp-edged and evenly stained. Artificial ageing: uniform orange rust over everything (including the handle fastening) and paint distressed in even patches.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent/crushed loops, broken wire strands, loose handles, missing paint. Light rust is fine/expected. Also: loops re-bent out of shape, a broken wire re-twisted, a replaced handle (fresh wood with an old head), rattan cracked or unravelled at the binding. Ask for a photo against a ruler and a close-up of the ferrule.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
New decorative rattan beaters (IKEA-style, Polish/German import $10–20 new) and "primitive" decor wire beaters from Asia with artificially aged finishes. Also heart-shaped wire "rug beaters" sold as farmhouse wall decor, made from mild steel with fake rust.

## Market
Farmhouse décor demand; moderate. 20–60 days. Buyers are decorators building primitive wall displays; Chairish and Etsy dealers ask far more than eBay sells for.

## What sellers usually miss
Patent dates on the ferrule; complex wire designs.

## Shipping / handling
Long (28–36 in) and flimsy; box ~36×14×4; $12–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Length, wire pattern, handle paint, any marks; note primitive age wear. Photograph the whole beater on a plain background, the head shape flat-on, the ferrule close up, and the handle paint.

### What to tell the buyer
"Antique [twisted-wire / rattan] rug beater, c. [1890–1930], [head shape: heart / triple loop / cross]. [Original painted / plain] turned wood handle with [tin ferrule / wire wrap]. [Marked 'PAT ____' on the ferrule / unmarked]. [L] in long, [W] in wide. Wire is hand-twisted spring steel with age patina; loops intact and in shape. Not a reproduction (no machine twist, no artificial rust)."

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): almost no rug beater clears this. Pass unless a patented/figural piece at ≤ $20.
