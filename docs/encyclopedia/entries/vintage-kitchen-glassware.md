## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mid-century household glass: mixing bowls, casseroles, refrigerator dishes, pitchers, tumblers and serving plates. Millions were made, so value comes from **pattern and colour scarcity** and **completeness**.
- **Pyrex (Corning, USA):** opal (white) glass with fired-on colour or printed patterns, embossed "PYREX" on the base with a model number (401–404 bowls, 441–444 Cinderella bowls, 471–475 casseroles, 501–503 refrigerator dishes). Tells: the pattern name, the base mark, gloss (dishwasher-dulled = lower value).
  - **Common:** Spring Blossom (green crazy daisy), Butterfly Gold, Old Orchard, Woodland (brown), Snowflake (white on turquoise is decent).
  - **Good:** Primary colours (blue/red/green/yellow) bowls set, Butterprint (turquoise Amish), Gooseberry (pink/black), Pink Daisy, Snowflake Garland, Pink Stems, Atomic Eyes, Friendship.
  - **Rare promos:** Lucky in Love (hearts and shamrocks, 1959 promo, $1,000–6,000), Atomic Eyes 1950s promos, Pink Gooseberry with gold, "Hex Signs", the turquoise Cinderella "Star" promos.
- **Fire-King (Anchor Hocking, 1941–76):** Jadeite (opaque green) in Restaurantware, Jane-Ray and Charm, Turquoise Blue, Peach Lustre, Swirl, and milk-glass mugs with characters (Snoopy, Holly Hobbie). The base is embossed "Fire-King Oven Ware" with an anchor.
- **Glasbake (McKee/Jeannette, 1917–83):** milk glass with printed decals (Currier & Ives blue, Fruit), J- model numbers on the base. Mostly $10–30.
- **Depression glass (1920s–40s):** pressed, thin, mould-made colours (pink, green, amber, cobalt). Makers Hazel-Atlas, Federal, Jeannette, Anchor Hocking, Indiana, Hocking, U.S. Glass. Rare patterns and colours (Royal Lace cobalt, Mayfair blue) sell high. Most pieces are $5–30.
- **Indiana Glass / Tiara Exclusives (1970–1998):** home-party sales of reissued Indiana patterns: "Sweet Pear" (also "Avocado", pattern 601), "Sandwich", "Constellation". Colours: Burnt Honey/amber, Chantilly green, Sunset (amberina), Spruce, amethyst, coral. Mostly $10–60 a set.
- **Federal Glass (Columbus OH, 1900–1979):** shield F mark. Star (square starburst) pitchers and tumblers from the 1950s, Heritage, Moroccan Amethyst. Low.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Corning Glass Works (Pyrex), 1915–1998** in the US. After 1998, World Kitchen's "pyrex" (lowercase) is soda-lime glass and low value.
- **Anchor Hocking** (Lancaster OH): Fire-King, Fairfield (1970s textured pressed glass, avocado, amber, clear), Wexford, Early American Prescut.
- **Hazel-Atlas** (WV): Criss-Cross refrigerator dishes, Moderntone, Ships and Sailboat barware, Kitchen Aids. HA monogram.
- **Jeannette Glass**: Jadite (spelled "Jadite") kitchenware, Delphite blue, Swirl.
- **McKee Glass**: Glasbake, Jadeite (McKee's Seville yellow, Skokie green).
- **Indiana Glass / Tiara Exclusives**: as above. **Federal Glass**: as above.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Pyrex: high gloss = hand-washed. A dull, chalky finish = dishwasher damage ("DWD"), which takes 50–80% off.
- Jadeite: genuine is a uniform translucent "jade" glow under strong light, with mould-marked bases. Reproductions are opaque, heavier, often have a bubbly texture, or are unmarked.
- Depression glass: thin with small bubbles and mould seams. Modern repro glass is thicker, with wrong colour tones and crisp new edges.
- Decals and printed patterns: crisp, no fading. Fading and scratches (utensil marks) lower value.
- Amber Tiara and Indiana: deep honey colour, heavy pressed pattern. The weight makes shipping expensive relative to value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips (rims, handles, feet), cracks (especially Pyrex thermal cracks), utensil-mark scratches, dishwasher dulling, faded or worn patterns, cloudiness ("sick glass").
- Missing lids (casseroles and refrigerator dishes lose half their value without the matching lid).
- Glued repairs on stems and handles.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Jadeite is heavily reproduced (1990s–present Chinese "Jadeite" and Martha Stewart/Fire-King reissues). Old Fire-King has the anchor-and-"Fire-King Oven Ware" mark. Modern reissues say "Fire-King" in new fonts or nothing.
- Depression glass repros (Mosser, Summit, Chinese) in pink and cobalt. Use the "Depression Glass reproductions" guides by pattern.
- Tiara and Indiana "Sweet Pear" is itself a 1970s–80s reissue of the 1920s Indiana "Avocado" pattern. The originals are pink, green and crystal and worth more.
- Pyrex patterns have not been reproduced much, but post-1998 lowercase "pyrex" decorated bowls exist and are low.

## Market
Pyrex has a large, active collector base. Common patterns are saturated, while rare patterns sell in days. Jadeite is steady. Depression glass prices have fallen 50%+ since the 2000s peak. Tiara, Indiana, Federal and Glasbake are slow and low, and heavy glass costs eat the margin. Q4 brings a mild bump (gifting, holiday baking).

## What sellers usually miss
- **Pyrex promo and rare patterns** listed as "old bowl" or "Pyrex dish". Check the pattern (hearts and shamrocks, eyes, pink/black gooseberry).
- **Genuine Jadeite** mixed into kitchen lots (check for the "Fire-King Oven Ware" anchor mark).
- **Complete sets with lids.** A full Pyrex primary-colour 4-bowl set is worth much more than the sum of single bowls.
- The reverse: sellers over-value amber and clear pressed glass, deviled-egg plates and Glasbake (very common), and Lens AI often inflates Tiara sets.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy and fragile. A Pyrex bowl set is 8–12 lb ($25–40 double-boxed). A pitcher and 6–8 tumblers is 8–10 lb ($25–35). A single casserole or plate is 2–4 lb ($14–20). Double-box with 2" of padding. Breakage claims are common for under-packed glass. Sets fit a passenger seat, but the shipping cost both ways kills most of the profit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the maker, the pattern name, the model number from the base, the size and capacity, and the colour name (Burnt Honey, Turquoise, Jadeite).
- Show the base mark, gloss (photograph under a light to show the shine), chips, utensil marks and dishwasher dulling.
- Say "vintage, 1970s Tiara reissue" etc. honestly, and don't call reissues "Depression glass".
- For sets, show the pieces together and list the count.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). With glass costing $20–35 each way, buy only:
- a rare Pyrex pattern or promo, or a complete primary or pink Pyrex bowl set with gloss, at ≤ 25% of sold
- genuine Fire-King Jadeite (anchor mark visible) at ≤ $20 a piece
- Depression-glass rarities identified from the photo

Pass on clear pressed glass, Glasbake, deviled-egg plates, amber Tiara and Indiana sets, Federal Star and all other low-tier glass at any price over about $5. No authentication service is needed, but reproduction Jadeite and Depression glass must be ruled out from base-mark photos.
