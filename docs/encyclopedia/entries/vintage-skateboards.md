## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Three eras show up:
- **1970s fibreglass / early urethane**: Gordon & Smith Fibreflex (1964–80s): laminated maple/fibreglass deck, reddish-brown
  epoxy top with a shield-shaped "Fibreflex skateboard by G&S" decal; models Bowlrider, Teamrider, Pipeline (kicktail),
  Cutaway. Period hardware adds a lot: Tracker Fultrack/Midtrack trucks (cast aluminium, "Tracker" on the baseplate),
  Kryptonics (clear amber/translucent 65–70 mm wheels with rainbow lettering), Road Rider, Sims Snakes, Powell Bones.
  Other 1970s names: Logan Earth Ski, Bahne, Sims Taperkick, Z-Flex, Alva.
- **1980s decks**: wide (10"), fish tail, concave with a nose kick, rails/tail bone screw holes, "pig" shape with a short
  nose. Powell Peralta (Caballero dragon, Hawk skull, McGill, Mountain, Ripper, Skull & Sword), Santa Cruz (Roskopp,
  Kendall), Vision (Gator, Psycho Stick, Double Vision), Alva, Sims, Dogtown. **Originals**: 7-ply maple with thin (not
  glossy) screen print, wear through the graphic, old-style four-hole truck pattern, 1980s copyright line, stamp on top
  ("Made in USA", Vision; Powell Peralta stamp; Taylor Dykema/NHS stamp), wear consistent with age.
- **Reissues** (Powell Bones Brigade series, Santa Cruz reissues, Vision reissues 2000s+): glossy, crisp print, the word
  "reissue" or a modern Skate One/Bones Brigade series stamp, © date under the logo (e.g. "© 2010"), modern bar code,
  modern wheelbase; retail $70–90 new. Santa Cruz Everslick = slick plastic bottom, 1990s; TMNT collaborations are 2010s.
  Note on reissue start date: the earliest write-up says Powell reissues run "2005–present"; a later one says Powell
  started ~1999 and has been continuous since. The later (skateone.com-sourced) statement is likely the more accurate.

Sellers say "rare", "limited edition", "vintage", "OG", "NOS", "vintage 70s skateboard".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Powell Peralta** (1978–91 originals; reissues since ~1999/2005): Hawk, Caballero, McGill, Ripper, Skull & Sword,
  Mountain; Hawk Chicken Skull NOS and Bones Brigade first runs top.
- **Santa Cruz**: Roskopp, Kendall originals collectible; Everslick (1990s); TMNT collabs (2010s, decent).
- **Vision**: Gator, Psycho Stick, Double Vision originals $150–400.
- **G&S Fibreflex**: Bowlrider, Teamrider, Pipeline, Cutaway.
- **Alva, Dogtown, Z-Flex, Sims (Taperkick, Snakes), Logan Earth Ski, Bahne** (1970s; Dogtown/Alva/Z-Flex top).
- **Schmitt Stix, Variflex pro models** (decent rider decks).
- **Nash, Roller Derby, Free Former, Variflex toy boards** (junk).
- **Hardware**: Tracker Fultrack/Midtrack, Independent 169 ($40–80 used), Gullwing, Kryptonics wheels ($80–150 set),
  Bones/OJ wheels, Road Rider.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
70s: fibreglass/maple laminates (Fibreflex), solid hardwood, aluminium, or plastic (toy boards). 80s: 7-ply maple, matte
screen print that wears through. Reissues: glossy crisp print, modern stamps. Wear consistent with age (graphic worn
where trucks and feet sit) vs fresh print. Truck baseplate markings identify Tracker/Independent/Gullwing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Razor tail (worn tail), chips and delamination, cracks at truck holes, water damage, stickers over graphics, extra drilled
holes. NOS in shrink is worth multiples of used. Graphic condition matters most for wall-hanging collectors. Repainted or
re-screened decks exist; look for overspray and fresh paint over wear.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reissues are the main issue: most "rare limited edition" CL listings are reissues — check the © date under the graphic
and any "reissue" text. Counterfeit Powell/Santa Cruz decks from overseas exist (general knowledge, verify); check ply
count, stamps and print quality.

## Market
Collectors (Gen-X nostalgia, 40–55), wall-art buyers and riders for reissues. Reissues have lots of supply and sell at
or below retail; originals sell steadily on eBay and in Facebook vintage-skate groups; NOS and first runs go to
specialist auctions. Reissue supply keeps a lid on mid-tier prices (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Reissue vs original (the © date under the graphic). Most "rare limited edition" CL listings are reissues — ask for the
bottom graphic edge/copyright. Wheel brand (Kryptonics clear amber) and truck brand (Tracker) – a "vintage 70s
skateboard" with those is a $300+ board (but see the Terapeak median above). Fibreflex decal is usually named. 1980s
Independent trucks and Bones/OJ wheels on a junk deck are worth more than the deck; complete setups with
Independent/Tracker trucks and era wheels add $50–100.

## Shipping / handling
Deck 3–4 lb, needs a 33×10×4 box, $18–25; 70s complete ~5 lb, 30" long, $20–30 each way; 80s complete 8–10 lb,
$25–35 long box. eBay 13%. Resell on eBay or Facebook vintage-skate groups.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model/rider, year, original vs reissue (photo of copyright line and stamps), dimensions (length × width),
NOS/used, and hardware brands. Photograph top, graphic, both ends, truck baseplates and wheel lettering. Title: maker,
rider/model, year, "original" or "reissue", "NOS"/"complete", truck and wheel brands.

## Buy rule
Merged rules from the earlier write-ups:
- Buy when: an original 1980s Powell/Santa Cruz/Vision ≤ $100, or NOS reissue ≤ $40 (first write-up); confirmed 1980s
  original Vision/Powell/Santa Cruz ≤ $60 (second); original 1980s pro deck (no "reissue", period copyright) at ≤ $150,
  or a complete with Indy/Tracker/Gullwing + Bones at ≤ $100 (fourth); complete Fibreflex/Tracker/Kryptonics under $120,
  or any Kryptonics-wheeled 70s board under $60 (third). Use the strictest that applies to the item's tier; for
  Fibreflex completes, the Terapeak median ($204) means buy ≤ ~$75.
- Pass when: reissue priced ≥ $60, TMNT Everslick ≥ $40; seller says reissue or quotes eBay; price ≥ $150 on a 2000s
  build; Fibreflex complete at $200.
- Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
