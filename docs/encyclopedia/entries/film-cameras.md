## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Body name on the prism/top plate; lens name on the front ring. The money is in: Nikon F/F2/F3/FM2/FE2, Canon AE-1 Program/A-1/F-1, Olympus OM-1/OM-2, Pentax K1000/LX, Minolta X-700/XD-11, Contax (any), Leica (any – stop everything), Rollei TLRs, Hasselblad, Mamiya 7/RZ67, Pentax 67, Olympus Stylus Epic/mju-II (compact, $150–300), Contax T2/T3 ($800–1,500), Yashica T4 ($200–400). Also fast primes: 50/1.4, 85/1.8, 35/1.4 Nikkor/Canon FD/Zuiko.
Cine: Bell & Howell Filmo 70-DR/DL (16 mm, turret) $150–300; Bolex H16 $300–800; Super-8 Canon 814/1014 $150–400.
See also the separate, more detailed entry `film-slr-cameras` (overlapping scope).

Reading a photo, in order:
- **Format**: an SLR has a prism "hump" over the lens. A rangefinder is flat-topped with two windows on the front (viewfinder plus rangefinder). A TLR has two stacked lenses. A compact is a pocket-size plastic or titanium shell with a retracting lens. Cine cameras have a crank or spring-motor key, often a lens turret (Filmo, Bolex).
- **Where the identifiers sit**: the model name is on the prism front or top plate. The serial number is on the top plate (Leica, Nikon F-series), the back of the body, or the base plate. The lens name, focal length, f-number and serial are on the front name ring.
- **Seller title words**: "old camera", "vintage camera with case", "box of cameras", "estate camera lot", "Leica style", "untested", "as is".
- **Look-alikes**: Soviet Zorki/FED and Kiev bodies look like Leica and Contax rangefinders (see Fakes). Store brands were made by the big Japanese makers: Sears/Tower (Mamiya, Ricoh, Fujica), Revue, Vivitar, and Argus Cosina. They resell for $20–80, not as the maker's own model. The 2026-10-13 review note in Seen in sweeps covers one.

- **Nikon**: F/F2/F3 (F3 HP top), FM2/FE2 collectible; EM decent.
- **Canon**: AE-1 Program, A-1, F-1 collectible.
- **Olympus**: OM-1/OM-2 collectible; Stylus Epic/mju-II compact $150–300.
- **Pentax**: K1000/LX collectible; ME decent; Pentax 67 top.
- **Minolta**: X-700/XD-11 collectible; X-370 decent.
- **Contax**: any; T2/T3 $800–1,500.
- **Leica**: any – stop everything (M and screw-mount III).
- **Rollei** TLRs, **Hasselblad** 500C/M, **Mamiya** 7/RZ67: top.
- **Yashica** T4: $200–400.
- **Zeiss Ikon** Contina/Contessa: decent.
- **Cine**: Bell & Howell Filmo 70-DR/DL, Bolex H16, Canon 814/1014.
- **Junk**: Kodak Disc, 110s, Instamatic, Polaroid 600/640/OneStep.

Nuances that move value (general knowledge, verify per model):
- **Lens kit vs body only**: a body with a fast "normal" lens (50/1.4, 50/1.7, 50/1.8) is the usual sellable unit. A body alone sells for less, except Leica, Contax and Hasselblad bodies.
- **Contax T2 retail history**: a clean T2 cost roughly $400–500 fifteen years ago and passed $1,000 in the early 2020s. A *serviced* T2 now sells around $2,000 from specialist dealers in 2026 (Brooklyn Film Camera). An untested T2 from an estate is worth far less than a serviced one. The camera has a Carl Zeiss Sonnar 38 mm f/2.8 T* lens and comes in titanium-silver, champagne and black finishes (eBay listings).
- **Leica**: screw-mount (II, III, IIIf, IIIg) vs M-mount (M3, M2, M4, M6). The serial number on the top plate dates the body and is checked against published Leica serial lists. Factory engravings are sharp and paint-filled.
- **Nikon F3**: "HP" (high-eyepoint) finder is more desirable. Nikon F with a plain prism (no meter) is the classic collector body.
- **Black vs chrome**: black versions of some bodies bring more (general knowledge, verify per model).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the model name on the prism or top plate and the lens front ring (focal length and f-number). An f/1.4 or f/1.2 lens is worth a closer look.
- Check whether the box or lot includes a Leica, Contax, Hasselblad, Rollei, Mamiya 7 or premium compact (mju-II, T2/T3, T4). These names are the reason to buy a lot.
- On anything called "Leica", look for a round rangefinder roller under the top plate and sharp, paint-filled engravings. A metal coupling arm or crude, unfilled numbers mean it is a Soviet conversion (Eckman).
- Look for battery-door corrosion (green crust) in a photo of the base or the battery cover.
- Look through the lens at a light, if the seller has a photo. Haze, fungus webs or separation lower the value.
- Check the shutter curtain for dents or holes and the film door for crumbling foam seals.
- A brand on the camera that is not the real maker (Sears, Revue, Vivitar) means the camera is a rebadge and is priced lower.
- Ask whether it was film-tested or only "shutter fires". Untested halves the price.

### Golden-egg tells
- Leica M factory black paint (M2, M3, M4) — "well over £2,000" vs a chrome M4 "priced in the hundreds" (~5–10×) — paint brassing on top-plate edges and baseplate matching the body, black paint (not black chrome) on the rewind knob and shoe; many chrome M3s are repainted, so check the serial against black-paint batches before paying up (https://www.macfilos.com/2017/04/25/2017-4-25-black-paint-leicas-much-ado-about-nothing/)
- Nikon F2 Titan / unmarked F2 Titanium — ~3–5× vs the $200–350 chrome F2 — thick mottled, textured black finish on titanium top plate, baseplate and prism; serial beginning 79 ("Titan" engraved on the front) or 92 (unmarked, rarer); meterless titanium prism as standard (https://www.thephotoforum.com/threads/nikon-f2-t-titanium-body-new-condition.76798)
- Contax T2 gold / 60 Years limited edition — above the $700–2,000 serviced T2 band — gold-tone body with anniversary engraving on the top plate instead of titanium-silver, champagne or black (https://www.aol.com/articles/12-vintage-cameras-shockingly-valuable-201534785.html)
- Canon FD 55 mm f/1.2 S.S.C. Aspherical — ~€1,399 average dealer sold vs $60–90 for the FD 50/1.4 (~15×) — "ASPHERICAL" engraved on the front ring with "S.S.C.", 55 mm not 50 mm (https://kamerastore.com/products/canon-55mm-f1-2-s-s-c-aspherical-1)
- Olympus OM-1 black vs chrome — black "fetches higher prices" (entry: black collectible vs $120–200 chrome) — black top and bottom plates with OM-1 in white, not a black-leatherette chrome body (https://www.aol.com/articles/12-vintage-cameras-shockingly-valuable-201534785.html)
- Rollei 35 made in Germany (and gold-plated limited editions) vs Singapore — German bodies "often cost more"; gold editions above the Rollei 35 S top tier — "Made in Germany by Rollei" on the back plate or under the lens vs "Made in Singapore"; gold-plated body with lizard covering (https://www.aol.com/articles/12-vintage-cameras-shockingly-valuable-201534785.html)
- Polaroid SX-70 folding (Alpha 1, Sonar) vs Polaroid 600/OneStep — $100–several hundred vs $5–25 junk tier (~10×) — flat folding tan-leather-and-chrome (or black) body that collapses to a wedge, not the boxy 600 (https://www.aol.com/articles/12-vintage-cameras-shockingly-valuable-201534785.html)
- Leica M6 with early serial or limited-edition engraving — kits near $3,000 (~40% up) vs the $500–3,000 top tier — "LEICA" red dot on front, engraving on the top plate for editions, serial on the hot shoe (https://www.aol.com/articles/12-vintage-cameras-shockingly-valuable-201534785.html)
- Soviet FED/Zorki engraved as "Leica" — ~0.05× of a real screw-mount Leica — metal rangefinder coupling arm instead of a round roller, crude unfilled engraving, mismatched serials (https://mikeeckman.com/how-to-spot-a-fake-leica-tldr)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
All-metal mechanical bodies (F2, FM2, K1000, OM-1) are robust; electronic bodies (AE-1P, X-700) depend on working electronics. Lens front ring names the focal length and aperture – fast apertures (f/1.4, f/1.8) add value. Black paint versions of some bodies bring more (general knowledge, verify).
- **Mechanical vs electronic**: a mechanical shutter (F2, FM2, K1000, OM-1, Leica M) fires without a battery at most speeds. Electronic shutters (AE-1/AE-1P, X-700, most compacts) are dead without a battery and can fail outright. Fully electronic compacts such as the T2 cannot easily be repaired. A broken flex cable can mean needing a donor camera (Brooklyn Film Camera).
- **Quality tells**: brass top plates with crisp engraving and even chrome, smooth focus helicoids, and multi-coated glass (amber or purple reflections) mark quality. Plastic bodies and moulded lettering mark budget lines.
- **Age tells**: worn brassing at the edges of black paint is genuine wear and is liked by collectors. Shiny unworn edges on a "1950s" black body are suspicious. Original leatherette lifts at the corners. Fresh, too-uniform leatherette means the camera has been recovered.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Shutter not firing or capping, sticky aperture blades, lens fungus/haze/separation, light seals degraded, meter dead, battery-door corrosion, Canon AE-1 "shutter squeal", Minolta X-700 capacitor faults (general knowledge, verify). "Untested" halves the price.
- **Minolta X-700**: a failed capacitor locks the winder tight. The fix is an expensive capacitor replacement. Check that the winder moves freely (Jim Grey).
- **Minolta Maxxum 7000**: a failed autoexposure magnet makes it shoot only at the smallest aperture. Canon EOS Rebel shutters fail often; look for goo or an arc of marks on the curtain (Jim Grey).
- **Premium compacts (T2 and similar)**: autofocus hunting in low light or close up, battery drain, light leaks at the film door, sticky aperture blades, broken flex cables, aged seals and battery corrosion. A full service (CLA) costs $400–500 (Brooklyn Film Camera).
- **Repairs in photos**: new light-seal foam, replaced leatherette, mismatched screws or scratched screw heads on the top plate mean the camera has been opened. That is fine if a technician did it. It is a problem if the seller did.
- **What to ask**: does it fire at every speed? Does the meter respond? Is the lens clear with a flashlight shone through it? Has it shot a roll of film? Is there corrosion in the battery chamber?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Leicas (Soviet Zorki/FED bodies re-engraved as Leica) are common; check serial numbers and engraving (general knowledge, verify).
Specific tells of a Zorki/FED conversion (Mike Eckman):
- The exposure counter and shutter-speed dial numbers are crude and not paint-filled.
- There is no round rangefinder roller; a metal coupling arm sits there instead.
- At infinity, the lens focusing tab sits near 9 o'clock (near 7 o'clock on a real Leica).
- The front window frame runs up to the top of the camera.
- There is a wide, soft-edged gap left of the accessory shoe.
- There is a black painted border between the covering and the top and bottom plates.
- The shutter release has internal cable threads and a fixed collar.
- The cloth shutter curtains have no stitching along the edges.
Fake "Luftwaffe", "Kriegsmarine" and gold-plated Leicas are almost all conversions. There are also legitimate new-production film cameras, such as the Leica M6 reissue (2022) and Pentax 17 (2024) (general knowledge, verify). Date them by serial number and packaging, not by looks.

## Market
Strong film revival demand from younger shooters since ~2018; tested bodies sell fast on eBay, KEH buys outright at lower prices. Premium compacts (T2, mju-II) have been very hot (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Compact point-and-shoots (Olympus mju-II, Yashica T4, Contax T2) listed as "old film camera $10"; lenses – a "camera with lens" where the lens is a 50/1.4 or 85/1.8 worth more than the body. Leica/Contax/Hasselblad names in a "box of cameras" lot.

## Shipping / handling
1–2 lb, $10–15 each way. Sell on eBay (13%); "untested" halves the price, so only buy where the seller says film-tested or shutter fires.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State body and lens model, serial, shutter fires at all speeds, meter works, light seals, lens glass clear (no fungus/haze), film-tested or not. Photograph top plate, lens front ring, glass with a light behind it, and the film chamber. Title: "[Brand] [Model] 35mm film SLR + [lens], film tested".

### What to tell the buyer
Give the exact body and lens model with both serial numbers. Say what was tested and how: "shutter fires and sounds correct at 1 s to 1/1000", "meter responds to light", or "film-tested with a roll, scans available". If it was not tested, say "untested". Describe the glass honestly as clear, light haze, or fungus, checked with a flashlight through the lens. State the battery chamber condition (clean, or corrosion) and whether the light seals are original or replaced. Note any service history with the date and shop. Name the finish (black or chrome; titanium or champagne on compacts) and list what is included (cap, case, strap, manual, box). For a Leica, state that it was checked against the genuine Leica tells: round rangefinder roller and paint-filled engravings.

## Buy rule
Buy when: collectible-tier body+lens under 40% of sold median and seller says it works; any Leica/Contax/Hasselblad/mju-II under $100. Pass when: decent-tier at $40+, Polaroid/Disc anything. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
