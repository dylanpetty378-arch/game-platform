## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Bags printed with a bank's name and town. Two forms: **canvas drawstring coin bags** (tall, with a cord) and **zippered deposit pouches** (canvas or vinyl).
- Collectors value **small-town and defunct banks**, bags with **building pictures or logos**, old "Member Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation" wording, and state-specific names (Wyoming, Montana, New Mexico draw regional collectors).
- **Federal Reserve** and **US Mint** canvas bags (with "$1000" or coin denominations stenciled) are a separate, popular category.

## Makers, brands and variants
Bags were made by industrial bag makers (e.g., MMF, Ampad, Sentry, Canvas specialties) and printed for each bank. The bank name is the variant.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bright, legible print; no handwriting; working drawstrings or zippers; clean canvas.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Ink writing (owner names), stains, fading, tears, broken zippers. Writing reduces value a little but is common.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Novelty "money bag" props and decor reproductions exist; Federal Reserve style bags are reproduced as decor. Small-town bank bags are not faked.

## Market
Moderate volume. Banking-memorabilia and regional collectors. Singles take 2–6 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers dump bags as "misc" for a few dollars; regional bank names (Wyoming, Montana, Alaska, Hawaii) sell well individually.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, light. Singles ship in poly mailer for $5–6; lot $8–12 (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Bank name and town, style (drawstring/zipper), dimensions, any writing.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − shipping − ~$10 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Singles at $15 each net about $8 after shipping, so a lot needs ~8 good canvas bags to clear $50. Labour is high (many listings). Only buy very cheap lots.
