## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small play figures of animals. Material is the key value tell:
- **Hollow-cast lead** (Britains, Johillco, Crescent, Taylor & Barrett, 1900–1966): heavy, dull painted surface with paint chips revealing grey metal, often a small round hole (casting vent), "Britains Ltd" + "Made in England" cast under the base.
- **Composition** (Elastolin, Lineol, 1904–1960s): sawdust/glue body over wire armature, matte painted, cracks and chips show a brown-grey core; "Elastolin" or "Lineol" stamped under the base; often on a small integral base.
- **Hard polystyrene/painted plastic** 1950s–70s (Britains "Herald", Starlux France, Timpo, Crescent): crisp detail, hand-painted, marked.
- **Soft PVC/polyethylene**: Marx playset animals (unpainted single colours, 1950s–70s, often "MARX" under), Hong Kong generic "rack toys" (1960s–90s; crude moulding, airbrushed colours, sold by the bag), Imperial Toy, AAA (1980s–2000s). Examples 7955943094, 7951210188, 7947527567 are all this tier.
- **Modern collectible PVC** (Schleich – "©Schleich" + year + "Germany/China" under the belly; Safari Ltd – "©Safari Ltd" + year; Papo – "Papo"; Bullyland; Collecta; CollectA): detailed painted, heavier.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Britains** (lead farm, zoo and hunting series 1921–1966; plastic 1966+) – lead animals $10–60 each, rare zoo pieces (giraffes, elephants with howdahs) $50–200.
- **Elastolin** (O&M Hausser, Germany) and **Lineol** – composition zoo/farm animals $20–150 each; boxed sets more.
- **Starlux** (France 1950s–80s) $5–30; **Timpo**, **Crescent**, **Charbens**, **Johillco** (lead) $5–40.
- **Marx** playset animals (Noah's Ark, Farm, Zoo, Jungle) $1–5 each, $20–60 for a complete playset's animal complement.
- **Schleich** – retired 1980s–2000s figures $5–30, a few retired rarities (early Smurf-era animals, some Bayala/dinosaurs) $50–150; current $5–15 retail.
- **Safari Ltd / Carnegie Collection dinosaurs** – retired Carnegie dinosaurs $10–60.
- **Hong Kong / Taiwan / China generics** – $0.25–2 each.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Weight (lead is heavy), paint (hand-painted vs airbrushed), base markings, crispness of the moulding. Marked hard plastic and lead with original paint and no breaks are what collectors buy; soft generic plastic is bulk filler.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Lead: broken legs/horns, lead rot (white powder), repainting (bright gloss, no chips) – repainted lead is worth half or less. Composition: cracks, missing ears/tails, flaking paint, wire showing. Plastic: chew marks, sun-fading, stickiness from PVC breakdown, missing horns/tails.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Britains reissued some lead designs as "Britains Collectors' Edition" metal figures (1990s–2000s, marked with modern dates). Recast lead figures (white metal, solid, heavier) sold as Britains – check for hollow cast and correct base mark. Elastolin reissues ("Preiser" and "Elastolin repro") from the 1980s onward are plastic, not composition.

## Market
Steady among toy-soldier collectors (lead, composition) and Schleich collectors; bulk generic plastic sells slowly, bought for crafts and dioramas. Christmas bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Lead or composition animals mixed into a bag of plastic ("vintage miniature animals") – look for chipped paint showing grey metal or brown core, and flat painted bases. Sellers usually over-estimate generic plastic as "vintage".

## Shipping / handling
Bags of plastic 1–3 lb, $10–15; lead lots get heavy fast (each figure 1–2 oz) – $15–25. Wrap lead figures individually (legs snap).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + material + series + count + era: "Britains lead farm animals lot of 12", "Elastolin composition zoo lion", "Schleich retired", "Marx playset animals", "vintage Hong Kong plastic animals bag". Junk: "miniature animals", "toy lot", "vintage" on generic plastic. Example: `Britains Hollow Cast Lead Farm Animals Lot of 12 Cows Horses Sheep 1950s England`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (toy-soldier and Schleich collectors; generic plastic sells slowly for crafts); boxed Britains/Elastolin sets to Vectis/Morphy-type toy auctions; Christmas bump.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (generic bags $10–25, Marx/common Schleich $2–8 each, retired Schleich/Carnegie/Britains plastic $8–40 each, lead/composition $15–150 each); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) the whole lot arranged in rows on a plain cloth; then base marks (maker, country), each lead/composition figure individually, paint chips showing metal or core, breaks and repaints, a ruler for height, series sorted (farm, zoo, marine).
- **State:** count, maker, material (lead, composition, plastic), sizes, series, base marks, breaks and repaints by photo number, retired/current for Schleich, "generic unmarked" said honestly.
- **Pack / haul:** plastic bags 1–3 lb, $10–15; lead lots 1–2 oz per figure, each wrapped individually (legs snap), single box with fill, 3–5 lb class, $15–25.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Britains hollow-cast lead / Elastolin composition] figures, marked [England / Germany] on the bases in photo [2], not the Hong Kong plastic they get bagged with — [N] pieces, [X] with original paint; comparable lots sell for [$X]."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$12) − ship to Dylan (~$12) − price ≥ max($50, price). A lot must sell for ≥ ~$85. Only lots containing marked Britains lead, Elastolin/Lineol composition, or ≥ 15 retired Schleich/Carnegie figures can make it, bought at ≤ $3 per figure. Pass all unmarked soft-plastic bags at any price above ~$5.
