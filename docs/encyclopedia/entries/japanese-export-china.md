## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ceramics made in Japan for US import. Marks date items: "Nippon" (1891–1921), "Made in Japan"/"Japan" (1921–41, 1952–), "Made in Occupied Japan" (1945–52), paper labels for Thames, Lefton, Napco, Norcrest, Enesco (1950s–70s). Thames pieces: relief-moulded fruit with twig handles, white body, often foil/paper "Thames Hand Painted" label. Nippon: raised slip (moriage), gold beading, scenic painting.
More: US customs (McKinley Tariff, 1891) required a country of origin, so "Nippon" appears from 1891; from 1921 US customs required "Japan" instead. Occupied Japan marks appear on exports from about 1946/47 to 1952. Most 1950s–70s giftware was marked only by a paper or foil label (often lost) plus a stamped "Japan" or importer code, so an unmarked piece with a ghost of a label outline is normal.
What the categories look like: **Nippon era**: thin porcelain vases, ewers, chocolate sets, humidors with hand painting, heavy gold, moriage (piped slip dots and lines), "coralene" beading, and backstamps in green, blue or magenta (M-in-wreath, maple leaf, rising sun, RC) under the glaze. **Noritake Art Deco (1920s–30s)**: lustre-glazed figural dishes, powder boxes, lady figures, marked with the M-in-wreath "Noritake Made in Japan". **Mid-century giftware**: Thames teapots and fruit sets; redware (red clay) teapots with black glaze and painted faces (Thames clown teapot); Lefton bluebirds, Christmas, "Miss Priss" cat, angels; Napco planters (head vases, Christmas); Holt Howard Pixieware condiment jars, Cozy Kittens, Christmas candle-holders; Norcrest and Enesco figurines.
Look-alikes: Chinese and Taiwanese 1980s–2000s copies of all the above (marked Taiwan or China, or decal "Japan"); Satsuma-style modern export (crude gold, "Satsuma" with Shimazu cross on new pieces).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Thames (1950s–60s)**: importer brand; paper label "Thames — Made in Japan" (or "Thames Hand Painted"); some pieces stamped "Pat. Pend."; relief-fruit teapots and condiment sets with twig handles; figural redware teapots, notably the clown teapot with wire handle (est. $75–125 by an antiques columnist), plus matching shakers, creamers and stacking sets.
- **Lefton (Geo. Zoltan Lefton, Chicago, 1940; Japanese production through the 1970s, then Taiwan and Malaysia; sold 2001)**: marks "Lefton's", "Lefton China", "Geo. Z. Lefton", "G.Z.L.", an "L", plus red-and-gold paper labels and a stock number; Occupied Japan pieces (1946–52, much made by Miyawo). Collected: Christmas (holly, Santas, angels), bluebirds, Miss Priss cat, figural kitchen pieces, Occupied-era floral pieces.
- **Napco (National Potteries Co., Cleveland)**: head vases and planters, Christmas, "Napcoware" stamps, often "C" + number codes.
- **Norcrest, Enesco, UCAGCO, Relpo, Inarco, Lipper & Mann, Kreiss (Psycho Ceramics)**: importers with paper labels; Enesco Christmas and kitchen figurals collected.
- **Holt Howard (US importer, Stamford CT; Pixieware 1958–early 1960s)**: stamped or stickered "Holt Howard" and a date such as "© 1958" or "© 1959"; Pixieware condiment jars, cruets, Cozy Kittens, Christmas.
- **Noritake / Morimura Bros.**: Nippon-era and later Noritake; M-in-wreath mark; Art Deco lustre is the prize.
- **Unmarked Nippon factories**: dozens of Nippon backstamps from other factories (maple leaf, rising sun, RC Nippon, etc.).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the base: "Nippon" (1891–1921) vs "Japan"/"Made in Japan" (1921+) vs "Occupied Japan" (1945–52) vs paper label only (1950s–70s).
- Nippon mark colour and placement: under the glaze, worn with the base; green M-in-wreath, maple leaf or rising sun.
- Hand painting (brush strokes, slight variation) vs decal (dot screen, perfect edges).
- Moriage: raised slip you can see in raking light, not flat gold paint.
- Label remnants: Lefton red/gold, Thames, Napco, Holt Howard, with stock numbers.
- Holt Howard pieces: "© 1958/1959 Holt Howard" stamp, pixie face, spoon slot.
- Material: thin translucent porcelain (Nippon/Noritake) vs thick earthenware or redware (mid-century giftware).
- Chips on spouts, lid rims, finials and pixie noses; crazing on earthenware.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Fine hand painting vs decal; raised gold; heavy moriage. 1950s–70s giftware is earthenware with crazing. More: Nippon and Noritake are hard-paste porcelain (thin, translucent, rings when tapped); gold on genuine Nippon is soft and wears on high points; moriage stands proud and is often chipped at tips. Mid-century giftware is moulded earthenware or redware with cold paint (unfired paint that rubs off, e.g. on Thames fruit) under or over a clear glaze; paper labels and fresh cold paint mean little use. Old vs new: genuine old bases show grey grit wear on the foot rim; new repros have clean unworn bases, bright gold, and decal marks sitting on top of the glaze.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on lid/spout, crazing, paint wear on fruit. Also: lid replaced by a different maker's lid, rubbed gold, glued finials, hairlines in thin porcelain (hold to light), restored moriage dots (painted filler). Ask for base photos, close-ups of spout and finial, and a photo of the lid seated.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Nippon marks (common reproductions from 1980s); compare mark guides (maple leaf, M-in-wreath). More: fake Nippon pieces are usually decal-decorated with bright, unworn gold, and the mark is a crisp decal (often in odd colours or with misspelt wording) that is not worn into the glaze. Pixieware and Holt Howard have been reproduced (later Asian copies without the dated stamp, or with blurry stamps); compare to known shapes. "Occupied Japan" marks have been faked on new figurines (check whether the mark is under the glaze or a later stamp). Satsuma: post-war and modern export Satsuma with crude gold is the norm; antique Meiji Satsuma is a separate specialist market.

## Market
Slow; plentiful. The kitsch end (Holt Howard, Lefton Christmas, figural teapots) has an active mid-century collector market on eBay and Etsy; the Nippon/Noritake market is older collectors and slower.

## What sellers usually miss
Fake vs real Nippon goes both ways; Holt Howard kitsch sells. Also: the dated Holt Howard stamp on an "unmarked" kitsch jar; Lefton paper labels that identify a Christmas or Miss Priss piece; Noritake Deco lustre listed as "lustreware dish Japan".

## Shipping / handling
Fragile, $12–15. Double-box; lids wrapped separately; pixie and figural parts padded.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Mark/label photo, chips, set completeness. Give the maker or importer, the exact mark text and colour, height, and whether decoration is hand-painted or decal; include raking-light photos of moriage.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker/importer], made in Japan [era from mark: Nippon 1891–1921 / Occupied Japan 1945–52 / 1950s–60s]. Mark on the base reads '[text]' in [colour] [under the glaze / paper label]. [Hand-painted / moriage raised slip / lustre glaze]. [H] in tall. No chips, cracks or repairs [or: describe], light crazing normal for earthenware. Lid original and fits."
