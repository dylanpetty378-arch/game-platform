## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
All-metal barrels with engraved focus scale and aperture ring. Nikon: "Nikkor" on the front ring, AI-S has a scalloped rabbit-ear prong and a small lug on the mount. The Micro-Nikkor 200mm f/4 IF is long and slim with a built-in tripod collar. Vivitar: "Vivitar Series 1" is the valuable line (70–210 f/3.5, 90mm f/2.5 macro, 135 f/2.3); plain "Tele Vivitar" 400mm f/5.6 preset T-mount lenses are $20 lenses. Kodak Ektanar projection lenses are near-worthless ($10). Sellers write "vintage lens", "Nikon lens", "telephoto".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Nikon Nikkor** (F mount): pre-AI (1959–77, no AI ridge), AI (1977–), AI-S (1981–, orange minimum aperture number), Series E (budget, $10–40 for 50 f/1.8). Decent: AI 50 f/1.4, 105 f/2.5, 24 f/2.8. Collectible: 200 f/4 Micro IF, 180 f/2.8 ED, 85 f/1.4 AI-S. Top: 58 f/1.2 Noct, 300 f/2.8 ED-IF, 13 f/5.6.
- **Vivitar Series 1**: 70–210 f/3.5 (decent, $60–140), 90mm f/2.5 macro (collectible), 135 f/2.3. Many made by Komine/Kiron/Tokina; the maker is identifiable by serial-number prefix (general knowledge, verify — e.g. serials starting 22 = Kiron, 28 = Komine).
- **Tele Vivitar** 400 f/5.6 preset T-mount: $20–25.
- **Kodak Ektanar** projection lenses: ~$10.
- **Carsen**: budget 1960s–70s import lenses, low value (general knowledge, verify).
- Other makers with value outside this entry's focus (general knowledge, verify): Leica, Zeiss Contax, Canon FD L, Olympus OM (see olympus-om-lenses), Pentax SMC Takumar.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal barrels, engraved (not printed) markings, smooth damped focus. ED/IF on Nikkors signals the expensive glass. Mount matters: Nikon F and M42 have adapter demand from mirrorless shooters; T-mount and projection lenses have little. Front ring text and serial dates the version (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fungus (web-like threads), haze, separation (rainbow edges on cemented elements), oily or stuck aperture blades, dents in the filter ring, heavy scratches on the front element. Ask for a back-lit photo through the lens and a photo of the aperture blades stopped down. Cleaning fungus is a specialist job and can leave etching (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes. Risks are mislabelling (plain 200 f/4 sold as Micro, pre-AI sold as AI, Vivitar non-Series-1 sold as Series 1) and "AI'd" conversions of pre-AI lenses, which are worth somewhat less to collectors (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Deep, liquid market on eBay, KEH and Japanese/European dealers (kamerastore); buyers are film shooters and mirrorless users adapting vintage glass (general knowledge, verify). Good Nikkors sell within days to a few weeks at fair prices. Commodity lenses are slow and cheap.

## What sellers usually miss
Series 1 vs plain Vivitar; Micro vs plain Nikkor 200mm (plain 200 f/4 AI-S is $60, Micro is $250+). Fungus/haze: ask for a back-lit photo. ED / IF / AI-S markings.

## Shipping / handling
1–2 lb, $12–15 to ship, double-box with caps on both ends. Resell on eBay/KEH consignment.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State exact model and mount (AI/AI-S/pre-AI), serial, glass condition (fungus/haze/separation/scratches, with back-lit photo), aperture blades dry and snappy, focus smooth, caps/hood/case/tripod collar included. Title keywords: "Nikon Nikkor 200mm f/4 Micro AI-S IF manual focus lens", "Vivitar Series 1 90mm f/2.5 macro".

## Buy rule
Buy when: Nikon Micro/ED lenses ≤ 40% of kamerastore price; Vivitar Series 1 ≤ $30. Pass when: plain Tele Vivitar, projection lenses, or seller already quotes "legendary" and asks market.
