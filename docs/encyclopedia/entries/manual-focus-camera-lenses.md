## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
All-metal barrels with engraved focus scale and aperture ring. Nikon: "Nikkor" on the front ring. The Micro-Nikkor
200mm f/4 IF is long and slim with a built-in tripod collar. Vivitar: "Vivitar Series 1" is the valuable line (70–210
f/3.5, 90mm f/2.5 macro, 135 f/2.3); plain "Tele Vivitar" 400mm f/5.6 preset T-mount lenses are $20 lenses. Kodak
Ektanar projection lenses are near-worthless ($10). Sellers write "vintage lens", "Nikon lens", "telephoto".

Nikon mount-generation tells (KEH):
- **Non-AI / pre-AI (1959–1977)**: metering prong ("rabbit ears") without holes, plain stainless bayonet base with 5
  screws, single row of aperture numbers, no recessed cut-outs around the aperture ring.
- **AI (1977–)**: prong with holes ("nostrils"), double row of large and small aperture numbers, recessed areas on the
  aperture-ring edge by the mount.
- **AI'd**: a non-AI lens with its aperture ring replaced or modified (recess added), often with a sticker for the second
  number row; a lens that is not multi-coated is most likely AI'd (rule of thumb).
- **AI-S (1981–)**: as AI plus a Lens Type Signal notch cut into the metal bayonet ring; the minimum aperture number is
  orange (general knowledge, verify). (Earlier entry text described AI-S by a "scalloped rabbit-ear prong" — the prong
  with holes is an AI-generation feature, not specific to AI-S; corrected.)
- **Series E**: budget AI lenses for the Nikon EM; no metering prong at all.
Where the serial and name sit: front name ring (Nikkor/Vivitar name, focal length, aperture, serial), or barrel; Vivitar
serials are on the barrel or front ring.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Nikon Nikkor** (F mount): pre-AI (1959–77, no AI ridge), AI (1977–), AI-S (1981–, orange minimum aperture number),
  Series E (budget, $10–40 for 50 f/1.8). Decent: AI 50 f/1.4, 105 f/2.5, 24 f/2.8. Collectible: 200 f/4 Micro IF, 180
  f/2.8 ED, 85 f/1.4 AI-S. Top: 58 f/1.2 Noct, 300 f/2.8 ED-IF, 13 f/5.6. Compatibility (KEH): non-AI lenses fit the F
  Photomic FTN, early F2, Nikkormat FT/FTN/FT2/EL/ELW, can be used unmetered on early AI bodies (FM, FE) but should not be
  mounted on later bodies like the FM2 (can damage the camera); AI lenses work on manual bodies after 1977 and most
  AF DSLRs except low-end ones (manual focus only); AI-S matters for shutter-priority/program bodies (FG, FA, N2000,
  N2020, N8008, F4).
- **Vivitar Series 1** (Ponder & Best, Los Angeles, 1938; incorporated as Vivitar 1964; sold to Hanimex 1985; brand to
  Sakar 2008 — Apotelyt): launched early 1970s. 70–210 f/3.5 (decent, $60–140): the Kiron and Tokina f/3.5 versions and the
  Komine f/2.8–4 are rated best; the later Cosina f/2.8–4 is weaker. 90mm f/2.5 macro ("Bokina", made by Tokina) is the
  collectible one. 135 f/2.3. Fast manual Series 1 primes are well regarded; slow f/4.5–5.6 and autofocus versions are not
  (Apotelyt).
- **Vivitar serial prefixes, 1970–1990** (Apotelyt; confirms the earlier "22 = Kiron, 28 = Komine"): 09/9 Cosina, 13
  Schneider, 22 Kino/Kiron, 25 Ozone, 28 Komine, 32 Makinon, 33 Asanuma, 37 Tokina, 42 Eugen Bauer, 44 Perkin Elmer, 47
  Chinon, 51 Tokyo Trading, 56 Kyoe Shoji, 61 Samyang, 75 Hoya, 77 Kobori, 81 Polar. Not used after 1990.
- **Tele Vivitar** 400 f/5.6 preset T-mount: $20–25.
- **Kodak Ektanar** projection lenses: ~$10.
- **Carsen**: budget 1960s–70s import lenses, low value (general knowledge, verify).
- Other makers with value outside this entry's focus (general knowledge, verify): Leica, Zeiss Contax, Canon FD L, Olympus OM (see olympus-om-lenses), Pentax SMC Takumar.

### Quick ID checklist
- Front ring text: maker, focal length, max aperture, "Micro", "ED", "IF", "Series 1"; serial number.
- Nikon prong: no holes = pre-AI; holes = AI/AI-S/AI'd; no prong = Series E (or a removed prong).
- Aperture ring: one row of numbers (pre-AI) vs two rows (AI); sticker second row = AI'd conversion.
- Bayonet: notch in the mount ring = AI-S; orange smallest f-number = AI-S.
- Vivitar serial prefix → maker (28 Komine, 22 Kiron, 37 Tokina; 09 Cosina = weaker 70–210).
- Mount type: Nikon F / M42 (adapter demand) vs T-mount / projection (little demand).
- Back-lit photo through the lens: fungus threads, haze, separation (rainbow edges), scratches.
- Aperture blades: dry and snappy vs oily/stuck.
- Accessories: caps, hood, tripod collar (200 Micro), case.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal barrels, engraved (not printed) markings, smooth damped focus. ED/IF on Nikkors signals the expensive glass. Mount
matters: Nikon F and M42 have adapter demand from mirrorless shooters; T-mount and projection lenses have little. Front
ring text and serial dates the version (general knowledge, verify). Multi-coating (coloured reflections, often marked
"C" on some late pre-AI Nikkors) indicates later production (general knowledge, verify; KEH uses coating as an AI'd
rule of thumb).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fungus (web-like threads), haze, separation (rainbow edges on cemented elements), oily or stuck aperture blades, dents
in the filter ring, heavy scratches on the front element. Ask for a back-lit photo through the lens and a photo of the
aperture blades stopped down. Cleaning fungus is a specialist job and can leave etching (general knowledge, verify).
KEH inspection method: shine a flashlight through and rotate the lens at different angles (fine scratches and mould
show in reflection); mould may fluoresce under UV; dust that shifts when tilted is minor; mould is the most serious
issue because it spreads and is hard to remove; scratches matter by depth and location (flare, lower contrast); also
check focus/zoom rings for smoothness, the mount for corrosion or wear, and the exterior for dents and missing parts.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes. Risks are mislabelling (plain 200 f/4 sold as Micro, pre-AI sold as AI, Vivitar non-Series-1 sold as Series 1)
and "AI'd" conversions of pre-AI lenses, which are worth somewhat less to collectors (general knowledge, verify). Also:
late Cosina-made Series 1 70–210 sold as the Kiron/Komine versions — check the serial prefix (Apotelyt). Post-2008
Vivitar-branded products are Sakar-era and unrelated to the classic lenses.

## Market
Deep, liquid market on eBay, KEH and Japanese/European dealers (kamerastore); buyers are film shooters and mirrorless
users adapting vintage glass (general knowledge, verify). Good Nikkors sell within days to a few weeks at fair prices.
Commodity lenses are slow and cheap.

## What sellers usually miss
Series 1 vs plain Vivitar; Micro vs plain Nikkor 200mm (plain 200 f/4 AI-S is $60, Micro is $250+). Fungus/haze: ask
for a back-lit photo. ED / IF / AI-S markings. Also: the Vivitar serial prefix (Tokina "Bokina" 90 macro, Kiron/Komine
70–210); whether a pre-AI Nikkor has been AI'd (some buyers want original pre-AI for older bodies, others need AI).

## Shipping / handling
1–2 lb, $12–15 to ship, double-box with caps on both ends. Resell on eBay/KEH consignment.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State exact model and mount (AI/AI-S/pre-AI), serial, glass condition (fungus/haze/separation/scratches, with back-lit
photo), aperture blades dry and snappy, focus smooth, caps/hood/case/tripod collar included. Title keywords: "Nikon Nikkor
200mm f/4 Micro AI-S IF manual focus lens", "Vivitar Series 1 90mm f/2.5 macro".
