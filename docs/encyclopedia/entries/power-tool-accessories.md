## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Saw blades**: steel (one-piece, small teeth, often rusty, worth scrap) vs carbide-tipped (brazed grey tips). Brand on the plate: Master Mechanic (True Value), Craftsman, DeWalt, Vermont American = cheap; Forrest Woodworker II (no branding colour, laser-etched), Freud (red/orange coating), Systimatic, Amana, CMT (orange) = good.
- **Dado sets**: stacked chippers and two outer blades in a case.
- **Flexible shafts**: drill-attachment shafts with chuck at one end; Foredom motors hang from a hook with a foot pedal.
- **Forrest blades** carry an etched serial number (examples cited on forums: 384494, 860161), useful for confirming a genuine Forrest.
- **Foredom machines** (Foredom Electric Co., Bethel, CT, "SINCE 1922" on the plate): hanging motor with a strap, a 39 in sheathed shaft, a handpiece and a foot control. Motor plate shows model letters and series (e.g. "FOREDOM B / SERIES R", SR = current 1/6 hp model); handpieces are numbered (#30 etc.); control marked SR-1 or SM-1 (Ingenium; Gesswein).
- **Table-saw jigs**: Delta tenoning jigs (cast-iron body that runs in the miter slot, with a vertical clamp and handwheel) are marked with part numbers 34-172 (older, heavier), 34-182, 34-184 (replacement) (Fine Woodworking forum).
- **Title words**: Forrest, Woodworker II, Dado King, Freud SD508, Systimatic, Foredom, flex shaft, tenoning jig, Delta 34-172, molding head, dado set.

## Makers, brands and variants
Forrest (NJ), Freud (Italy), Systimatic, Amana, CMT, Ridge Carbide = premium. DeWalt, Diablo (Freud's consumer line), Irwin Marples, Oldham, Vermont American, Master Mechanic, Craftsman = consumer.
- **Forrest Manufacturing (Clifton, NJ)**: Woodworker II general-purpose blade (40T common), Dado King, Chopmaster; Forrest offers factory resharpening. One forum estimate puts ~475,000 Woodworker II blades made since the mid-1990s (forum inference, not confirmed).
- **Freud**: red-coated industrial line (SD508/SD208 dado sets, LU-series blades); Diablo is the consumer line.
- **Foredom Electric (Bethel, CT, since 1922)**: jewellers' and carvers' flex-shaft machines; SR is the standard 1/6 hp model; handpieces interchange across most models (except the H series handpiece fitting) (Gesswein).
- **Delta / Rockwell**: older cast-iron accessories (tenoning jigs, molding heads with cutter sets) that woodworkers still use.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the plate of every blade: Forrest, Freud (red), Systimatic, Amana, CMT (orange), Ridge Carbide = keep looking; Master Mechanic, Vermont American, Craftsman steel = scrap.
- Carbide or steel? Brazed grey tips = carbide; one-piece teeth = steel.
- Count missing or chipped carbide tips in a close photo.
- Dado set: count chippers and shims, and check both outer blades are present and match.
- Flex shaft: motor with hanging loop + foot pedal = Foredom-type machine; a shaft with a hex or spline end that goes in a drill = cheap attachment.
- Foredom plate: read model/series; ask for the foot control and handpiece number.
- Delta tenoning jig: read the part number (34-172 older/heavier, 34-182, 34-184).
- Rust: surface rust on the plate is cleanable; pitting at the tips is not.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Delta 34-172 tenoning jig (discontinued, heavier cast version)** — forum reports of $400+ on eBay in medium condition (2008, dated, verify) vs $10–25 generic jigs — cast-iron miter-slot jig with handwheel, "34-172" casting or tag.
- **Forrest Dado King or Freud SD508 dado set, complete** — $80–200 (existing ladder) vs $2–10 per mixed blade — boxed set of chippers + two outers, brand on plate.
- **Forrest Woodworker II 10 in** — ~$50–70 used (existing estimate, verify) vs $10–25 used Diablo/DeWalt — plain grey plate with small etched "Forrest" and serial number.
- **Foredom SR flex-shaft machine with foot control and handpiece** — $50–200 (existing ladder); a used SR motor and shaft was listed at about $200–254 by dealer Gesswein, shown sold out (dealer listing, verify) vs $10–25 drill flex shafts — hanging motor, "FOREDOM" plate, strap, foot pedal.
- **Systimatic, Amana or Ridge Carbide blades** — $25–80 good tier (existing ladder) vs $2–10 — brand etched on plate, heavy plate, large carbide teeth.
- **Freud premium (red) vs Diablo** — premium tier vs mid — Freud industrial blades are red with "Freud" logo; Diablo is red/black with "Diablo" logo.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Carbide tips intact, flat plate, no missing teeth; resharpenable. Rust spots and missing tips = scrap.
- Thick plates, large carbide tips (room for many resharpenings) and laser-cut expansion slots signal premium blades.
- Old vs new: older premium blades have more resharpenings behind them and smaller remaining carbide; look at tip height in a side photo.
- Foredom: original braided strap, metal plate with series letter, and a matched SR-1/SM-1 control point to an older complete outfit.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dull blades need sharpening ($15–30 per blade) = repair; warped plates are junk.
- Forrest blades returned to Forrest for sharpening are a normal part of ownership; forum complaints exist about uneven factory resharpening.
- Foredom: worn inner shaft and sheath are consumables; motor brushes can be replaced. Missing foot control is a real cost.
- Tenoning jigs: guide rod needs cleaning and oiling; mounting on a left-tilt Delta saw puts the blade about an inch farther from the slot, which some owners fix with spacers (forum).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit Freud/Diablo blades sold online; not a concern at this level.
- Asian-made tenoning jigs copy the Delta design; lighter, but some owners prefer them (forum).

## Market
Liquid for premium brands; mixed blades sell as scrap or $10 lots.
- Users, not collectors, buy these; cast-iron Delta/Rockwell jigs have a following among vintage-machinery woodworkers.

## What sellers usually miss
A Forrest blade (plain grey, small laser etch "Forrest") among rusty blades; Systimatic blades in garage lots.
- Delta tenoning jigs and molding-head cutter sets lumped in as "table saw stuff".
- A Foredom motor listed as "Dremel-type thing".

## Shipping / handling
Blades are heavy, flat, sharp: cardboard sleeves; $10–20 per few blades.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Diameter, arbor, tooth count, kerf, carbide condition, sharpness.

### What to tell the buyer
State brand and model as etched on the plate, diameter, arbor size, tooth count and kerf, and whether every carbide tip is present (photograph the teeth close up). Say whether it has been resharpened and when, if known. For dado sets: list every chipper and shim. For Foredom: model/series from the plate, motor runs, foot control and handpiece included. For jigs: part number and that all parts move freely.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price. Mixed used blades: pass. Premium dado set ($120) at ≤ $30: 104 − 30 − 30 = $44 — just short; buy only ≤ $20.
