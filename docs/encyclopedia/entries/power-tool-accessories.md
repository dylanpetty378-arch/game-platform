## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Saw blades**: steel (one-piece, small teeth, often rusty, worth scrap) vs carbide-tipped (brazed grey tips). Brand on the plate: Master Mechanic (True Value), Craftsman, DeWalt, Vermont American = cheap; Forrest Woodworker II (no branding colour, laser-etched), Freud (red/orange coating), Systimatic, Amana, CMT (orange) = good.
- **Dado sets**: stacked chippers and two outer blades in a case.
- **Flexible shafts**: drill-attachment shafts with chuck at one end; Foredom motors hang from a hook with a foot pedal.

## Makers, brands and variants
Forrest (NJ), Freud (Italy), Systimatic, Amana, CMT, Ridge Carbide = premium. DeWalt, Diablo (Freud's consumer line), Irwin Marples, Oldham, Vermont American, Master Mechanic, Craftsman = consumer.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Carbide tips intact, flat plate, no missing teeth; resharpenable. Rust spots and missing tips = scrap.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dull blades need sharpening ($15–30 per blade) = repair; warped plates are junk.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit Freud/Diablo blades sold online; not a concern at this level.

## Market
Liquid for premium brands; mixed blades sell as scrap or $10 lots.

## What sellers usually miss
A Forrest blade (plain grey, small laser etch "Forrest") among rusty blades; Systimatic blades in garage lots.

## Shipping / handling
Blades are heavy, flat, sharp: cardboard sleeves; $10–20 per few blades.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Diameter, arbor, tooth count, kerf, carbide condition, sharpness.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price. Mixed used blades: pass. Premium dado set ($120) at ≤ $30: 104 − 30 − 30 = $44 — just short; buy only ≤ $20.
