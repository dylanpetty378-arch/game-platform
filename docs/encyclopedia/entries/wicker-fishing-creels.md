## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Split-willow/rattan creels with a hole in the lid (to drop trout in), leather harness. Value makers: George Lawrence
(Portland OR, leather-trimmed, stamped "Geo. Lawrence Co."), McMonies, Turtle-brand, Peters, Japanese Hong Kong export
creels (common, $20–50). European tackle baskets/seat-boxes (French ARCA/Eurosporta, English Brady bags) are
"cottagecore" decor, $40–120. Sellers write "fishing basket", "wicker creel", "trout basket". Lid hole position (centre vs
side) and leather binding are the quick tells.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **George Lawrence Co.** (Portland, OR): #1–#4 leather-bound creels (collectible); center-hole whole-willow with tooled
  leather (top); stamp on the leather.
- **McMonies** (Portland, OR): leather-trimmed, collectible.
- **Turtle** brand, **Peters** (top): marked American creels.
- **Japan/Hong Kong export** (1960s–80s): common, often with a paper label or none.
- **French ARCA / Eurosporta**, **English Brady**: seat baskets and tackle bags — decor tier.
- Decorative reproductions (general knowledge, verify): modern import creels sold as decor.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Split willow (finer, tighter weave = better), rattan, whole willow; leather binding on rims and corners, leather harness
and hinges; brass hardware. Patina and wear consistent with age; tight weave and hand-tooled leather mark the better
makers. Modern decor creels have loose weave, thin bright reed and glossy finish (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or missing weave strands, holes in the bottom, dried/cracked or missing leather, missing harness, broken lid
peg/latch, mould, rodent damage, varnish over old surface. Re-leathering lowers collector value. Ask for photos of the
bottom, inside, lid hinge and any stamps.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Decor reproductions are common (see junk tier); faked maker stamps on leather are possible on high-value Lawrence
creels — compare stamp style to known examples (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Fishing-tackle collectors (Lang's Auction, NFLCC shows), lodge/cabin decorators and cottagecore buyers. Marked American
creels sell well at specialist auctions; decor creels sell slowly on eBay/Etsy. Spring/summer fishing season and
Father's Day lift (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The Lawrence/McMonies stamp on the leather; leather-bound rims; centre vs side hole.

## Shipping / handling
2–5 lb, bulky, $20–25 each way. eBay/Etsy.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker (photo of stamp), dimensions (width × height × depth), weave type, leather condition, harness present,
hole position, any repairs. Photograph all sides, the bottom, inside, stamp and harness. Title: maker, "wicker fishing
creel", "leather bound", size/number, "trout basket", era.

## Buy rule
Buy when: leather-bound American creel with any maker stamp ≤ $80. Pass when: Hong Kong/Japan creels or European
seat-baskets over $30. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat.
