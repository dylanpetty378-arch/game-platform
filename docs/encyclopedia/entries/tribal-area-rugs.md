## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-knotted pile rugs and flatweaves from tribal and village weavers (and commercial workshops copying them).
- **Pakistani "Bokhara" / Jaldar (1960s–2000s)**: rows of repeated octagonal Tekke-style guls on salmon, rust, red, ivory, or taupe grounds, very even and clipped short, soft lustrous wool, cotton foundation, white cotton fringe; extremely common; $20–80 small.
- **Afghan Turkoman (Ersari, Tekke, Yomut)**: deep red guls, coarser older wool, sometimes wool warps; older ones collectible.
- **Persian tribal**: Baluch (dark blues/aubergine, small), Qashqai/Shiraz (geometric, animals), Hamadan villages; wool warps and wefts in many tribal pieces.
- **Turkish Anatolian village (Konya, Yahyali, Milas, Kars)**: bold geometric medallions, natural dyes in older pieces; much 1980s–2000s production with chemical washes ("overdyed", "faded", "Oushak-style").
- **Caucasian (Kazak, Shirvan)**: 19th–early 20th c., large geometric medallions; collectible.
- **Machine-made "oriental"**: printed or power-loomed; fringe sewn on, back shows perfect grid, design identical front and back is not the case (back is jute/plastic). Value $10–40.

## Makers, brands and variants
Village/tribal weavings are anonymous; attribution is by region, structure (knot type: symmetrical Turkish vs asymmetrical Persian; warp/weft material; selvedge finish) and design. Commercial labels: "Hand made in Pakistan", "Hand knotted Iran" sewn tags; Indian Jaipur copies of Persian designs.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Back: hand-knotted rugs show the design clearly on the back with slight irregular knots; machine-made show perfectly uniform grid or latex/jute backing.
- Fringe: an extension of the warps (hand-knotted) vs sewn-on fringe (machine/hooked).
- Abrash: colour banding from natural-dyed wool batches is a positive age/quality sign; harsh synthetic oranges/pinks and bleed indicate later dyes.
- Foundation: wool warps/wefts = tribal/older; cotton warps = workshop.
- Knot density (KPSI): finer = more labour, but tribal value depends more on age, colour and drawing than fineness.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Worn pile down to knots, moth damage (bare patches, larvae), dry rot (stiff cracking foundation), colour run, pet stains/odour, re-dyed/painted worn areas, rewoven patches, missing fringe and sides (overcast). Odour and moth are deal-breakers.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chemically aged and "antique washed" new rugs presented as old; painted-in wear; tea-washed Indian/Pakistani copies; machine-made rugs with sewn fringe sold as handmade.

## Market
Big but buyer-picky; small rugs sell steadily; rug dealers dominate quality pieces. Decor trend favours faded Oushak/Turkish looks.

## What sellers usually miss
- Older Baluch and Turkoman tribal rugs sold as "handmade rug" with a family-heirloom tag; tells = wool foundation, abrash, dark blues/aubergine, asymmetric drawing.
- Most sellers overprice Pakistani Bokharas as "Persian".

## Shipping / handling
Roll (pile in), wrap in plastic and a tube or box; 2x3 ~3 lb ($12–15), 4x6 ~10–15 lb ($20–30). A 4x6 rolled fits a passenger seat; 5x8+ usually not worth it.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** origin ("Afghan Turkoman", "Persian Baluch", "Turkish Anatolian") + "hand knotted" + "wool" + "tribal"/"village" + size in feet + colour + era. Junk: "oriental rug", "Persian" on a Pakistani Bokhara, "antique" unless pre-1940. Example: `Vintage Hand Knotted Afghan Turkoman Tribal Wool Rug 3x5 Red Natural Dye`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (small rugs sell steadily; buyer-picky); Etsy at the same price for decor buyers; pre-1940 collector pieces to a rug dealer or auction, not eBay.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (Turkish Anatolian ~$115, mid-century tribal 3x5–4x6 $80–400, Pakistani Bokhara mats $25–60); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 45 days.
- **Photos:** 1) full rug flat, straight-on, daylight; then the back (knot structure), pile close-up with a coin for height, both ends and both sides (selvedge, fringe), any wear/moth/repair, tape measure on length and width.
- **State:** exact size in inches, "probably [origin]" attribution, knot type if known, wool/cotton foundation, pile height and wear areas, ends and sides condition, moth, odour, repairs, colour run, all by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** roll pile-in, plastic sleeve, tube or box; 2x3 ~3 lb, $12–15; 4x6 10–15 lb, $20–30; a rolled 4x6 fits a passenger seat.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Hand-knotted [Turkoman/Baluch] village rug, wool on wool with the abrash and asymmetric drawing of a tribal piece in photo [2] — not a Pakistani Bokhara or a machine copy; comparable [size] rugs have sold for [$X]."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Pakistani Bokharas, Indian copies, machine-made: pass above ~$10.
- Older tribal rug ≤ 4x6 with wool foundation and natural dyes, no moth: buy at ≤ $60 if resale ≥ $250 (back photo required).
- Rugs larger than a rolled passenger-seat load (≥ 5x8): pass.
