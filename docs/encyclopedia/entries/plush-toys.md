## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Stuffed fabric animals and bears. The modern mass-market majority (polyester plush, foam/fibre fill, sewn-on safety eyes, printed
care label) is worth $5–25. Value sits in **maker tags and age**: a Steiff ear button, an early cloth/paper tag, mohair fur, glass or
shoe-button eyes, disc joints and excelsior stuffing.

Age tells readable from a seller's photo (Appraisily ID guide):
- **Fur:** mohair 1902–1950s (silky, with patina and bald spots at high points); alpaca 1910s–30s; silk plush 1920s; cotton plush 1930s–50s;
  synthetic plush from the late 1940s, common from the 1950s–60s, never develops patina.
- **Eyes:** boot/shoe buttons c. 1900–1920s; glass (amber or black, painted backs) 1920s–1950s; plastic safety eyes from the 1950s, standard by the 1960s.
- **Stuffing:** excelsior/wood wool 1900s–1930s (firm, crunchy, rustles); kapok 1920s–50s; cotton 1940s–60s; polyester/foam from the 1960s.
- **Shape:** long limbs, back hump and long pointed snout = 1900–1920s; shorter rounded muzzle 1920s–40s; chubby short limbs 1930s–50s; big-headed "cute" 1950s on.
- **Joints:** cardboard/metal disc joints with cotter pins c. 1900–1950s; string joints = cheap or wartime; unjointed common from the 1940s.
- **Paw pads:** felt 1900–1930s; velvet/ultrasuede 1930s–60s; synthetic from the 1960s.

Where the marks sit: Steiff — metal button (and usually a cloth tag) in the **left ear**, later also a chest tag; Merrythought — red, white
and blue **chest tag** and foot label; Chad Valley — colourful oval chest tag, sometimes with a royal warrant; Chiltern — diamond tag
("Hugmee" bears); Hermann — rectangular fabric ear tag. Jellycat and modern Gund/Ty — sewn fabric tags at the seam, plus swing tags.

Title words sellers use: "vintage teddy", "mohair bear", "jointed", "button in ear", "growler", "Steiff", "Gund", "Jellycat retired".
Look-alikes: modern "artist bears" and Steiff replicas (mohair but crisp, unworn, with white replica tags), and 1980s–90s Gund/Dakin
"vintage style" bears.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Steiff** (Giengen, Germany; first teddy 1902). The button was introduced in 1904 to stop imitations; the "Knopf im Ohr" mark was
  protected by May 1905 (Bear Essentials). Button chronology (P&M Antiques and Bear Essentials agree the **elephant button is first,
  1904–1905**, then **blank buttons**, then **"Steiff" lettering with the long trailing F**). Collector-standard dates beyond that
  (general knowledge, verify): long-trailing-F button c. 1905–1950; raised script "Steiff" c. 1950s–1970; incised script c. 1970–77;
  brass button from c. 1977/80s. Note: Appraisily's table puts the blank button first and the elephant at 1926–1952 — that conflicts
  with the other two sources and with collector consensus; treat it as an error.
  Modern ear-tag colours (P&M / Bear Essentials): **yellow tag, red lettering** = open/"Original" classic lines; **white tag, red
  lettering** = limited editions (with certificate); **white tag, black lettering** = replicas of archive models (carry the button of
  the matching year); ecru tag, dark grey lettering = Originals; white with blue lettering = open editions for collectors. Steiff began
  a new tag style in January 2024. Older tag colours (general knowledge, verify): white c. 1905–26, red c. 1926–34, yellow from c.
  1934; post-war "Made in US-Zone Germany" cloth tag c. 1947–53.
- **Merrythought** (UK, founded 1930): often orange-gold mohair; "Cheeky" bears (general knowledge: from 1957, bells in ears).
- **Hermann** (Germany, founded 1920): sloped forehead, prominent nose, growlers.
- **Chad Valley, Chiltern, Farnell** (UK), **Bing, Schuco** (Germany), **Ideal** (US, earliest American teddies, distinctive face stitching).
- **Gund** (US, 1898–): 1950s–60s mohair/plush animals and bears are the collectable band; modern Gund is retail.
- **Ty** (Beanie Babies and larger Ty plush): almost entirely $1–10; "rare tag error" claims are mostly myth (general knowledge).
- **Jellycat** (London, 1999–): retired designs and sizes trade above retail; current lines sell at or below retail.
- **Charlie Bears** (UK, 2005–): retired/limited bears with certificates hold value with collectors.
- **Melissa & Doug giant plush / FAO Schwarz giants:** decor/kids' market, local only, too big for the size rule.

Appraisily value bands (dealer/auction overview, verify): Steiff pre-1950 $500–10,000+; Hermann pre-1950 $200–3,000; Merrythought
pre-1970 $200–2,000; Ideal pre-1930 $150–1,500; Chad Valley pre-1960 $100–900; original tags add ~20–40%. Christie's world records
(2000–2002) for Steiff bears run $55,000–$193,000 — museum-grade, not sweep-grade.

### Quick ID checklist
- Zoom on the **left ear**: metal button? Elephant, blank, script "Steiff", or brass? Any cloth tag behind it, and what colour?
- **Fur:** mohair (silky, thin at the nose/paws, visible backing weave) vs synthetic plush (uniform, shiny, dense).
- **Eyes:** shoe-button or glass = pre-1950s; plastic safety eyes on a post or washer = 1950s+.
- **Joints:** do the arms/legs/head rotate on discs (look for the round bulge at the shoulder)?
- **Body shape:** hump, long arms, long snout, felt pads with claw stitching = early.
- **Stuffing:** seller can squeeze — crunchy/firm (excelsior) vs soft and squishy (fibre fill).
- **Tags:** chest tag (Merrythought, Chad Valley, Chiltern, Steiff bear-head), sewn label text ("Made in US-Zone Germany", "Jellycat London").
- Replica check: crisp mohair, no wear, white tag with black lettering = Steiff replica, not antique.
- Size: under ~20 in passes; anything near a passenger seat is out.

### Golden-egg tells
- Steiff with **elephant or blank button** (1904–c.1909) — museum/auction tier, many thousands — tiny elephant or plain domed button in the ear on a long-armed humped mohair bear with shoe-button eyes.
- Steiff **long-trailing-F button** bear/animal, pre-1950 — ~20–100× common plush ($500–10,000+ per Appraisily) — "Steiff" with the F tail sweeping under the word; mohair, glass or shoe-button eyes, excelsior.
- Steiff with **US-Zone tag** (c. 1947–53, general knowledge) — premium over the same 1950s animal without tag — small cloth tag reading "Made in US-Zone Germany", usually on the body seam.
- Steiff 1950s–60s small mohair animals with **button + chest tag + ear tag all present** — tags add ~20–40% (Appraisily) and roughly 2–3× an untagged example (general knowledge) — raised-script button, yellow ear tag, bear-head chest tag.
- **Pre-1950 Hermann / Merrythought / Chad Valley** bears with original chest or ear tag — $100–3,000 range (Appraisily) vs $5–25 modern — printed fabric tag in maker colours; mohair, glass eyes, disc joints.
- **Retired Jellycat** designs/sizes — $30–150 vs $10–25 current (old entry, general knowledge) — swing tag or sewn tag present; design not on the current Jellycat site.
- **Schuco "Yes/No" bears** (general knowledge, verify) — hundreds — moving the tail turns the head; small mohair bear, often with metal-framed body.
- Ty/Beanie "rare tag" claims — **not** a golden egg; $1–10 regardless of what the asking price says.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Mohair** (angora goat) plush is the quality fibre of 1900–1950s bears; worn mohair shows the woven cotton backing at the bald spots.
  Synthetic fur never thins that way; it mats and pills.
- **Excelsior** (wood wool) gives a firm, crunchy, heavy bear; kapok is lighter; polyester is squishy and springs back.
- **Disc joints** with cotter pins = quality jointed construction; modern "jointed" bears often use plastic locknut joints.
- **Stitching:** hand-embroidered noses and claws (vertical satin stitch on early Steiff noses) vs machine-stitched or moulded plastic noses.
- **Growler/squeaker:** bellows growlers in the tummy (Hermann, Steiff) — a working growler is a plus.
- Old vs new: real age shows as even wear on high points (nose, paws, ears), faded colour, and soft compressed stuffing. Fakers tea- or
  coffee-stain new bears; mixed-era features (long snout with plastic safety eyes) mean a married or fake bear (Appraisily).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common: stains, odour (smoke, mildew), matted or moth-eaten mohair, bald patches, re-covered paw pads, replaced eyes, missing ear button
(holes in the ear where it was), loose joints, split seams, flattened or shifted stuffing, insect damage in excelsior. Value killers:
smoke/mildew odour, moth damage, replaced eyes and pads on a pre-war bear (unless sympathetic and disclosed), missing button on a Steiff
(the two small holes remain). Acceptable: even wear, faded colour, worn pads on early bears. Ask: smoke-free home? any odour? have pads,
eyes or nose been replaced? photo of the ear front and back?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Fake Steiff buttons** are added to other makers' bears and to new bears; check the button type matches the bear's era (eyes, fur,
  shape) — a brass button on a bear with shoe-button eyes is wrong.
- **Steiff replicas** of archive models (white tag, black lettering) carry period-style buttons; they are worth replica money, not antique money.
- Distressed new bears: tea/coffee staining, deliberate fur wear, "mint" condition on a supposed antique, machine-perfect stitching,
  modern joints (Appraisily).
- Counterfeit Jellycat exists on marketplace sites (general knowledge): wrong tag fonts, poorer fabric.

## Market
Huge volume. Mass-market plush is near-unsellable singly. Vintage mohair bears and Steiff animals have a steady collector market
(eBay, specialist auctions such as Theriault's, Etsy); Jellycat sells fast to younger collectors when retired. Q4 gift peak.

## What sellers usually miss
Steiff buttons (often hidden in the ear fur, or only the two holes remain); small Steiff animals in toy lots; glass eyes and disc joints
that date a bear pre-1950; maker chest tags on Merrythought/Chad Valley; "Made in US-Zone Germany" tags.

## Shipping / handling
Giant plush dimensional weight $40+. Small bears and animals: $6–12 in a box; bag first (odour, moth). Don't vacuum-compress mohair or
excelsior bears.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Tags, size. State height (standing and seated), fur (mohair/synthetic), eyes, joints, stuffing, every tag/button with a close-up,
odour status, and any repairs.

### What to tell the buyer
"Mohair teddy bear, about __ in tall, fully jointed at the neck, arms and legs on disc joints, with glass eyes and firm excelsior
stuffing. The left ear carries a Steiff button with __ script (photographed front and back); the button style dates it to roughly
__. Wear is consistent with age: thinning at the nose and paws, pads __. No odour, from a smoke-free home. No replaced eyes or pads
that I can see." For Jellycat: "Retired design, no longer on the Jellycat site; original sewn tag and swing tag present."

## Buy rule
Giant plush too big. Buy Steiff/early mohair at ≤ ~$20–40 when the button or tag is visible and condition is honest; pass modern
plush, Ty, and any odour/moth damage. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
