## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A beaded necklace with flower-shaped "blossom" pendants along both sides and a crescent **naja** at the bottom.
Older Navajo pieces: hand-formed (bench-pressed or hand-hammered) silver beads, slightly irregular; blossoms cut and
soldered by hand; naja often **sand-cast** (grainy surface, thick section) — sometimes with a cast star or hands.
All-silver pieces (no turquoise) are an older/collector style. Modern/import tells: uniform machine beads,
stamped thin naja, "925 Mexico" or "Sterling" factory stamps, lobster clasps.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Navajo (Diné)**: the classic style; cast or hand-fabricated naja, hand-formed beads, often with turquoise; most old-pawn pieces are unsigned.
- **Zuni**: needlepoint/petit-point turquoise clusters and inlay squash blossoms — a distinct, also valuable style (general knowledge, verify).
- **Pueblo (Santo Domingo/Kewa)**: occasional squash-blossom forms (general knowledge, verify).
- **Signed/known smiths**: collectible to top tier; verify with experts.
- **Modern Native-made and 1970s "turquoise boom" pieces**: abundant; quality ranges widely (general knowledge, verify).
- **Imports** (Mexico, Philippines, China) and silver-plate: common tier.
- Variants that raise value: heavy weight (5 oz+), cast naja (with star or hands), all-silver early style, high-grade natural turquoise from named mines (e.g. Lone Mountain, Lander Blue — general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Coin silver (pre-1930s, often unmarked) and sterling; hand-formed beads with visible seam and slight irregularity; hand-cut blossoms; sand-cast naja with grainy back. Weight is a major value driver — get it in grams. Quality cheap tells: uniform machine beads, thin stamped naja, plated base metal (green showing through), lobster clasp. Turquoise: natural vs stabilized vs block/reconstituted/dyed howlite (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Replaced clasps (seen on the SF find — acceptable but disclose), restrung necklaces, missing or replaced blossoms, cracked or replaced stones, dents in hollow beads, re-soldered naja. Heavy polish removing patina lowers collector appeal (general knowledge, verify). Ask for the naja back, length, and weight first.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large market in imported "Native-style" jewelry sold as Navajo (illegal under the Indian Arts and Crafts Act if misrepresented, general knowledge, verify); fake turquoise (dyed howlite/magnesite, plastic); silver-plated base metal; forged "old pawn" tags. Tells: factory beads, stamped naja, modern findings, uniform stones.

## Market
Strong, long-running collector and wearer market for Southwest silver on eBay, Etsy, and specialist dealers/auctions; squash blossoms are fashionable again (general knowledge, verify). Buyers: Native-jewelry collectors, Western-wear and boho fashion buyers. Well-photographed vintage pieces sell readily.

## What sellers usually miss
"Tested silver, no marks" sellers often price by weight/melt. Cast naja + hand-made beads = Navajo work worth 3–5× melt.
