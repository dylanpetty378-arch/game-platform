## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- "Junk wax" era (1987–94 Topps/Donruss/Fleer/Upper Deck) = worthless in bulk ($10–30 per 5,000). Huge print runs. Shiny 1990s card stock, neon or marble borders, monster boxes.
- Value needs:
  - pre-1980 cards (1950s–70s Topps stars, 1952 Topps, 1975 minis)
  - graded cards (PSA/BGS slabs)
  - rookie autos or parallels of current stars (Prizm Silver, cards numbered /25)
  - complete vintage sets
- Pre-1980 tells: thin grey or cream card stock, simple cartoon backs, off-centre printing, rounded corners from play (general knowledge, verify).
- Words sellers use: "baseball cards", "card collection", "box of cards".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Topps** (1951+): the core vintage brand. 1952 Topps (Mantle #311) is the hobby's landmark set; the 1975 Topps minis are a short-print variant.
- **Bowman** (1948–55, revived 1989): early 1950s Bowman stars are valuable.
- **T206** (1909–11 tobacco cards): top tier and heavily faked.
- **O-Pee-Chee (OPC)**: Canadian Topps; the 1979 OPC Gretzky rookie is a key hockey card.
- **1986 Fleer**: Michael Jordan rookie (#57), the key modern-vintage basketball card.
- **Junk wax** (1987–94 Donruss, Fleer, Score, Upper Deck, Topps): bulk value only. Exceptions are a few high-grade rookies (1989 UD Griffey) (general knowledge, verify).
- **Panini Prizm/Optic, Topps Chrome** (modern): value is in rookie autographs, colour parallels and low serial numbers (/25, /10, 1/1).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Grading companies: PSA (red label), BGS (silver/gold label, with subgrades), SGC (black label). The slab's cert number can be checked on the grader's website (general knowledge, verify).
- Centring, corners, edges and surface decide the grade. Soft corners or creases send vintage cards to grade 1–3.
- Modern parallels show their serial number stamped on the front or back. "RC" logo = rookie card.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Creases, rubber-band dents, writing, tape, water damage and trimmed edges kill value.
- Trimmed or recoloured vintage cards are a known problem: compare the card size to a known card and look for overly sharp edges (general knowledge, verify).
- Cards stored in basements or attics often have mould or warping. Ask for photos of the backs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reprints of the 1952 Mantle, T206 Wagner/Cobb and 1986 Fleer Jordan are everywhere. They are often glossy, the wrong size, or labelled "reprint" on the back (general knowledge, verify).
- Fake PSA slabs and counterfeit modern autos exist; verify the cert number. Dylan's authentication rule applies: pass on high-dollar raw cards.

## Market
Very liquid on eBay; 130point aggregates sold prices. Modern cards are volatile and follow player performance and season (football in the fall, basketball in the winter/spring). Vintage stars are steady. Buyers are collectors and flippers who check comps instantly, so mispricing is rare except in estate lots (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Rarely anything: craigslist card sellers are usually collectors. The arbitrage is in estate lots where a 1960s–70s binder is listed as "old baseball cards" for $20–50.

## Shipping / handling
- 5,000 cards ≈ 25 lb. Singles go in bubble mailers ($4) in a penny sleeve and top-loader; eBay's standard envelope service is cheaper for under-$20 cards (general knowledge, verify). Sell on eBay.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Year, brand, card number, player, RC/auto/parallel/serial, and the grade with its cert number. For raw cards, show the centring and the corners.
- Photograph the front and back in good light, plus close-ups of the corners and the slab label.
- Title keywords: "1968 Topps Nolan Ryan RC #177", "PSA 7", "/25", "Prizm Silver".

## Buy rule
Buy when: visible pre-1980 stars in a lot ≤ $40, or a graded card ≤ 40% of 130point.
Pass when: junk-wax bulk; "1990 Nolan Ryan $25"; raw modern autos (they need authentication); high-value raw vintage (authentication rule).
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
