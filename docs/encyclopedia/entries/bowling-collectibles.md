## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Shoes**: lace-to-toe derby/blucher style, smooth leather, one slide sole (felt/microfibre leather) and one braking heel; rental shoes have size numbers on heel and two-/three-colour panels. Vintage (1950s–80s) have leather uppers and soles, Goodyear welt or stitched sole, maker stamp inside (Brunswick, AMF, Hyde, Dexter, Linds, Stylemaster, Etonic, "Champion"). Modern (1990s+) have synthetic uppers and rubber.
- **Shirts**: 1950s camp-collar, gabardine/rayon, chain-stitch embroidered names, union labels; makers King Louie, Hilton, Nat Nast, Swing-ster, Wolverine, Classic.
- **Bags**: 1950s–60s round "bowler" vinyl bags with Brunswick/AMF logos, two-tone; 1970s leather.
- **Pins/balls**: used maple pins with stamped makers (Brunswick, AMF, Vulcan); rubber/plastic balls (Brunswick Mineralite, Ebonite) heavy – not worth shipping.

## Makers, brands and variants
Brunswick (1845–, bowling from 1880s; "Black Beauty"), AMF (1946 automatic pinspotter, shoes, balls), Ebonite (1905 first rubber ball), Columbia 300, Hyde Athletic, Dexter (Mass. shoemaker; top bowling shoe brand 1970s–), Linds, Etonic, Stylemaster, Hammer. Shirt makers: King Louie (Pittsburgh), Hilton, Nat Nast (1930s–), Swing-ster, Ward's.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Vintage shoes: full-grain leather, leather insole stamped with maker and size, hand-stitched seams, leather sole with felt/microfibre slide patch. Cheap: vinyl uppers, glued soles, "Made in China". The example (pid 7964758011) is a two-tone tan leather/suede-panel lace-to-toe shoe of 1970s–90s style without visible logo – generic.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked leather, worn slide soles, odour, missing laces, broken heels; shirts with sweat stains, moth holes, faded rayon. Re-soling is a repair – excluded.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern retro bowling shirts (Charlie Sheen, Steady, Hawaiian-cut "retro" from 1990s–2000s) – screen print instead of chain stitch, polyester, modern care labels. "Vintage-style" bowling shoes from fashion brands (Gola, Puma) – contemporary.

## Market
Shoes: steady fashion/costume demand (retro sneakers look), but unbranded used shoes sell slowly at $20–40. Shirts: strong vintage-clothing demand for chain-stitch 1950s. Bowling balls/pins: low value per lb of shipping.

## What sellers usually miss
1950s chain-stitched shirts with business names on the back (sellers price them like thrift shirts). NOS shoes still in Brunswick/AMF boxes. Advertising signs and pin-setter memorabilia.

## Shipping / handling
Shoes: shoebox, 2–3 lb, $10–14. Shirts: poly mailer $5. Balls/pins: heavy, $20+ – avoid.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give insole measurement in inches, size marking, maker, sole condition, and whether the slide sole is left/right-handed. Note any odour honestly.
