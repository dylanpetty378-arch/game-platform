## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tuxedo accessories, sold in boxed sets.
- **Fabric sets**: pleated cummerbund (pleats face up), pre-tied bow tie and pocket square. Mostly polyester from rental houses (Selix, After Hours, Gingiss).
- **Jewelry sets ("dress sets")**: shirt studs, usually 3–4, plus cufflinks, sometimes with vest buttons, in a fitted velvet or satin box. The maker's mark is on the back of the stud or cufflink, on the post, or on the box lid lining.
- **Fronts**: mother-of-pearl, onyx, moonstone, enamel, plain gold or engine-turned.
- **Stud backs** show the age: one-piece collar-button style and spring "Bodkin clutch" backs are early (Krementz had a Bodkin-clutch stud c.1916). Bullet/toggle cufflink backs are mid-century, and fixed-swivel "whale backs" are mid-century onward. Patent numbers only date the mechanism, not the piece (VFG forum).
- **Title words**: "dress set", "tuxedo studs", "Krementz", "14K", "rolled gold", "Swank", "MOP".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Fabric**:
  - After Six (1960s–80s colorful and paisley sets $20–50), Lord West, Selix, Ike Behar, Pierre Cardin, Ralph Lauren.
  - Top end: Brioni, Charvet and Turnbull & Asser silk bow ties (self-tie, label on the band).
- **Jewelry**:
  - Swank, Anson and Hickok: gold-tone/gold-filled, $15–60. Marks are the brand name on the back, with "STERLING" or "1/20 12K GF" when applicable.
  - Krementz: rolled gold $30–80; solid 14k $150+. Details below.
  - Larter & Sons (Newark): fine gold dress sets.
  - Cartier, Tiffany, David Webb: $500–5,000.
- **Krementz & Co., Newark, NJ (founded 1866)**:
  - History: George Krementz began the firm in 1866 (first "Genung & Krementz", then with his cousin Julius Lebkuecher, and "Krementz & Co." from 1869). His May 1884 patent covered a one-piece collar button made on cartridge-shell machinery, and c.1900 the firm made most of the world's collar buttons.
  - Core product: rolled gold plate, advertised as several times thicker than competitors' plate, with a lifetime-replacement guarantee.
  - Brands c.1950: KREMENTZ, KREMALOY, SNAP-BAR and DIANA. Diana was a 10k/14k solid-gold line from c.1938–40.
  - So a Krementz set is usually rolled gold, but some are solid 14k: read the stamp.

### Quick ID checklist
- Photograph the backs: read "14K"/"18K" (solid), "STERLING"/"925", "1/20 12K GF" or "R.G.P." (gold-filled or rolled), or nothing (gold-tone).
- Maker: Krementz, Larter, Carrington, Cartier or Tiffany (high) vs Swank, Anson or Hickok (mass).
- Fronts: genuine mother-of-pearl (iridescent and cool) or onyx vs plastic.
- Completeness: the right number of studs, both cufflinks, vest buttons, the original fitted box.
- Box lid lining: often printed with the maker and the retailer's name.
- Fabric sets: check the fiber tag (silk vs polyester) and the label (rental house vs Brioni/Charvet).
- Weigh gold pieces if possible: solid 14k is noticeably heavier than rolled gold.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Solid 14k dress set (Krementz Diana, Larter, unmarked-maker 14k)**: about 8× a fabric set. Terapeak median for "14k gold tuxedo studs" is $182.50 vs $22.95 for cummerbund sets. Photo tell: a "14K" stamp on the stud post or cufflink back.
- **Cartier / Tiffany / David Webb sets**: $500–5,000, about 20–200× (existing entry; authentication needed). Photo tell: the signature on the piece plus a period box (red Cartier, blue Tiffany). Excluded from buying without authentication.
- **Art Deco gold and mother-of-pearl or seed-pearl sets (1920s–30s)**: a premium over mid-century (1stDibs type reference; no sold $). Photo tell: geometric engine-turning, MOP with a pearl centre, early clutch backs.
- **Krementz rolled gold vs solid**: the same look, but a 3–5× difference ($30–80 vs $150+). Photo tell: a "14K" mark vs no karat mark, or "Krementz" alone.
- **Sterling sets (Swank, Anson, Georg Jensen-type modernist)**: about 2× gold-tone (general knowledge, verify). Photo tell: a "STERLING" stamp.
- **1960s–70s After Six colorful or paisley cummerbund sets**: 2× rental polyester ($20–50 vs $8–25). Photo tell: the After Six label and a loud print.
- **Moonstone or onyx Krementz dress sets (10-piece)**: a multiple of a plain set (asking-price reference only; verify). Photo tell: domed blue-white moonstones, and count the pieces.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Fabric**: silk vs polyester (fiber tag). Self-tie silk bow ties beat pre-tied polyester.
- **Jewelry inlays**: mother-of-pearl or onyx inlays on studs.
- **Marks**: "14K", "STERLING", "1/20 12K GF".
- **Rolled gold plate** is a thick gold layer bonded to base metal. It wears through at edges as brass-coloured spots, while solid gold never shows a different metal underneath.
- **Gold-tone electroplate** flakes and pits.
- **Old vs new**: one-piece and clutch-back studs and fitted leather or velvet boxes are pre-1950. Toggle and whale backs and plastic boxes are later.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Stains, fading, broken stud backs and missing pieces.
- Jewelry: cracked MOP or onyx, replaced backs (solder blobs) and missing studs; a set short one stud loses a lot of value.
- Plating wear on rolled gold edges.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake hallmarks on luxury studs; high-end pieces need authentication (excluded). "14K GE" or "14K HGE" means gold electroplate, not solid gold. A "14K" stamp on a piece that shows brass at the wear points is suspect; ask for an acid or XRF test.

## Market
Fabric sets: low demand, with a prom and wedding season. Gold sets: steady, with a melt floor. Vintage-menswear and Art Deco buyers pay for period boxed sets.

## What sellers usually miss
Solid gold stud sets mislabeled "gold tone"; Krementz sets assumed to be rolled gold when they are marked 14K; the box-lid retailer name.

## Shipping / handling
Small envelope/box $5–8; insure gold sets.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Color name, fabric content, adjustable size; for jewelry, marks close-up.
### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker and exact mark as stamped ("Krementz", "14K"; or "1/20 12K GF"), the piece count (e.g. "4 studs, 2 cufflinks, 3 vest buttons"), the stud diameter in mm, the front material (mother-of-pearl, onyx, moonstone) and the back type (clutch, toggle, whale back). Say whether the original fitted box is included and what is printed in the lid. For gold, give the gram weight. For fabric sets, give the fiber content from the tag, the colour name, and pre-tied or self-tie.
