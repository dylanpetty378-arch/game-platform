## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Antique mechanical** (1860s–1930s): schoolhouse (octagonal top, short drop), regulators (long case, pendulum window; Seth Thomas, Ansonia, Waterbury, E. Ingraham, Gilbert, Sessions, New Haven), Vienna regulators (German/Austrian, glass case, often large). Key-wound; winding arbors through the dial.
- **Electric** (1930s–70s): Telechron, GE, Sessions, Westclox kitchen clocks; synchronous motors (cord on the back). These are electronics — test before buying.
- **MCM**: George Nelson for Howard Miller (1949–60s: Ball, Sunflower, Asterisk, Spike; Vitra reissues since 1999), Howard Miller starbursts; Welby and Elgin starbursts with 8-day spring or battery movements; Syroco wood-composition sunbursts.
- **Novelty**: Lux Clock Co. (Waterbury CT) pendulette clocks (1930s–50s; animated figures — dogs, birds, Woody Woodpecker), Keebler; Kit-Cat Klock (1930s–present; early ones with a wind-up or electric motor).
- **Décor reproductions** (1990s–present): quartz battery movements in "antique-look" frames and dials, e.g., Ren-Wil shadow-box pendulum clocks (Canadian home-décor company), oversized station clocks with distressed dials (e.g., "Casablanca 5300" dial — a decorative brand name printed on the dial, not a maker of antiques), Uttermost, Sterling & Noble, Infinity Instruments.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Seth Thomas** (CT, 1813–2009), **Ansonia** (1851–1929), **Waterbury** (1857–1944), **E. Ingraham** (1831–1967), **Gilbert**, **Sessions**, **New Haven**: schoolhouse $150–500 working; regulators $200–1,500 (often too big).
- **Howard Miller** (Zeeland MI): Nelson originals $400–2,500; modern décor $15–80.
- **Lux** pendulettes $80–400 working; **Kit-Cat** 1950s $50–150.
- **Telechron** kitchen clocks $20–70 working.
- **Ren-Wil** (décor, quartz): $20–60 resale.

- Quartz pendulum "regulator" with an AA battery box and a printed crackle dial is the common version regardless of frame — $15–60 (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Mechanical: brass plates stamped with maker and patent dates, key winding holes in the dial, paper/painted dial with real age (crazing), solid oak/walnut cases.
- Quartz décor: battery box on back (AA), printed "crackle" dial, resin or composite frame, no winding holes, pendulum driven by a magnet on a quartz unit.
- Nelson originals: Howard Miller paper label and a brass "Howard Miller" movement plate; Vitra reissues have Vitra labels.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Mechanical: not running (needs service $150–400), broken springs, missing pendulum/key, replaced dials, refinished cases, quartz conversions (lower value). Electric: frayed cords, dead motors. Décor: broken glass, cosmetic chips.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Décor reproductions of schoolhouse and station clocks with quartz movements; Asian-made "Nelson style" clocks; reissued Kit-Cats (modern battery); fake Vienna regulators assembled from parts.

## Market
Décor clocks: weak, mostly local pickup. MCM and Lux: strong collector demand, fast sales. Antique mechanical: soft, slow, buyers want running clocks; shipping large clocks is expensive and risky.

## What sellers usually miss
- Small Lux pendulettes and early Kit-Cats in kitchens.
- Howard Miller labels on MCM starbursts.
- Reverse: sellers price quartz décor clocks as antiques ("vintage pendulum clock").

## Shipping / handling
Large flat décor clocks 20–30" need oversized boxes ($30–60) and glass protection. Small pendulettes/kitchen clocks $12–18. Remove pendulums and secure movements.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, model, movement type (spring/electric/quartz), running status with video, dimensions, condition, included key/pendulum.

## Buy rule
Large décor clocks can't clear shipping. Buy only Lux pendulettes, Nelson/Howard Miller MCM originals, and small working American spring-wound clocks at ≤ 30% of sold and ≤ passenger-seat size. Electric clocks count as untested electronics unless seen running. Pass on all quartz décor.
