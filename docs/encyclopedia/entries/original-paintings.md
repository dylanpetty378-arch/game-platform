## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Commercial "studio" oils (1960s–90s):** painted quickly in workshops (Hong Kong, Taiwan, Mexico, Italy, later China) for furniture and department stores: palette-knife winter streams, birch forests, European street scenes, seascapes, florals. Generic signatures ("Antonio", "M. Hansen", "Wallace", "Taylor"), often in ornate gold or carved-look frames with linen liners. Thousands of near-identical compositions.
- **Amateur paintings:** hobby work, estate studio clear-outs (alcohol ink, acrylic pours, impasto), workshop exercises. Little resale demand.
- **Regional/listed artists:** trained artists with gallery histories; signature, label, exhibition stickers or gallery stamps on the back; quality brushwork and drawing.
- **Tells for printed vs painted:** raised brushstrokes that catch light at an angle, paint over the canvas edges, varied texture, pencil underdrawing. Prints on canvas ("giclée", textured transfers) have even surfaces and dot patterns.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Studio/decorator brands: "Original oil" with gallery labels from furniture chains; Mexican winter scenes signed "Antonio" or similar; Italian/Austrian street scenes; Hong Kong palette-knife landscapes. Typical $25–150.
- Listed-artist check: search the exact signature on askART, LiveAuctioneers, Invaluable; look for a record of sales, not just a biography.
- Notable sold examples this session: a European street-scene oil in ornate gold frame $142 (Sep 2026); palette-knife forest landscape $150 (May 2026); yellow roses still life in ornate velvet frame $250 (Sep 2026); Victorian roses oil in original frame $295 (Nov 2025).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Support:** stretched linen with wood keys (better/older), cotton canvas on staples (later), Masonite/board (amateur or 1940s–60s), canvas board (hobby).
- **Back of painting:** gallery and framer labels, exhibition stickers, artist inscriptions and titles add value and dateability. Staples on the back edge = post-1950s stretching.
- **Frame:** a carved/gilded period frame can be worth $50–300 alone; compo and resin ornate frames add $20–60.
- **Craquelure:** fine even cracking suggests age; fake "crackle" is uniform and printed.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Tears, punctures, flaking paint, heavy grime and yellowed varnish, water damage, sagging canvas, frame chips.
- Professional cleaning/relining costs $150–500+ and is outside Dylan's rules.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Prints on canvas sold as "original paintings" (check the surface and the signature – printed signatures are flat).
- Hand-embellished prints (a few brushstrokes over a print) – look at the edges and shadows.
- Forged signatures of famous names on decorator art; any "famous artist" claim needs professional authentication – pass.

## Market
- Huge supply, decorator-driven demand; most decorator oils sell for $20–75 after weeks. Shipping cost (oversized boxes) kills margin; local selling works better.
- Florals and autumn/winter landscapes sell seasonally (fall/holidays).

## What sellers usually miss
- Listed artists: an obscure signature can have a real auction record – always search it.
- Period frames: carved gilt or Arts & Crafts frames may be worth more than the painting.
- Pre-1940 American regional paintings (California plein-air, Hudson River–style, WPA-era) at estate clear-outs.
- Conversely: sellers price studio decorator oils from Lens matches to gallery asks ($150–500) when they sell for $20–75.

## Shipping / handling
- Corner protectors, cardboard sandwich, bubble, double box. 16x20 framed ships ~$20–30; 24x36 framed oversized $45–80.
- Toronto/Canadian listings (as in this sweep) cannot be bought and shipped economically.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "original oil painting", subject (street scene, roses still life, winter landscape), "signed" + name if legible, frame (ornate gold, velvet), support (canvas/masonite), size, "vintage MCM". Junk: "listed artist" unproven, "museum", "masterpiece". `Vintage Original Oil Painting Yellow Roses Still Life Signed Ornate Gold Frame 15x17`
- **Where:** large decorator oils on FB local (oversized shipping kills margin); listed artists and under 20×24 on eBay BIN with offers; list florals and autumn/winter scenes for fall and holidays.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (winter landscape $35, signed/framed landscape $70, street scenes in ornate frames $142, roses in ornate frames $93–250), OBO, accept −20%, floor $20 plus shipping; cut after 30 days.
- **Photos:** full piece straight-on; raking-light shot showing brushwork; signature close-up; back of canvas with labels/stamps; frame corners and ornament; cracks, flaking, dirt or varnish bloom.
- **State:** medium and support, image and frame size, signature, "decorative original oil, c. 1970s" when the artist is unlisted, auction record if found, frame type, condition (cracks, flaking, varnish) by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** corner protectors, cardboard sandwich, bubble, double box; 16×20 framed $20–30, 24×36 oversized $45–80; passenger-seat class ~7 lb, larger is local only.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is an original [oil on canvas], [signed], in a period [ornate gold] frame, [15×17]; comparable framed [florals] sell for $[90–250], and the frame alone is worth $50 up."

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; fits a passenger seat. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$25–45) − shipping to Dylan (~$25–45 or $0 local) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Unlisted decorator oils (median $35–70) never clear it shipped; local only and only at ≤ $10–20 for the best ornate-frame pieces.
- Buy only when the signature has documented sales with a median ≥ ~$200 (verify on LiveAuctioneers/askART) or the piece is clearly a quality pre-1940 painting, at ≤ 30% of that median.
- Never buy "famous artist" claims (needs authentication), damaged canvases, or estate lots sight-unseen.
