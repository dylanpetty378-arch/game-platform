## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A small figure group, usually 4–8" tall, placed on top of a wedding cake. Most show a bride and groom standing on a round base. Many have an arch, bell, or column frame over them, often decorated with wax orange blossoms, cloth flowers, tulle, or spun glass.

Photo tells by era (general knowledge, verify):
- **1900s–1920s:** German or Japanese **bisque** couples (matte, unglazed porcelain with fine painted faces). Groom often in a tailcoat. Bases are plaster or wire with wax flowers. Brides have long cloth veils.
- **1920s–30s Art Deco:** slim, elongated **composition or chalkware** figures with bob-cut brides, standing under a wire-and-tulle arch or a satin bell. These are the most collected.
- **1940s war era:** groom in Army, Navy or Marine uniform. Military grooms carry a premium.
- **1950s:** **chalkware/plaster** couple painted by hand (black tux, white gown with painted bouquet), glued onto a **white plastic lattice or scrolled base**. Mass-made, very common.
- **1960s–80s:** all-plastic (hard styrene) couples, often with plastic bells, doves, and a plastic heart arch. Wilton and Coast Novelty sold huge numbers.

## Makers, brands and variants
- German and Japanese bisque makers (unmarked, or "Germany" / "Japan" / "Made in Occupied Japan" 1945–52 incised or stamped under the base).
- American chalkware workshops of the 1930s–50s, mostly unmarked.
- **Wilton** (Chicago, cake decorating supply), plastic toppers from the 1950s onward; usually marked on the base.
- **Coast Novelty** and other cake-supply wholesalers (plastic, 1950s–70s).
- Specialty: ethnic or interracial couples, comic couples, military grooms, toppers with real lace/silk gowns.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Bisque:** matte, fine-grained porcelain, crisp painted eyes and lips. Good sign of age and quality.
- **Composition:** sawdust-and-glue mix, slightly rough, often crazed paint. 1920s–40s.
- **Chalkware/plaster:** heavy for its size, chalky white where chipped. Paint is often flaking. Mostly 1940s–50s.
- **Plastic:** light, shiny, and has mould seam lines. 1950s onward.
- Original **wax orange blossoms, cloth flowers, and tulle** add value. Replaced modern silk flowers reduce it.
- Faces are the quality tell: delicate painted lashes and lips mean an early, better piece; blobby, quick paint means mass production.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chalkware chips at the hems, hands and noses. Paint flakes and yellows.
- Plastic bases crack (the example listing shows a crack in the lattice base) and yellow with age.
- Tulle and veils turn brittle and brown. Wax flowers crack.
- Re-glued figures and repainted faces are common and lower value. Check the joint between the figure and the base.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little faking. Retro-style reproductions of Art Deco couples (resin) are sold as wedding decor today. Resin is heavier and glossier than composition and has no crazing. Bisque reproductions exist from the 1980s–2000s.

## Market
Steady, low-volume demand from wedding decor buyers (people re-using a grandparent's topper), vintage-wedding collectors, and crafters. Spring and early summer are a little stronger. Common 1950s toppers are plentiful and sit unsold.

## What sellers usually miss
- A **military groom** in uniform is worth 2–3× a tuxedo groom.
- **Wax flowers and bell arches** of the 1920s–30s are the valuable part, even if the figures are plain.
- "Occupied Japan" marks under bisque couples add collector interest.
- Sellers overprice 1950s chalkware because it looks old.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile. Chalkware and bisque break at the neck and hands; wax flowers crush. Double-box with the figure wrapped and immobilised. Roughly $10–14 to ship (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the material (bisque, composition, chalkware or plastic) and the approximate decade. Photograph the base underside for marks. List every chip, paint loss and base crack. Say if the flowers and veil are original. Title with "vintage 1950s wedding cake topper bride groom chalkware".

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 ship to buyer − ~$12 ship to Dylan − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). A $40 sale nets about $11 before the price, so common toppers never work. Only an early Art Deco or military piece selling for $120+ works, and only at ≤ $25–30. Small (fits easily in a passenger seat). No repairs, so skip re-glued or cracked pieces.
