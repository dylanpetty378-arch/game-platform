## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Buffalo Pottery was founded 1901 by the Larkin Soap Company (Buffalo, NY) to make premium china given with soap orders. Products: semi-vitreous dinnerware (incl. one of the best American Blue Willow lines), a series of colour-transfer "art" jugs/pitchers c.1904–1910 (Holland, Geranium, Robin Hood, Gloriana, John Paul Jones, George Washington, Roosevelt Bears, Pilgrim, Hounds & Stag, Whirl of the Town, Dr. Syntax...), and the famous Deldare Ware (1908–1909, revived 1923–25): olive-green/khaki body with hand-tinted English village and Dr. Syntax / Fallowfield Hunt scenes. Later (1915+) became Buffalo China, a restaurant/hotel ware maker (Oneida from 1983).
Photo tells: bulbous jug shapes with a printed polychrome scene band and a dark rim line; Deldare's distinctive olive-khaki ground with a painted-over transfer and an artist's signature at the edge; base mark showing a buffalo with "Buffalo Pottery" and a year (1905–1925 dated marks are common on art wares). "Holland" jugs show Dutch figures (girl with rake/basket, windmills, cottages) on a cream body.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Multicolor jugs (1904–1910): Holland (c.1906–07), George Washington, Pilgrim, Robin Hood, Cinderella, Gloriana, Chrysanthemum, Roosevelt Bears (rare), Whirl of the Town, Fallowfield Hunt, Dr. Syntax. Marks: buffalo, "Buffalo Pottery", often a date.
- Deldare Ware (1908–09, 1923–25): marked "Deldare Ware Underglaze" plus buffalo and date; artist initials on face (e.g., L. Newman, W. Foster, M. Gerhardt).
- Emerald Deldare (1911): Dr. Syntax scenes with art-nouveau emerald borders, scarce, $$$.
- Abino Ware (1911–1913): sailing/landscape scenes, rare.
- Blue Willow / Gaudy Willow (1905–1916): Gaudy Willow (blue plus rust/gold) is worth notably more than plain Blue Willow.
- Commemorative and advertising plates, Larkin/Christmas plates (1950–1962 Christmas plates are cheap).
- Buffalo China restaurant ware (1915–1990s): mostly $5–30, except railroad/hotel-logo pieces.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Semi-vitreous earthenware with a clear glaze. On good pieces the transfers are crisp, colours rich, glaze glossy with fine overall crazing. Deldare has a matte-ish olive body; real hand tinting overlaps the transfer lines. Later restaurant ware is heavy, thick vitrified china with a plain backstamp.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Main problems: hairline cracks (often running vertically down from the rim or around the belly), rim and spout chips, heavy crazing with brown staining, worn colours, and restored spouts/handles. A crack cuts value by 50–70%. Check under a UV light for overpaint on handles and spouts. Staining in the crazing is common and can partly be lightened with peroxide, but hairlines can't be fixed invisibly.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Deldare has some decorator/fake pieces (an old blank decorated later); the 1923–25 Deldare revival is real but valued slightly lower. Few outright reproductions of the jugs exist. Watch for "Buffalo" Christmas plates being sold as "antique".

## Market
Collector base is older and shrinking; perfect Deldare still sells steadily, but jugs and dinnerware are slow. eBay sells roughly 20–60 Buffalo Pottery art items per month (order of magnitude, estimate). Typical days to sell: 30–90.

## What sellers usually miss
- The olive-khaki Deldare ground often gets listed as "old English pottery".
- Gaudy Willow (with rust/gold over the blue) is worth 2–3× plain Blue Willow.
- An Emerald Deldare border (green art-nouveau band) is a big multiplier.
- The year in the base mark: anything dated 1905–1913 is the art period.

## Shipping / handling
Double-box it: wrap in bubble, put 2" of fill all round inside a box, then put that box in a second box. A jug weighs 2–4 lb packed and costs $14–22 to ship in the US.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the base mark and date, both sides of the scene, the inside, and the rim and spout under a light. Say whether there is crazing or staining, and name the pattern (Holland, Deldare, etc.) in the title. Disclose any hairlines plainly; buyers look for them.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$18 ship-out − ~$18 ship-in − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). In practice: Deldare and rare jugs only, perfect condition, priced ≤ ~35% of the sold median. Pass on any crack, chip or restoration, and on restaurant ware. Ask for a base-mark photo and a light-through photo for hairlines.
