## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
This is a reading guide, not an item type. Each brand's type entry covers its lines, values and comps; this entry collects, in one place, the small marks that date a toy or figure and tell an original from a reissue. The common traps are the same across brands:
- **A copyright date is the date of the design or mould, not the production year.** Hot Wheels "©1967", Kenner "© LFL 1977", Barbie "© MCMLVIII"/"©1966", Fisher-Price "©" dates and Breyer moulds all run for years after the date shown (Muncle Mike's; This Old Toy; general knowledge, verify).
- **Country of origin (COO) is a period tell.** Hong Kong / USA / England / Japan / West or US-Zone Germany = original period; China, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia = later, except where noted.
- **Reissues reuse the old marks plus a new date or maker.** Look for a second, later date or a "Hasbro", "China", "Reproduction" line.

**Where marks sit:** die-cast — on the base plate; action figures — on the back of a leg, the buttock or inside a foot; dolls — on the buttock or back of the neck; plush — the ear (button) and a chest tag; tin toys — lithographed on the side or bottom, or stamped on a tab; trains — on the frame, motor plate or bottom; LEGO — on top of the studs and the underside; vinyl figures — sticker on the box front; figurines — underside (impressed or printed backstamp).

**Words sellers use when they haven't read the mark:** "old Hot Wheels", "toy car lot", "Matchbox lot", "old Star Wars figures", "action figure lot", "vintage doll", "old Barbie" (no body marks), "teddy bear" (no ear photo), "tin toy Japan", "wind-up", "old train", "Fisher Price toy", "LEGO bulk", "Funko lot", "horse figurine", "Goebel figurine", "angel figurine", "Doulton lady".

### Golden-egg tells
- **Redline Hot Wheels in a "toy car lot"** — $8–150+ each vs $1 (8–150×). Photo tell: a thin red line on the tire wall; Hong Kong or U.S.A. on the base.
- **Spectraflame pink/purple/antifreeze 1968–72 redline** — 2–10× the common colour (general knowledge, verify). Photo tell: a candy colour that glows at the edges.
- **Super Treasure Hunt in a modern Hot Wheels lot** — 20–80× a mainline. Photo tell: rubber tires and a "TH" on the car.
- **Lesney "Made in England" regular-wheel Matchbox, grey plastic or metal wheels** — several times a later Matchbox (general knowledge, verify). Photo tell: base text and grey wheels.
- **Kenner COO leg stamp in a "toy figure" lot** — $5–250 vs $1–5 Hasbro. Photo tell: "© LFL 1977" + "Hong Kong" on the leg.
- **1964 "Patent Pending" G.I. Joe** — the earliest and most wanted vintage Joe (general knowledge, verify ~$50–200 loose). Photo tell: "Patent Pending" on the buttock, not a patent number.
- **#1 Barbie** — $3,000–25,000+ (general knowledge, verify). Photo tell: holes in the soles of the feet with copper visible inside.
- **Steiff button in an unbranded "old teddy" listing** — 5–100× a generic bear (steiff-teddy-bears). Photo tell: a small metal button in the left ear, or an empty button hole.
- **"US-Zone Germany" Schuco** — about 2–3× the guide price in near-mint (Mister Atom, 2024). Photo tell: "US Zone" lithographed on the toy or box.
- **TN/MT/Yonezawa/Bandai tin car or robot** — $100–10,000+ (tin-robot-toys). Photo tell: a diamond or letter logo on the litho.
- **Hummel crown or full bee mark** — 2–5× later marks. Photo tell: an incised crown with WG, or a bee inside a V.

### Quick ID checklist
- Turn it over first: base, leg, foot, ear, stud or bottom. Ask for a sharp, close photo of the mark.
- Read the COO (Hong Kong, USA, England, Japan, Germany/US Zone = period; China/Malaysia = later unless the type entry says otherwise).
- Read the date as a design copyright, not a production year.
- Look for a second maker or date (Hasbro, China, "Reproduction", 1990s+) that marks a reissue.
- Check period features that the mark alone doesn't prove: redline tire stripe, spectraflame, grey wheels, foot holes, button, logo era.
- For chase/variant claims (STH, chase sticker, vinyl-cape Jawa, rare colours), check the tells in the tables and pay authentication-free prices only.
- Then go to the type entry for values and comps.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Die-cast: original zinc bodies with clean rivets; restored cars show drilled or re-set rivets and paint in the base. Kenner figures: hard ABS with slightly chalky sheen and mould seams; Hasbro reissues are crisper. G.I. Joe: hard plastic heads with painted hair (1964–69), flocked hair from 1970 (general knowledge, verify). Barbie: early ponytails have a hard vinyl body that may turn green at the ears from earrings. Steiff: mohair, excelsior stuffing, felt paws, glass or boot-button eyes on early bears. Tin: lithographed steel with crimped tabs; repros have bright modern litho and Phillips screws. LEGO: cellulose acetate bricks warp and discolour; ABS (1963+) is stable. Figurines: early Hummel marks are incised; later printed decal marks sit on top of the glaze.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Die-cast: paint chips, toned spectraflame, bent axles; C9–C10 cars sell multiples of C6–C7 (hot-wheels-redlines). Figures: loose joints, yellowing, missing weapons/accessories, reproduction parts. Dolls: green ear, cut hair, chewed feet (common on Barbie and Joe — and chewed feet hide the #1 holes). Plush: moth damage, bald mohair, missing button. Tin: rust, non-working motors, missing keys; battery corrosion. Trains: rust, cracked shells, burnt motors. Figurines: chips, restored fingers and flowers (look under UV), crazing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Hot Wheels:** reissues with redline-style wheels (1993 anniversary, later "vintage" lines); customs; repainted spectraflame; fake STH customs.
- **Matchbox:** Lesney-style reissues marked China; wheel swaps.
- **Star Wars:** Hasbro reissues; repro weapons/capes; fake COOs; bootlegs (vintage-star-wars-figures).
- **G.I. Joe:** 1990s Masterpiece/Timeless reissues and repro uniforms.
- **Barbie:** 1994 35th Anniversary repro, repro stands and shoes with holes.
- **Steiff:** transplanted buttons; reproduction buttons.
- **Tin:** Schylling and Chinese copies of Marx and Japanese tin; Schuco and Märklin factory replicas.
- **LEGO:** clones; counterfeit minifigures (lego-lots).
- **Funko:** counterfeit figures and stickers.
- **Hummel/Doulton:** see hummel-figurines, royal-doulton-figurines and ceramic-and-glass-marks-that-multiply-value.

## Market
Each brand has a large, active collector base that searches by the mark ("redline", "Lesney", "COO", "Patent Pending", "#1 Barbie", "button in ear", "US Zone", "TN Japan", "full bee"). Correctly titled listings sell within days; mis-titled lot listings are where the finds are. Q4 is stronger for toys. Prices in 2026 are steady for vintage die-cast and Star Wars, soft for Precious Moments, Hummel commons and modern Funko commons (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- The base, leg, ear and bottom never get photographed — the mark is the date and the brand.
- Sellers read copyright dates as production years (both ways: under-valuing a real 1968 car read as "1967 toy", over-valuing a 1990s Barbie read as 1966).
- Redline stripes, spectraflame, grey Matchbox wheels, Barbie foot holes, Steiff buttons, "US Zone" and Japanese maker logos all go unnamed in titles.
- Hummel sellers say "Goebel" and stop; the crown or full bee is the money.
- The reverse error: "Super rare chase", "vintage 1966 Barbie", "rare Precious Moments" and "1977 Star Wars" on a Hasbro figure are over-reads.

## Shipping / handling
Die-cast and figures: small padded box, $5–8. Dolls: box with tissue, $8–12. Plush: box (don't crush), $10–15. Tin: double-box, fragile tabs and wind-up keys, $10–15. Trains: wrapped in paper (not bubble wrap on painted surfaces long-term), double-box, $12–25. Figurines: double-box with 2 in. of padding, $10–15. All fit a passenger seat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the mark exactly as read (base text, leg stamp + COO, buttock marking, button type, logo, TMK number, HN number), with a close-up photo of it; then condition, completeness (weapons, accessories, box, key) and what is reproduction. Title keywords: brand + the mark words ("Redline Spectraflame Hong Kong", "Lesney Made in England regular wheels", "Kenner 1977 LFL Hong Kong COO", "G.I. Joe 1964 Patent Pending", "Steiff raised script button", "Schuco US Zone", "Nomura TN Japan tin", "Hummel TMK-2 full bee", "Royal Doulton HN1234").
### What to tell the buyer
"Base/leg/ear marked [exact text], pictured. This mark dates it to [period] per [source]. Original [parts]; [parts] are reproduction / missing. Condition: [wear]."

## Buy rule
Buy when: the mark is visible in a sharp photo and puts the piece in a period tier, and the price is ≤ ~40% of that tier's sold comp (profit ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways); lots at the per-piece rules in the type entries. Pass when: no mark photo and the seller won't send one; China/Malaysia-base or Hasbro-stamped pieces priced as vintage; reissues; variants whose value depends on authentication (vinyl-cape Jawa, prototype colours, Barbie #1 without visible tubes); Precious Moments and Breyer Reeves at more than a few dollars each; untested battery toys priced as working. Must fit a passenger seat (all do).
