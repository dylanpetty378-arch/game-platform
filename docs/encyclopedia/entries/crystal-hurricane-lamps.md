## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Electric table lamps with a glass chimney shade over a socket on a glass base. Lead crystal: very heavy, brilliant, deep hand cutting (wedge cuts) that is crisp to the eye but polished smooth to the touch, acid-etched signature. Pressed glass: mold seams, rounded "cuts", lighter, less sparkle, diamond-quilt or "cane" pattern formed in the mold.
- Two forms: the **two-piece hurricane** (crystal base holding a socket, with a separate crystal chimney on top, often 9–12 in tall) and the **crystal table lamp** with a fabric shade; both are called "hurricane" by sellers.
- Where the mark is: underside of the base (Waterford: etched word or seahorse; Baccarat: engraved/etched name or logo). Chimneys are often unmarked; marks may be faint and need raking light (Hunker).
- Lead crystal held to light looks clear, silvery-white; ordinary glass shows a yellow or green tint at thick edges (Hunker).
- Seller titles: "Waterford hurricane", "crystal boudoir lamp", "Lismore lamp", "pair crystal lamps", "Gone with the Wind" (usually painted-globe parlour lamps, a different type).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Waterford (Ireland)**: etched "Waterford" or seahorse; lamps $120–400. Marks by period (Hunker): acid-etched "Waterford" word mark on the base on most pieces; a logo stamp in use since about 1950; a script-font stamp from 1987 for small surfaces; the seahorse added around 2000 for the millennium (seahorse tail curls outward, shamrock in the centre). Older pieces also carried a green-and-gold seahorse foil label. Production later moved to Slovenia, the Czech Republic and Germany; "Made in Ireland" on a box or label indicates Irish production (Hunker). Named lamp patterns seen: Lismore, Alana/Inishmaan, Inishturk, Penrose, Prescott, Ha Chara Failte (comps below; 1stdibs).
- **Baccarat**: $300–900. Marked by engraving from 1930 on; older pieces had paper labels that are often lost (1stdibs).
- **Godinger, Lenox, Gorham, Marquis**: $50–150. Marquis is Waterford's lower-priced line (general knowledge, verify).
- **American pressed (Fostoria, Imperial, L.E. Smith, Westmoreland, Indiana)**: $20–100.
- **Generic boudoir (1950s–80s)**: $20–60/pair.

### Quick ID checklist
- Etched word "Waterford", script "Waterford", or a seahorse on the base underside? Photo needed; foil labels alone are weak evidence.
- Pattern named (Lismore, Alana, Inishturk, Penrose, Prescott)? Match it to Replacements.
- Mold seam running up the base or chimney? Pressed glass, not crystal.
- Thick edges clear/silvery (crystal) or greenish (soda glass)?
- Turned wooden or pierced brass stem pieces and key switches? Typical of inexpensive pressed boudoir lamps.
- Chimney present and uncracked; rim chips checked?
- Box with "Made in Ireland" or the country label?
- Baccarat: engraved "Baccarat" or the round logo under the base.

### Golden-egg tells
- Baccarat electric hurricane or candle lamp — ~2–3x a Waterford hurricane (Terapeak median $250 vs $95) — engraved Baccarat name or logo under the base; very clear, heavy glass.
- Waterford Lismore or other named-pattern electric hurricane in good order — ~3–5x a pressed pair (Terapeak median $95, p75 $170 vs $20–60/pair) — etched Waterford mark plus the recognisable Lismore diamond-and-wedge cutting.
- Larger or older Waterford lamps (e.g. Inishturk, Alana/Inishmaan pairs) — top of the Waterford band ($250 single sale; pairs higher) — tall cut base with fabric shade or tall chimney; Made in Ireland label.
- Matched pair of signed crystal lamps — roughly double one lamp, sometimes more (general knowledge, verify) — two identical lamps with marks in the photos.
- American better glass (Fostoria, Imperial, Westmoreland) — ~1.5–2x a generic boudoir pair ($40–100 vs $20–60) — known patterns such as Fostoria American (cube pattern) on the base; maker labels.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Check mold seams on shade and base; turned wooden/brass stem pieces are typical of inexpensive pressed lamps.
- Lead crystal is heavy for its size, rings when tapped and feels cool-then-warm in the hand; facets throw rainbow sparkle (Hunker).
- Hand-cut crystal: cuts meet cleanly at points with polished faces; pressed glass shows rounded ridges and a seam.
- Old vs new: pre-2000 Waterford has the word mark only; the seahorse etched mark points to about 2000 or later (Hunker). Post-production-move pieces are still genuine Waterford but buyers ask about country.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on rims, cracked chimneys, old cord, missing finials.
- Ground-down rims (to remove chips) show as a flat frosted band at the chimney rim; ask for a straight-down rim photo.
- Replacement chimneys from a different pattern are common; check the cut pattern matches the base.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Unmarked "Waterford style" imports; seller "Waterford" claims without etched mark.
- Copies are made worldwide (Hunker); verify the pattern against Replacements before paying.

## Market
Slow; glass lamps ship poorly; local sale often best.

## What sellers usually miss
Etched Waterford signature on the base; few miss on pressed glass. Sellers also rarely give the pattern name, which is what Waterford buyers search.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile, double-box; $25–40 each.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photo marks, weight, rewire status, light test.
- Title with maker + pattern + height: "Waterford Lismore 11 in Electric Hurricane Lamp".

### What to tell the buyer
State the maker only if the base is marked, describe the mark (etched word, script or seahorse) and show it. Give the pattern name, height, weight, number of pieces (base, chimney, shade), whether it lights (with a lit photo), cord condition or rewire, and any chips or grinding at rims. Say whether a box or country label is present. Ship double-boxed with the chimney packed separately.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$35 out − ~$30 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Pressed: pass. Waterford pair $400 resale: buy ≤ $90.
