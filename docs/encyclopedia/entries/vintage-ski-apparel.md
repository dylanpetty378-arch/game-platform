## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ski jackets, bibs, one-piece suits, sweaters and fleece from the 1960s–2000s. The money is in loud, era-correct technical pieces: neon/colour-block one-piece suits with padded shoulders and stirrup cuffs (1980s), Bogner "B" zip pulls, Ellesse half-ball logo, Fila "F-box" with "Made in Italy" label, Descente three-arrow logo, TNF Steep Tech/Extreme colour-blocking. Fleece vests (like the Marker example) and resort-embroidered pieces are souvenir-grade.

Where the information sits:
- **Brand/size/country tag** at the inside neck; a second **care/fabric tag** in a side seam or inside pocket names the membrane (Gore-Tex, Entrant, Hydrofil) and the country. Photograph both.
- **Zip pulls** are branded on good pieces (Bogner "B", Descente, TNF), and the zip maker (YKK, Riri, Lampo, Opti) is stamped on the slider.
- **Logos** on chest, sleeve or leg: Ellesse half-ball, Fila "F", Descente arrows, TNF half-dome.
- **Era tells from the cut**: 1970s — quilted down/poly-fill jackets with snap storm flaps, narrower fit, stretch "warm-up" pants. 1980s — one-piece suits with padded shoulders, elastic waists, stirrup or gaiter cuffs, neon/pastel colour-blocking, diagonal zips. 1990s — oversized shell + bib sets, Gore-Tex shells with pit zips, geometric prints, teal/purple/black, TNF/Columbia "Bugaboo" interchange systems.

Look-alikes: modern "retro" fast-fashion one-pieces and costume "gaper suits" (thin, printed rather than pieced colour-blocks, Asia-made, recent RN/CA numbers on tags); brand reissues (see Fakes).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bogner (Germany)**: top tier; "B" logo zipper pulls, fur-trimmed hoods, often Made in West Germany. Jackets $80–300 (general knowledge, verify). A **"Made in West Germany"** tag dates a piece to before German reunification (Oct 1990) — a free dating tell. Bogner labels changed script over time and are hard to date precisely; Vintage Fashion Guild moderators read a script-style Bogner label as 1980s–early 90s, and note that co-branded retailer labels (e.g. a Swiss ski-shop label) were used for decades and are not reliable dating tools (VFG forum). Bogner also made the "Fire + Ice" younger line from the 1990s (general knowledge, verify) — lower than mainline Bogner.
- **Ellesse / Fila (Italy)**: 1980s Italian labels, Fila "Made in Italy" ski tops and suits $60–200. Fila's 1980s white-line ski range was worn by Italian racers (general knowledge, verify). Italian-made tags are the value tell; Asia-made is later/reissue.
- **Descente / Spyder / Kneissl / Head / Skea / Roffe / Obermeyer / Sport Obermeyer / Columbia "Powder Keg"**: mid tier $30–120. Descente (Japan) is known for racing suits and 1980s–90s one-pieces with the three-arrow logo; one-piece suits are the commonly traded Descente style on eBay (eBay.de listings). Spyder (Boulder, CO) early pieces with the spider logo and race-suit styling sell above later mall-era Spyder (general knowledge, verify).
- **The North Face**: Extreme, Steep Tech, Gore-Tex mountain parkas from the 80s–90s: $80–250 (general knowledge, verify). Precise dating: **Steep Tech launched in stores in 1991**, developed from skier Scot Schmidt's earlier "Extreme Gear" suit; features are Cordura reinforcement in friction zones, foam padding at knees/shoulders/elbows, many pockets (up to 10), daisy-chain webbing, removable shoulder pads and flip-out padded seats. Line variants: High Line jacket (1994), Apogee jacket/one-piece (1996, yellow/black; radio chest pack, built-in harness). Schmidt's last design year was 1998; the line was relaunched in 2000 (urban focus, strong to 2007), revived in a 2016 Supreme collab and relaunched again winter 2020–21 (Complex). So a "Steep Tech" is only "vintage 90s" if the tag and details fit 1991–1999.
- **Demetre / Meister / Dale of Norway sweaters**: hand-knit ski sweaters $40–150; Dale "Made in Norway" Olympic designs higher. Dale of Norway: textile mill in Dale, Vaksdal (near Bergen), dating to 1872/1879; pure-wool knitwear; has made official sweaters for the Norwegian national alpine team for Olympics and World Championships since 1956 with a new design each event, and later held IOC rights to official Winter Olympic sweaters (Wikipedia). Event-specific designs (e.g. a named Olympics year) are what collectors search.
- **Marker, Gerry, Alpine Designs, Swing West, Sun Valley**: Marker is a binding company that sold logo outerwear ("Quality outerwear for quality times"); fleece vests sell $5–23 (comps).

### Quick ID checklist
- Neck tag: brand + country. Italy, West Germany, Japan, Austria, Norway = better; "West Germany" = pre-1990.
- Fabric tag names Gore-Tex/Entrant/Hydrofil and the seams are taped inside = technical piece; plain "100% nylon / polyester fill" = budget.
- Branded zip pulls and YKK/Riri sliders; generic unbranded zips = low tier or replaced.
- One-piece suit: pieced (sewn) colour-blocks, padded shoulders, stirrup/gaiter cuffs = genuine 80s; printed blocks and thin fabric = modern costume.
- TNF Steep Tech: Cordura panels, padding, daisy chains, removable shoulder pads; check tag style against 1991–99 vs 2000+ relaunch.
- Sweater: Dale "Made in Norway" label, pure wool, pewter clasps; event/year design named in the knit.
- Lining photo: look for flaking PU coating or peeling membrane (white flakes, sticky feel).
- Fleece vest with resort embroidery = souvenir tier regardless of brand.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Gore-Tex/Entrant labels, taped seams, YKK or Riri zips, "Made in Italy/West Germany/Japan" tags = better. Thin nylon with poly-fill and generic tags = low. Original stretch stirrup pants and matching suits sell better as sets.

More detail:
- **Membranes and coatings**: Gore-Tex (laminated membrane, labelled) holds up best; 1980s PU-coated nylons (often labelled "waterproof coated" or just "coated") are the ones that flake and go sticky with age (general knowledge, verify).
- **Construction**: bar-tacked stress points, storm flaps over zips, powder skirts, pit zips, and articulated elbows mark technical pieces; cheap pieces have overlocked raw seams and no internal taping.
- **Old vs new**: older tags are woven, often with a separate country tag; modern reissues carry printed satin care tags with multilingual text and recent RN/CA numbers. Modern "vintage-look" one-pieces are lighter and printed.
- **Wool knits**: hand-knit (Demetre, Meister, Dale hand-knit lines) have slightly irregular stitches and heavier weight; machine knits are flatter and uniform.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Delaminated Gore-Tex (peeling inner membrane), sticky/flaking PU coating, broken zips, cuff wear, pilling on fleece, smells. Coated jackets from the 80s often shed coating — a big value killer and hard to see in photos.

Also: faded neon (sun fade on shoulders/hood is common and visible as a colour shift), cracked/peeling heat-applied logos, ski-edge cuts on the inner pant leg, missing removable hoods or powder skirts, moth holes in wool sweaters (check underarms and cuffs), and stained fur trim on Bogner hoods. Replaced zips are acceptable if the brand pull is kept; mention them. Ask for: lining photos, a photo of the inside of the cuffs, zip function, and smoke/pet odour.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fila and Ellesse reissue retro styles (made in Asia, newer tags). TNF reissued Steep Tech in 2010s–2020s. Check tag style and country.

More: TNF Steep Tech returned in 2000 (urban relaunch), again via Supreme in 2016, and again in winter 2020–21 (Complex) — modern pieces carry modern printed tags and are not "90s vintage". Counterfeit TNF (embroidered logo spacing, cheap zips without branded pulls) is common in the resale market (general knowledge, verify). Dale of Norway continues to make Olympic-design sweaters; recent ones are not vintage.

## Market
Souvenir fleece: many sales, slow, low. Branded 80s technical gear: steady demand, strong Oct–Feb. Days to sell 30–60. Buyers: vintage resellers, "retro ski day" and après costume buyers (one-pieces), streetwear buyers (TNF Steep Tech, which has had fashion revivals), and skiers who actually wear Gore-Tex shells. Depop/Grailed suit streetwear-leaning pieces; eBay for everything else.

## What sellers usually miss
That an ugly neon one-piece suit or a Bogner jacket is worth 5–10× a nice-looking fleece; country-of-origin tags (Italy, West Germany, Japan) mark the good ones. Also: the Steep Tech/Apogee model names (not on the outside — on the tag), Dale event designs, and that a matching jacket + pants set sells better than separates.

## Shipping / handling
Soft, 1–3 lb, poly mailer $6–12.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Tag photos (brand, size, country, fabric), pit-to-pit and length measurements, note any coating flaking or delamination, odour/smoke-free statement.

### What to tell the buyer
State the brand, the country on the tag (and "West Germany = made before 1990" where it applies), the decade (say "1980s" or "1990s" only with the cut/tag evidence), the membrane (Gore-Tex etc.) as written on the tag, and model name if tagged (e.g. Steep Tech, Apogee). Give pit-to-pit, length, sleeve and (for suits) inseam, because vintage sizing runs differently from modern. State lining condition plainly ("no flaking/peeling inside" or "coating flaking at cuffs, photographed"), zip function, and fade. For Dale sweaters, say "Made in Norway, 100% wool" only if the label says so, and name the event design if identified.
