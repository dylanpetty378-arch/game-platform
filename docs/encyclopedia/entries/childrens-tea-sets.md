## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Miniature tea services for children/dolls. European makers (Haviland, Wedgwood and others) made child-size services from the 1700s; Japanese porcelain and lusterware imports arrived in the 1900s; Ohio Art entered toys in 1917 with stamped, printed tin; Akro Agate made glass sets from the 1930s until it closed in 1951 (Post-Bulletin; BDN/Kovels).
- **Japan lusterware**: iridescent tan/peach/blue glaze, hand-painted flowers, "Made in Japan" or "Hand Painted Japan" (1921–41) or "Made in Occupied Japan" (1945–52). Many pieces in a set are unmarked; often only the sugar or teapot is marked.
- **Akro Agate**: marbled or opaque pressed glass in bright colours; boxed sets "Little American Maid" and "Play-Time Glass Dish Set". Pressed patterns with broad panels or bands; mark is a crow flying through a capital A (not always present).
- **German**: thin porcelain with transfer prints, gold trim, often unmarked.
- **Tin**: lithographed metal (Ohio Art, Wolverine, Chein).

## Makers, brands and variants
Japan (Noritake child sets, generic), Occupied Japan, Akro Agate, Moderntone "Little Hostess" (Hazel-Atlas), Germany/Bavaria, Disney-licensed 1930s.

**Akro Agate (Clarksburg, WV; founded 1910 in Akron OH, moved to Clarksburg within three years; closed 1951).** Turned from marbles to toy dishes in the 1930s; boomed during WWII when Japanese imports stopped (Post-Bulletin; BDN). First toy-dish line was **Stippled Band**, made from reused toothpick-holder and flowerpot moulds. Other patterns: **Interior Panel**, **Octagonal** (open or closed handles), **Concentric Ring / Concentric Rib**, **Raised Daisy**, **Stacked Disc**, **Chiquita**, plus J.P. (J. Pressman) marked pieces. Boxed brands: **"The Little American Maid Tea Set"** (CMOG example No. 335, 1940s, 17 pieces in blue, green and white, "H"-pattern panels, closed handles, raised crow-in-A on each piece, box printed "MANUFACTURED BY The Akro Agate Co. Clarksburg, W.VA.") and **"Play-Time Glass Dish Set"**. Common colours: yellow, white, orange, cobalt, oxblood, purple, light blue, topaz, green. Rare colours: **pumpkin** and **oxblood red**; marbled "lemonade and oxblood" sets were also made until the end (BDN; Marble Connection). A full single-place set is 21 pieces in some patterns (cup, saucer, plate, bowl, teapot+lid, sugar+lid, creamer) per collectors.

**Hazel-Atlas Moderntone "Little Hostess Party Set"** (1950s): pastel opaque glass (pink, turquoise, yellow, grey, burgundy). Hake's also records a 1930s Japanese Mickey set boxed as "The Little Hostess Set", so the name alone is not a Hazel-Atlas tell.

**Disney 1930s lusterware (Japan)**: tan lustre with Mickey/Minnie decals; some pieces marked "Made in Japan" (sometimes only the sugar), some carry a Walt E. Disney copyright line. Figural Mickey-head teapot lids exist (Hake's).

**German porcelain**: small transfer-printed services (nursery rhymes, children playing, Halloween and holiday themes), gold trim, sometimes in a fitted presentation case. Often unmarked or with a small Germany / Bavaria mark.

**Tin**: Ohio Art (from 1917), Wolverine, Chein, J. Chein; boxed Ohio Art sets from ~$80, loose tin sets $6–20 (Post-Bulletin).

### Quick ID checklist
- Glass, marbled or opaque bright colours, panelled/banded → Akro Agate (look for crow-in-A) or Hazel-Atlas (pastels, no mark).
- Box printed "Little American Maid" / "Play-Time" / "Akro Agate Co. Clarksburg W.VA." → the box itself is worth photographing and stating.
- Lustre glaze with Mickey/Minnie decals → 1930s Disney Japan; check every piece for decal wear and the teapot lid for repair.
- Count pieces and match colours: Akro sets are often married from mismatched pieces (BDN).
- Porcelain thin enough to see light through, gold rims, nursery or holiday transfer scenes → German.
- Lithographed tin with "Ohio Art" or "Wolverine" printed on the tray/plates → tin; box raises it a lot.
- Turn pieces over: "Hand Painted Japan" (1921–41), "Made in Occupied Japan" (1945–52), "Germany" or "Bavaria".
- Original box with insert and straw mat or printed doily support → premium.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Akro Agate boxed "Little American Maid" / "Play-Time" set, complete** — ~$60–250 (Play-Time to $250 at auction, Post-Bulletin; Terapeak boxed median $48, p75 $87) vs $15–35 lusterware — printed box with Clarksburg W.Va. maker line, matching colours throughout.
- **Akro Agate in rare colours (pumpkin, oxblood, lemonade-and-oxblood marble)** — top Terapeak row $220 for a 3250 Oxblood Swirl set vs ~$48 median — opaque orange (pumpkin) or creamy yellow glass with blood-red swirls.
- **1930s Disney Mickey/Minnie lusterware, figural or boxed** — $230 boxed figural set (Hake's 2015) vs Terapeak median $43 loose — Mickey-head teapot lid, full-body Mickey decals, "Little Hostess Set" box.
- **German porcelain set with Halloween or holiday transfers (c.1908–13)** — dealer-priced well above plain German sets (general knowledge, verify) — black cats, jack-o'-lanterns or witches on thin gold-trimmed porcelain.
- **Victorian German set in its fitted case** — 2–3× an uncased set (general knowledge, verify) — velvet- or paper-lined fitted box with recesses for each piece.
- **Ohio Art tin set in original box** — from ~$80 vs $6–20 loose (Post-Bulletin) — box with Ohio Art name, unrusted lithography.
- **Noritake child's set (marked)** — modest premium over unmarked Japan (general knowledge, verify) — Noritake wreath/"M" backstamp.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Moriage or detailed hand painting > crude floral; complete service for 6 with plates > partial.
- Lusterware: real lustre is a thin iridescent glaze that rubs off at rims and handles; hand painting shows brush strokes and slight variation piece to piece; decals are identical and flat.
- Akro: pressed glass with mould seams, slight bubbles, swirl that differs on every piece. Colour consistency across a set matters (BDN).
- German porcelain: translucent, crisp transfers, real gold that wears pink-brown, not yellow paint.
- Old vs new: period lusterware has crazing and rim lustre wear; modern Chinese lustre-look sets are heavier, bright, uncrazed, often with stickers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Lustre wear, chips on lids/spouts, missing lids. Also glued teapot lids (the Hake's set had a glue repair on the Mickey head lid), hairlines in cups, decal loss on Disney pieces, chipped Akro rims (chips and losses make complete Akro sets hard to find — BDN), rust and dents on tin, water-stained or torn boxes.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese lusterware-look sets; reproduction Akro boxes. Akro-like glass toy dishes by other makers in similar colours (BDN warns shades were also made by other companies); check for the crow-in-A and pattern match.

## Market
Slow; many sets listed. Akro Agate has a dedicated glass/marble collector base; Disney sets cross over to Disneyana buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Disney 1930s sets and Akro Agate in boxes. Also rare Akro colours (pumpkin, oxblood) listed as "old glass doll dishes".

## Shipping / handling
Fragile, small pieces; $12–15. Wrap each piece; box-in-box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Piece count, marks, chips.
