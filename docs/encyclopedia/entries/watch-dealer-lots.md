## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Listings of several watches at once, either a seller's "lot" (bag or box of watches) or a dealer/antique-mall showcase where each watch carries a paper tag price. The photo usually shows tag prices like "$69", "RUNS", "AUTOMATIC". A showcase photo is a sign the seller is a dealer who already priced each item at retail — no edge.
Recognising valuable pieces from a photo:
- **Solid gold cases**: rich even yellow colour with no brassing at lugs/edges; 14K/18K stamps inside the caseback (needs a photo). Gold-filled (GF, RGP, "10K gold filled", "rolled gold plate") shows brass at edges when worn — worth $1–5 scrap only.
- **Desirable makers/models**: Hamilton (Ventura asymmetrical, Electric 500, early rectangular "Piping Rock", military), Bulova Accutron (tuning fork, open dial "Spaceview"), Omega, Longines, Universal Geneve, Gruen Curvex, Benrus military (DTU-2A/P), Elgin/Waltham military A-11 hackers, Rolex/Tudor (rare in lots, but the jackpot).
- **Low tier**: Timex (Marlin, Viscount, mechanical 1950s–80s), Westclox, Ingersoll, Swiss pin-lever brands (Chalet, Caravelle by Bulova, Waltham post-1957 Swiss, Sheffield), Japanese/Hong Kong "17 jewels" novelties.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid gold vs. gold-filled vs. plated vs. stainless vs. chrome-plated base metal. Jewel count (17+ = full-jewelled lever; 1–7 jewels = pin-lever). Applied (raised) numerals and hands = better quality; printed dials = cheaper. Signed crowns and original bracelets add value. Refinished ("redialed") dials have crisp new printing, misaligned text, wrong fonts — they cut collector value in half.

## Condition, damage and repairs
"Runs" means little: it may run for minutes and lose an hour a day. Service costs $100–250 per mechanical watch, far exceeding most values. Common issues: broken balance staffs, rusted movements (water), missing crowns/stems, cracked crystals (cheap), dial damage, worn gold-filled cases. Auto-winding rotors on 1950s Bulova/Timex wear their bushings. Batteries leaking in quartz watches.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Rolex/Omega/Cartier common in lots; Franken-watches (case of one, movement of another, redial). Any luxury brand in a lot needs authentication: outside Dylan's rules — do not buy for the luxury piece. Bulova Accutron reissues (2010s) and Timex Marlin reissues (2017+) exist; check movement.

## Market
High volume: thousands of vintage watch sales per month on eBay. Running 1950s–60s Bulova/Elgin automatics sell in 1–3 weeks at $40–100. Repair/parts lots sell well to watchmakers ($3–8 per mechanical watch). Showcase-priced watches are retail; there is no margin.

## What sellers usually miss
Solid 14K cases in "old watch" lots (melt value $100–400 for a men's case); Accutron "tuning fork" movements; Hamilton Electric; military watches with government markings on caseback; Seiko automatic chronographs. Most sellers miss nothing in a dealer showcase.

## Shipping / handling
Small, light: padded box, $5–9 for a single watch (USPS Ground Advantage), lots $9–15. Insure gold. Wrap each watch so crystals don't rub.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker (Bulova, Elgin, Hamilton, Timex), model/caliber (Accutron, 747, Spaceview), decade from the date code, movement (automatic, 17 jewels, electric), case (14K, gold filled, stainless), size mm, "running"/"for parts"; lots: count and "watchmaker lot". Junk: "Rolex-style", "gold" on gold-filled, "rare", "antique" on 1960s. `Vintage 1955 Bulova 23 Jewel Self Winding Gold Filled Mens Watch Running 33mm L5`
- **Where:** eBay — 7-day auction for running 1950s–60s automatics, Accutrons and solid gold (thousands sold a month), BIN with offers for singles; non-runners in parts lots to watchmakers ($3–8 per mechanical).
- **Price position:** at the sold median (running Timex $15–45, Bulova/Elgin/Hamilton gold-filled $40–120, Accutron/Hamilton Electric/14K $150–600, quartz lots $17, mixed project lots $60), OBO, accept −15%, floor melt for gold, $10 per lot; cut after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** dial straight-on; caseback interior with material stamps; movement with date code/caliber; case sides and lugs; crown and crystal; band; head on a gram scale; video of it running.
- **State:** runs and for how long tested, timekeeping if known, "not serviced", case material stamps, movement caliber and date code, dial originality, size without crown, band, crystal scratches by photo number; non-runners "for parts/repair".
- **Pack / haul:** pocket; padded box $5–9 single, lots $9–15, each watch wrapped so crystals don't rub; insure gold.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [1955] [Bulova 23-jewel automatic] (date code [L5]), [gold filled], running [48 hours] on test, not serviced; running 1950s Bulovas sell for $[40–120], and the solid-14K ones are the $200+ pieces."

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$6 to buyer − ~$8 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A single $60 watch needs a $140+ median: only Accutron/Hamilton Electric/solid gold qualify. Lots: buy only if (count of mechanical watches × ~$5 parts value + any identifiable solid-gold melt) clears the rule. No untested electronics (quartz/Accutron must be shown running — Accutrons hum). No luxury brands that need authentication. Dealer showcase listings: pass.
