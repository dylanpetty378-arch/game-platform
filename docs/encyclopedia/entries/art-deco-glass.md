## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass made roughly 1920–1940 in the geometric, streamlined, high-contrast Art Deco style: stepped and faceted forms, frosted (acid-etched) figural panels of nudes, birds and fish, and bold colour contrasts. The most common pieces sellers call "Art Deco glass":
- **Czech "Tango" glass**: bright orange-red (cased or enamelled) with black enamel bands or black glass rims, often on clear twisted or plain stems. Candlesticks, vases, console bowls, perfume bottles. Ground/polished pontil; "Czechoslovakia" acid stamp (an oval or circle) or a paper label underneath.
- **Enamelled Czech/Bohemian glass**: black-enamel rims, painted florals and birds on frosted or coloured glass (Harrach, Moser-Karlsbad, Loetz late period, many unnamed Novy Bor/Kamenicky Senov studios).
- **French frosted/opalescent**: René Lalique (moulded "R.LALIQUE" pre-1945; "Lalique France" after), Sabino (opalescent with blue-gold glow, "Sabino Paris"), Etling, Hunebelle, Verlys (French and later Heisey/Holophane US), Marius-Ernest Sabino.
- **American Deco**: Consolidated "Ruba Rombic" (1928–32, cubist, very valuable), Phoenix and Consolidated sculptured vases (frosted/pastel backgrounds), Fostoria, Cambridge Statuesque/"Nude stem" (Cambridge Crown Tuscan, Carmen, Royal Blue), New Martinsville, Paden City, Fenton "Mandarin Red" and "Jade", Imperial, L.E. Smith black amethyst, Tiffin black satin.
- **English**: Davidson and Bagley pressed "cloud" and frosted figural centrepieces (flower frogs with nude figures), Walther (German) figural centrepieces.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Period Czech**: soft, slightly uneven enamel with brush texture, polished pontil or ground flat foot, mould seams fire-polished away on hand-made pieces, slight asymmetry between a "pair". Wear on the black enamel underneath from 80+ years on shelves.
- **Applied vs. painted colour**: cased/applied orange glass shows colour in the thickness of the glass at the edge; cold-painted (unfired) orange sits on the surface and scratches off with a fingernail — cold paint is lower value and often later.
- **Twisted stems**: hand-twisted stems show slightly irregular spirals and slight drift between the two sticks; perfectly identical machine-pressed twists with seam lines suggest 1960s–1990s production.
- **Lalique**: crisp moulding, satiny frosted acid finish, no seams on top quality; signature placement known per model (check Marcilhac catalogue numbers).
- **Weight**: crystal (lead) pieces are heavy and ring; soda-lime pressed pieces are lighter and thud.
- **UV**: many 1920s–30s clear glasses fluoresce yellow-green (manganese/selenium); modern clear glass usually does not (weak test).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips to cup rims and foot edges, flea bites on twisted stems, enamel loss (especially on the underside of feet where they were set down), cloudiness ("sick glass") in vases, grinding of rims to remove chips (a pair with one shorter stick is a tell), glued stems on candlesticks (look for a hairline ring at the joint). Any chip or ground rim cuts value 40–70%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Lalique: fake "R. LALIQUE" signatures added to post-war or other-maker pieces (wrong font, wrong place, sand-blasted signature on a piece that should be moulded). Sabino: 1960s–70s reissues from the same moulds. Czech Tango: continuous production through the 1950s–60s and imports from Poland, Romania and China since the 1990s in the same orange/black palette; modern pieces have bright, perfect, sprayed colour and no wear. "Ruba Rombic" was reproduced in small numbers. Deco "nude" flower frogs reproduced widely (Asian imports, 1980s–2000s).

## Market
Steady but niche; decorators buy Tango colours for mid-century/deco rooms. Unsigned pairs: a few dozen eBay sales per month across all forms; sell-through modest (~30–40%); 2–8 weeks to sell at fair price. Signed French glass sells faster and at auction.

## What sellers usually miss
- The acid "Czechoslovakia" stamp under the foot (a faint frosted oval) — they call it "unmarked".
- R.LALIQUE moulded signatures on the side of the base, not underneath.
- Ruba Rombic and Consolidated pieces sold as "weird old vase".
- Conversely, sellers OVER-value: any orange/black or frosted nude is titled "Art Deco" and priced like signed glass.

## Shipping / handling
Double-box, each candlestick wrapped separately, stems supported, 2 in. of padding all round. Pair of 8–9 in. candlesticks: ~5 lb boxed, $12–18 ground. Insure; glass breakage claims are common.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State measurements, weight, any mark (photograph it), ground/polished pontil, enamel wear, and whether colour is applied glass or cold paint. Say "interwar Czech style" unless marked; never call it Lalique/Moser without a mark. Photograph against white, then lit from behind to show colour and any chips.
