## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Solid-colour glazed earthenware dinnerware with concentric rings ("bands") near the rim and in the centre, Art Deco shapes. Recognition tells:
- **Concentric rings**: vintage plates have rings on the rim; fruit bowls ("nappies" and 5½" fruits) have rings inside the centre (example 7949371872).
- **Marks**: vintage = ink stamp "Fiesta HLC USA" (lowercase "fiesta" in Art Deco script) or in-mold "fiesta / HLC USA"; many vintage pieces are unmarked (chop plates, candleholders, cups, some bowls). Post-86 = in-mold "FIESTA ® HLC USA" with a capital F and ® plus a date code/letter; later Post-86 have "Fiesta" with a lazer-etched ® and "LEAD FREE".
- **Feet**: vintage foot rings are typically glazed, with three small sagger-pin dots or a dry (unglazed) narrow ring; Post-86 have a wider dry foot.
- **Weight and body**: vintage is lighter earthenware; Post-86 is heavier vitrified china.

## Makers, brands and variants
**Vintage Fiesta colours and dates**:
- Original 1936: red (orange-red "Radioactive red", uranium oxide; 1936–43 and 1959–72), cobalt blue (1936–51), ivory (1936–51), yellow (1936–69), light green (1936–51). Turquoise 1937–69.
- "Fifties colours" 1951–59: gray, rose, chartreuse, forest green – $15–80 a piece; gray is often the most sought of the four.
- Medium green 1959–69 – the most valuable colour; even plates $30–60, serving pieces $150–1,500.
- Fiesta Ironstone 1969–72: antique gold, turf green, mango red (= red renamed) – low value.
- Kitchen Kraft (1939–) oven ware, Fiesta Casuals (decal), Amberstone (1967–) – low–mid.
**Sister lines**: Harlequin (1938–64, Woolworth's, no rings on rims, unmarked; $8–80), Riviera (1938–50, square, unmarked), Fiesta Kitchen Kraft.
**Post-86 Fiesta** (1986–present): many colours (Persimmon, Sunflower, Peacock, Scarlet, Lapis, Shamrock, Chartreuse, Slate, Pearl Gray, etc.). Most pieces $3–25; some retired-colour pieces (Sapphire, Raspberry, Lilac, Juniper, Chartreuse) $20–200.
**Rare vintage forms**: tripod candleholders (1936–43), syrup pitcher, 12" comport, covered onion soup (turquoise is $5,000+), bud vases and flower vases (8", 10", 12"), 11¾" fruit bowl, stick-handled demitasse pot, marmalade, mustard, carafe, relish tray with inserts, disk water pitcher in medium green, juice tumblers in rare colours, 10" cake plate.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Vintage red is a deep orange-red with slight orange-peel texture; Post-86 Persimmon is coral/salmon, Scarlet is true deep red, Paprika is brick.
- Vintage light green is a soft, slightly bluish mint (example 7948741255 chop plate fits); Post-86 Shamrock and Juniper are much stronger.
- Vintage gray is a warm smoky gray (example 7949371872); Post-86 Pearl Gray (1999–2001) is lighter and glassier, Slate darker.
- Uranium-glazed red reads on a Geiger counter – a useful vintage confirmation.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips and flea bites (common on bowls and chop plates), glaze crazing, utensil marks on plates (gray lines), hairlines, crazing-stains, glaze skips and factory pops (acceptable if noted), repairs with filler/epoxy (check under UV). Plates lose half their value with heavy utensil marks.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Post-86 Fiesta reissued many shapes in new colours, including the bulb candleholder and disk pitcher – learn the colour differences. Fiesta lookalikes: Vernon Kilns "Early California", Bauer, Taylor Smith & Taylor "Luray", Franciscan "El Patio", Mexican-made ringware – none carry the Fiesta mark. Some sellers call any colourful ringware "Fiesta". Fake uranium red is not an issue.

## Market
Large, steady collector market (Homer Laughlin China Collectors Association), strongest for vintage medium green and rare forms; common vintage plates are slow sellers and price-compressed by abundant Post-86 supply. Estate sales flood the market. No strong seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
- Medium green (1959–69) pieces mixed in with Post-86 greens.
- Unmarked vintage serving pieces (chop plates, candleholders, cups) described as "not Fiesta" or "unmarked platter".
- Sellers over-estimate Post-86 as "vintage Fiesta" and price single vintage plates too high.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy and chip-prone. Single bowl/candleholder 1–2 lb boxed $12–15; chop plate 3–4 lb, needs a 16"+ box, $18–25; sets ship poorly. Double-box with rim protection.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Vintage Fiesta" + Homer Laughlin + exact colour name (medium green / red / cobalt / gray) + form (chop plate / tripod candleholder / teapot) + size + "1936–72" era. Junk: "Fiestaware" (misspelt), "retro dishes", "Post-86" sold as vintage. `Vintage Fiesta Medium Green Teapot Homer Laughlin 1959-69 Original`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers; Facebook Fiesta collector groups sell medium green and rare forms for more with no fee; Strawser auctions for top pieces. Post-86 and single plates local or in sets.
- **Price position:** at the sold median for colour and form (red bulb candleholder pair $80–120; chop plate $35–60), accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** piece on white in daylight (colour true); backstamp or unmarked foot; rim all round; every chip and utensil mark close up; colour next to a known piece; dimensions against a ruler.
- **State:** vintage vs Post-86 with mark photo (or "unmarked as made"), exact colour name and dates, dimensions, every chip/utensil mark/crazing by photo number, "uranium glaze" noted for vintage red, not marketed for food use.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox, bowl 1–2 lb $12–15; chop plate 3–4 lb in a 16"+ box $18–25; double-box with rim protection, pieces never touching.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Vintage Homer Laughlin Fiesta [form] in [colour], made [dates] only, no chips; Post-86 pieces sell for [$] and vintage [medium green/red] of this form brings [$]."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$15–20) − ship to Dylan (~$15–20) − price ≥ max($50, price). Single common pieces never pass. Buy: vintage medium green, tripod candleholders, vases, comports, syrups, carafes, pairs of red/cobalt bulb candleholders, or mixed vintage serving lots at ≤ 1/3 of sold median. Pass Post-86 (unless retired rare colours you can confirm), chipped pieces, single plates/bowls, Ironstone, lookalike ringware.
