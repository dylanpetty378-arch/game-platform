## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Staffordshire pottery teapots, 1900–1990s. The common Sadler look: white glazed earthenware, fluted (scalloped) body, gold-lined spout/handle/rim, decal flowers (yellow or pink roses) — the example is exactly this. Flip for marks: "SADLER ENGLAND" impressed or printed (often with shape number), "Arthur Wood England", "Price Kensington", "Royal Albert Bone China England" (crown), "Shelley" (shield), "Bizarre by Clarice Cliff" (hand-painted Art Deco), "Royal Winton Grimwades" (chintz).

## Makers, brands and variants
- James Sadler & Sons (Burslem 1899–2000): fluted floral pots, cube teapots, novelty shapes (racing car OKT42 1930s, Thomas the Tank, Old Curiosity Shop). Floral = common; OKT42 racing car $150–400.
- Arthur Wood, Price Kensington, Wade: novelty/cottage ware, low-mid.
- Brown Betty (various makers, Cauldon/Alcock, Lindrup): $15–40.
- Royal Albert (Old Country Roses etc.) $60–150; Shelley bone china (Dainty, Art Deco Vogue/Mode shapes) $100–600; Clarice Cliff Bizarre $300–1,500+; Royal Winton chintz $100–300; Portmeirion Botanic Garden $50–100; Wedgwood jasper teapots $60–200.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Earthenware (opaque, heavier, crazing common) vs bone china (translucent when backlit, thinner). Hand-painted (brush strokes, enamel raised) beats decal/transfer (uniform dots). Gold that rubs off with a finger = low-fired, typical of everyday ware. Older Sadler impressed marks; later printed backstamps with "Staffordshire".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Check spout tip chips, lid flange chips, hairlines at handle joints, crazing and tea staining, worn gilt. Restoration (glued handle/spout) shows under UV. Lid mismatch common — confirm pattern match.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Clarice Cliff fakes exist (wrong backstamp, muddy painting). Sadler racing car reissued (later versions with "Sadler" printed and slightly different colors); Churchill reissued Sadler designs post-2000. Floral pots are not faked—just common.

## Market
Floral Sadler: high supply, steady low demand; 30–90 days. Novelty and Deco pieces sell fast to collectors. Q4 gift season helps.

## What sellers usually miss
Shelley/Clarice Cliff marks underneath unremarkable-looking teapots; Sadler racing car's value; that a chintz all-over pattern is collectible.

## Shipping / handling
Double box, lid wrapped separately, bubble-wrapped spout. 2–4 lb packed; $12–18.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker and backstamp photo, capacity (cups), dimensions, condition of gilt, any crazing, lid fit.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). A $35 floral Sadler nets negative at any price above $0. Buy only Tier High/Top pieces under ~$60. Fits easily.
