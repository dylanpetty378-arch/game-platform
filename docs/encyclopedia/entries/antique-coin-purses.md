## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small purses for coins. Types:
- **Kiss-lock / frame purses** (1880s–1970s): metal frame with two balls that click; leather, vinyl, beaded, tapestry. Double-frame (two compartments) and squeeze-spring types.
- **Advertising giveaways** (1900s–1970s): leather/vinyl with gold-stamped business name and phone numbers (short local numbers like "500", "270" indicate pre-1960s rural exchanges, before 7-digit dialing in the 1950s–60s). The example lists 3-digit local numbers – likely 1940s–50s.
- **Mesh** (1890s–1940s): Whiting & Davis (Attleboro MA; tag "Whiting & Davis Co. Mesh Bags"), Mandalian Mfg. (enamelled "Armor Mesh", paisley/geometric designs, fringe) – gold or silver tone. 
- **Victorian**: miser's purse (long crocheted tube with rings), beaded, sterling hinged, chatelaine purses hung from a belt.

## Makers, brands and variants
Whiting & Davis, Mandalian, Evans (1920s compacts/purses), Napier, Prince Gardner (squeeze purses, 1950s–70s), Buxton, Rolfs, Amity, Hickok (men's coin purses), Elgin American, Bakelite-frame French purses, Czech beaded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Quality: sterling or silver-plate frames stamped "Sterling", fine glass seed beads, intact linings, enamelled mesh with crisp patterns. Cheap: vinyl, stamped steel frames, printed rather than hot-stamped gold lettering.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Worn/shredded linings, loose frame (lost clasp tension), missing beads, broken mesh links, verdigris, peeling gold stamping. Restringing beads is a repair (excluded).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese "vintage-style" beaded and kiss-lock purses; Whiting & Davis continued making mesh into the 1980s–2000s (newer tags, lighter weight). Advertising repros are rare (low value).

## Market
Plain coin purses: oversupplied. Advertising: niche but steady (town-name searchers). Mesh/beaded: good vintage-fashion demand on Etsy/eBay.

## What sellers usually miss
Mandalian enamel mesh and sterling frames lumped into "old purses"; sellers of local advertising purses typically price fairly.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket-size, padded envelope $5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the business, towns, and era (phone-number format), material, clasp function, and interior condition; title with every town name for local-history searchers.

## Buy rule
Local advertising purse resale $10–30 – profit impossible under max($50, price). Pass. Buy only mesh/Victorian pieces with conservative resale ≥ $80 at ≤ $20. Pocket-sized; no authentication issues except sterling claims (look for stamps).
