## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass beads made in Venice/Bohemia for African trade (1800s–1930s): millefiori (cane slices), chevron (layered star), "white hearts", eye beads; often restrung in Africa on fiber. Pairings with Tibetan/Nepali repoussé brass/white-metal beads, yellow "amber" resin barrels, and black glass seed beads indicate modern assembled jewelry. Example: black seed-bead strand with two yellow resin/possible-Bakelite barrels, two black glass ovals (one with small millefiori eye), central Nepali brass-and-white-metal bead, hook clasp — modern assembled ethnic necklace.

## Makers, brands and variants
Venetian (Moretti, etc.), Bohemian (Jablonec), Dutch, Indian; modern Ghana recycled glass; Nepali/Tibetan white-metal beads.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old trade beads: worn surfaces, irregular holes, pitting. Bakelite: hot-water/Simichrome test, heavy, clunky sound; resin is lighter. Amber floats in salt water, warm; copal fails acetone.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chipped beads, broken strings, restrung components.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Indonesian/Indian "trade beads", fake Bakelite ("fakelite"), fake amber resin.
