## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass beads made in Venice/Bohemia for African trade (1800s–1930s): millefiori (cane slices), chevron (layered star), "white hearts", eye beads; often restrung in Africa on fiber. Pairings with Tibetan/Nepali repoussé brass/white-metal beads, yellow "amber" resin barrels, and black glass seed beads indicate modern assembled jewelry. Example: black seed-bead strand with two yellow resin/possible-Bakelite barrels, two black glass ovals (one with small millefiori eye), central Nepali brass-and-white-metal bead, hook clasp — modern assembled ethnic necklace.

Venetian chevrons date from around 1500 (Murano), made from drawn, layered glass canes cut and ground to show a star or zigzag at each end; the classic is blue, white and red in 7 layers, with 4- and 6-layer variants (The Bead Chest). Millefiori ("thousand flowers") were made at Murano and traded to Africa until the early 20th century. Old trade strands are typically sold on raffia/fibre cord, not on wire with a clasp (The Bead Chest).

Photo routine: whole strand laid flat with a ruler; close-up of the ends of the biggest beads (chevron star, layer count); close-up of the stringing material; any clasp.

## Makers, brands and variants
Venetian (Moretti, etc.), Bohemian (Jablonec), Dutch, Indian; modern Ghana recycled glass; Nepali/Tibetan white-metal beads.
- **Venetian chevrons (rosetta)**: 4, 6 or 7 layers; more layers and points and crisp patterns = higher quality; fine antiques have 6–7 layers; green, blue and red variants (Bead Chest; Africa Direct stock).
- **Millefiori**: Murano, pre-20th-century examples hard to find; "elbow" millefiori and feather beads (combed patterns) also Venetian (Africa Direct; Napoleon Exhibit).
- **King beads**: large Venetian drawn beads with stripes, sold as rare by dealers (Napoleon Exhibit).
- **Moon beads**: opalescent glass, probably Murano (similar beads made there by 1693), limited 19th-c. output (Bead Chest).
- **Snake beads**: interlocking beads modelled on vertebrae, 19th c., made by Bapterosses (France — scarcer, pricier; pioneered Prosser moulding), Redlhammer Brothers (Bohemia) and Risler (Germany) (Bead Chest).
- **White hearts** (red/brick glass over a white core) and **Russian blues** (faceted cobalt) are classic trade types (general knowledge, verify).
- **Mixed trade strands**: mixed Venetian beads that may include millefiori and chevrons, occasionally a rare red or white heart, often on old raffia (Bead Chest).
- **Modern**: Ghana recycled-glass and powder-glass beads; Indian and Chinese chevrons; current Italian chevrons (Bead Chest).

### Quick ID checklist
- Count chevron layers at the bead end: 6–7 crisp layers with a ground star = top; 4 = lesser; painted or moulded "stripes" = faux.
- Wear: old trade beads have worn, slightly irregular surfaces, pitting, irregular holes; modern beads are uniform (Bead Chest).
- Stringing: old raffia/fibre cord = traded strand; modern wire, nylon and a lobster clasp = assembled jewellery.
- Graduated strand of one bead type (all millefiori, all chevrons) = trade strand; mixed metal "Tibetan" beads + resin + seed beads = assembled.
- Big single beads over ~30 mm (chevrons, kings) are the value carriers — ask for a ruler photo.
- Yellow/butterscotch barrels: test before claiming Bakelite or amber.
- Opalescent glow (moon beads) or interlocking segments (snake beads) are specialist types worth a closer look.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Large 6–7-layer antique Venetian chevron (single beads ~30–50 mm, or a full strand)** — ~5–15× an assembled necklace ($80–500+ vs $10–35) — ground ends showing a crisp 6–7-layer star in blue/white/red, wear and slight irregularity.
- **Full strand of old Venetian millefiori on raffia** — ~2–4× ($30–120 vs $10–35) — repeated cane-slice flowers, worn surfaces, raffia string, no clasp.
- **King beads / feather beads in a strand** — upper tier ($80–500+; sold as rare by dealers) — large striped barrels (king), combed feather patterns.
- **Bapterosses snake beads** — scarcer and pricier than Bohemian/German snake beads (Bead Chest; magnitude unverified) — interlocking vertebra-shaped beads.
- **Tested Bakelite beads** — ~3–10× resin look-alikes (the $80–500+ tier vs $10–35) — butterscotch/cherry tones, heavy, deep clunk; positive Simichrome or 409 test.
- **Baltic amber (genuine)** — upper tier — warm, light, floats in salt water, natural inclusions; copal fails acetone.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old trade beads: worn surfaces, irregular holes, pitting. Bakelite: hot-water/Simichrome test, heavy, clunky sound; resin is lighter. Amber floats in salt water, warm; copal fails acetone.
Bakelite tests in detail (Trashy Diva): hot water or friction gives a formaldehyde smell; heavy and substantial for size; tapping two pieces gives a deeper "clunk" not a "clink"; Simichrome on a cotton swab turns deep tan/tobacco brown; 409 cleaner or baking soda on a swab turns yellow. Run more than one test. Bakelite was invented in 1907. Chevrons: crisp patterns and more layers/points = quality; modern chevrons are more uniform (Bead Chest).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chipped beads, broken strings, restrung components. Chips on chevron ends reduce value most because the end is what buyers inspect; restringing on modern cord is acceptable if disclosed, but it removes the "original raffia" appeal.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Indonesian/Indian "trade beads", fake Bakelite ("fakelite"), fake amber resin. Chevrons are still made in Italy, India and China (Bead Chest); dealers list "faux chevron" beads openly (Africa Direct), and these often have printed or very regular layers. Ghana recycled-glass beads are new and honest but not "Venetian".

## Market
Steady among bead collectors; low-tier oversupplied. Collectors buy by bead type and size, not by "necklace"; dealers (Africa Direct, The Bead Chest, Tribal GH) sell individual graded beads and strands.

## What sellers usually miss
Bakelite in "plastic" necklaces; full millefiori strands. Also the layer count on a chevron and rare types (king, moon, snake beads) buried in mixed strands.

## Shipping / handling
Small padded envelope, $5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Bead types, length, materials testing results.

### What to tell the buyer
Name each bead type by its trade name (millefiori, 6-layer chevron, white heart, king bead), give strand length, the size of the largest beads in mm, bead count, and the stringing (original raffia vs restrung). Show the chevron ends close up and state the layer count. Give test results and the test used for any Bakelite or amber claim ("Simichrome positive"), and say "untested" otherwise. Do not claim age or "Venetian" without the wear and construction to support it; "Venetian-style" or "Ghana recycled glass" are honest alternatives.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price). Assembled necklaces fail. Fits pocket.
