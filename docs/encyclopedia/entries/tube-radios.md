## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Zenith H725/K725/Y723 (1951–55)**: maroon or brown bakelite, a big round gold dial with "Zenith" below, a carry handle. Working $70–130; untested $30–60.
- **Philco Transitone, RCA 8X, GE 400-series**: similar, $40–90.
- **Catalin** (swirled or marbled, translucent: Fada 1000 "Bullet", Addison, Emerson AU-190) is $800+. It never looks flat brown.
- Other forms (general knowledge, verify): wooden cathedral and tombstone radios (1930s), console floor radios (bigger than a passenger seat), and chairside sets.
- Seller words: "vintage Zenith radio", "tube radio", "bakelite radio", "antique radio".
- **AA5 ("All American Five")**: the standard 5-tube AC/DC table radio of the 1940s to late 1950s/early 1960s; most bakelite, plaskon and catalin table sets are this type (antiqueradio.org). No power transformer, so they are light for their size.
- **Where the ID is:** model number on a paper label or stamp on the back panel or chassis, often also a tube-layout chart glued inside the cabinet; Zenith models on the back cover; date codes on speakers and capacitors help date a set (general knowledge, verify).
- **Catalin shapes:** Fada "Bullet" (streamlined, round end, handle, 1940–47 across five Fada models; Model 1000 introduced 1945); Emerson AU-190 (c. 1938, cathedral/tombstone shape in catalin); Motorola 50XC; RCA 66X8 "Tuna Boat" (c. 1948) (WSHU, antiqueradio.org).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Zenith**: H725/K725/Y723 brown bakelite AM/FM; Trans-Oceanic portables (separate market) (general knowledge, verify).
- **Philco** Transitone; **RCA** 8X; **GE** 400-series; **Emerson**; **Sentinel** (named in the original index row).
- **Catalin makers**: Fada (1000 "Bullet"), Addison, Emerson (AU-190 "Tombstone"). Colour combinations multiply the price (general knowledge, verify).
- **Fada 1000 Bullet detail:** six-tube superheterodyne with loop antenna (12SK7, 12SA7, 12SK7, 12SQ7, 35L6GT, 35Z5GT); contrasting colours for body, knobs, dial bezel, handle and sometimes grille; the "butterscotch/amber" body seen today was usually alabaster white originally; typical Bullet $500+, rare colour combinations thousands (antiqueradio.org).
- **Auction tops reported:** Fada Bullet up to $6,000, a Motorola catalin up to $11,000, a near-perfect Emerson catalin tombstone $25,000 (WSHU, 2015).
- Variants that move value: catalin colour (reds, greens, blues and multi-colour beat butterscotch), matching original knobs/handle/bezel, uncracked cabinet; for wood sets, cathedral and tombstone shapes and brand (Philco 90 cathedral etc.) over plain 1940s boxes (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Cabinet: opaque dark brown/black (bakelite), opaque light solid colour with colour only on the surface (plaskon/urea), or translucent marbled with depth (catalin)?
- Back/chassis label with maker and model number photographed?
- Cracks: catalin around chassis screw holes and above tubes ("tube burn" discolouration); bakelite cracks at corners.
- Knobs, handle, bezel original and matching? (Swapped repro parts are common on catalin.)
- Back panel present? Line cord original and intact, or replaced?
- Dial glass/lens intact, pointer present?
- Has it been recapped/restored (receipts, new caps visible)? Tested working, by whom?
- Size: table set (fits) vs console (excluded).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Bakelite: an opaque, dense brown or black phenolic. Catalin: cast phenolic, translucent and marbled, often yellowed with age. Plaskon/urea: an opaque light colour (white, ivory, pastel).
- The chassis label on the back carries the model number. An original back panel and knobs matter.
- Catalin (American Catalin Corp., 1930s) is coloured through the material; plaskon only on the surface. Tap test: catalin sounds soft, like wood; plaskon sounds like plastic. Swab test on a hidden spot with a cleaner (e.g. Formula 409): catalin releases some colour, plaskon only dirt (WSHU).
- Catalin darkens by oxidation and heat (white → amber/butterscotch); it can be sanded and polished back because the colour is consistent through, but harsh chemicals damage it; inside you see unpolished mould marks (antiqueradio.org). Water slowly dissolves it; attic/barn storage kills cabinets.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cracked cabinets (catalin shrinks and cracks), missing knobs or back, a frayed cord. Old capacitors need replacing before powering up ("recapping").
- Untested sets are a pass under Dylan's rule. Plugging in an unrestored set can damage it (general knowledge, verify).
- Over-tightened chassis screws are the biggest cause of catalin cracks; heavy chassis in larger sets add strain (WSHU).
- Safety: AA5 sets are "hot chassis" — one side of the AC line can be tied to the chassis, so exposed screws or a missing back can shock; wax/paper capacitors fail most; restorers replace them, fit a polarized plug and switch the hot side (antiqueradio.org). Do not power up untested sets to "check".
- Bakelite: cracks, chips at corners, dull/oxidised finish (polishable), broken dial glass.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction catalin cabinets and knobs exist, and modern "retro" radios (Crosley, Thomas) are sold as vintage (general knowledge, verify).
- Repro knobs, handles and bezels in contrasting colours are fitted to make a single-colour catalin look like a rare multi-colour set (antiqueradio.org).
- Modern retro radios have transistor/IC circuits, often FM/Bluetooth/cassette, and "Made in China" labels.

## Market
Collectors and decorators. Restored, working sets sell much better than untested ones. eBay, plus the local and antique-radio club market. Steady but aging buyer base (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Catalin vs bakelite: catalin is translucent and marbled.
- A butterscotch catalin Fada is often a faded white one, still catalin and still valuable; plaskon (opaque surface colour) is often wrongly called catalin by sellers too.

## Shipping / handling
- Table radios 5–10 lb, fragile cabinets, double-boxed $20–35 (general knowledge, verify).
- Catalin: avoid heat and cold in transit; wrap knobs separately; never ship loose chassis inside the cabinet without support.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker, model (from the chassis label), year, cabinet material, working or not, and whether it has been restored (recapped), cracks, knobs and back present.
- Title keywords: "Vintage Zenith H725 Bakelite AM FM Tube Radio Working".

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker and model from the label, approximate year, cabinet material stated plainly (bakelite, plaskon or catalin, and why), and exact condition: cracks (location and length), chips, discolouration, which knobs/back/handle are original. For function, say precisely what was done ("recapped by ___ in ___, plays AM", or "untested, sold for display or restoration; do not plug in without service"). Mention that AA5 sets are AC/DC hot-chassis designs that should be serviced before regular use. Do not call a set catalin unless it is translucent/marbled with colour through the material.

## Buy rule
Buy when: coloured catalin (Fada, Addison, Emerson): stop everything and evaluate it.
Pass when: brown bakelite above $40; untested is a pass for Dylan regardless.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (table sets do; consoles don't).
