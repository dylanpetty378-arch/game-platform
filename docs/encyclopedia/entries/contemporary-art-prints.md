## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Screen prints (serigraphs) on heavy cream or white paper, typically 18×24 in (Obey standard), 24×36 (Obey large), 70×50 cm (POW/Banksy), or odd sizes (KAWS 27×35, Invader). Sold flat in a tube or mailed flat; often seen framed. Look for: pencil signature lower right ("Shepard Fairey" scrawl, usually with the year; Banksy signature is a stencil-like "BANKSY" hand-written in pencil on signed editions only; KAWS signs "KAWS" with the year), pencil edition fraction lower left (156/550), an embossed blindstamp (Obey: a round Andre-the-Giant face/"OBEY" star stamp, often also a red/blue circular ink stamp in the image; POW: a small "Pictures on Walls" circular blindstamp lower corner; KAWS: publisher blindstamp e.g. Pace Prints), and the paper's texture under raking light. In the example listing the second photo shows the Obey circular ink star stamp in the red field and the pencil signature on the margin — that pattern (signature + stamp + fraction) is the real-print signature. Title words sellers use: "signed & numbered", "Obey", "screen print", "limited edition", "street art", "Shepard Fairey". Look-alikes: offset-litho posters (dot pattern under a loupe, thin glossy paper, no pencil fraction), "Obey Clothing" promo posters, and framed "prints" that are giclée copies.

## Makers, brands and variants
**Shepard Fairey / Obey Giant (Studio Number One, Los Angeles; obeygiant.com print archive is the authority).** Started 1989 with the Andre the Giant sticker; began selling signed-numbered screen prints ~1998-2000. Edition sizes grew with fame: 1998-2004 prints were 50-300 copies, 2005-2008 mostly 300-450, 2009-2025 mostly 450-550 (the "weekly Thursday drop" model at $50-85 retail). Value drivers: year (pre-2008 strongly outperforms), edition size (≤100 multiplies), format (24×36 and larger, "Large Format" HPM/retro-mix on wood or metal), subject (Obama "Hope"/"Progress" 2008, Andre/"OBEY" icon prints, Johnny Cash, Hendrix, Cassius Clay, Lotus Woman/Lotus series, "Peace Guard", "Make Art Not War"), and letterpress/relief editions. Appraisily's 2024-25 comp set: general signed prints $100-550, median $320, quartiles $150-780. A 2021-25 standard 18×24 ed. 550 signed-numbered print in the secondary market sells $100-350 (estimate from the comp set and triage history); the same image unsigned or an "open edition" offset $15-40. Obama "Hope" 2008 signed screenprint (editions 350 and 5,000 unsigned offset) — signed $3,000-10,000, unsigned offset $150-400 (estimates; well-documented at auction). Lotus series prints in the UK £450-550 (Appraisily). Artist proofs (AP) and HPM (hand-painted multiples) 2-5× the standard edition.

**Banksy (Pictures on Walls "POW", London, 2002-2017; Pest Control Office authenticates since 2008).** Editions of 150 signed + 600 unsigned (Girl with Balloon 2004, Flag, Pulp Fiction, Bomb Hugger, Love Is in the Air, Monkey Queen etc.), some 250/500/750. Unsigned "Girl with Balloon" 408/600 with POW blindstamp and a Pest Control COA was estimated £50,000-70,000 at Bonhams April 2025; MyArtBroker and Mark Littler put most Banksy prints at £10,000-50,000, signed editions higher. Without a Pest Control certificate a Banksy is unsellable at auction and a fraud risk on eBay. Outside Dylan's rules entirely (price > $200 and needs authentication) — the only reason to know it is to spot a POW print mis-described as "Banksy poster" at a garage sale: the tells are the POW blindstamp, the pencil fraction, the heavy wove paper and the 70×50 cm sheet; "Banksy" printed in the margin or a glossy surface = poster, $10-30.

**KAWS (Brian Donnelly; publishers Pace Prints, Modern Multiples, Kawsone).** Signed screenprints in editions of 100-250 (e.g. "Share", "Supermodel", "Ups and Downs" series £6,000-9,000 per MyArtBroker indicators). Unsigned "Kawsone" open prints and Uniqlo posters $20-60. Fakes are common on eBay (misprinted edition fonts, poor registration); needs provenance.

**Invader (Space Invader).** Signed "Invasion Kit" mosaic kits and "Rubik" prints, editions 100-500; €500-5,000; signature and a numbered certificate from the artist's site. Rarely under $200 unless unrecognised.

**Others in the same category seen at estate level:** Faile, D*Face, Mr. Brainwash (large editions, $100-500 signed, fast depreciation), Cleon Peterson, Bäst, Ron English, Eine; and Obey-adjacent "Thursday drop" artists (Munk One, Jeff Gillette). Mr. Brainwash is the one owners overvalue most.

### Quick ID checklist
1. Pencil signature and date lower right; pencil fraction lower left; both in graphite, not printed.
2. Blindstamp (embossed) — Obey star/Andre stamp; POW circular stamp; Pace Prints stamp.
3. Paper: thick cream speckletone or Coventry rag, matte; screen-print ink sits ON the paper in flat layers with slight relief and no dot pattern under a loupe.
4. Size matches the archive (Obey 18×24 or 24×36; POW 70×50 cm).
5. Title and year found on obeygiant.com archive (image, edition size, date, price). Banksy: Pest Control COA number; KAWS: publisher and edition.
6. Condition: fading (reds and blues go first), foxing, tape, pinholes, trimmed margins, acid-burn from a cheap frame mat.

### Golden-egg tells
- Shepard Fairey prints dated 1998-2004 (pencil year next to the signature) with edition ≤ 300 — $500-3,000 vs $100-350 for a 2012-25 ed. 450-550 print — look for the year after the signature and a fraction with a small denominator; early images are Andre/OBEY icon, "Giant" lettering, propaganda posters, often on thinner paper.
- Obama "Hope"/"Progress" 2008 signed-numbered screenprint (ed. 350 / ed. 400) — $3,000-10,000 vs $150-400 for the unsigned offset poster (ed. 5,000) — pencil signature + fraction on an 24×36 sheet; the offset has a printed credit line and no graphite.
- Edition fraction with a denominator ≤ 100, or "AP", "PP", "HPM" written in the margin — 2-5× the standard 450-550 edition of the same image — read the lower-left pencil.
- 24×36 or larger "Large Format" Fairey prints, and prints on wood/metal/collage (HPM) — $1,000-8,000 vs $100-350 for the 18×24 — the sheet dwarfs an 18×24 frame; HPMs show collage paper and hand-applied paint texture.
- Any Banksy-image print with a Pictures on Walls blindstamp and a graphite fraction (x/600 or x/150) — £10,000-70,000+ vs $10-30 for a Banksy poster — small round embossed "POW" stamp in the lower margin; sheet 70×50 cm; signed copies carry a pencil "BANKSY". Needs Pest Control; outside Dylan's rules but worth recognising.
- KAWS signed screenprint with "KAWS" + year in pencil and a publisher blindstamp (Pace Prints), edition ≤ 250 — £2,000-15,000 vs $20-60 for Uniqlo/Kawsone posters — crosses on the eyes taper to points; colours are flat and saturated, not dyed-looking.
- Fairey letterpress/Lotus series and collaborations with photographers (Glen E. Friedman "Minor Threat", Jim Marshall "Johnny Cash", Dennis Morris "Sex Pistols") — $400-1,500 vs $150 — subject is a photo-derived rock portrait and the sheet carries two signatures.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real screen prints: hand-pulled layers of flat opaque ink; where two colours meet you may see a hairline of mis-registration; metallic/gold inks have a visible sheen; paper edges are deckled or guillotined with no printer's bleed. Obey uses cream "Speckletone" (visible fibre flecks) on most 18×24s. Offset posters: full-colour halftone dots under a 10× loupe, thin glossy stock, printed credit text in the margin, perfectly even surface. Giclée copies: inkjet dot dither, slightly soft edges, and often printed on bright-white or canvas. Older prints (pre-2008) show slight toning of the cream paper and sometimes handling marks; newer ones are crisp. Framing: museum-glass, acid-free matting and hinge mounts preserve value; dry-mounted (glued flat) prints lose 30-50%.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fading from sunlight (compare the margin under the mat to the exposed area), foxing, mat burn (brown line around the image), pinholes in the corners (studio "pinned up"), tape on the back, creases/dents from tube storage, trimmed margins (cut to fit a frame — kills the edition tells), water tide-lines. Acceptable: faint handling marks, slight corner softness. Repairs show as flattened creases with sheen loss, or paper conservators' infills. Ask: has it been out of the frame; is the full sheet present (give sheet size); any fading; was it ever dry-mounted.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Obey: bootleg offset copies of popular images (Hope, Andre) and pencil signatures added to open-edition posters; verify against obeygiant.com (edition size, size, date) and look for the blindstamp. Fairey has also re-released images in new editions (e.g. anniversary versions) — the year in the pencil date tells them apart. Banksy: "Pictures on Walls" fakes with printed stamps; without Pest Control nothing is sellable. KAWS: the most faked; wrong font sizes on edition text, washed colours, uneven cross shapes (Highsnobiety). Mr. Brainwash: so many "signed" copies that value is thin regardless. Rule: without a COA or an archive match, treat as decorative.

## Market
Obey standard editions: dozens sell per week on eBay, sell-through moderate (~40%), 1-3 weeks at fair price; thousands of near-identical copies compete, so condition and framing matter little and price discovery is efficient. Early editions and large formats: sell in days, often via Heritage/Bonhams/Phillips online prints sales. Banksy/KAWS: auction and MyArtBroker-type brokers; eBay is a fraud zone. Buyers: 25-45 collectors, design-conscious decorators, political-poster collectors (Hope). Seasonality: Obey Thursday drops flood the market weekly; auction prints sales cluster spring and autumn. Trend: Obey standard editions have been flat-to-down since 2021 as edition sizes rose; early work steady.

## What sellers usually miss
The year and edition size (they say "signed & numbered Obey" and never the denominator or date); whether it is a letterpress/collab/AP; the sheet size; and that an unsigned open-edition "Hope" is not the signed edition. Framed prints are listed as "art" with no close-up of the signature or stamp. Conversely, sellers overvalue: a 2021 ed. 550 is listed at retail-plus ($200-400) because they paid $65 plus framing.

## Shipping / handling
Unframed: rolled in a 4-inch heavy tube with glassine, ~2 lb, $15-25 USPS; or flat between foam board for 18×24 ($20-30). Framed 18×24 with glass: 8-12 lb, $40-70 and high breakage risk — always unframe before shipping (and photograph the back when you do). Fragility medium; creases are permanent. Fits a passenger seat framed or rolled.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: artist – print title – year – signed & numbered x/edition – size – screen print – Obey Giant. State: edition size, year, sheet size and image size, paper, blindstamp/ink stamp present, signature in pencil, condition in full (fading, pinholes, tape, mat burn), framed or unframed, provenance (bought from obeygiant.com drop, gallery receipt). Photograph: whole sheet, signature and fraction close-up, blindstamp in raking light, back of sheet, corners. ## Buy rule
Buy when: a Fairey print dated ≤ 2008 or with edition ≤ 100, or a 24×36/HPM, is priced ≤ $200 with signature, fraction and stamp visible in photos and a clean full sheet, est. resale ≥ $350 (profit ≥ max($50, price) after 13% and ~$20 shipping each way). Standard 2012-25 ed. 450-550 prints only at ≤ $60 (resale $150-250). Pass when: the print is priced at or above $150 for a standard edition, any trimmed/dry-mounted/faded sheet, any Banksy/KAWS/Invader (needs professional authentication and exceeds $200), anything the archive cannot match, or a seller who won't photograph the signature and stamp out of the frame. Must fit a passenger seat (all do).
