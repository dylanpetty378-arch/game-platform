## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Peaches Records & Tapes** (1970s–80s chain) crates: a pine slat crate with a lithographed "Peaches" fruit label on the end. Two sizes: the LP crate, ~13×13×12" (the one people want), and the small cassette crate, ~8×12×6".
- **Napa Valley Box Co.** crates: 1980s–90s cassette/CD racks, common.
- **Brewery crates**: stencilled or branded ends, dovetail or nailed corners. Pre-1920 breweries (U.S. Brewing Co. Chicago, Schlitz, Pabst) bring the most. 1950s–70s Coke/Pepsi crates are $15–30.

Peaches background (Wikipedia): founded by Tom Heiman (the article gives both 1963 and 1975; the first store, on Hollywood Boulevard in Los Angeles, opened in 1975). At its peak 50 stores in 22 cities with 2,000+ employees, some stores up to 15,000 sq ft; records were sold out of wooden crates bearing the colourful fruit-crate-style logo. Chapter 11 in June 1981 (~$20 million debt, 35 stores); a New Orleans store survives under the name. So genuine store crates date roughly 1975–early 1980s, and they were store fixtures that customers later bought or took home.

Brewery and soda crates: wooden bottle cases with an internal grid of slats for the bottles, cut hand-holds (often lined with metal), sometimes a hinged lid; stencilled "Property of …", city/state, bottle count ("2 dozen small") and union marks (e.g. Berghoff Brewing Co., Fort Wayne, 21 × 14 × 12"). Fruit crates: open slat boxes with a paper label pasted on the end; the label is the collectible.

How to tell from look-alikes: modern "Peaches-style" decor crates and craft-store crates with printed transfers; new "vintage-look" brewery crates with laser-crisp stencils; generic orchard crates with no label.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Peaches**: LP size is the money ($60–100); cassette size is a third of the value ($25–45). Prices are regional, higher on the West Coast and in Atlanta.
- **Napa Valley Box Co.**: worthless to low ($8–20).
- **Pre-Prohibition brewery crates**: $75–150, more for rare or defunct breweries.
- **1950s–60s beer crates**: $25–45.
- **Soda crates (Coke/Pepsi 1950s–70s)**: $15–30. Note the minor overlap with the junk tier's "1970s soda crates" at $8–20.
- **Fruit-label crates**: covered by the title but not priced in research. Collectors often value the paper labels more than the crate (general knowledge, verify). Reproduction citrus labels exist (UC Riverside catalogues a "Reproductions" series), so the label must be checked.

Dating brewery crates by name: breweries changed names often, and a stencilled company name can be matched to the years it was used. Example: "Berghoff Brewing Co." was the name 1898–1908; the brewery was renamed in 1908, closed 1918 for Prohibition and reopened in 1933 (MSU Surplus listing). Look the brewery name up in a breweriana reference before calling a crate pre-Prohibition. A city with no state, a brewery name that ended before 1920, or Prohibition-era closure dates are the evidence; the seller's word is not.

### Quick ID checklist
- Measure it: LP crate ~13×13×12" vs cassette ~8×12×6" (Peaches) — the size decides the value.
- Peaches label: printed fruit-crate-style logo on the end, faded and worn consistently with the wood, not a bright new transfer.
- Fasteners: small wire nails or old staples consistent with age vs modern brad-nailer staples and glue on new crates.
- Wood: grey/oxidised, dirty end grain and handle wear vs fresh blond pine.
- Brewery crate: stencilled name, city/state, bottle count, union mark; internal bottle grid; hand-holds worn smooth.
- Look up the brewery name's years before accepting "pre-Prohibition".
- Fruit crates: label printed by lithography with rich solid colour (original) vs visible dot screen and glossy stock (reproduction).
- Corners: dovetail/finger joints or metal corner straps on older crates; check for broken slats.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Peaches crates are thin pine slats with a printed paper or litho label; the label condition drives value.
- Old brewery crates have dovetailed or box-jointed corners, branded (burned) or stencilled lettering, square nails or early wire nails, and an aged patina (general knowledge, verify).
- New reproductions have crisp stencils on fresh wood and modern staples.
- Old stencilling has soft, slightly bled edges and wear where hands and other crates rubbed; repainted lettering sits on top of grime and looks too bright.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Value loss: torn or faded labels, broken slats, water damage and mould, and repainted lettering. Ask for close-ups of the label/branding, the corners and the bottom. Also check missing internal bottle dividers and missing lids on brewery crates, and woodworm holes (fresh holes with powder = active).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Repro Peaches crates and labels have been made for collectors and fans. Decorative "vintage-style" brewery crates are sold new at home stores (general knowledge, verify). Check for staples, fresh wood and too-perfect printing. Peaches merchandise (stickers, apparel) with the logo is sold today, so a logo alone does not prove a store crate. Reproduction fruit labels exist.

## Market
Record collectors, nostalgia buyers and decorators buy them. Peaches is a regional nostalgia item. Sales are moderate on eBay and Etsy and local pickup works well. Pre-Prohibition brewery crates sell to breweriana collectors. No strong seasonality. Peaches nostalgia is strongest in its former markets (LA, Atlanta, New Orleans, Midwest cities where it had stores).

## What sellers usually miss
Size. Sellers say "Peaches crates" without saying cassette or LP, and the small ones are a third of the value. Label condition (full colour, not torn) matters. On brewery crates, the brewery name's date range (a name used only before 1920) is what makes it pre-Prohibition.

## Shipping / handling
LP crate ~4 lb in a 14" box, $15–20 to ship; cassette crates 1.5 lb, $10. Resell on eBay or Etsy. Brewery crates are heavier: an 18" crate is too heavy for cheap shipping.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: "Original Peaches Records & Tapes LP Record Crate 1970s Store Display". State the exact dimensions, which size (LP vs cassette), label condition and wood condition. Photograph the label close up and every side.

### What to tell the buyer
Give exact outside dimensions and state LP or cassette size. Describe the label (complete, faded, torn) and the wood (broken or loose slats, stains, nails). For Peaches, say it is a store crate from the Peaches Records & Tapes chain (stores 1975–1981, bankrupt 1981) only if it matches original construction; do not claim a specific store. For brewery crates, quote every stencilled word exactly (name, city, bottle count, union mark), give the years that company name was in use with the source, and say whether dividers and lid are present.
