Summary: Labels decide which decade a garment or textile comes from, and the decade sets the price. Care-tag 1980s–90s clothing sells for $15–60. The same item from before 1971 (no care tag, union label, metal Talon zip, Big E, single stitch, early Pendleton label) sells for $80–500, and grails sell for $500–5,000+. Small textiles follow the same pattern: Pendleton Beaver State blankets have a $169 sold median, and Navajo Two Grey Hills rugs on trading-post tags sell for $1,425 (May 2026 auction).
Buy rule (short): Read the labels and hardware before the title. Pay ≤ ~1/3 of the decade-tier sold median, only with label, zipper and hem photos. Pass on anything where a care tag, a modern RN or a reissue label contradicts the "vintage" claim. Never buy Hermès, Chanel or other authentication-only labels.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
This is a reading guide, not an item type. It covers every label, stamp and construction detail that turns "old jacket", "vintage shirt", "wool blanket" or "small rug" into a dated, priced piece. Use it alongside the type entry for the object: vintage-denim, vintage-wool-coats, pendleton-blankets, filson-clothing, vintage-quilts, tribal-area-rugs, wall-tapestries, needlepoint-rugs, tokusatsu-and-anime-vintage-tees, vintage-ski-apparel, leather-motorcycle-jackets, designer-silk-scarves.

**Where the marks sit**
| Object | Where to look | What the listing photo usually shows |
|---|---|---|
| Women's dresses, coats, skirts | ILGWU label sewn into a side seam, under the main label or at the lining hem; RN/WPL on the main label or a small white tag | a 1–2 in. woven or printed tag, often cropped out or blurry |
| Men's suits, tailored coats | ACWA label inside the breast pocket or on the inside-pocket facing | a small woven label, often mistaken for a size tag |
| Any garment | RN or WPL number on the brand label, or on the fibre-content tag; care label in a side seam or at the neck | "RN 12345" in small type |
| Jackets, trousers, dresses | the zipper pull and slider, plus the stop box at the bottom | a dull brass or aluminium pull with a stamped name |
| Jeans | red tab (back right pocket), top-button back, back-pocket stitching, cuff outseam, leather/paper patch | a close-up of the waistband and patch; the button back is almost never shown |
| T-shirts | neck tag, and the sleeve and bottom hems | the front print only; ask for the tag and hems |
| Blankets | woven label in a corner; HBC point lines at one edge | a folded blanket with the label hidden |
| Rugs | trading-post tag (paper or card) sewn or pinned on a corner; woven date or signature cartouche at one end; knot grid on the back | the face only; ask for the back and the tag |
| Tapestries | bolduc (cloth certificate label) sewn on the back, lower corner | rarely photographed |

**Words sellers use when they missed the label:** "old coat", "grandma's dress", "vintage jacket", "old Levi's", "work pants", "old t-shirt", "band shirt", "wool blanket", "Indian blanket", "camp blanket", "southwest rug", "small throw rug", "wall hanging", "no tags", "tag cut", "unknown age".

## Makers, brands and variants

### Dating labels (any garment)

**ACWA (Amalgamated Clothing Workers of America): men's suits, sport coats, overcoats, shirts**
- The union was founded in 1914 (general knowledge, verify). The label was updated in **1939, when "Made in U.S.A." was added along the side** (Collector's Companion).
- In 1976 ACWA merged into ACTWU, and in 1995 ACTWU merged into UNITE (general knowledge, verify). An **ACWA label therefore means before 1976**, an ACTWU label means 1976–95, and a UNITE label means 1995 or later.
- Look-alike: the United Garment Workers (UGW) label on 1900s–50s workwear (overalls, chore coats). It is older and adds value on workwear (general knowledge, verify).

**RN and WPL numbers (FTC registration numbers)**
- **WPL** (Wool Products Labeling Act): issued **1941–1959**, numbers **WPL 00101–13669**. A WPL number on a wool garment means it was made 1941–early 1960s, and makers used up old label stock for a few years after 1959 (Collector's Companion).
- **RN**: first issued from **1952** under the Fur Products Labeling Act, numbers **RN 00101–04086** (Collector's Companion). In 1959, when the Textile Fiber Products Identification Act was added, numbering **restarted at RN 13670**, continuing from the WPL sequence. It had reached about RN 165260 by July 2021.
- An RN gives the **earliest possible** date, not the garment's date, because a firm keeps its RN for decades. Use it to rule eras out, never to prove age (VFG thread; Collector's Companion).
- Estimate: year ≈ 1959 + (RN − 13670) ÷ 2,189 (VFG formula, accurate to about 1990). Numbers were issued faster after that: 165260 by 2021 works out to about 2,445 per year on average since 1959, and the Collector's Companion uses about 2,635 per year.

**Care labels:** the FTC Care Labeling Rule was adopted in **1971 and took effect in July 1972** (general knowledge, verify). A sewn care label ("Machine wash warm, tumble dry") means 1971–72 or later. **No care label and no sign of one being cut out** means the piece is probably earlier, and that is what moves a 501, a Pendleton or a dress into the pre-1971 tier (Underpriced: Pendleton care labels start in 1971). Care **symbols** in place of words point to **1997 or later** in the US (general knowledge, verify). Check for a cut-tag stub before believing a "no care tag" claim.

**"Made in USA" placement:** amendments to the Textile Act required a "Made in U.S.A." label on domestic garments from **1985** (Kheel Center). The rule also put the origin label at the **inside centre of the neck** on garments with a neck (general knowledge, verify). So "Made in USA" on the neck label generally means 1985 or later. Pre-1985 domestic pieces often say nothing, or carry it only on the union label (ILGWU 1974–95) or on the side of an ACWA label (1939 on). "Made in USA" is a premium on 1985–2000s Levi's, Pendleton and band tees, but it is not proof of pre-1980 age.

A metal zip shows only that a garment could be old. A **Talon with a square pull and the name on the stop box**, or a **Conmar or Crown**, moves it to the 1940s–50s tier. Check that the zip is original: look for matching thread and no re-stitching along the tape.

**Sanforized:** Sanford Cluett's pre-shrinking process for Cluett, Peabody, patented c.1930 (general knowledge, verify). A "SANFORIZED" tag means the 1930s or later and is common through the 1970s. Hand-sewn or chain-stitched woven "Sanforized" labels on workwear and chambray shirts point to the 1940s–60s, and "Sanforized-Plus" points to the 1950s or later (general knowledge, verify). It has little value on its own, but it is a strong supporting clue next to a union label or a Talon zip.

### Designer (authentication items: note them and avoid)
- **Hermès scarves:** "© Hermès" and usually the **designer's signature** (Ledoux, Dallet, Grygkar, d'Origny) printed in the design, a hand-rolled hem rolled toward the printed face, and a "Hermès Paris" care tag. Fakes copy all of these. **Excluded: needs authentication** (see designer-silk-scarves; Terapeak median for a Hermès carré is $215).
- **Chanel:** woven labels, serial stickers and hologram cards on bags; "CHANEL" labels on clothing. **Excluded: needs authentication** (designer-handbags).
- The same rule applies to Louis Vuitton, Gucci and Burberry. Note them in the dossier as excluded, and do not buy them, however cheap.

### Golden-egg tells
- **ILGWU label reading "AFL" without "CIO"** on a coat or dress listed as "old coat" means pre-1956. With a good maker (Lilli Ann, Forstmann) that is the $400–1,500 tier against $25–150 for a 1974–95 red-white-blue label piece, so about 3–10× (vintage-wool-coats; Kheel Center). Photo tell: small woven union tag, "AFL", no tricolour.
- **Levi's Big E with single digits on the button back and no care tag**: an original pre-1971 pair, $500–2,000 against a $56 median 501, so about 10–35× (vintage-denim). Photo tell: a capital E on the tab, a single-digit button number, chain-stitch hem roping.
- **WWII painted arcuate** on Levi's (1942–46): $2,000–20,000+ (general knowledge, verify). Tell: the pocket "wings" are paint, not thread, often worn faint.
- **Pendleton board shirt with "100% Virgin Wool, Made in USA" and no care tag**: $150–300 against $20–80 for a standard wool shirt, so about 3–8× (Underpriced). Tell: a loop on the back of the collar and the label without care text.
- **Screen Stars "Funky A" (1981–83), or an early-1980s tag with a dated band print and single-stitch hems**: genuine 1980s tour shirt, $100–1,000+ against $10–30 for a reprint (general knowledge, verify). Tell: the stylised "A" on the tag plus "© 198x" on the print.
- **Talon zip with a square pull and "TALON" down the stop box** on a jacket titled "old jacket": late-1940s piece. On leather and work jackets this is the difference between $40 and $150–600 (leather-motorcycle-jackets; general knowledge, verify).
- **Navajo rug with a trading-post tag** sold as a "southwest throw rug": $500–2,000+ against $20–80, so about 10–30×. Tell: a paper tag with a post name, no cut fringe, side cords.
- **Brown-label North Face or Seattle-label Eddie Bauer down**, titled "puffer jacket": $100–300 against $20–40 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Aubusson bolduc** on the back of a "French wall hanging": the bolduc is a certificate, so the piece is a signed atelier tapestry and potentially $1,500+ (general knowledge, verify). Tell: a white cloth label with a signature and an edition number.
- **Woven Hijri date and workshop signature** on a small Persian rug sold as an "oriental mat": a real signed Tabriz or Kashan rug at 2×3 to 3×5 can be $300–2,000 (general knowledge, verify). Tell: Persian numerals in a box at one end, plus a high knot count on the back.

### Quick ID checklist
- Ask for the **whole label set**: main label, fibre tag, union label, care tag (or its stub), the zip pull and stop box, the hems, and the button back on jeans.
- No care tag and no cut stub means pre-1971–72, or the label was removed. A union label, a WPL number, an RN below ~40000, a metal Talon, Conmar or Crown zip, or single-stitch hems all move the date earlier. Use two independent clues before paying the old-tier price.
- Read the RN or WPL number and estimate with the table. Treat it as the earliest possible date only.
- ILGWU: "AFL" only = before 1956; "AFL-CIO" = 1955 on; tricolour with "Made in USA" = 1974–95. ACWA = before 1976.
- A neck-label "Made in USA" generally means 1985 or later. Care symbols mean 1997 or later.
- Jeans: tab (E or e), button number (single digit vs 555), cuff (redline), back pockets (single stitch), patch (leather vs paper).
- Tees: tag, hems and the copyright line on the print.
- Blankets: corner label photo; point lines on HBC; jacquard (reversed pattern on the back) vs printed.
- Rugs: the back (knot grid), the ends (fringe from the warp vs sewn on), any tag or woven cartouche, and the size against the passenger-seat rule.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Construction dates the label too.** Before about 1960: hand-finished seams, pinked or bound seam allowances, metal zips set in a side seam (dresses before c.1960 rarely have back zips; general knowledge, verify), and bound buttonholes. After 1970: overlocked (serged) seams, nylon coil zips and printed (not woven) labels.
- **Woven vs printed labels:** woven labels with a 3D texture are older; printed satin or polyester labels are 1970s and later (general knowledge, verify).
- **Fibre tags:** fibre content became mandatory in 1960 (Textile Fiber Products Identification Act; general knowledge, verify). "Dacron", "Orlon", "Acrilan" and "Ban-Lon" trade names point to the 1950s–70s.
- **Denim:** selvedge vs overlocked outseam; chain-stitch hem roping; hidden rivets.
- **Blankets and rugs:** virgin wool, a jacquard pattern that reverses on the back, felt binding on trade blankets. In rugs: handspun wool with natural-dye abrash (colour banding) vs uniform commercial yarn, and a wool foundation on tribal pieces vs a cotton foundation on city or commercial ones (tribal-area-rugs).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Moth holes cut value by 50–70% on Pendleton wool (Underpriced), so ask for photos held up to a window. Collectors accept honest wear on Big E denim and on 1980s tees (fading, cracked prints), but not stains on light colours, underarm yellowing, pit holes on tees, dry rot on rubber-backed patches, or shattered silk linings on coats. A **replaced zip** or a **removed or swapped label** destroys the dating evidence. Check for different thread around the label or zip. On rugs: moth-eaten pile, worn-through foundation, rewoven areas (look at the back: a patch of different knots), painted-in worn spots (felt-tip touch-ups), dry rot (the rug cracks when folded) and end loss. On quilts: fabric shatter (silk crazy quilts), fading and stains; cutter quilts sell only as fabric.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Levi's Vintage Clothing (LVC) and Japanese repro brands** copy Big E tabs, selvedge, arcuates and button numbers. **A 555 Valencia button with a Big E tab is a 1990s–2000s reissue**, and **a Big E with a care tag** is LVC or a fake: the two never coexist on an original (vintage-denim). Most eBay "Big E" asks are LVC (active median $160).
- **Fake union labels and label swaps:** designer or union labels moved into a plain coat. Look for stitching that differs from the other interior stitching (vintage-wool-coats).
- **Reprint tees on old blanks**, tag swaps, and modern single-stitch blanks. Check that the print's copyright year and the tag era agree, and look for a crackle-free modern plastisol print.
- **Pendleton heritage reissues** (Chief Joseph, Harding, Glacier Park) carry modern labels with care text and RN numbers. **HBC licensed and modern versions** exist; the label era tells them apart (pendleton-blankets).
- **Navajo look-alikes:** Mexican Zapotec (Teotitlán) "Navajo-style" rugs have **cut and knotted warp fringe at both ends** and a different weave feel. Fake trading-post tags exist, so the construction must agree with the tag.
- **"Aubusson" jacquards and needlepoint:** machine-woven Belgian or French jacquards carry printed "Made in Belgium" or brand labels, not a bolduc, and no hand-woven slits (wall-tapestries, needlepoint-rugs).
- **Persian dates and signatures** can be altered or copied onto newer rugs (PCG Australia). Pakistani Bokharas and Indian copies are sold as "Persian" (tribal-area-rugs). Chinese silk "Hereke" fakes exist (general knowledge, verify).
- **Hermès and Chanel:** counterfeits are common and good. They stay excluded.

## Market
Vintage clothing is very liquid on eBay, Depop, Grailed and Etsy. Dated denim, tees and outdoor pieces sell in days to weeks, and the **label photo is what sells**. Buyers search by tag ("Screen Stars", "single stitch", "Big E", "brown label", "union made"). Coats and wool sell best from September to January. Blankets sell year-round to decorators and collectors, with a peak in autumn and winter. Navajo weavings sell best at specialist auctions (Hubbell, Santa Fe) and to dealers, and eBay discounts them. Small rugs sell steadily, and rug buyers want back photos and knot counts. Trend: 1980s–90s outdoor (Patagonia, TNF) and band tees are hot and well known to pickers, while union labels, RN numbers and zipper stamps are still widely unread by casual sellers.

## What sellers usually miss
- Union labels and WPL/RN numbers. These tags are tiny, sewn in a side seam, and cropped out of photos.
- That **no care tag** is the clue.
- The zip pull stamp (Talon, Conmar, Crown) and the stop box.
- The jeans button back, the cuff selvedge and the tab's capital E.
- The tee's tag and hems; owners photograph only the print.
- The corner label on a folded blanket, and the point lines.
- The paper trading-post tag on a rug ("old tag attached").
- The bolduc on the back of a tapestry, and woven dates on rugs (they look like "a pattern").
- The reverse error: sellers over-ask for LVC and reissue "Big E", for heritage-reissue Pendletons, and for single-stitch modern blanks.

## Shipping / handling
Clothing: poly mailer, 1–3 lb, $6–12. Coats and blankets: box, 3–6 lb, $12–20. Small rugs (≤ 4×6): rolled (never folded tight) in a tube or poly wrap, 5–15 lb, $15–35. Quilts: fold with tissue, 3–6 lb. Fragility is low; the risks are moths, odour and damp. Bag wool with cedar and air it out before photographing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the dating evidence in the listing: "ILGWU AFL label (pre-1955)", "no care tag", "WPL 0xxxx", "Talon zipper, 1950s", "single-stitch hems, Screen Stars 1983 tag, © 1985 print", "Big E, single-digit button '6', redline selvedge". Give measurements flat (pit-to-pit, length, shoulders, sleeves; waist and inseam measured, not tagged). For blankets: size in inches, label photo, points. For rugs: size, knots per square inch, foundation, any tag or woven date with its conversion, and back photos. Photograph every label and stamp square-on in good light. Title keywords: decade, brand, the key tell (Big E, Union Made, Single Stitch, Brown Label, Beaver State, 6 Point, Two Grey Hills, Trading Post Tag), size and "Made in USA".
### What to tell the buyer
"Dated by its labels: [ILGWU 'AFL' union label / WPL number / RN xxxxx], no care label (pre-1972), original [Talon] zipper. Measurements are taken flat. All labels are pictured."

## Buy rule
Buy when: two independent dating clues put the piece in a tier, the price is ≤ ~1/3 of that tier's sold median, and the profit is ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Pass when: the label or zip photos are missing and the seller won't send them; a care tag, a 555 button or an LVC label contradicts the "vintage" claim; there are moth holes, pit stains or dry rot; the item is a rug or textile too big to roll into a passenger seat; or the label is Hermès, Chanel, LV or another authentication-only brand (always pass).
