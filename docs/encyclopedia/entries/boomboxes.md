## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Portable radio/cassette (and later CD) players. The collectable era is about 1979–1987: wide silver or black metal-look fronts, chrome trim, big woofers with separate tweeters, VU meters, dual cassette decks, detachable speakers on some, 10–20 D-cell batteries. 1990s units are rounded grey/black plastic with top-loading CD, LCD displays, "Hyper-Bass", "Full Logic Control" — low collector interest.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **JVC**: RC-M90 (top grail), RC-M70, RC-M60, RC-550 "Biphonic"; 1990s PC-X series (PC-X100/X102/X200 detachable-speaker "portable component systems") are utilitarian, modest value.
- **Sharp**: GF-777, GF-9000, GF-575, Searcher series.
- **Conion/Clairtone C-100F, Lasonic TRC-931/920, Crown SZ-5100, Panasonic RX-7700 / RX-5500, Hitachi TRK-W5, Sanyo M-X920, Aiwa CS-880, Marantz PMS-7000, Sony CFS-W series**.
- **Budget brands (GPX, Emerson, Soundesign, Lloyds, Yorx)**: low.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal grills, heavy weight, multiple drivers, mechanical piano-key cassette controls, Made in Japan = better. Plastic, soft-touch logic buttons, "Made in Malaysia/China" = 1990s.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken cassette belts (very common — "radio works, tape doesn't"), dirty pots, dead CD lasers, battery-acid damage in battery compartment, cracked antenna, missing battery doors, foam speaker surround rot.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern retro-styled reissues (e.g., Lasonic i931, 2010s) and retro-look Chinese units — check model numbers, country of manufacture and USB/Bluetooth inputs.

## Market
80s grails sell fast to collectors; 1990s CD units slow, buyers want "fully working" with remote.

## What sellers usually miss
Owners price big 80s boomboxes like 90s CD units in garages; detachable speakers and remotes add value.

## Shipping / handling
10–25 lb, bulky; $30–60, needs heavy double-boxing; CD trays and tape doors break in transit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Video of each function (tape play/record, radio bands, CD), batteries/AC, cosmetic flaws, remote included.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$40 ship to buyer − ~$35 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Rule out untested electronics and anything needing belt replacement. A 1990s CD boombox never clears; an 80s big model needs resale ≥ ~$300 at ≤ $100 with seller video.
