## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cylindrical or square tin-plate containers for cocoa powder or chocolates, with lithographed (printed-on-metal) or paper labels. Baker's: La Belle Chocolatière (woman carrying a tray) on dark-blue/maroon, "Walter Baker & Co. Ltd., Dorchester, Mass., Established 1780". Droste: Dutch nurse carrying a tray with the same box on it (the "Droste effect"), Haarlem, Holland. Huyler's: New York candy company, cocoa tins with "Huyler's" script. Size matters: ½ lb, ¼ lb and tiny sample (1–2 in) tins.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Walter Baker & Co.** (Dorchester MA, est. 1780; La Belle Chocolatière trademark adopted 1883). Breakfast Cocoa tins 1890s–1950s; tiny "sample" tins (given away at fairs, e.g. 1893 Columbian Expo era) are the best. Later General Foods (1927+) tins say "General Foods". Paper-label pre-1900 rare.
- **Droste** (Haarlem, founded 1863; nurse image from ~1904). Still makes tins today – most Droste tins found are 1950s–present, $5–20. Early pre-WWII tins with Dutch text and older nurse styling $20–60.
- **Huyler's** (NYC, 1876–1920s peak; later a small brand): cocoa and candy tins $20–60.
- **Van Houten** (Dutch), **Bensdorp** (Dutch/Austrian), **Runkel Brothers**, **Lowney's** (Boston), **Rockwood**, **Wilbur's**, **Hershey's** cocoa (1920s–60s Hershey cocoa tins $10–40), **Ghirardelli** (San Francisco, ground chocolate tins $15–50), **Nestlé**, **Cadbury**, **Fry's**, **Rowntree** (UK).
- Confection: Whitman's Sampler tins, Schrafft's, Loft, Page & Shaw, candy pails (figural candy pails are the high end, $100–1000+).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old: tin-plate with lithography, hand-soldered seams on very early cans, slip lids or hinged lids, slight overall patina, no barcode, address with no ZIP code, "Est. 1780" and "Dorchester, Mass." 
- New: barcodes, ZIP codes, "Made in China", crisp bright colours, rolled machine seams, nutrition panel. Baker's and Droste both issued nostalgia reproduction tins in the 1980s–2000s.
- Sample tins: under 2 in high; much rarer than full size.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Grading is everything (TCCA scale 1–10). Rust, dents, scratches through the litho, fading, missing lids kill value. Repainting/touch-up exists on rare tins. Full-size common tins in grade 6–7 are $10–25.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Common: 1980s–90s Baker's nostalgia tins, Droste current production, and Chinese tin-litho "vintage style" tins sold at craft stores. Tells: barcodes, perfect litho, lightweight thin metal, "replica" stamped in small print.

## Market
Mature, slow, collector-driven; older collectors aging out. Dozens of cocoa tins sell per month on eBay, mostly under $30. Sample tins and rare brands sell in 1–3 weeks; commons sit for months.

## What sellers usually miss
- Tiny sample tins get thrown in a "lot of old tins" for $10–20.
- Paper-label Baker's (pre-1900) look shabby and get under-priced.
- Most sellers over-price modern Droste and nostalgia Baker's as "antique".

## Shipping / handling
Light (4–12 oz). Pack so lids don't pop and corners don't dent: small box, bubble wrap. $5–9 Ground Advantage.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** brand (Walter Baker / Droste / Huyler's / Van Houten / Lowney's) + "cocoa tin" + size (sample / 1/2 lb / 1 lb) + litho/paper label + era. Junk: "antique advertising", "rare", "country store" alone. `Walter Baker & Co Breakfast Cocoa Sample Tin 1900s La Belle Chocolatiere`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (TCCA collectors search by brand); lots of 1950s+ commons locally or bundled.
- **Price position:** at the sold median ($10–30 common; $40–120 sample/pre-1920), accept −15%, floor 75%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** front panel straight on; each side; lid on and off; interior; base with maker/address; every dent and rust spot close up; next to a ruler.
- **State:** brand, size, approximate date from address/litho, lid present, contents empty, no barcode, dents/rust/scratches by photo number, repro or reissue if so.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox, 4–12 oz, small box with bubble so the lid can't pop and corners don't dent, $5–9 Ground Advantage.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Brand] [size] cocoa tin, [era] from the [address/trademark], lithographed not paper-labelled, lid intact; the 1950s commons go for [$] and clean pre-1920 [sample] tins for [$]."

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$7 to buyer − ~$7 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A $100 resale tin bought at $15 makes ~$58 – so only true sample tins/rare brands at ≤ $15–25 qualify. Ordinary cocoa tins: pass. Size: pocket/shoebox, fine.
