## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Wooden track-compatible trains (peg-and-hole "jigsaw" track, ~1.5 in. wide, grooved both sides).
- **Thomas Wooden Railway (TWR):** Learning Curve (Chicago, founded 1992 by John W. Lee; Thomas line developed by Roy Wilson; made Thomas toys 1993–2012) — wooden bodies with magnets, painted faces. RC2 (2002/03 onward) owned Learning Curve; Mattel/Fisher-Price took over in 2013 and changed to a different face style and some plastic parts; later "Thomas Wood" (2017–) and a 2022 relaunch range (Thomas wiki; general knowledge for design changes, verify).
- **Copyright stamps** on the underside help date Thomas pieces: "©Britt Allcroft" (earliest), "©Gullane", "©HIT", then Mattel/Fisher-Price (general knowledge, verify).
- **BRIO** (Sweden; "BRIO" stamped on wheels/underside): toys since 1907, miniature railway sets from 1957; red wheels from the 1950s to the mid-2000s; early sets used metal hook-and-eye couplers, "floating" flat-disk magnetic couplers by 1964, and fixed magnetic couplers held by round-head tacks after a 1974 patent (Old Wood Toys; woodenrailway.info).
- KidKraft, Melissa & Doug, IKEA, Imaginarium are low value.
- Look-alikes: unbranded Chinese "compatible" trains and track (lighter wood, fuzzier paint, no stamps).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Learning Curve / RC2 (Thomas Wooden Railway)** 1993–2012: the collectible era. 2007 lead-paint recalls covered over 1.7 million Wooden Railway items made Jan 2005–May 2007 (June 2007), plus a smaller September recall (Thomas wiki) — check CPSC lists for specific pieces; recalled items are unsellable.
- **Fisher-Price Thomas Wood (2017–)** and 2013–16 Fisher-Price Wooden Railway: lower collector interest.
- **BRIO:** vintage Swedish-made pieces (natural wood bodies with painted details, red wheels, hook couplers) are the collectible end; the 1964 Harbour Set (31405/40) had set-exclusive pieces such as small lighthouses and wharf track (Old Wood Toys).
- KidKraft, Melissa & Doug, Imaginarium, Chuggington wooden, IKEA Lillabo — user-value only.

### Quick ID checklist
- Underside stamps: "Learning Curve", "©Britt Allcroft/Gullane/HIT", "RC2", "Fisher-Price/Mattel", "BRIO Sweden".
- Faces: painted wooden faces (Learning Curve) vs printed/plastic-faced later pieces.
- Magnets: Learning Curve round magnets (old entry notes flat-magnet early pieces; verify) vs BRIO tack-held magnets vs hook-and-eye (early BRIO).
- Wheels: BRIO red wheels; Learning Curve metal axles.
- Character list: look for retired or short-run characters (Lady, Spencer, early Diesel 10, Sidney, limited "Fat Controller's" sets).
- Count pieces and engines; heavy track-only lots are low value per lb.
- Check for chewed paint on faces and missing magnets.
- Any boxes: boxed retired engines sell far higher than loose (general knowledge, verify).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Retired Learning Curve engines (Lady, Spencer, early Diesel 10, Sidney 2011 LC98138)** — $20–150 each vs the ~$15 Terapeak median for mixed Thomas/Brio/KidKraft singles and small lots — Learning Curve stamp, painted face, character name; sellers list them "RARE".
- **Learning Curve limited/"Fat Controller's" sets** — upper end of $20–150+ — set packaging or matching special-paint pieces.
- **BRIO World Cargo Railway Deluxe Set (complete)** — $102 sold (May 2026) vs $30 track lots (~3×) — BRIO stamps, set-specific pieces.
- **Vintage 1960s–70s Swedish BRIO with hook-and-eye or floating-magnet couplers** — $50–150 sets vs generic $20–40 — natural wood bodies, red wheels, metal hook couplers, "BRIO Sweden".
- **BRIO Harbour Set (1964) exclusive pieces (lighthouses, wharf track)** — premium within vintage BRIO (verify) — small lighthouses, wharf-shaped track.
- **Large 100+ piece lot with many named engines** — $80–200 vs $20–40 — pile of track plus 15+ painted-face engines.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Solid wood bodies, metal axles; dates moulded "©Gullane", "©HIT" on bottom help date Thomas pieces.
- BRIO: glossy multi-layer lacquer; early pieces had natural finish with only wheels/details painted.
- Knock-offs: soft pale wood, fuzzy printed faces, loose wheels, no copyright stamps.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Paint chips (big value hit on faces), chewing, missing magnets.
- Repainted faces kill collector value.
- Recalled pieces (Jan 2005–May 2007 production) must be checked against CPSC lists.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Chinese knock-offs ("Thomas" wooden fakes), re-releases of popular engines.
- Fisher-Price-era re-releases of Learning Curve characters look similar; check stamps.

## Market
- Steady; Q4 peaks.
- Buyers: parents (lots, track), adult Learning Curve collectors (specific engines), vintage-toy/Scandinavian design buyers (old BRIO).

## What sellers usually miss
- Retired Learning Curve characters mixed in bins.
- Vintage BRIO with hook couplers; underside copyright stamps.
