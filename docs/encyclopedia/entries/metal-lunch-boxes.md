# Metal lunch boxes (Aladdin, Thermos/King-Seeley, Universal, ADCO, Ohio Art, Okay Industries), 1950–1985
Updated: 2026-10-12 · upgraded 2026-10-13 · Sources: eBay listing 176418919499 (1959 Aladdin Gunsmoke, ask $1,150 OBO), appraisily.com lunch box value guide (auction lots of 2–4 boxes $300–425, mint = 2–3x good), history.com "nine most collectible lunch boxes", tastingtable value lists, general collector knowledge (Scott Bruce price guide tiers).
Slug: metal-lunch-boxes · Aliases: vintage lunch box, lunchbox, lunch pail, metal lunchbox with thermos, dome lunch box, tin lunch box
Summary: 1975–85 flat character boxes $20–60; 1960s–early 70s TV/Western boxes $60–175; 1950s–60s key titles $200–900 (Jetsons dome/Green Hornet $500–1,500 excellent); rare titles (1954 Superman, 1962 Astronaut dome) $1,500–10,000.
Buy rule (short): Buy 1950s–60s boxes with intact graphics under $75, dome-tops under $50, or key titles under 25% of sold range; pass 1975+ flat boxes over $15 and rusted boxes.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Steel box ~8x7x4 in, lithographed on all sides, wire-and-plastic handle, two clasps; dome-tops (1950s–60s) look like a loaf of bread. Maker is embossed on the side or bottom: **Aladdin Industries** (Nashville), **American Thermos / King-Seeley Thermos (KST)**, **Universal (Landers, Frary & Clark)**, **ADCO Liberty**, **Ohio Art**, **Okay Industries**. Matching steel-and-glass thermos (pre-1970s) or plastic thermos (1970s+) roughly doubles value. Sellers' titles usually just say "vintage lunch box" + character name.

Key year tells: **Gunsmoke** – 1959 Aladdin (Matt Dillon standing, Dodge City street scene, embossed) is the valuable one; 1962 "double-L" and 1972/73 (Marshal Dillon on a grey horse with Festus, yellow prairie) are common. **Star Trek** 1968 dome = top; 1979 flat = common. **Beatles** 1965 Aladdin blue = top.

Look-alikes: vinyl lunch boxes (separate, sometimes higher-value market), plastic boxes (1980s+, low), and modern tin reproductions/retro tins (see Fakes).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Aladdin Industries** (Nashville): most of the key titles — 1959 Gunsmoke, 1962 Astronaut dome, 1965 Beatles, Jetsons 1963 dome (attribution general knowledge, verify per title).
- **American Thermos / King-Seeley Thermos (KST)**: major rival; 1957 Toppie elephant (top tier), many 1960s–70s TV titles.
- **Universal (Landers, Frary & Clark)**: 1954 Superman (top).
- **ADCO Liberty**, **Ohio Art**, **Okay Industries**: smaller makers; often scarcer titles (general knowledge, verify).
- Variant multipliers: dome-top vs flat, embossed vs flat-litho, the earliest year of a recurring title, matching thermos (steel-and-glass pre-1970s), cup present.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lithographed steel with embossed (raised) images on 1960s–70s boxes; plastic handles on later boxes, metal/wire on earlier. Matching thermos with glass liner (pre-1970s) or plastic body (1970s+). Mint boxes bring 2–3x good condition. Embossing, maker's mark and copyright line on the box rim/side date the box (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust (especially on the bottom and inside rim) halves value; rust-through is a pass. Paint loss on edges and the lid, dents, broken or replaced handles, missing clasps, interior stickers or scratches, and odour. Thermos: broken glass liner (rattles), missing cup, stained interior. Repainted/touched-up graphics and replaced handles show as glossy patches or mismatched plastic (general knowledge, verify). Sellers don't photograph the bottom (rust) or the inside — ask.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Licensed reproductions and retro tins exist (e.g. later reissues of Beatles, Star Wars and other popular titles; small "tin tote" versions) — usually thinner metal, modern copyright date, no wear consistent with age (general knowledge, verify). Married box/thermos sets (thermos from a different year or title) are common. Check the copyright line and maker stamp.

## Market
Established collector market since the 1980s (Scott Bruce guides); eBay is the main venue, with auction houses (Milestone, Hake's) for top titles (general knowledge, verify). Buyers are baby-boomer and Gen-X nostalgia collectors and TV/character collectors (Western, Beatles, Star Trek, superhero crossover demand). Common 1970s–80s boxes are plentiful and slow; key early titles in good condition sell readily. Prices for mid-tier boxes have been flat to soft as the core collector base ages (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The year/version. Same character, different decades, 10x price difference (Gunsmoke 1959 vs 1973; Star Trek 1968 dome vs 1979). Sellers also don't photograph the bottom (rust) or the inside (stickers removed, scratches). A box with the original thermos AND cup is listed as "lunch box" with no mention of thermos surprisingly often.

## Shipping / handling
~1.5 lb, 12x10x6 box, $12–18 each way. eBay is the market (13%). Wrap the thermos separately; glass liners break.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State title, maker, year (from copyright line), dome vs flat, embossed or not, with/without thermos (and cup), thermos liner intact, condition of all four sides plus lid and bottom, rust location and extent. Photograph every side, the bottom, the inside, the maker stamp and copyright line, the thermos and its cup. Title keywords: "1959 Aladdin Gunsmoke Metal Lunch Box with Thermos", "Vintage Dome Lunchbox", character name, maker, year.

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1950s–60s box with intact graphics under $75, or any dome-top under $50, or a known Top/Collectible title under 25% of its sold range. Pass when: 1975+ flat boxes at any price over $15, rust-through, or seller already names the year and prices at eBay level. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (it does).
