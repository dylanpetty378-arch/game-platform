Buy rule (short): Pass on mid-century office units; buy ornate pre-1920 brass/nickel perforating protectors in working order at ≤ $25.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Desk machines that emboss/perforate an amount into a check to stop alteration. Rows of number keys or column levers, a pull handle, ink roller; heavy cast iron base. The F&E (Hedman Mfg., Chicago) machines have vertical number columns set by levers or sliding tabs and a side pull lever with a round knob, black japanned base with brass nameplate "Keep machine well inked"; Todd Protectographs have keyboards of round keys; Paymaster (Chicago) are 1940s–80s grey/beige office models.
- **Three generations, visible in photos:**
  1. **Check punches/perforators (1869–c.1900):** small hand-lever machines that punch the figures as holes; ornate japanned or nickel castings, often with gilt; many makers (Williams Automatic Bank Punch, Lightning Check Punch, Chicago Check Perforator, Abbott, Standard/Lowden) (Early Office Museum).
  2. **Dial/lever "protectors" (c.1896–1920s):** Todd's early Protectograph sets the amount on a dial and prints "NOT OVER TWO THOUSAND"-type limits; Defiance (Rochester) has dials on both sides (c.1906 model) (Early Office Museum).
  3. **Check writers (1913 on):** write the full amount in coloured, shredded figures — F&E (1914), Todd Protectograph Check Writer (1913), Paymaster (1932) — later grey crinkle-finish office units.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hedman Manufacturing Co. (Chicago), "F&E" Check Writer (1914–c.2011)**: Hedman Mfg. Co. was organised in 1912 by Carl M. Hedman and his son Herbert; the **F&E Check Writer** came out in 1914 (about $20); "F&E" honours **Fesler & Evans**, two Todd agents who helped market it (Todd sued). The **F&E Lightning Check Writer** followed (patented 1918–20, advertised from 1920). Over 300,000 F&E machines reportedly sold by 1921. Factory at 1158 Center St. (now W. Armitage Ave.), Chicago, from 1924; company gone by c. 2011 (Made in Chicago Museum; Early Office Museum). The old entry's "F&E Check Protector Co. → Hedman (1900s–1990s)" is corrected: there was no separate F&E company; F&E is Hedman's brand. Models seen by collectors include M, 7, 11, Premier, R, 500, 2100 (model letters/numbers unverified).
- **G.W. Todd & Co. → Todd Protectograph Co. (Rochester NY)**: dial Protectograph check protector (patents 1896–1913, advertised 1902–24; Model H c.1915); Protectograph Check Writer models "Exactly", "Thoroughbred", "Double Quick" (introduced 1913, advertised to 1930); Personal Check Writer Model 1500 (advertised 1925–30); Securograph Junior; in 1924 Todd absorbed Beebe, Peerless and Safety (Early Office Museum).
- **Hall-Welter Co. (Rochester NY)**: made the **Sentinel / National Check Writer** (patents 1916–25). Correction: Hall-Welter was a separate Rochester maker, not Todd's Protectograph (the old aliases "Hall-Welter Protectograph" / "Protectograph (Hall-Welter)" conflate the two).
- **Paymaster (Chicago)**: 1930s–80s, Series S-600, S-1000, 875 — common office units $15–50. History: founded 1917 as Checkometer Sales Co.; became American Check Writer Co. and introduced the **Paymaster** line in **1932**; renamed Paymaster Corporation in **1940**; locks and keys added in the 1950s; closed by 2000 (Wikipedia).
- **Victorian perforators**: **Williams Automatic Bank Punch** (John Newton Williams, patents 1884–85, advertised 1884–1914, $25 in 1892) — the "1890 Williams" comp is this family; **Lightning Check Punch** (Lightning Check Punch Co., Bridgeport CT, patented 1890, advertised 1890–94) and its **Climax** sibling — not the same as the 1920s F&E "Lightning"; **Chicago Check Perforator** (B.F. Cummins, 1890–1930); **National Safety Check Punch** (patented 1870); **Monitor/Monarch** (R.H. Smith, Eaton & Ellsworth name cast on); **Abbott**, **Standard (Lowden)**, **Acme**, **Champion/Nafew** (japanned or nickel), **Royal** and **Wesley** perforators (Early Office Museum).
- **Safeguard, Speed-O-Print, Defiance, Peerless, Sengbusch, Williams, Lightning (Victorian perforators)**: ornate 1890s–1910s pieces command most. Notes: Safe-Guard Check Writer Model Y (Lansdale PA, 1917–28); Defiance (Rochester, 1899–1924) machines are large (about 13" tall, 30 lb); Peerless Senior/Junior (Rochester, 1912–30). **Sengbusch** does not appear in the Early Office Museum lists (Sengbusch is known for inkstands) — treat a "Sengbusch check writer" attribution as unverified.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the nameplate/casting: "F&E", "Hedman", "Todd", "Protectograph", "Paymaster", "Defiance", "Safe-Guard", "Williams", "Lightning Check Punch", "Chicago".
- Mechanism: lever punch that makes holes (Victorian perforator) vs dial (early protector) vs number columns/keys and pull handle (1913+ writer).
- Finish: black japanning with gold pinstripes or nickel plate = pre-1930; grey/beige crinkle paint = 1940s+; Paymaster with a lock and key = 1950s+.
- Patent dates on the plate give a not-earlier-than date.
- Look for an imprint sample on paper — proves it works.
- Size: perforators are small (a few pounds); Defiance and Sampson machines are big (30–52 lb).

### Golden-egg tells
- 1870s–1890s ornate check punch/perforator (Williams Automatic Bank Punch, Lightning/Climax, Monitor/Monarch, National Safety) — ~3–8× an office check writer ($80–250 vs $15–50; one Williams sold $90) — small cast lever machine with gilt or nickel decoration, punches holes rather than prints.
- Early Todd dial Protectograph (Model H etc., c.1902–24) — ~$40–100+ vs $15–50 — round dial on top/front, "Protectograph" casting, "NOT OVER" limit printing.
- Japanned F&E with full gold pinstriping and brass plate, working, with cover — $40–100 vs $15–30 for crinkle-grey — black base, gilt lines, "Keep machine well inked" plate.
- Defiance (Rochester) protectors with double side dials (c.1906) — unusual; price by condition (no sold data) — dials on both sides.
- Paymaster 875 (signer) and other signer/dater models — top of the office tier ($50 sold) vs $16 for an S-600 — model plate; signature plate attachment.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast iron with original japanning, gold pinstriping, nickel or brass plates = early and decorative. Crinkle grey or beige paint and plastic keys = 1950s+ and low. Original fabric dust cover and key help a little.
- F&E advertised cold-rolled steel and die-cast steel type, shredding the amount into two-colour figures with ink forced into the fibres (Made in Chicago Museum).
- Todd Personal Check Writer Model 1500 is recorded in gray crinkle finish (1961 reference) — crinkle grey alone does not prove post-war on Todd (Early Office Museum).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Frozen mechanisms, dried ink, missing handle knobs, broken column tabs, rusted plating, missing keys. Most are restorable but that's labour Dylan doesn't do.
- Worn japanning and rubbed pinstripes are normal; repainting kills collector interest.

## Market
Thin, decorators and office-antique collectors. 30–90 days.

## What sellers usually miss
Victorian perforators look like "old staplers"; ornate decals. Patent dates on the plate.

## Shipping / handling
8–15 lb cast iron, $20–35 shipped. Tie down handle.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model and serial plate, demonstration of imprint on paper, decals/pinstriping close-up.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker and model from the plate and quote the patent dates. Place it historically: e.g. "F&E Check Writer by Hedman Mfg. Co., Chicago — the F&E line dates from 1914" or "Williams Automatic Bank Punch, patented 1884–85". Say whether it perforates or prints, show an imprint on paper, and describe finish (original japanning and pinstriping, nickel wear). State weight and dimensions; mention cover/key if present.
