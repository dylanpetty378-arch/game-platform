## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Desk machines that emboss/perforate an amount into a check to stop alteration. Rows of number keys or column levers, a pull handle, ink roller; heavy cast iron base. The F&E (Hedman Mfg., Chicago) machines have vertical number columns set by levers or sliding tabs and a side pull lever with a round knob, black japanned base with brass nameplate "Keep machine well inked"; Todd Protectographs have keyboards of round keys; Paymaster (Chicago) are 1940s–80s grey/beige office models.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **F&E Check Protector Co. → Hedman Manufacturing Co. (Chicago, 1900s–1990s)**: "F&E Lightning", "Checkwriter", models M, 7, 11, Premier. 
- **Todd Company (Rochester NY)**: Protectograph (Hall-Welter), Todd Protectograph with keyboards (1910s–50s).
- **Paymaster (Chicago)**: 1930s–80s, Series S-600, S-1000, 875 — common office units $15–50.
- **Safeguard, Speed-O-Print, Defiance, Peerless, Sengbusch, Williams, Lightning (Victorian perforators)**: ornate 1890s–1910s pieces command most.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast iron with original japanning, gold pinstriping, nickel or brass plates = early and decorative. Crinkle grey or beige paint and plastic keys = 1950s+ and low. Original fabric dust cover and key help a little.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Frozen mechanisms, dried ink, missing handle knobs, broken column tabs, rusted plating, missing keys. Most are restorable but that's labour Dylan doesn't do.
