## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Nemadji (Moose Lake, MN; 1920–2002) made "Indian pottery": red earthenware dipped in a bath of floating oil-based paints that swirl onto the surface, matte unglazed exterior, glazed (often green or black) interior. Garden of the Gods (Colorado) did the same. Example 7954045022: ~6" round vase with matte green, turquoise, white and rust surface swirls and glossy dark green interior — classic Nemadji (look for the "Nemadji / Indian Pottery / Made in USA" ink stamp or paper label; an Indian-head symbol in the stamp means 1980 or later).
- **Nemadji process ("cold striping")**: synthetic enamel paints were dripped into a tub of water, the fired pot was dipped and twisted, and the paint film wrapped onto it (Bungalow Club). The colour sits on the surface; the clay underneath is plain red/orange.
- **Niloak Mission (swirl) ware**: differently coloured Arkansas clays were blended and thrown on the wheel, so the swirl is in the clay itself; the outside is left soft and unglazed and the inside is glazed to hold water (Just Art Pottery). Inside the rim and on the foot the same swirl continues.
- **Shapes**: Nemadji used simple Southwest-style forms (bulbous vases, bowls, wedding vases with two spouts, small bottles). Niloak Mission pieces are mostly wheel-thrown vases, bowls, candlesticks and humidors with crisp, thin walls.
- **Title words**: Nemadji, Indian pottery, Native Clay, Badlands clay, Black Hills pottery, swirl, marbleized, Mission swirl, Niloak, Ozark pottery, Arkansas pottery.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Nemadji Pottery** (Moose Lake, MN), stamp or paper label; colours earthy orange/brown typical, greens/turquoise later.
  - History (Bungalow Club / Collectors Weekly): founded 1920 by ceramist Frank Johnson as Northern Clay Products Co. in Nemadji Township, with capital from Clayton J. Dodge; renamed Nemadji Tile and Pottery Co.; made brick and floor tile as well as pottery. Danish potter **Eric Hellman** joined in 1929, made the master shapes for the moulds and introduced cold striping; he left for Colorado in 1931 to start his own studio. Closed **2002**. Not made by Native Americans despite the "Indian pottery" marketing.
  - Stamps (on the base): **early years — arrowhead design**; **1930s to 1980 — the word "Nemadji" with no symbol**; **1980 onward — Indian-head symbol on all stamps** ("Earth Pottery" era 1980–1995, final years 1995–2002). Stamp texts include "Nemadji Indian Pottery – Native Clay USA", "Contains Badlands Clay", "Nemadji Blackhills Pottery".
  - Colourways named: brown and green, orange and red, orange/red/brown, brown and aqua, light and dark green.
- **Garden of the Gods Pottery** (Colorado), similar.
- **Niloak** (Benton AR) Mission/Swirl: true swirled multi-coloured clays (the swirl goes through the wall — visible inside the rim), interior glazed clear; impressed "NILOAK" mark; valuable.
  - History (Encyclopedia of Arkansas / Kovels): Charles Dean "Bullet" Hyten with potter Arthur Dovey (ex-Rookwood, Ouachita Pottery) developed the swirl; first piece sold March 1910; Niloak Pottery Co. incorporated 1911; production 1910–1918 and 1921–1947. "Niloak" is "kaolin" backwards. From 1931 a cast, glazed line was sold as **Hywood** (marked "Hywood" or "Hywood by Niloak"); castware returned after WWII. Dissolved in 1947 and became Winburn Tile.
  - Collectors want the Mission swirl; Hywood and later cast pieces (figurines, planters) are a much lower tier.
- **Desert Sands / Evans**, **Ozark Roadside** swirl.

### Quick ID checklist
- Look inside the rim: swirl continues through the clay = Niloak; plain clay or solid glaze under a painted surface = Nemadji type.
- Exterior: matte, sandy, unglazed swirled clay = Niloak; smooth enamel-paint film that can scuff off = Nemadji.
- Base mark: impressed "NILOAK" (Niloak); "Hywood" (Niloak cast line); ink stamp "Nemadji" (Nemadji).
- Nemadji stamp with an Indian head = 1980–2002; word only = 1930s–1980; arrowhead = early.
- Chip test from photos: a Nemadji chip shows red/orange clay under thin colour; a Niloak chip shows the same swirl.
- Interior: Niloak usually a clear or light glaze over the swirl; Nemadji usually a solid green, black or brown glaze.
- Wall thickness at the rim: thin and crisp = Niloak wheel-thrown; thick and rounded = moulded tourist ware.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Niloak Mission swirl, small 4–6"** — $75–200 vs $10–35 for a same-size Nemadji (existing ladder; Terapeak "Niloak Mission swirl vase" median $86, p75 $195) — swirl visible inside the rim and on the unglazed foot, impressed NILOAK.

- **Niloak Mission vs Niloak Hywood** — Mission is the collector tier; Hywood cast and glazed ware sells far lower (no comps fetched, est. 1/5–1/10) — glossy or matte glaze over plain clay with a "Hywood" mark = not Mission.
- **Early Nemadji with arrowhead stamp (1920s)** — modest premium over common Nemadji (no comps; most Nemadji sells well under $50 per Bungalow Club) — arrowhead symbol on base stamp.
- **Large Nemadji 10"+ or two-spout wedding vase** — $40–80 vs $10–35 small (existing ladder) — height in photo, two spouts joined by a handle.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Surface-only colour that can chip/wear and sometimes rubs off with solvent = Nemadji. Through-body colour layers visible on unglazed foot ring = Niloak (valuable). Sharp, thin walls and clear interior glaze also point to Niloak.
- Nemadji clay: local Minnesota red clay; some pieces stamped as containing Badlands or Black Hills clay.
- Niloak: wheel-thrown from blended coloured Arkansas clays; soft unglazed exterior (Just Art Pottery).
- Old vs new Nemadji: stamp type dates it (arrowhead / word only / Indian head). Paint on later pieces tends to be brighter and glossier.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint scuffs, chips to red body, interior glaze crazing.
- Niloak's unglazed exterior absorbs dirt and stains; aggressive cleaning dulls it. Ask about chips at the rim and foot and any restoration (overpaint on a Niloak shows as a shiny patch on a matte surface).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Nemadji continued until 2002 and current makers produce similar ware; age matters little.
- The risk is a Nemadji-type painted piece listed as "Niloak swirl". The through-the-wall test settles it.

## Market
Nemadji: slow regional decor. Niloak: established collector market.
- Bungalow Club: most Nemadji sells for well under $50. Niloak collectors use Gifford's *Collector's Encyclopedia of Niloak* as the reference.

## What sellers usually miss
Niloak gets called "Nemadji" or "swirl pottery"; check for through-body swirl.
- Sellers do not photograph inside the rim, which is the one photo that decides Niloak vs Nemadji. Ask for it.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Mark/label photo, height, colours, interior glaze, no chips.

### What to tell the buyer
For Nemadji: made by Nemadji Pottery of Moose Lake, Minnesota (1920–2002), decorated by dipping in floating enamel paints, matte exterior and glazed interior; date the piece from its base stamp (word-only stamp 1930s–1980, Indian-head stamp 1980 on). For Niloak: Niloak Pottery, Benton, Arkansas; Mission swirl made from blended coloured clays thrown on the wheel, so the pattern goes through the body; unglazed exterior, glazed interior; production 1910–1947. Give height, mark, and every chip or scuff.
