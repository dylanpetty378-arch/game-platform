## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Nemadji (Moose Lake, MN; 1923–2000s) made "Indian pottery": red earthenware dipped in a bath of floating oil-based paints that swirl onto the surface, matte unglazed exterior, glazed (often green or black) interior. Garden of the Gods (Colorado) did the same. Example 7954045022: ~6" round vase with matte green, turquoise, white and rust surface swirls and glossy dark green interior — classic Nemadji (look for the "Nemadji / Indian Pottery / Made in USA" ink stamp or paper label with an Indian head).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Nemadji Pottery** (Moose Lake, MN), stamp or paper label; colours earthy orange/brown typical, greens/turquoise later.
- **Garden of the Gods Pottery** (Colorado), similar.
- **Niloak** (Benton AR) Mission/Swirl: true swirled multi-coloured clays (the swirl goes through the wall — visible inside the rim), interior glazed clear; impressed "NILOAK" mark; valuable.
- **Desert Sands / Evans**, **Ozark Roadside** swirl.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Surface-only colour that can chip/wear and sometimes rubs off with solvent = Nemadji. Through-body colour layers visible on unglazed foot ring = Niloak (valuable). Sharp, thin walls and clear interior glaze also point to Niloak.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint scuffs, chips to red body, interior glaze crazing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Nemadji continued until ~2000s and current makers produce similar ware; age matters little.
