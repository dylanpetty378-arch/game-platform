## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wool (or silk) stitched over a canvas grid, framed as a picture.
- **Gros point / kit needlepoint (1950s–80s):** large visible stitches, stepped "pixel" edges (like the example), wool yarns, printed-canvas kit designs (fruit baskets, florals, pastoral copies). Decorator gilt frames with velvet liners are typical of the 1960s–80s.
- **Petit point:** tiny stitches (often 18–40 per inch) that read like a painting from a distance, usually Austrian ("Made in Austria" label), often with a small figurative scene.
- **Berlin woolwork (c.1830–1880):** bright aniline wools, sometimes with glass beads or silk highlights, in rosewood or gilt Victorian frames.

## Makers, brands and variants
Kit makers: Bucilla, Paragon, Columbia-Minerva, Sunset, Elsa Williams. Austrian petit point workshops (Vienna). Many unsigned, home-made.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Stitch count per inch is the main quality tell (finer = more valuable). Even tension, no gaps, no missing areas. Original period frame adds value; the frame can be worth more than the needlework on mid-century pieces.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fading, moth damage, water stains, foxing on the canvas, broken frame ornament (composition gilt chips easily).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Machine-made "tapestry" pictures (woven jacquard) are often sold as needlepoint — woven pieces have a flat, uniform surface and no diagonal stitches.

## Market
Slow; local pickup better than shipping. Decor buyers.

## What sellers usually miss
Petit point in tiny frames gets mixed in with kit pieces; check stitch fineness.

## Shipping / handling
Large framed pieces with glass ship badly; oversize box $35–60 (general knowledge, verify). Big frames will not fit a passenger seat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Outside and sight dimensions, stitch type and count, frame material, any fading or damage.
