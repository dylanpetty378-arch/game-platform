## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Authentic**: pre-1960 trays with "Drink Coca-Cola" and copyright line on the rim edge, maker (e.g. "American Art Works, Coshocton O."), date; porcelain signs (heavy, glossy, chip edges with black steel); thermometers with "Robertson" maker marks.
- **Nostalgia reproductions (1970s–2000s)**: "Hilda Clark" or Victorian-lady images, laminated placemats, large tin signs with "©19xx The Coca-Cola Company" and "Advertising for Coca-Cola since 1904" lines. Low value.
- **Furniture**: red metal Coca-Cola stacking/folding chairs (mostly 1990s–2000s licensed retro), 1950s ice-cream parlour chairs (wire), coolers (Westinghouse, Cavalier).
- **Red rim serving trays (1926–1942, plus 1948)**: rectangular 10½ × 13¼ in. lithographed tin trays with a bright red inner rim; 21 issues (JOAC). On 1930–42 originals the printer's name is at the base of the image area.
- Where the marks are: trays – printer line at the bottom of the image or on the rim; porcelain signs – maker name and date in a small area near an edge; thermometers – maker line at the bottom edge; reproductions – modern copyright line ("©19xx The Coca-Cola Company") and often "Made in USA/China".

## Makers, brands and variants
American Art Works, Stelad Signs/Passaic, Robertson (thermometers), Tuscarora, Donaldson; Westinghouse coolers; Vendo machines.
- **Trays** (JOAC): American Art Works (Coshocton, Ohio) printed all red rim issues except two; Tindeco printed the 1927 Curb Service and 1929 Swimsuit Girl trays (considered lower quality). Red rim issues: 1926 Golfing Couple; 1927 Soda Jerk and Curb Service; 1928 Bobbed Hair Girl; 1929 Swimsuit Girl (bottle and glass versions); 1930 Bather Girl and Telephone Girl; 1931 Barefoot Boy; 1932 Yellow Bathing Suit Girl; 1933 Frances Dee; 1934 Weissmuller (Johnny Weissmuller and Maureen O'Sullivan); 1935 Madge Evans; 1936 Hostess; 1937 Running Girl; 1938 Girl at Shade; 1939 Springboard Girl; 1940 Sailor Girl; 1941 Skater Girl; 1942 Two Girls at Car; 1948 Redhead Girl.
- **Tray scarcity in near-mint/mint** (JOAC): hardest – 1927 Curb Service, 1932 Yellow Bathing Suit, 1926 Golfing Couple; scarce – 1928 Soda Jerk, 1928 Bobbed Hair, 1929/1930 Bather, 1930 Telephone; semi-scarce – 1931 Barefoot Boy, 1933 Frances Dee, 1934 Weissmuller; underrated – 1935 Madge Evans, 1936 Hostess; most common – 1937–1942; easiest – 1948.
- **Porcelain signs**: Coca-Cola strictly controlled its red; solid blue or green signs never existed (EarlyCoke).

### Quick ID checklist
- Copyright or printer line: printer's name (American Art Works, Tindeco) at the base of a tray image = period; "©19xx The Coca-Cola Company" with a 1970s+ date = reproduction or reissue.
- Tray size: red rim originals are 10½ × 13¼ in.
- 1934 Weissmuller and 1937 Running Girl trays: check for the printer mark; reproductions lack it (the 1934 repro carries the 1934 date).
- Porcelain: visible "shelving" (raised edges between colour layers) at an angle; chips at mounting holes and edges with dark brown/black rust.
- Colour: correct Coke red; no all-blue or all-green porcelain.
- Logo and lettering crisp and in proportion; no computer fonts or stretched logos.
- Mounting holes in the positions of known originals; grommets aged, not shiny.
- Back of porcelain: rail or track marks are common on recent repros (some post-1950 originals have them too).
- Nostalgia slogans ("since 1886/1904", Hilda Clark images) = reproduction.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Porcelain (glass fused to steel, layered colours, grommet holes) vs painted tin; real ageing (rust from edges, fading) vs artificial distressing (uniform, no oxidation in scratches).
- Fake porcelain (EarlyCoke): bright orange powdery rust in chips; continuous or uniform edge chipping; crude lettering; added maker stamps or old dates (a date alone proves nothing); fantasy designs that never existed.
- Genuine outdoor porcelain: dark brown or black rust; small random chips at the holes and where the sign rubbed a frame; maker and date stamp in a discreet area.
- Trays: original lithography with crisp registration and an even, aged gloss; repros are brighter, sometimes on thinner steel.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Grade 1–10 scale used by collectors; rust, dents, scratches drop value sharply.
- Tray grading (JOAC): NOS mint 9.7–9.9 (original shine, no rim chips); near mint+ 9.5; near mint 9.0; excellent 8.5 (moderate handling marks); good 8.0 is the minimum to consider except for very rare trays; fair 6.0–7.5 not recommended.
- Touch-up paint and clear-coating lower value and should be disclosed.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Extremely common: fake porcelain signs from India/China ("distressed"), reproduction trays (1970s–90s), placemats. Repros carry recent copyright dates.
- Only two of the 21 red rim tray issues (1934 and 1937) have been reproduced; the repros lack printer marks, and the 1934 repro carries the 1934 date and is sometimes sold as original (JOAC).
- India-made "Mumbai" porcelain fakes: see the EarlyCoke tells above.
- Ande Rooney porcelain reproductions appear in "Coca-Cola porcelain sign" sold searches (see Comps).

## Market
Big collector market for authentic; repro items saturate eBay. Red rim tray values peaked from the late 1990s to 2005 and then declined, partly because of reproductions and fantasy pieces; mint/NOS trays hold value best (JOAC).

## What sellers usually miss
Real 1950s trays/thermometers mixed with repros; bottle carriers (wood/metal) with embossed bottler city.
- The printer's line on trays and the maker/date stamp on porcelain, which prove age.

## Shipping / handling
Trays/placemats flat, $8–15; chairs unshippable economically. Protect tray rims and porcelain edges with corner guards and rigid backing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Copyright line, maker mark, dimensions, honest reproduction disclosure.

### What to tell the buyer
Say whether the piece is a period original, a licensed reissue or a reproduction, and give the evidence: printer or maker line, copyright date and dimensions, with photos. For trays, name the issue (e.g. "1937 Running Girl red rim tray, American Art Works printer mark") and give a condition grade with rim and image flaws described. For porcelain, show the back, the mounting holes and the chips. Disclose touch-up or clear-coat.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price. Repro placemats: loss. Authentic 1950s tray ($120) at ≤ $25: 104 − 25 − 25 = $54. **Chairs fail the size rule: pass.**
