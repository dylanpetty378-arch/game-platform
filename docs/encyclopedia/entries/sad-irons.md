## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Solid cast-iron iron (2–10 lb) heated on a stove, 1800s–1930s. One-piece irons (handle cast integral) are oldest; wire/strap-handled irons (like the example: forged strap handle with a wooden or cast grip) and detachable-handle sets (Mrs. Potts, 1870s+) followed. Size numbers (#4, #6, #8) cast on top indicate weight in lb.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Mrs. Potts (patent 1871; Enterprise Mfg, Philadelphia; American Machine Co.; many licensees): double-pointed irons with detachable walnut/black-painted handle, sets of 3 + handle + stand, sometimes boxed.
- Dover, Sensible (Ober Mfg.), Geneva, Bless & Drake "Asbestos" (hollow with cover), Sargent & Co. (New Haven), Colebrookdale, Le Bon, Grand Union Tea Co. giveaways.
- Specialty: fluting irons/machines (Geneva, American, "Knox" rockers), tailor's "goose" irons (heavy, 12–20 lb), charcoal box irons with chimney, gas and alcohol irons, children's toy irons (Dover, Pearl, "Sensible No. 0" etc.).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast iron with forged or cast handle; some nickel plating. Older irons: crude casting, hand-forged strap handle riveted into the body. Look for raised maker name/number on top. Pebbly/rusty top = old; smooth paint with crisp fake rust = repro.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust, chipped soleplate edges, cracked handles, broken detachable latches, missing handles. Most are sold "as decor/doorstop" so rust is tolerated.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction "sad irons" (Taiwan/India decor) with smooth faux-black finish, sharp detail, often painted "Antique"; toy irons and trivets heavily reproduced. Many repro trivets bear old patterns.

## Market
High supply, low price; ~300+ sell monthly on eBay at $10–30; good ones sell slowly to a small collector base.

## What sellers usually miss
Children's/toy irons and fluting machines dumped in "old iron" lots; complete boxed Mrs. Potts sets.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy: a #4–#6 iron is 4–6 lb → $12–20 shipping, which equals the sale price. Durable.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "sad iron" + maker (Sargent & Co, Mrs Potts, Dover, Geneva) + size number + type (detachable handle, fluter, child's, tailor's goose) + "cast iron" + "antique". Junk: "doorstop", "rustic", "old iron". Example: `Antique Mrs Potts Sad Iron Set of 3 Detachable Walnut Handle Stand Enterprise`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for sets, fluters, children's and figural irons; plain single irons are FB local or a lot, since $12–20 shipping equals the price.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (plain $10–25, named single with handle $20–50, boxed sets/fluters/children's $50–200); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 45 days.
- **Photos:** 1) iron in profile with handle; then top casting/maker name, base, handle and latch, stand, box and contents for sets, rust close-up, ruler for length.
- **State:** maker, size number, weight, handle type (fixed/detachable, wood), set completeness, rust/pitting by photo number, paint original or stripped, doorstop/bookend use suggested for plain ones.
- **Pack / haul:** 4–6 lb each, durable; wrap handle, single box with crumpled paper, 5–8 lb class, $12–20; sets 12–18 lb, $25–35.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A [Mrs Potts / Geneva fluter / child's Dover], not a plain foundry iron — [complete set with stand and box]; these sell for [$X] while unmarked sad irons go for $15."

## Buy rule
Plain sad iron ($15–25 sold) can never clear $50 profit. Pass unless a set, fluter, toy or patent iron with median ≥ $120 at price ≤ $30.
