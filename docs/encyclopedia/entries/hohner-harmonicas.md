## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hohner (Trossingen, Germany) dominates. Diatonic 10-hole harmonicas (Marine Band, Special 20, Blues Harp, Golden Melody) have the model and "M. Hohner" stamped on the cover plates; key letter stamped on the end/cover. Modern Marine Band 1896 ~$45 new, so used ones are $15–25. Money is in: pre-war Marine Bands with the "Hohner trademark" star/medals cover and wood comb in original box ($40–100), Chromonica 270/280 and Super 64 ($80–200), 1990s limited editions in gold/wood display boxes (Marine Band 100th Anniversary 1896–1996 gold-plated set ~$60–120 if complete with box/certificate), and Hohner Echo/Auto-Valve tremolos in boxes. Chromatics (slide button) have their own entry: hohner-chromatic-harmonicas.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hohner Marine Band** (1896–): the classic diatonic; pre-war covers show medals/trademark; anniversary and limited editions.
- **Hohner Special 20, Blues Harp, Golden Melody, Big River** – common, modern.
- **Hohner Chromonica 270/280, Super 64, Super 64X, CBH 2016** – chromatic; 64X and CBH 2016 are higher-end.
- **Hohner Echo / Auto-Valve** – tremolo/octave harmonicas, boxed ones collected.
- Other makers (general knowledge, verify): Suzuki (Japan), Seydel (Germany, oldest maker, stainless reeds), Hering, Lee Oskar (by Tombo).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Pear-wood comb (old Marine Band), plastic comb (Special 20), brass reeds, nickel or chrome covers; gold-plated covers on anniversary sets. Old vs new: pre-war boxes in lithographed card or tin, older trademark/medal stamps on the covers, nails vs screws holding covers (nailed = traditional Marine Band). (general knowledge, verify)

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dead or flat reeds, cracked/swollen wood combs, rusted covers, missing boxes/certificates on limited sets. Hygiene is the biggest objection – buyers want new for playing; collectors want boxed and unplayed. Incomplete limited sets lose most of the premium.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No significant fakes. Hohner reissues vintage-style packaging on current models, and the 1896 name is used on the current Marine Band – don't confuse "1896" on a modern harmonica with the date. (general knowledge, verify)

## Market
Low-value, steady eBay market: players want new; collectors want pre-war boxed and limited sets. Estate lots of mixed harmonicas are common. No seasonality. (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
Little – harmonicas are low value. Only boxed anniversary/limited sets and chromatics are worth opening.

## Shipping / handling
<1 lb, $6–8. Ship in the box with padding.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Hohner" + model (Marine Band / Chromonica 270 / Super 64X / CBH 2016) + key + "pre-war"/"anniversary"/"limited" + "original box" + "Germany". Junk: "harmonica lot", "used harmonica", "blues harp" alone. `Hohner Marine Band 100th Anniversary 1896-1996 Gold Set Complete Box Certificate`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (collectors want pre-war boxed and limited sets; players want new). No local premium; used diatonics in lots.
- **Price position:** at the sold median ($60–120 Chromonica/anniversary sets; $120–300 pre-1930 boxed), accept −15%, floor 75%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** harmonica on white; cover stamps close up; both ends; reed plates/comb edge; box exterior and interior; certificate; case.
- **State:** model, key, era from box and cover marks, box/certificate/case completeness, played or unplayed, reeds tested (all notes sound), comb swelling or plating wear by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** pocket, under 1 lb, $6–8; ship in the box padded inside a small box, no double-box.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Hohner [Marine Band 100th Anniversary gold / pre-war Chromonica], complete with box and [certificate], reeds tested; a new Marine Band is [$] and the boxed [limited/pre-war] sets bring [$]."
