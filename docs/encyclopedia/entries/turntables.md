## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A turntable is the platter/tonearm unit of a hi-fi system (no built-in speakers or amp). In photos:
- **Technics SL-1200/1210**: heavy silver (1200) or black (1210) die-cast/rubber plinth, big strobe dots around platter edge, a red/orange pop-up target light at front-left, pitch slider on the right, "Technics" in the lower left, rectangular start/stop button lower left. Dust cover is a smoky separate lid. Lens commonly mislabels any silver deck as a 1210.
- **Stanton (T.52, T.62, T.80, STR8-80/150)**: DJ-styled silver-and-black, "STANTON" printed on the dust cover/platter mat; lighter plastic plinth; lower value.
- **Dual (German)**: CS-5xx/CS-6xx series, brushed-aluminium top plate, "ULM Series" and "Electronic Direct Drive" text on CS-608/CS-627/CS-630Q, front-panel speed/start buttons.
- **Pioneer PL-**: PL-12D, PL-15, PL-514, PL-518 (belt, common), PL-530/570/630 (quartz direct drive, better).
- **Thorens**: TD-145/160/166 (Swiss/German, suspended sub-chassis, wood plinth), TD-124/125 (premium).
- **Garrard**: 301/401 idlers (very high value as bare decks), common Zero 100 / SL-95 changers (low).
- All-in-one consoles and suitcase players (Crosley, Victrola, GPX, Magnavox console) have speakers built in = low value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Technics (Matsushita/Panasonic)**: SL-1200 (1972), SL-1200MK2 (1979–2010, the DJ standard), MK3/MK3D/M3D, MK5 (2002–2010), MK7 (2019–). SL-1210 = black version. SL-1500C, SL-1300/1400/1600/1700 (1970s fully auto, $200–450). SL-Q2/Q3, SL-B2/B3 (budget, $80–180). SL-D2/D3 ($100–200). SP-10 MK2 motor units ($1,000+).
- **Dual**: 1219/1229/1249 idler/belt changers ($100–250), CS-5000 ($300+), CS-505 ($120–200), CS-606/608/616/627 direct drives ($120–220). ULM = Dual's "Ultra Low Mass" tonearm marketing.
- **Pioneer**: PL-12D ($80–150), PL-518/514 ($80–150), PL-530/570 ($200–450), PL-L1000 ($400+), PL-50L etc.
- **Kenwood**: KD-500 ($250–450), KP-9010, L-07D ($1,500+).
- **Sansui** SR-212/222/838; **Marantz** 6100/6300 (6300 $250–450); **Yamaha** YP-211/D6 (YP-D6 $150–300); **Denon** DP-30L/45F/60L; **Sony** PS-X600/X800 ($150–500 for Biotracer).
- **Stanton** T.52/T.62/T.80/STR8 (2000s DJ, $60–180); **Numark** TT-1/TTX; **Audio-Technica** AT-LP60 (cheap, $30–60), AT-LP120 ($120–180).
- **Thorens** TD-125 ($500–1,200), TD-160 ($250–500), TD-166 ($150–300).
- **Garrard** 301 ($1,000–2,500), 401 ($700–1,500) — bare decks in wood plinths are the jackpot items.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight: a Technics SL-1200 weighs ~24 lb; a light plastic plinth deck (Stanton T.52, Audio-Technica LP60) weighs 8–12 lb. Heavy, die-cast = better.
- Quartz-locked direct drive, separate headshell with SME-style bayonet, anti-skate dial, counterweight with tracking-force scale = real hi-fi. A fixed cartridge, no counterweight, built-in speakers = toy.
- Era: 1970s–early 80s Japanese decks have brushed aluminium and real wood-veneer plinths; late 1980s+ go to black plastic.
- Cartridge: a Shure V15, Ortofon (2M, Concorde, OM), Audio-Technica AT-VM series or Stanton 681 adds $40–300; a replacement stylus alone can be $20–200.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Untested is the norm on Craigslist. Common faults: dead motor/speed control (direct drives), stretched belt (belt drives, $10 fix but a repair), seized auto-return grease, broken dust-cover hinges, cracked lids, missing headshell/counterweight, bent tonearm, worn stylus.
- Missing dust cover takes 20–30% off; missing headshell/counterweight takes 30%+.
- Dylan's rule: no untested electronics and no repairs. Only buy if the seller shows it spinning at correct speed with sound.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- No real fakes, but beware Chinese "Technics-style" clones (Reloop, Audio-Technica LP1240, Hanpin OEM like Stanton STR8, Numark TTX, Pyle). They look like a 1200 but sell for a fraction.
- Lens matches anything silver with a strobe ring to the SL-1210; confirm the Technics logo, pitch slider and target light.
- Modern Crosley/Victrola "vintage-look" suitcase players are not vintage.

## Market
Steady demand all year; vinyl revival keeps working mid-fi decks moving within 1–3 weeks at fair price. Technics 1200s sell in days. Local pickup sales do better than shipped for anything under $150 because shipping is $35–60.

## What sellers usually miss
- A Garrard 301/401 or Thorens TD-124 sitting in a homemade plinth, sold as "old record player".
- An expensive cartridge (Shure V15, Ortofon) on a cheap deck.
- Sellers over-ask on brand names (Technics budget SL-B/SL-Q priced like a 1200) far more often than they under-ask.

## Shipping / handling
Hard to ship: remove platter and mat, lock the tonearm with a twist tie, remove counterweight and cartridge/headshell, screw down transport screws if the deck has them, pack the dust cover separately. Double box. Ship cost $35–60 for 15–25 lb. Transit damage risk is high (tonearm, lid, hinges).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State the exact model, that it was tested at 33/45 with audio, speed accuracy (strobe), cartridge and stylus model and condition, lid and hinge condition, and what is included (headshell, counterweight, 45 adapter, manual). Show video of it playing.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200 and profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$45 ship to buyer − ~$25–45 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). That works out to Technics SL-1200/1210 at ≤ $150–200 tested, or a premium deck. Pass on untested decks, DJ-clones, suitcase players and anything needing a belt or service. Size: fits a passenger seat. Not an authentication item.
