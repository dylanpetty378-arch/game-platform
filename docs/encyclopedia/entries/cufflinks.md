## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Paired shirt-cuff fasteners: face plus back mechanism (bullet/whale-back toggle, chain link, fixed back, snap). Vintage US makers stamped the back of the toggle. Value is mostly metal and maker.
- **Back types (Rau Antiques):** *bullet back* (bullet-shaped cylinder that flips 90°; the classic mid-century US type), *whale back* (a solid toggle shaped like a whale's tail that flips flat), *chain link* (two faces joined by a short chain; the oldest form, decorative on both sides of the cuff), *fixed back* (no moving parts), and the modern *pivoting toggle*. Snap/"dumbbell" separable links were also common in the early 20th century (general knowledge).
- **Where marks sit:** on the flat underside of the toggle, on the post, or on the back of the face. A loupe photo is needed; most phone photos don't show them.
- **Period style (Rau):** Victorian = highly decorative; Edwardian = simpler; Art Nouveau (c. 1900) = flowing natural forms; Art Deco = bold colour and geometry (enamel, onyx, MOP). 1950s–70s US = big novelty faces, Lucite, coins, stones, "Florentine" textured gold-tone.
- Title words: "cuff links", "Swank", "Anson", "Krementz", "sterling", "14K", "tie clip set", "lot".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Swank** (Attleboro, MA, 1897–2010s): huge volume; marks "SWANK" on the toggle; most $8–25. Exceptions: novelty/figural (cars, golf), Swank "Disney", jade/stone sets.
- **Anson**, **Hickok**, **Shields**, **Foster**, **Dante**, **Speidel** — similar mid-market; Anson gold-filled better.
- **Krementz** (gold overlay/14k): $25–80 base; 14k $200+. Krementz & Co. was founded in Newark, NJ in 1866 by George Krementz (1stDibs). Most Krementz men's jewelry is a heavy gold overlay (rolled/bonded gold on base metal), signed "KREMENTZ"; solid-gold pieces are additionally stamped "14K" or "18K". "14K Overlay" or "Rolled Gold Plate" is **not** solid gold.
- **Sterling designer**: Georg Jensen (Denmark), Hans Hansen, Danecraft, Mexican Taxco, Ed Wiener, modernists.
- **Luxury**: Tiffany, Cartier, Dunhill, Hermès, Van Cleef — need authentication.
- **Military/organizational**: Masonic, corporate, NASA/aerospace pieces — niche demand.

**Nuances that move value within a maker:** a matching tie bar/tie tac (complete set) and original box; figural or themed faces (cars, guns, golf, fishing, nautical, zodiac); real stones (onyx, lapis, tiger's eye, jade) vs glass/plastic; enamel in good condition; signed modernist designs; 14k gold weight (melt floor).

### Quick ID checklist
- Read every stamp on the toggle and post: "14K"/"18K"/"585"/"750" (solid gold), "STERLING"/"925" (silver), "1/20 12K GF" (gold-filled), "14K Overlay"/"RGP" (plated), maker name only (usually base metal).
- Back type: bullet/whale back (mid-century US), chain link or snap (older), modern toggle (recent).
- Is it a full matched pair? Same size, wear and stamp on both.
- Any brand on the face back (Georg Jensen, Tiffany & Co., Cartier, Dunhill)? Photograph it close.
- Stone or enamel faces: chips, cracks, missing stones, cloudy glue.
- Plating wear: brass showing through on edges (brassing) means plated base metal.
- Box and tie bar/clip present? Note the box maker name.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Stamp check: "14K", "18K", "STERLING", "925", "1/20 12K GF". Silver-tone with toggles stamped only "SWANK" = base metal (rhodium/nickel plated brass). Heft and stone quality (onyx, lapis, MOP) help.
- Gold-filled (GF) has a much thicker gold layer than plating and wears slowly; plated gold-tone shows brass on high points.
- Sterling tarnishes grey-black in recesses; base metal goes green/brown or shows copper-coloured wear.
- Solid gold is heavy for its size and the colour is the same in scratches as on the surface.
- Old vs new: mid-century US pieces have bullet/whale backs with a stamped maker name; modern pieces tend to have hinged T-bar toggles and laser-etched marks.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Plating wear (brassing), broken toggles, chipped enamel, missing stones, mismatched pairs. A replaced (soldered-on) back usually shows as a solder seam of a different colour; it cuts collector value but not melt value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Tiffany/Cartier common; fake gold marks rare but exist. Luxury brands need authentication — Dylan avoids.
Tiffany tells (Era Gem): genuine marks read "Tiffany & Co." or "T & Co." (always the ampersand); stamps are centred, crisp and evenly deep with bevelled (not rounded) edges; gold is 14k or 18k ("750"), never 10k; silver "925"; solder joints are invisible and the back is as well finished as the front. The very common "Please Return to Tiffany" oval links are a frequent fake.

## Market
Very high volume, mostly low prices; lots of 20–50 pairs sell to crafters/resellers at $1–3/pair. Buyers: men's vintage fashion, wedding parties (matching sets), theme collectors (cars, golf), and scrap/melt buyers for gold.

## What sellers usually miss
Small "14K" or "STERLING" stamps on the toggle post; designer signatures on the face back. Also themed faces (a Swank Jaguar XK120 pair sold at the top of the Swank range above).

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size, $5 envelope/small box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph stamps, dimensions, mechanism, any wear; say plated if not marked.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker and the exact stamp as it reads (e.g. "toggle stamped SWANK", "stamped KREMENTZ 14K"), the metal that the stamp indicates (base metal, gold-filled, overlay, sterling, solid gold) and the gram weight for precious metal. Give face size in mm, back type (bullet back, whale back, chain), and whether it is a matched pair with box or tie bar. Disclose plating wear, enamel chips and any replaced backs. Do not describe unmarked gold-tone as gold, or a luxury brand as authentic without proof.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$5 − ~$5 − price ≥ max($50, price). Base-metal pairs never qualify. Stamped 14k qualifies if price ≤ melt/2. Luxury brands need professional authentication → pass. Fits easily.
