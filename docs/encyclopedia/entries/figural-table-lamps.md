## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A lamp whose base is a figure (person, animal, mythological). Photo tells by family:
- **Art Deco metal (1925–40)**: Frankart (Brooklyn), Nuart, Dodge, JB Hirsch, Ronson – spelter/white metal with green-bronze or gold "art finish", stylised nude women holding or dancing around a crackle or frosted glass globe/cylinder; base stamped "Frankart Inc. Pat. Appl'd For" or with a patent number. Heavy for size, thin-wall casting.
- **Moss Manufacturing (San Francisco, 1937–68)**: Lucite/plexiglass panels with ceramic figurines (often Dorothy Kindell or Hedi Schoop figures), revolving turntables, built-in planters – unmistakable clear plastic sheet frames.
- **MCM pottery TV lamps (1945–65)**: Kron (Texans Inc.) and Royal Haeger black/green panthers, gazelles, stallions; Claes ceramic; Lane (Van Nuys) figurals; Shawnee and Royal Haeger "Blackamoor/Nubian" head lamps with turbans; sizes 8–15 in, often with built-in planter or a night-light bulb.
- **Boudoir lamps (1940s–60s)**: small (12–20 in) porcelain/ceramic ladies in crinoline gowns, "Southern belle", Dresden-style lace figurines (genuine German Dresden lace lamps are higher); makers Gilbert, Lefton, Inarco, Japan exports, Leviton fittings.
- **Courting couple / "George & Martha" / Fragonard lamps (1940s–60s)**: tall (24–30 in) porcelain urns with transfer cartouches of 18th-century couples, gilt scroll handles, often ruby/cobalt/purple "marbled" lustre glaze, metal filigree base (example 7960610673).
- **Japanese moriage / Satsuma-style (1920s–60s export)**: Kannon/Kwan Yin, geisha, Samurai, or a figure on an elephant, with raised slip dots (moriage), heavy enamel and gilt; wood bases (example 7968101841).
- **Contemporary resin fantasy (1990s–2010s)**: matte white poly-resin unicorn/fairy/angel figure with two or three tulip/lily "Tiffany-style" glass shades on wire stems (example 7968475059) – gift-store/catalogue lighting (Dale Tiffany, Quoizel-type importers, Lenox-style).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Frankart originals: spelter with soft, worn green/bronze patina and original glass; reproductions are cast resin or heavy brass, too crisp, glossy gold, new hardware.
- Pottery TV lamps: glaze quality, impressed marks, bisque foot; Royal Haeger/Kron panthers are heavy with sleek modelling; Japanese copies lighter and cruder.
- Porcelain boudoir ladies: real porcelain lace (Dresden) is fragile and finely detailed; ceramic "lace" is thick.
- Wiring: original cloth or brown rayon cord, Bakelite plugs; replaced wiring is fine for buyers but note it; shades almost always replaced (original Moss and Frankart glass is a value driver).
- Resin: light, warm to the touch, mould lines, matte paint.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken fingers/ears/tails (glued), chips to gilding, cracked or replaced glass globes/shades, worn patina, corroded spelter (white crusty "zinc pest"), drilled-out bases, frayed cords (must be rewired before use – a repair), missing finials and harps, lustre wear on courting-couple urns. Ceramic heads often have crazing and chips on earrings/turban jewels.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Frankart and Art Deco nude lamps are heavily reproduced (resin and brass, 1980s–present, "Art Deco style" imports).
- Moss-style lucite lamps reproduced by small makers (2000s).
- Blackamoor ceramic heads reproduced/imported from Italy and China (1970s–2000s), and Italian carved wood Blackamoor figures are a separate high market.
- Courting-couple urn lamps were made continuously from the 1940s to the 1990s; "antique/Victorian" claims are almost always wrong.

## Market
Lamps are a slow, shipping-constrained category: most sell locally. Frankart and Moss have national collector demand and sell within days at the right price. Mid-century pottery TV lamps had a decor boom 2010–2020 that has cooled. eBay policy risk: Blackamoor/Nubian/"Mammy" figural items can be removed under eBay's offensive-material policy (racist or demeaning portrayals in figurines and housewares are prohibited); many are listed, but takedowns happen – sell on Etsy/Chairish/local or pass.

## What sellers usually miss
- Frankart/Nuart marks on the base edge of "old metal lady lamp" – the main find.
- Moss lamps listed as "plastic lamp with figurine".
- Original glass shades/globes – sellers sometimes separate or discard them.
- Conversely, sellers over-value modern resin fantasy lamps (Lens even returned a nonsense $150,925 median for example 7968475059).

## Shipping / handling
Lamps are awkward: remove bulbs/harps/shades and ship separately or not at all; 12–20 in pottery lamps 5–10 lb, $20–35; 24–30 in lamps or pairs $35–60+ and often break; glass globes double-boxed. Resin lamps with glass lily shades – local only. eBay 13%. Many buyers want shades included but shades crush in transit – sell base-only and say so.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Height to socket and overall, maker mark photos, whether wiring is original, tested working with bulb (photo lit), every chip/repair, and whether shades are original. Don't say "Victorian" for 1950s courting-couple lamps or "antique" for 1990s resin. For ceramic Black-figure lamps, describe neutrally and only list on platforms whose policies allow them.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$30) − ship to Dylan (~$30) − price ≥ max($50, price). With ~$60 of round-trip shipping, a lamp must sell for ≥ ~$130 even at $0 cost. Buy only: Frankart/Nuart Art Deco metal nude lamps with original glass at ≤ $80; Moss lucite figurals at ≤ $80; Royal Haeger/Kron panthers at ≤ $25. Pass: resin fantasy lamps, courting-couple urns, Japan boudoir ladies, moriage pairs over $30, anything with frayed wiring (repair) or that can't be photographed lit (untested electric). Pass on Blackamoor/Nubian items for eBay (policy). Lamps over ~30 in or with glass lily shades do not ship well – local only.
