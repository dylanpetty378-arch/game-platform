## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A compound microscope: base (horseshoe or rectangular), pillar/arm, stage, nosepiece with objectives, body tube with eyepiece, mirror or lamp below. Age tells: lacquered brass tube and fittings (pre-1920s), black-japanned iron horseshoe foot with brass (1900–1930s, Leitz/Zeiss/B&L "continental" pattern), black enamel with chrome (1930s–60s), grey/beige crackle paint (1950s–80s AO, B&L), plastic/die-cast with integral lamp (1970s+ student). A fitted wooden case with lock and accessory drawers multiplies value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bausch & Lomb (Rochester, NY)**: "BAUSCH & LOMB OPTICAL CO ROCHESTER NY" engraved on arm, serial number; brass to ~1915, black 1915–60s. Patent dates on stage.
- **Spencer Lens Co. (Buffalo)** → **American Optical (AO)** after 1935: "SPENCER BUFFALO USA" then "AO SPENCER". Black then grey crackle.
- **E. Leitz Wetzlar** and **Carl Zeiss Jena**: serial on arm; brass/black horseshoe stands 1880s–1930s are the most saleable "real" antique scopes.
- **Reichert (Vienna)**, **Watson & Sons / Beck / Swift (London)** brass Victorian stands — highest tier.
- **Nikon / Nippon Kogaku, Olympus, Tiyoda** 1950s–60s: good optics, black-and-chrome, sell decently with box.
- **Swift, Unitron, Tasco, Sears/Gilbert, Ehrenreich, Edmund** and unbranded Japanese 1960s student scopes: black or white enamel with chrome knobs, single objective turret, mirror; this is the commodity tier.
- Example 7954138060 matches this last tier: black enamel horseshoe-ish base, chrome knobs, inclined monocular tube, triple nosepiece, mirror, objective engraved "40X 0.65"; no maker visible.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Quality: rack-and-pinion coarse AND separate micrometer fine focus, dovetail slide-in nosepiece, Abbe condenser with iris, mechanical stage, engraved (not printed) maker name and serial.
- Cheap: single focus knob, stamped stage clips only, fixed disk diaphragm, printed/decal brand, plastic parts.
- Old vs new: brass lacquer wear, slotted screws, RMS objective threads; reproductions/decor scopes (Indian-made "nautical" brass) have soft polished brass, no serial, crude optics.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Stuck/dried focus grease, missing objectives or eyepiece, fungus/haze in lenses, chipped paint, missing mirror, bent stage clips, broken case keys. Re-lacquered brass reduces collector value. Missing eyepiece alone can kill a sale.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian/Pakistani polished-brass decorator microscopes ("antique brass microscope with wooden box") are everywhere; tell: no maker/serial, crude threads, 1 non-working lens, very shiny brass, case with brass corners and red velvet. Also "steampunk" assemblies.

## Market
Steady eBay demand from hobbyists and decorators; student scopes are slow and abundant (hundreds listed). Brass continental stands sell within weeks. Q4 gift demand slightly higher.

## What sellers usually miss
Owners price all microscopes alike. The value is in brass Victorian stands, the fitted case with extra objectives (Leitz/Zeiss oil-immersion 90x/100x objectives alone sell $40–150 each), and accessory items (camera lucida, micrometers, slide boxes of prepared slides from 1880–1920 makers like Klaus Kemp, Bourgogne, Watson).

## Shipping / handling
Heavy (6–15 lb). Remove objectives and eyepiece, wrap separately, lock focus, double-box. ~$20–35 to ship.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, serial, objective magnifications/NA, focus action condition, optics clarity (photo through eyepiece), whether lamp/mirror present, case/key.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$25) − ship to Dylan (~$25) − price ≥ max($50, price). A student scope at $60 sold → loss at any price. Only buy brass-tubed or black-and-brass horseshoe stands with an engraved maker/serial (Leitz, Zeiss, B&L, Spencer, English makers) at ≤ $60–100, ideally with fitted case. Passenger-seat size OK. No authentication issue.

## Field notes (learned in triage)
- 2026-10-14: B&L "jug-handle" stand = black-japanned horseshoe foot with a looped arm and lacquered-brass coarse-focus knobs (c.1900-1920); Lens calls it "Bausch & Lomb or Spencer jug handle"; confirm by engraved "BAUSCH & LOMB OPTICAL CO ROCHESTER NY" + serial on arm (serial dates it).
