## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Genuine ancient oil lamps**: mould-made or wheel-made fired clay (terracotta, buff/orange/red slip), 2.5–4" long, wick nozzle, fill hole, soot blackening at the nozzle, root marks and calcified mineral deposits, worn relief. Stone lamps exist but are rare and plain (e.g. Yemeni/South Arabian alabaster, Islamic steatite).
- **Reproductions**: crisp moulded detail, uniform grey/black colour with applied white wash or "dirt" in recesses, no soot at nozzle, resin feels warm/light, plaster chalky; motifs mixed from different cultures (fish + crowned deity + scales in a lozenge = invented composite). The example shows exactly this – a decorative tourist piece, not a lamp of any ancient type.
- **Museum replicas**: marked on base ("MMA", "© British Museum", "Alva Museum Replicas", "Israel Museum").

## Makers, brands and variants
Genuine categories: Hellenistic, Roman (Loeschcke types, discus lamps), Herodian "knife-pared", Byzantine "slipper"/candlestick lamps, Islamic. Repro sources: Holy Land/Egypt/Turkey tourist workshops, Indian/Indonesian décor, Alva Museum Replicas (US, 1930s–90s), MMA shop, Inca/Mayan market copies (Mexico/Peru).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Genuine: aged clay with consistent burial patina, crystalline mineral encrustation inside fill hole, wear on high points. Fake: paint-on patina that comes off with water, modern drill marks, too-perfect symmetry, resin seams.

## Condition, damage and repairs
For genuine pieces: chips at nozzle, restoration fills, repainted slip. For repros: irrelevant.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
This category is mostly fakes. "Ancient" coins, lamps, scarabs, and pre-Columbian figures sold by private owners are overwhelmingly souvenirs. Genuine pieces require provenance documentation (pre-1970 UNESCO) and professional authentication.

## Market
Reproductions: slow, low. Genuine: dealer/auction market (Artemis Gallery, Pegasi, Christie's), heavily policy-restricted on eBay.

## What sellers usually miss
They over-call repros as ancient; real undervaluation is rare and unverifiable from photos.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "decorative reproduction" / "replica" plainly, type (Roman-style oil lamp, ushabti, pre-Columbian style figure), material (terracotta, carved stone, resin), museum-shop maker (Alva, MMA, British Museum), size. Junk: "ancient", "authentic", "excavated", "artifact", "antiquity" without documentation. `Roman Style Terracotta Oil Lamp Decorative Reproduction Museum Replica 4 in`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers, decor category only (genuine pieces are policy-restricted and belong with a dealer or auction house like Artemis Gallery); FB local for tourist stone.
- **Price position:** repros at the sold median (carved stone lamps $65, Roman-type terracotta lamps $49 — mixed with replicas, tourist pieces $5–30, marked museum replicas $15–60), OBO, accept −25%, floor $5; cut after 30 days.
- **Photos:** piece on plain ground; base with any replica mark or sticker; material close-up (resin seams, tool marks); spout/handle chips; interior; ruler; piece on a scale.
- **State:** "decorative reproduction", material, dimensions, weight, replica maker mark if any, chips, repairs by photo number; never "ancient" without documentation.
- **Pack / haul:** small and fragile (stone/resin), padded box $8–12; shoebox class ~1 lb.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [museum-shop] reproduction of a [Roman discus] lamp in [terracotta], sold as decor; marked replicas sell for $[15–60], and genuine lamps need a dealer's provenance before anyone should pay $40–200."
