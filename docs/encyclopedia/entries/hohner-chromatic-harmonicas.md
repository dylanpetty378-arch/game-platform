## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Chromatic = slide button on the right end. Hohner 270 Super Chromonica (12-hole, 3 octaves, wood comb) and 280C "64 Chromonica" (16-hole, 4 octaves, the Stevie Wonder model) are the pro standards; CX-12 is the modern plastic-shell version (one-piece black or gold mouthpiece/cover). Pre-1960s German ones with "Made in Germany" and the old box are collectible. Diatonic "Marine Band"/"Special 20" (no slide) are $20–40 used. Flex-Rack holders (neck holders) $10–15. Model is stamped on the cover plates ("Super Chromonica", "Chromonica 64", "Hohner Made in Germany"). See also hohner-harmonicas for the broader harmonica entry.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hohner 270 Super Chromonica** – 12-hole, 3 octaves; also 270 Deluxe. New ~$180.
- **Hohner 280C "Chromonica 64"** – 16-hole, 4 octaves; new ~$300.
- **Hohner CX-12** (1992–) – plastic shell, Black/Gold/Jazz versions (general knowledge, verify).
- **Hohner Silver Concerto**, pre-war "Chromonica" in original tin – top tier.
- Other makers (general knowledge, verify): Suzuki (Japan) SCX/Sirius/Hammond chromatics, Seydel (Germany) Saxony/Deluxe – Seydel Saxony is a high-end chromatic; Hering (Brazil).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Wood comb (270/280 older), plastic comb (later), chrome or nickel-plated covers, brass reed plates. Old vs new: older covers stamped with "Made in Germany" and model in a different script; boxes in period graphics; pre-war tins. Quality: slide moves freely and springs back, no missing windsavers (valves). (general knowledge, verify)

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common: sticky or bent slide, swollen wood comb, corroded/broken reeds (silent holes), missing or curled windsavers, dented covers. Used mouth instruments carry a hygiene stigma – buyers prefer clean and serviced. A chromatic needing reed work is a repair (pass for Dylan). Ask: all holes play draw and blow with slide in and out, any dead reeds.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No meaningful fakes; Chinese generic chromatics with Hohner-like styling exist (no Hohner stamp). Reissued boxes/cases are sometimes mixed with older instruments. (general knowledge, verify)

## Market
Players (jazz/blues chromatic players) and Stevie Wonder fans; modest eBay/Reverb volume. Used mouth instruments sell slower than new. No seasonality. (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
Little; "Chromonica 64" sellers have looked it up. Estate "box of harmonicas" lots are where a 280 hides.

## Shipping / handling
Under 1 lb, $6–8. eBay 13%. Used mouth instruments sell slower. Ship in the case/box with padding – the slide can bend.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State model, number of holes/octaves, key (usually C), comb material, playing test results (all reeds, slide), cleaned/sanitised, case/box included. Photograph the cover stamp, mouthpiece, slide button, case. Title keywords: "Hohner 280C Chromonica 64", "Super Chromonica 270", "Made in Germany", "key of C".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 280C ≤ $40, 270 ≤ $25, or a lot with several Hohner chromatics ≤ $10 each. Pass when: seller names the model at ≥ $90. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat.
