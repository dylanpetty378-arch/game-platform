## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Just Andersen (Copenhagen, 1918–1970s): bronze or "diskometal" (grey-green pewter alloy) vases, bowls, bookends, busts and animal figures. Mark: "JUST A" or "JUST ANDERSEN DENMARK" stamped underneath, with a model number (D- prefix = diskometal, B- = bronze, e.g. D2111) (prefix scheme is agent knowledge, verify; 1stDibs lists the bronze Fanø girl simply as "stamped and numbered 2111"). Sculptor names appear on figural pieces (Carl Emil Ruge, Jean Gauguin, Gudrun Lauesen). Also Tinos, Ildfast, Buch & Deichmann, Bing & Grøndahl bronze dogs. Patina must be original – polished pieces lose half.
- Just Andersen (1884–1943), born in Greenland, trained as sculptor and silversmith, founded his workshop with his wife Alba in 1918 making jewellery, silver, pewter, bronze and diskometal. Diskometal (named after Disko Bay, Greenland) is a tin–lead–antimony alloy he devised as a cheaper alternative to bronze; the exact recipe died with him. It suits Art Deco fluting and low relief (Klassik).
- Typical objects: fluted table lamps, vases (tall and low), bowls and footed (often hexagonal) bowls, candlesticks, jardinières/planters, figures and small busts, ashtrays, bookends, smaller utilitarian pieces (Klassik; 1stDibs).
- Scale: many figural pieces are small. The Fanø girl bust (model 2111, Carl Emil Ruge) is about 4.25" (10.8 cm) tall in bronze; a 1940s diskometal Ruge figurine is about 4.3" (11 cm) (1stDibs). Don't expect a large bust from the name.
- Tinos (Tinos Bronce, Denmark, active 1930s–1950s): solid ("massive") patinated bronze bowls, chargers, vases, candlesticks, bookends, boxes, lighters and figures; Art Deco fluting/ribbing and figural motifs (eagles, swans, centaurs, kneeling women) (1stDibs).
- Look-alikes: Swedish/Norwegian Deco pewter (GAB, Svenskt Tenn) and generic spelter, which is lighter, grey-white at scratches and usually unmarked.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Just Andersen** (Copenhagen): diskometal (D- numbers) and bronze (B- numbers) (verify prefix scheme); figural busts like the Fanø girl (Carl Emil Ruge); animal figures; Art Deco vases and bowls. Sold in Denmark, France, Germany, Italy and the USA (Klassik). Many pieces carry the stamped mark plus a model number that traces to the workshop. Early models (1920s–30s) and lamps attract dealers; footed hexagonal bowls and fluted vases are signature forms.
- **Tinos** (Copenhagen bronze foundry/workshop) – marks seen: "TINOS BRONCE Denmark", "Tinos Ægte Bronze Denmark" ("ægte" = genuine), "Tinos Made in Denmark Bronze", engraved "Tinos" on the back; sculptor-signed figures e.g. "Hans Kongslev, Tinos, bronze". Pieces dated into the 1940s–50s (one bowl dated 1948). Note "Tinos style" pieces by other makers are common (1stDibs).
- **Ildfast**, **Buch & Deichmann** – Danish Deco bronzes and pewter.
- **Bing & Grøndahl** bronze dogs.
- Sculptors: Carl Emil Ruge, Jean Gauguin, Gudrun Lauesen, Hans Kongslev (Tinos).
- Note: the Fanø girl bust D2111 is recorded here (and in the sweep) as "bronze", but a D- number per the entry's own scheme means diskometal – verify the alloy when one turns up. 1stDibs shows the model in both alloys: a cast-bronze Fanø girl "stamped and numbered 2111" and a diskometal Ruge figurine of the same size, so both exist.

### Quick ID checklist
- Base: stamped "JUST A" / "JUST ANDERSEN DENMARK" (or "JUST" in a frame) plus a model number; Tinos: "TINOS" with "Bronce"/"Ægte Bronze"/"Denmark".
- Alloy: diskometal is dark grey-brown to greenish-black, matte, heavy for its size, soft (dents); bronze is warmer brown/gold under patina and shows golden metal at wear points.
- Scratch colour on the base edge: grey (diskometal/pewter) vs gold-brown (bronze) vs whitish grey and light (spelter).
- Patina: even, original, unpolished; bright shiny metal = polished (half value).
- Form: Deco fluting, low relief, footed hexagonal bowls, stylised animals, small Fanø-girl head busts.
- Sculptor signature on figural pieces (Ruge, Gauguin, Lauesen, Kongslev).
- Size: measure; most busts and figures are under 6".
- Dents, bends and solder at joins (feet, handles) visible in photos.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Diskometal is a grey-green, soft pewter-like alloy with a matte, slightly greenish patina; bronze is heavier, warmer brown/gold under patina. Klassik describes diskometal as dark, bronze-like and calm/matte; unlike bronze it does not turn green with age but deepens in tone. Quality tells: crisp casting, clear stamped mark and number on the base, even original patina. Polishing to bright metal halves value. Modern copies are lighter, sharper-edged, with weak marks (general knowledge, verify). Tinos bronzes are described as solid ("massive") cast bronze with green, brown or golden patina (1stDibs). Old vs new: dirt in recesses, soft wear on high points and a mark that has worn with the base vs crisp new casting, uniform chemical patina and sharp seams.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents and bends (diskometal is soft), polished-off patina, corrosion spots, broken-off ears/tails, lead solder repairs. Ask for base mark photo, all-round photos of the patina, and close-ups of any dents. Diskometal is lead-bearing and soft: feet and rims bend, and old repairs show as bright solder lines.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Unmarked Deco pewter from other makers is often attributed to Just Andersen; some recasts of popular figures exist (general knowledge, verify). Insist on a stamped mark. Dealers also sell "Tinos style" bronzes made by others (1stDibs).

## Market
Scandinavian-design and Art Deco collectors; active in Denmark (Bruun Rasmussen, Lauritz) and on eBay/1stDibs/LiveAuctioneers in the US. Moderate volume; marked pieces sell steadily (general knowledge, verify). Danish dealers (e.g. Klassik) price by condition, originality and demand.

## What sellers usually miss
The "JUST A" stamp – pieces get listed as "vintage pewter vase" or "bronze bust". Any grey-green metal Danish-looking Deco vase deserves a base photo. Also "ægte bronze" (Danish for genuine bronze) and Tinos marks, which sellers often can't read.

## Shipping / handling
2–8 lb, $15–30 each way. Sell on eBay/1stdibs; Scandinavian design buyers are active. Wrap well – diskometal dents easily.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, model number, alloy (diskometal vs bronze), sculptor if any, height/diameter, original patina (unpolished), any dents. Photograph the base mark and number, all sides, and close-ups of the patina. Title: "Just Andersen Denmark Diskometal Vase D-xxxx Art Deco".

### What to tell the buyer
"Just Andersen, Denmark, [form], model [number], in [diskometal/bronze], c.[1930s–40s]. Stamped '[exact mark]' on the base. Diskometal is Just Andersen's own tin-lead-antimony alloy, developed as a more affordable alternative to bronze and named after Disko Bay, Greenland, where he was born. [Designed by Carl Emil Ruge, if marked.] Original, unpolished patina; [no dents / note dents]. [Height × diameter]." For Tinos: "Tinos, Denmark, solid patinated bronze, marked '[exact mark]'." Don't claim a model is bronze unless the metal shows bronze colour at wear points.
