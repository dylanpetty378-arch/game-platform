## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Steel-string guitar. Value tells: headstock logo and shape (Martin square with decal; Gibson open-book; Guild "chesterfield"), label through the sound hole, solid top (grain runs uninterrupted through sound-hole edge), binding, inlay quality.
Esteban (Home Shopping Network guitars by Esteban/Stephen Paul) American Legacy series: many limited "Fireworks", "Starlight", "Midnight Steel" editions, abalone rosette, Martin-like D-45 styling, preamp. The example is an American Legacy AL-100 natural-finish dreadnought with abalone purfling and black pickguard.
- **Body shapes:** dreadnought (big square shoulders, Martin D), jumbo (round lower bout, Gibson J-200), slope-shoulder (Gibson J-45), 000/OM and parlor (smaller, narrow waist), round-back (Ovation bowl), 12-string (twelve tuners, wide headstock).
- **Where the ID is:** Martin — "C.F. Martin & Co, Nazareth PA" burned/stamped and serial stamped on the neck block (look through the sound hole toward the neck), model stamped there too on most post-1930 guitars; Gibson — label or ink-stamped serial inside, logo inlaid on the headstock; Yamaha/Japanese — paper label visible through the sound hole (colour matters on Yamaha); Esteban — label inside and "Esteban" or American Legacy logo on the headstock (general knowledge, verify).
- **Solid vs laminate top in a photo:** look at the sound-hole edge — a solid top shows the same grain running through the edge; a laminate shows layered plies or a different-coloured edge, and grain on top that does not continue (Andertons).

## Makers, brands and variants
- US: Martin (D-18, D-28, D-35, 000), Gibson (J-45, Hummingbird), Guild (D-25, D-40), Taylor.
- Japan 1970s: Yamaha FG red/black labels (Nippon Gakki), Takamine F-series "lawsuit", Aria, Ibanez Concord, K. Yairi, Morris, Alvarez Yairi.
- Budget: Esteban, Rogue, First Act, Johnson, Mitchell.
- **Yamaha FG:** FG series began in 1966 with the FG-180, Yamaha's first folk guitar (designer Hideo Ueda), nicknamed "Aka (red) label". Red-label FGs (FG-180, FG-150, FG-140, FG-110 etc., Nippon Gakki, late 1960s–early 1970s) are the collected ones; later labels (black, then green/other) are later and worth less (general knowledge, verify label dates). Yamaha revived the FG Red Label in 2019, so a new "FG Red Label" guitar (FG3, FG5, FGX) is a modern reissue, not vintage.
- **Esteban:** stage name of Stephen Paul, who sold guitars on HSN and QVC (on HSN by 2000). American Legacy is an imported budget line; "Elezan" appears on some American Legacy guitars (Terapeak comp). Limited-edition names do not raise value much (Terapeak median $90).
- **Takamine lawsuit era:** 1970s Takamine with Gibson/Martin-style headstocks, ended after Martin/Gibson legal pressure (general knowledge, verify). "Lawsuit" is also used loosely for Ibanez, Aria and others.
- **What moves value inside a brand:** all-solid wood (top, back, sides) > solid top > all laminate; rosewood back/sides (D-28) > mahogany (D-18); vintage era (pre-1970 Martin, pre-1970 Gibson) and original finish; original hard case; 12-frets-to-body and pre-war features at the top.

### Quick ID checklist
- Headstock logo and shape (Martin tapered square with decal; Gibson open-book; Guild Chesterfield inlay); "ghosting"/filler around inlays = fake warning.
- Photo through the sound hole: label, label colour (Yamaha red?), neck-block stamp and serial.
- Sound-hole edge: solid top or laminate?
- Bridge and fretboard: real ebony/rosewood vs painted; bone or Tusq nut/saddle vs plastic.
- Straight-edge or side-view of neck: high action, bridge lifting, belly behind the bridge (neck reset needed).
- Top cracks along the grain beside the fretboard extension and below the bridge; pickguard shrinkage cracks.
- Electronics: preamp panel on the side, battery box; tested working?
- Case: original hard case adds value and protects in shipping.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid spruce top vs laminate; solid rosewood sides/back on high end; bone nut/saddle; neck angle and action (straight-edge test). Abalone trim on Esteban is cosmetic.
- Finish: old nitrocellulose shows fine "checking" (spider-web cracks); thick glassy polyester that chips or peels is typical of cheap and counterfeit guitars.
- Bracing (mirror in the sound hole): clean scalloped braces and tidy kerfing = quality; heavy machine-cut bracing with glue squeeze-out = cheap/fake.
- Tuners: period-correct (e.g. Grover, Waverly on Martins) vs generic sealed tuners.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Top cracks, bridge lifting, neck reset need, fret wear, broken pickup preamp. Electronics must be tested.
- Other common issues: dried-out fretboards, loose braces (buzz), pickguard shrinkage cracking the top, heavy belt-buckle wear on the back, overspray refinishes (hide repairs, cut vintage value).
- Ask for: action at the 12th fret, photos inside with a light, whether it has had a neck reset or crack repair.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese counterfeit Martins/Gibsons ("Chibson") exist; check serials and construction.
- Counterfeit tells: flat untapered Martin headstock, ink-stamped (not burned/stamped) neck block, wrong font, sanded area around serial, painted bridge, plastic nut, hazy logo inlay, chemical-smelling poly finish, rough bracing (acousticvibesmusic.com).
- Reissues: Yamaha FG Red Label (2019 on) and many brands' vintage reissues are legitimate modern guitars, not vintage.

## Market
Huge liquid market; budget models sell but cheaply. Reverb is better for $200+.

## What sellers usually miss
Japanese 1970s Yamaha/Takamine sold as "old guitar"; US Martin/Guild in attics.
- The Yamaha label colour; model and serial stamped on a Martin neck block; solid-top status.

## Shipping / handling
Guitar box 44×18×6, 12–15 lb, $45–70. Fits passenger seat lengthwise.
- Detune strings partly, pad the headstock and neck joint, fill the case; ship in hard case inside a box when possible.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Label/serial photo, top and back, neck relief/action measurement, electronics tested, case included.

### What to tell the buyer
State brand, model and year or era as shown by the label, neck-block stamp or serial (and how it was dated), country of manufacture, and the woods with whether each is solid or laminate (only claim "solid" when the sound-hole edge or specification shows it). Give action at the 12th fret, note any cracks, repairs, refinish or neck reset honestly, say whether electronics were tested plugged in, and list what is included (case, strap, tags). For a Yamaha red label, say "original late-1960s/early-1970s red label, Nippon Gakki" only if the label is red and reads Nippon Gakki; for an Esteban, describe it as an Esteban American Legacy imported acoustic-electric, not as a Martin-style or limited collectible.
