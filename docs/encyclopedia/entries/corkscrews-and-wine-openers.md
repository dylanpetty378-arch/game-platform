## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Bar-mounted cork pullers clamp to a counter; a lever handle drives an internal worm in and pulls the cork. Originals: cast iron, nickel-plated, embossed maker/patent ("Yankee No. 7 Arcade Mfg Co Freeport Ill", "Chief", "Quick & Easy", "Phoenix", "Swift", "Champion", "Rapid"). Repros: embossed grape-vine relief, names like "Estate", "Brunswick", "Napa", gold-tone/antiqued finishes, wooden handles, generic C-clamp.
- Bar-mount types (Wikipedia): wall, clamp or top mount (some converted to floor stands); manual "crank and pump"/"coffee grinder" types (crank the worm in, lever the cork out, crank back to release); automatic types that drive the worm in and out with one lever stroke via a worm nut or rack. American, German and French bar mounts are usually cast iron, often nickel-plated; British and Spanish ones are often brass. Breweries and cigar makers used them as point-of-sale advertising, so brand plates appear (and add value).
- Model names were chosen for function (Extractee, Pullmee, Yankee), size (Midget, Little Giant, Jumbo), strength (Champion, Hero, Invincible, Titan), speed (Quick & Easy, Simple, Express, Safety, Perfect, Presto, Swift), class (Ritz, Luxury), or celebration (New Century, Jubilee, Triumph, Victor) (Wikipedia). A name from these families on an iron puller is a good sign.
- Hand corkscrews: T-handles (wood, bone, antler), wire "Clough" types twisted from one piece of steel, folding "finger"/bow types (Williamson), Walker "bell" types, and English mechanical barrel types (Thomason, King's screw).
- Lever types: single lever (Wienke 1882), double lever (Heeley 1888, the "A1"), compound/concertina lever (Wier 1884); prong pullers ("Ah-So") have two blades instead of a worm (LoveToKnow).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Arcade Mfg (Yankee No. 1/7/8)**, **Gilchrist (Yankee)**, **Enterprise**, **Chief**, **Quick & Easy**, **Swift**, **Champion**, **Phoenix**, **Rapid**: US bar-mount originals 1890s–1930s. Arcade (Freeport, Illinois) held 45 cork-puller patents; Gilchrist Mfg Co. (Chicago) – R. Gilchrist "Lightning" patent 384,839 (1888) and bottle-holder patent 823,678 (1906) used on the Yankee No. 7 (c.1910; strong internal mechanism with only two screws; some carry distributor plates, e.g. Nathan Dohrmann Co., San Francisco). Other US makers: Freeport Novelty, Stover (Freeport IL); Bradley & Hubbard, Landers Frary & Clark, Manning Bowman, Meriden Malleable Iron (Meriden CT); Erie Specialty / E. Walker Tool (Erie PA; Edwin Walker, 16 patents); Albert Pick (Chicago); Enterprise (Philadelphia) (Wikipedia; Wine History Project). Note: whether Gilchrist or Arcade made a given Yankee depends on the embossing; read it, don't assume.
- **Thomason, Heeley, Lund, Dowler, King's screw**: English mechanical corkscrews 1800s. Sir Edward Thomason's patent no. 2,617, 7 May 1802: male and female threads so one direction of turning inserts the worm and extracts the cork; about 130,000 made during the 14-year patent; 16 known designs. Badge reads "Thomason Patent" with "NE PLUS ULTRA"; the Thomason IX collar reads "EDWARD THOMASON'S PATENT NE PLUS ULTRA". Thomason II (cut-away barrel windows, "3½ windows") is the common one; Thomason III (T or oval top handle), V (serpent, rare), VIII (fruiting vines), XV (brass barrel, four equal windows) are scarcer. After expiry, Heeley, Thomas & George Dowler, Robert Jones & Sons and Cope & Cutler made Thomason II types through the 19th century (corkscrewsonline). English bar-mount makers: Gaskell & Chambers, Farrow & Jackson, Loftus, Parnall (Wikipedia).
- **Williamson, Clough, Walker**: US T-handle/wire corkscrews (common). William Rockwell Clough (1875) – one-piece twisted steel wire, nearly monopolised the market; William A. Williamson – finger/bow corkscrews, some collapsible with the worm folding into the bow (1870s–80s); Edwin Walker – "Walker bell" with a bell cap for stability (LoveToKnow).
- **Modern**: Screwpull (Le Creuset), Rabbit, Laguiole (French knife-makers; authentic Laguiole en Aubrac/Forge de Laguiole); Rapid (Swiss) bar mounts of the 1970s appear on Chairish (later, not antique).
- **Repros**: "Estate", "Brunswick", Chinese "King's" rack-and-pinion repros sold as "antique style".

### Quick ID checklist
- Read every embossing: maker, model name (Yankee, Chief, Quick & Easy, Swift, Champion, Phoenix, Rapid), "Pat." dates, town (Freeport Ill, Erie Pa, Chicago, Meriden).
- Metal and finish: worn nickel over cast iron (US original) vs antiqued gold-tone paint or bright brass-tone (repro); brass body with English maker = British original.
- Motif: grape-vine relief and names like Estate/Brunswick/Napa = repro.
- Screws: slotted, period screws vs Phillips heads (repro or later repair).
- Worm: original helical worm with even wear vs broken/replaced worm.
- English barrels: "Thomason Patent / NE PLUS ULTRA" badge, cut-away windows count, bone/wood handle with brush (originals) vs crisp new castings and plastic "bone".
- Advertising plate (brewery, cigar maker, distributor) on a bar mount adds value.
- Clamp and bottle holder complete; handle not replaced.

### Golden-egg tells
- Thomason V "serpent" (c.1802–1816) — $2,000–5,000 vs $150–800 for the common Thomason II (~5–10×) — barrel cast as an entwined serpent/snake with traces of silver plating, "Thomason Patent / NE PLUS ULTRA" badge; corkscrewsonline calls it very rare and highly desirable (https://corkscrewsonline.com/corkscrew_guide_thomason.html)
- Thomason VIII (fruiting vines) and XV (brass barrel, four equal windows) — $800–2,000 vs $150–800 for the Thomason II "3½ windows" (~2–4×) — VIII shows cast grapes, vine leaves and autumn fruit covering the barrel; XV shows a plain brass barrel with four identical cut-outs; a II has a bronzed barrel with three and a half cut-away windows (https://corkscrewsonline.com/corkscrew_guide_thomason.html)
- King's Screw (rack-and-pinion with side handle) in original bronzed brass with turned bone handle and brush, Dowler/Heeley/Wilmot & Roberts marked — $400–1,200 vs $21.55 Terapeak for the Chinese "Kings" repro (~20–50×) — side-mounted crank with a toothed rack running through the barrel, bone handle with a bristle brush and a hanging ring, "DOWLER PATENT" or "WILMOT & ROBERTS" on the barrel; the repro has bright new brass, no brush, plastic "bone" (entry comp)
- Registered-design and early-patent English pieces: Dray's registered design (dealer ask ~$1,300), Lund's "London Rack" with lever and bottle grip (Lund 1855 patent), Charles Hull's "Royal Club" (1864), Wier's 1884 concertina with "Wier's Patent 12804" — $300–1,300 vs $94 Terapeak "antique mechanical corkscrew patent" median (~3–14×) — the registration diamond (lozenge) stamp or "PATENT" with a number on the frame, "LUND / LONDON / PATENTEE" on the lever (https://www.lovetoknow.com/home/antiques-collectibles/pull-collectible-corkscrews-styles-cherish)

- Silver-mounted or ivory/bone-handled Georgian direct-pull corkscrews with a sheath (Samuel Henshall "Obstando Promoves" button, 1795 patent) — $300–1,000 vs $10–40 for a plain wooden T-handle (~10–25×) — a disc "button" above the worm cast with "OBSTANDO PROMOVES / SOHO PATENT", silver-capped handle with hallmarks, often with a brush (https://www.lovetoknow.com/home/antiques-collectibles/pull-collectible-corkscrews-styles-cherish)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original bar mounts: nickel plating wear to iron, crisp patent dates, heavy. Repros: rough castings, grape motif, antiqued paint, Phillips screws, bright brass-tone thumb screws. English mechanicals: turned bone or wood handles (often with a dusting brush), brass or bronzed barrels, steel worms; good examples show crisp badge lettering and even patina. Clough wires are a single piece of twisted steel; Williamson bows show maker stamps on the shank (general knowledge, verify). Re-plated (re-nickeled) originals look too bright and fill the lettering.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing clamp, broken worm, replaced handle, re-nickeling (lowers collector value), rust. English mechanicals: dented barrels, chipped handles, faulty mechanisms, broken worms (corkscrewsonline). Ask for: a photo of the worm tip, a function test on a cork, close-ups of all embossing and the clamp screw.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
High: decorative bar mounts sold as antiques, Chinese mechanical repros of English King's screws. Also later production by period-licensed firms (Thomason II made by Heeley, Dowler etc. after 1816) – genuine but not "Thomason-made" (corkscrewsonline).

## Market
Active collector base (ICCA), originals sell quickly; repros slow and cheap. Demand for antique corkscrews described as consistent in recent years (LoveToKnow).

## What sellers usually miss
"Yankee" and patent embossing on dirty cast iron bar mounts at garage sales. Brewery/cigar advertising plates and distributor plates; Thomason badges ("NE PLUS ULTRA") on "old English corkscrew" listings; Williamson collapsible bows.

## Shipping / handling
Bar mounts 3–6 lb, $12–18; T-handles small.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photos of every embossing, function demo, original vs repro stated.

### What to tell the buyer
"Original [maker] [model] [bar-mount cork puller / mechanical corkscrew], embossed '[exact text incl. patent dates]'. [Cast iron with original nickel plating, worn as shown / brass barrel]. Mechanism [tested on a cork and works / stiff]. Worm intact, clamp complete, handle original. Not re-plated. [Advertising plate reads '…']." For repros: "Decorative cast-metal bar-mount wine opener, modern reproduction in antique style; works." Never call an Estate/Brunswick piece antique.

## Buy rule
Repros: median ~$40 → pass. Originals: buy when marked Yankee/Arcade/Chief and price ≤ $60 vs median ≥ $150.
