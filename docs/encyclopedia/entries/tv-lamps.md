## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1950s lamps placed on or near the TV to soften glare: ceramic or chalkware figure on a base with a small bulb behind a pierced back panel, or a fiberglass "spaghetti" cone shade. Look underneath for an incised or stamped mark and a cork or felt bottom with the socket.
- Period: TV sets reached the market in 1946 and TV lamps followed; the heyday ended by the 1960s as picture quality improved (Journal of Antiques). Sold as a dime-store item for about $5–15 new, with new designs every year; at least 75–100 US makers plus some Japanese imports (Collectors Weekly; JoA).
- Size: typically 8–14" (JoA). Bulb ≤ 40 W behind a figural or scenic panel, giving indirect backlight; figures cast a silhouette on the wall.
- Forms: panthers (the most popular, crouching/stalking), Siamese cats (one Texans Inc. cat-with-kitten design sold over 30,000 in a year), roosters, horses, deer, gazelles, dogs, swans, mallards, swordfish, ships/junks, shells, mermaids, covered wagons, locomotives, Model Ts, Comedy/Tragedy masks with cut-out glowing eyes, planter lamps with a well for plants (sometimes plastic flowers) (Collectors Weekly; JoA).
- Look-alikes: plain ceramic figurines or planters (no socket/cord hole), modern "MCM-style" accent lamps, and 1970s reissues/late-production pieces.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Kron (Texans Inc., Bangs, Texas)**: elongated glossy black panthers, stylized; marked "Kron" incised. Texans Inc. opened 1952, was sold in 1982 to Challenger Lighting, then ran as American Quality Ceramics until closing in the 1990s. Howard Kron was the main designer (Richard Gunter a second designer). Marks: "Texans Inc. Bangs, Texas" and/or "Kron"; glossy glazes with gold trim, cut-glass or coloured glass eyes. A "B edition" mark on a Kron poodle/pug lamp has been reported as c.1971, i.e. later production (Collectors Weekly). Kron "Sportsman Panther" in white ceramic, signed, 1955, appears on 1stDibs.
- **Royal Haeger / Haeger** (Dundee, Illinois): gold-crackle, earth-wrap glazes, panthers and gazelles; paper labels or incised "Royal Haeger USA". A Royal Haeger "Comedy/Tragedy" TV lamp is documented (JoA).
- **Lane & Co. (Van Nuys CA)**: horses, sea horses, deer, panthers; "Lane & Co. ©" incised. Produced in large numbers but little documented history (Collectors Weekly).
- **Maddux of California**: deer, flamingos, horses; marked.
- **Royal China & Novelty Co.**: stalking/prowling black panther TV lamps and planter lamps (Chairish dealer listings; usually paper label or unmarked, verify).
- **Claes Originals**, **Premco**, **Moss**, **Royal Fleet**, **Fuhry & Sons (Cleveland)**, **A.N. Brooks (Chicago)**, **Akron**: various; many lamps carry only "Pat. Pend." (Collectors Weekly).
- **Metro Ware / unmarked chalkware**: painted plaster figures (Asian couples, cowboys, ladies) with fiberglass cones — lowest tier.

### Quick ID checklist
- Base: incised/stamped "Kron", "Texans Inc. Bangs, Texas", "Lane & Co. ©", "Royal Haeger", "Maddux", or a foil/paper label; "Pat. Pend." alone is common and not a maker.
- Material: glossy glaze that shows white/ivory ceramic at chips (good) vs chalky white plaster under flaking paint (chalkware, low).
- Eyes: inset glass or cut-glass eyes on panthers/cats are a better-maker tell (Texans Inc.).
- Light path: a pierced back panel, cut-out eyes, or a fiberglass cone; socket and cord hole present.
- Shade: original fiberglass "spaghetti" cone or Venetian-blind metal shade present and uncracked.
- Cord and switch: cloth or early plastic cord, bakelite/inline switch = original; new white zip cord = rewired (fine, say so).
- Glaze extras: gold crackle, earth-wrap, drip glaze (Haeger); gold trim (Kron).
- Form rarity: masks, wagons, locomotives, Model Ts, mermaids and large stalking panthers beat common deer/horse pairs.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Glazed ceramic (shiny, cold, heavy) beats chalkware (plaster, chalky white chips, flaking paint, light). Fiberglass cone shades in red/white/turquoise; brittle and often cracked. Original cord with cloth or early plastic, bakelite switch. Better makers show crisp moulding, even glossy glazes, gold trim applied neatly and glass eyes; cheap production shows soft moulding, cold-painted (unfired) colour that rubs off, and seams left unfettled. Shades on some lamps are "parchment-like" or metal Venetian-blind style (JoA; Collectors Weekly). Old vs new: aged felt or cork on the base, period wiring and a fired-in incised mark vs fresh felt, modern UL-tagged cords, and "Made in China" labels.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chalkware chips and paint loss (very common), cracked/yellowed fiberglass shades, glued-on figures, rewiring needed (frayed cords), broken ears/tails on ceramics. Readers also report worn felt bases, missing cords and broken shades, with replacement shades hard to find (Collectors Weekly). Avoid lamps with noticeable cracks or chips: restoration rarely returns original condition (JoA). Rewiring is recommended and acceptable. Ask for: base photo, close-ups of ears/tails/tips, the shade held to light (cracks show), and a lit photo.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Some modern ceramic reproductions of panthers; Chinese "MCM-style" lamps. Fiberglass shades reproduced. Texans Inc. kept producing into the 1970s–80s, so late pieces ("B edition", c.1971) exist alongside 1950s originals (Collectors Weekly).

## Market
Steady MCM decor demand; shipping fragility depresses eBay prices; local/Etsy sometimes better. 2–6 weeks. Common lamps are abundant on eBay, which keeps prices down; only unusual pieces reliably profit (Collectors Weekly). Estate-fresh, original-owner lamps are favoured (JoA).

## What sellers usually miss
Incised maker marks (Kron, Lane) underneath; often sold as "old lamp". Also "Texans Inc. Bangs, Texas" marks, glass eyes, and that a planter or figurine with a cord hole is a TV lamp.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile (ceramic + fiberglass), 4–8 lb, needs double boxing; $20–35 to ship. High breakage risk. Remove the bulb and pack the shade separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photos lit and unlit, all chips, marks, cord condition; say "tested working" only after testing; recommend rewiring.

### What to tell the buyer
"1950s glazed ceramic TV lamp by [maker], marked '[exact mark]' on the base. [Form, e.g. stalking black panther with glass eyes], [height × length] in. TV lamps were made c.1946–early 1960s to soften glare from early television screens; a low-watt bulb behind the figure throws a backlit glow and silhouette. Original [cord/switch/fiberglass shade]; [tested working / rewired / untested, recommend rewiring]. Condition: [list every chip, crazing, glaze wear]." Do not name a maker for an unmarked lamp; say "attributed" only with a matching documented form.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship − ship − price. Chalkware median ~$70 → $61 − $50 shipping − price = loss. Pass. Kron/Haeger/Lane ceramic with intact shade and median ≥ $200: buy ≤ $40. Must be tested working (lamp is simple, but no repairs).
