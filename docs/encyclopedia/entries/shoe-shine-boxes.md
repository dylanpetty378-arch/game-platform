## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A wooden box (8–16 in long) holding brushes, polish tins and rags, with a raised footrest on top on which the customer places a shoe. Footrests are either carved wood blocks shaped like a sole or cast-iron "shoe last" plates on two legs, often pierced with lettering or patterns. Many were boy-scout/shop-class or WWII-era homemade; others were promotional kits from polish makers (Shinola, Griffin, Esquire, Kiwi, Bixby, 2-in-1).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Homemade/shop-class (1920s–1960s)**: pine/oak, nails, hinged lid or open trough; $20–45.
- **Cast-iron footrest boxes**: footrest pierced with a star, name ("STAR"), or patent date; some footrests by foundries also sold loose ($15–40 loose). Box with original footrest $40–90.
- **Advertising kits**: Griffin Allwite, Shinola ("Shinola" stenciled), Esquire Boot Polish, Kiwi, Bixby, Whittemore. Graphics and original contents (tins, brushes) add value; $50–150.
- **Folk art**: chip-carved, painted, inlaid, tramp-art or soldier-made boxes with names/dates; $100–300+.
- **Shoe shine stands/chairs**: furniture, too big.

### Golden-egg tells

- Griffin Allwite / Esquire Boot Polish / Kiwi promotional boxes — same $60–150 band — printed or stencilled brand graphics, matching branded tins (entry comp)
- Old cast-iron pierced footrest with foundry lettering ("STAR", patent date) on a period box — $40–90 (~2–3×); the rest alone $15–40 — lettering cast through the plate, high points worn smooth and shiny; crisp casting with bright paint = repro (entry comp)
- Early polish tins inside (Shinola 1910s, Whittemore's Gilt Edge, Bixby's Royal Polish, 2-in-1) — tins can be worth more than the box, +$10–40 each — lithographed lids, small 2–3" sizes (entry comp)
- 19th-c. bootblack's stand/box with pedestal footrest and strap — $250–600 (~10–20×) — tall pedestal rest, iron strap handle, square nails, polish-black interior (entry comp)
- No premium: homemade pine box with Phillips screws or uniform stain — $20–45 (entry comp)


## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: square/cut nails, dark grimy patina, polish-stained interiors, mismatched wood. New "rustic" decor: uniform stain, Phillips screws, distressed pine, new cast-iron footrests (very common in repro). Original contents (dated tins, horsehair brushes) add $10–40.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Wood rot, split boards, missing lids/drawers, replaced footrests, refinishing (kills folk value). Dirt and polish stains are expected and fine.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
New cast-iron shoe-shine footrests and "antique style" boxes are mass-produced (Pakistan/China/India) with crisp casting, bright rust or black paint, and no wear on high points. Old footrests show smoothed high points from decades of shoe use.

## Market
Steady but low-priced rustic-decor market; eBay sells many per month; local resale often as good. Heavy relative to value.

## What sellers usually miss
Contents: early polish tins (Shinola 1910s, Griffin) and brushes can be worth more than the box. Pierced footrests with names sometimes attract foundry collectors.

## Shipping / handling
5–12 lb; shoebox-to-carry-on; USPS Ground Advantage/UPS $15–25. Low fragility.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "shoe shine box" + "wooden" + "cast iron footrest" + maker/advertising (Shinola, Griffin, Esquire) + "folk art"/"painted"/"dated" + "with contents" + "vintage". Junk: "rustic decor", "primitive storage", "valet". Example: `Vintage Wooden Shoe Shine Box Cast Iron Star Footrest Shinola Tins Brushes`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for footrest, advertising and folk-art boxes; plain homemade boxes sell as well on FB local without the $15–25 shipping.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (plain ~$25–45, with footrest $40–90, advertising kit $60–150, folk art $100–300); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) box with footrest at a three-quarter angle; then footrest marks/pierced name, interior with contents laid out, joinery and nails, stenciling or painted name/date, underside, tape measure.
- **State:** dimensions, footrest marks, "homemade" said honestly, contents listed (polish tins by brand, brushes), stenciling/paint original or refinished, provenance if known, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 5–12 lb, low fragility; contents wrapped separately inside, single box with 2" padding, 10-lb class, $15–25.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A [1930s–40s] box with the original [Star] cast footrest and [early Shinola tins] — the tins alone sell for more than a plain box; comparable boxes with footrest and contents sell for [$X]; [painted/dated by a soldier] boxes are the folk-art ones."

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$20 out − ~$20 in − price ≥ max($50, price). A $70 box nets ~$21 before price: plain/footrest boxes never qualify. Only advertising kits or real folk art with resale ≥ $150 at price ≤ $40 qualify.
