## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Thin white porcelain hand-painted with roses, scenes, Art Nouveau borders, heavy gold, raised slip "moriage" beading and drips, cobalt bands.
- Marks (base, green/blue/magenta): "Hand Painted NIPPON" with M-in-wreath (Morimura/Noritake), maple leaf (earliest, 1891+), Royal Kinran crown, RC Noritake. After 1921 US customs required "Japan".
- Unmarked "pre-Nippon" pieces exist (pre-1891) and were also faked.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Morimura Bros (Noritake), Royal Kinran, Royal Nishiki, Royal Moriye, many small decorating studios.
- Styles: Moriage (slip-trailed raised decoration), coralene (glass beads fused — rare), tapestry (textured, cloth-imprinted), Wedgwood-style (white jasper look), blown-out (molded relief) humidors.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Quality: fine artwork, crisp gold, moriage intact. Gold wear on rims common.
- **Fakes:** see below. Cobalt glazed pottery (not porcelain) is NOT Nippon.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Gold wear, hairlines, chips on scalloped rims and handles, flaking moriage, restored handles.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Major problem since the 1970s–80s: fake Nippon marks (hourglass mark, "M in wreath" variants with wrong letters, "Nippon" with a crown-and-wreath combination, maple leaf fakes) on Chinese reproductions with crude decal decoration. Fakes often have decal flowers under glaze, perfect even gold, and a heavier body. Nippon Collectors Club publishes fake marks.

## Market
- Collector base aging; prices down 50–70% from 1990s peak. Small pieces barely sell; high-end moriage still moves.

## What sellers usually miss
- Sellers attach "Nippon" to any Japanese-looking ceramic (including glazed pottery like a cobalt vase). Real value is in large moriage/scenic pieces with green M-in-wreath marks.

## Shipping / handling
- Double box; small dishes 1–2 lb ($10–12); vases 3–6 lb ($15–22).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Mark photo, size, gold wear, any hairlines; avoid "pre-Nippon" claims without proof.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$15) − ship to Dylan (~$12) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Only items with resale ≥ ~$100 work; small dishes never.
