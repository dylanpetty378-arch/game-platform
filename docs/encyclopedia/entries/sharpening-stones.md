## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Rectangular blocks (usually 6–8" × 2" × 1") for sharpening edge tools.
- **Man-made silicon carbide (grey/black, "Crystolon", "Carborundum") and aluminium oxide (brown/orange, "India"):** uniform colour, often two-sided (coarse/fine). Boxed in printed cardboard or wooden boxes.
- **Natural Arkansas novaculite:** white, grey, or translucent/black, waxy-looking, often with veins. "Washita" is a softer porous grade.
- **Razor hones:** **Belgian coticule** (yellow layer bonded to blue-grey or slate backing), **Thuringian/"Escher"** (blue-green or yellow-green slate with paper label), **Japanese natural** (tan to blue stone, often with a kanji stamp).
- Box graphics help date: Carborundum used an "Indian head" logo (the example) from roughly the 1920s–60s; Norton's "India" and "Pike" boxes.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **The Carborundum Company** (Niagara Falls NY, 1891; maker of silicon carbide, "Carborundum Brand" stones; Indian-head logo).
- **Norton** (Worcester MA; India, Crystolon, Hard Arkansas; absorbed Pike Manufacturing in 1932).
- **Pike Manufacturing** (Pike NH; natural stones, earlier).
- **Hall's, Dan's, Smith's** (Arkansas). Coticules: Ardennes quarries (La Lune, La Petite Blanche). Thuringian: Escher labels.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Natural stones have veins, inclusions and a polished glassy feel. Man-made are uniform and gritty.
- Flatness matters to users. A dished stone needs lapping (users accept this; buyers want photos with a straightedge).
- Original labels on razor hones add value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, cracks, dished surfaces, oil-saturation, broken boxes. Cracked natural hones lose most value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Labels moved onto lesser stones is a known razor-hone scam; Chinese slate sold as Thuringian. Man-made stones are not faked.

## Market
Tool and straight-razor shavers form an active, knowledgeable buyer base for natural hones; man-made oilstones are cheap and abundant.

## What sellers usually miss
A plain grey or yellow-green slab in a barber's kit may be a Thuringian or coticule worth $100+. Any stone that is two-coloured (yellow layer on blue/grey) should be checked.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy for size (1–3 lb); $8–12. Wrap well; natural stones crack if dropped.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give dimensions, weight, grit or stone type, flatness, any chips, box/label condition.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$10 to buyer − ~$10 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Only natural razor hones selling for $100+ work. Pass on man-made stones at any price.
