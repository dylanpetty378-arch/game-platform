## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Metal urn for heating water for tea. Coal/charcoal samovars have a central chimney tube and fire box at the base. Electric samovars (from the 1950s Soviet period) have a heating element and cord socket on the side or base. Imperial pieces carry stamped medal impressions (exhibition medals) near the top, and maker names in Cyrillic.

## Makers, brands and variants
Batashev (Tula), Vorontsov, Shemarin, Lomov; Soviet: Tula factory "Shtamp", Kolchugino; Persian/Iranian; Turkish çaydanlık-style; modern Chinese.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Brass/copper (older, valuable), nickel-plated brass, chrome-plated steel (later). Stamped medals and cyrillic marks = Imperial. Plastic knobs + electric socket = Soviet 1950s–80s.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, plating wear, missing tap/lid/drip tray, burnt element.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake medal stamps on later bodies.
