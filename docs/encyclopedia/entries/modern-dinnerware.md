## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Dinnerware and serving pieces from c.1980 to now. Identification is by backstamp (always ask for it) and by the look of each brand:
- **Mass-market (China/Philippines/Indonesia)**: Gibson Designs, Tabletops Unlimited/Tabletops Gallery, Certified International, Oneida "Kitchen" lines, 222 Fifth, Better Homes; hand-painted-looking floral earthenware with thick glaze (Tuscan sunflowers, rooster, "Sunset Bouquet"), embossed fruit white ware; big tureens, pitchers, canisters.
- **Retailer brands**: Pottery Barn (white "tea"/"coffee" typewriter-font pieces from the Coffee House line, Sausalito, Emma), Crate & Barrel, Williams-Sonoma, Rae Dunn (Magenta; tall black serif words); value limited.
- **Premium**: Vietri (Italian hand-painted, "Vietri Italy" + hand signature), Juliska (Berry & Thread), Mackenzie-Childs (Courtly Check black-and-white, gold and signed), Heath Ceramics (Sausalito, impressed mark), Arabia (Finland), Le Creuset and Emile Henry stoneware.
- **Mid-century and vintage (out of scope here)**: see american-dinnerware entry.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Gibson Designs / Gibson Everyday (City of Industry CA importer): embossed white fruit pitchers $10–25.
- Oneida "Sunset Bouquet" and similar hand-painted earthenware lines (2000s–10s): tureen $25–50.
- Tabletops Unlimited / Tabletops Gallery: Tuscan hand-painted lines $10–40.
- Pottery Barn Coffee House "tea" teapots: $15–35.
- Rae Dunn: early (2016–17) and rare words briefly sold for $30+; now $5–20.
- Mackenzie-Childs: $60–400 per piece; Courtly Check enamel and majolica.
- Vietri, Juliska: $30–200.
- Heath, Arabia (Paratiisi, Ruska), Dansk Kobenstyle (enamel), Le Creuset: $20–200 depending on piece.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Backstamp: printed brand + country; "hand painted" on mass-market pieces usually means painted decals or quick brushwork over moulded body.
- Weight: cheap earthenware is thick and chips to reveal red/buff body; stoneware is dense.
- Premium hand-painted pieces show brush variation and artist initials.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips at rims, lids and spouts; crazing; utensil marks; lid chips on tureens; tea stains inside teapots. Mass-market items lose most value with any chip.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked at the low end. Mackenzie-Childs and Le Creuset have knock-offs (Chinese check-pattern enamelware; enamel cast iron look-alikes). Fiesta reissued since 1986 (Homer Laughlin "HLC" mark with lower-case "fiesta" stamp on new; vintage has upper-case ink or moulded "fiesta").

## Market
Huge supply; buyers mostly replacing pieces. Bulky and fragile items (tureens, pitchers) rarely clear shipping. Premium brands sell steadily.

## What sellers usually miss
- Mackenzie-Childs and Vietri pieces mixed into household lots.
- Heath and Arabia marks on plain-looking stoneware.
- Mass-market sellers routinely overprice tureens and teapots using Lens retail asks.

## Shipping / handling
Tureens and pitchers: 6–12 lb boxed; $20–30 each way; double box. Teapots: protect spouts and lids separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Show the backstamp, dimensions, capacity; disclose chips and crazing; list exact pattern name from Replacements.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 to buyer − ~$20 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Mass-market and retailer brands: pass at any price.
- Mackenzie-Childs/Vietri/Juliska/Heath: buy at ≤ $30 if resale ≥ $130 (backstamp photo first).
