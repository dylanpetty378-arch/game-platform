## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Maiolica (tin-glazed earthenware):** opaque white tin glaze with hand-painted cobalt, yellow, green, ochre; Deruta Ricco and Raffaellesco patterns (dragons, grotesques); signed with town + painter initials on base.
- **MCM Italian art pottery (1950s–70s):** Bitossi Rimini Blu (stamped geometric patterns, bright cobalt/turquoise), Fratelli Fanciullacci and Fantoni (thick lava glazes, abstract figures), Raymor imports with paper labels and incised numbers ("Italy 453").
- **Vietri:** naive bright animals, Amalfi coast.
- **Modern import decor:** printed (decal/transfer) designs, perfectly uniform; vintage-label trays (e.g., "Peeled Tomatoes") are decorative reproductions.
- **Portuguese faience (Coimbra, Alcobaça):** similar look to Italian majolica — sponged blue, raised leaves/grapes, sacred-heart and religious motifs; marks "Portugal", "Coimbra", "Pintado à mão".
- **Deruta layout tells (Wikipedia):** the central design rarely covers the whole surface and is framed by a wide decorative border; figures are usually single and no more than half-length; open forms (plates, bowls) are usually painted on the top side only; historic Deruta lustre is yellow, ruby or olive-green. The Raffaellesco dragon is the signature local motif.
- **Rimini Blu in a photo:** deep cobalt-to-turquoise glaze pooled in impressed geometric or figural (fish, birds, faces) panels on a matte-to-satin ground; bottles, ewers, pitchers, lamp bases.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Deruta (Umbria):** dozens of workshops (Grazia, Sberna, Maioliche Artistiche); marks "Deruta Italy" + painter initials; modern pieces $20–100, older quality $100–400. Deruta has over 200 ceramic workshops today, most selling their own goods; no surviving piece is firmly attributed to Deruta before about 1490, and the artistic peak was the 15th–early 16th century; Deruta was the first Italian centre to use lustre; pieces can be hard to separate from nearby Gubbio (Wikipedia).
- **Bitossi / Aldo Londi (Montelupo):** Rimini Blu, Seta, Cat figurines; marks incised numbers, "Italy", Raymor labels. $60–500. Detail (Homes & Antiques): Londi (b. 1911) joined Bitossi in 1946 and soon became artistic director, working there 50+ years; Rimini Blu was introduced in 1959 and many shapes have been in near-continuous production since. Marks by period: a letter "B" (with or without full stop, sometimes underlined) until the early 1950s; 1950s–70s usually a handwritten design number plus "Made in Italy" or "Italy"; contemporary pieces have the Bitossi name and family crest impressed. US importer stickers to look for: Raymor and Rosenthal Netter. Animal figures (cats, dachshunds, hedgehogs, horses, lions, owls, ducks, hippos, turtles) — earlier ones most sought; signed mint animals in red, yellow or orange fetch the most. Ettore Sottsass Bitossi chalices/centrepieces reissued since the early 2000s; other Londi 1950s–70s designs recently reissued as limited editions.
- **Fratelli Fanciullacci, Fantoni, Guido Gambone (signed "Gambone Italy" with donkey):** $150–3,000. Gambone (1909–1969) trained in Vietri, became artistic director of I.C.S. in 1935, founded La Faenzerella in Vietri and later La Tirrena in Florence; signed pieces read "GAMBONE" with a donkey mark; textured glazes with geometric underglaze painting (1stdibs).
- **Capodimonte-type:** flower-encrusted porcelain (see capodimonte-porcelain entry).
- **Taormina-style modern import trays:** retail housewares (Pier 1, Home Goods). $5–15.

### Quick ID checklist
- Turn it over: unglazed foot showing pink/red/buff earthenware = Italian tin-glaze or MCM; white porcelain body = Capodimonte-type gift ware.
- Bitossi marks: "B" (pre-mid-1950s), handwritten number + "Italy"/"Made in Italy" (1950s–70s), impressed "Bitossi" + crest (contemporary).
- Paper labels: Raymor or Rosenthal Netter = vintage US import; their absence proves nothing.
- Rimini Blu: deep, viscous, varied cobalt with turquoise pooling = 1960s–70s; flatter, uniform colour + impressed crest = current production.
- Gambone: "GAMBONE" signature with donkey mark; check against known examples (fakes exist).
- Deruta: "Deruta" + workshop or painter initials hand-painted on the base; lustre (yellow/ruby/olive) or Raffaellesco dragons; brushstrokes visible.
- Decal check: dot pattern or perfectly identical repeats under zoom = printed import.
- Portuguese: "Portugal", "Coimbra", "Pintado à mão" — not Italian; tourist faience tier.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Hand painting shows brushstrokes, slight drips, unglazed foot with pink/red earthenware body; decal printing shows dots under loupe.
- MCM pieces: heavy, thick glazes, often incised numbers, crazing.
- Vintage vs new Rimini Blu (Mark Hill research via Homes & Antiques): 1960s–70s pieces have more depth and range of colour and a heavier, more viscous glaze.
- Misattribution risk: Bitossi supplied glazes and materials to other Italian makers, who made similar-looking, generally lower-quality pieces; one specialist estimates about 65% of "Bitossi" sold on online auctions is misattributed (Homes & Antiques). Some authentic export pieces carry no mark at all.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Glaze chips (tin glaze chips easily at rims), flakes on high points, hairlines, restored rims.
- Rimini Blu and other textured MCM glazes chip on raised relief and rims; restorers overpaint chips with cobalt — check under raking light or UV (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Bitossi still produces Rimini Blu; reissues are lighter and cleaner; Raymor label suggests period.
- Fake Gambone signatures exist.
- Bitossi look-alikes from other Italian makers are widespread; "Bitossi" in a title is not evidence (Homes & Antiques).
- Bitossi reissues: Sottsass designs (since early 2000s) and Londi limited editions (recent) — impressed crest marks them.

## Market

## What sellers usually miss
- MCM Italian pieces with just "Italy" and a number are often sold as "vintage vase" for $10–30 — that's the edge.
- An underlined "B" base mark (early Bitossi) and Rosenthal Netter stickers, which owners don't recognise.

## Shipping / handling
- Double box; vases 3–6 lb, $15–22.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Photograph base, numbers, labels; name designer only if signed/attributed.

### What to tell the buyer
Describe the mark exactly as it appears: a handwritten number with "Italy", an impressed Bitossi crest, a Raymor or Rosenthal Netter sticker, or a painted "Deruta" with initials. Give the date range that mark supports. For Bitossi, these are a "B" until the early 1950s, a handwritten number with "Italy" from the 1950s to the 1970s, and the impressed name and crest for contemporary pieces. Give the height, the body colour visible at the foot, and every chip or restoration. Write "attributed to" unless the piece is signed or labelled, because many look-alikes come from other makers. For Rimini Blu, mention that the line was introduced in 1959 and is still made, and say why you think your piece is vintage (glaze depth, the mark, a label).
