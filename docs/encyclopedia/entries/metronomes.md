Buy rule (short): Buy only 19th-century French "Maelzel/Paquet" or early Seth Thomas with bell, working, at ≤ $30; pass on modern Wittner/Taktell and electric metronomes.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pyramid wooden case with a front door that slides off to reveal a pendulum rod with a sliding weight and a tempo scale ("Largo 40" to "Prestissimo 208"); wind key on side; optional bell (ring every 2, 3, 4, 6 beats). Maelzel's 1815 patent design is generic; "Metronome de Maelzel" appears on many makes. Paper tempo scale vs engraved brass scale; key wind on right side.
- **Label wording:** many American and European metronomes carry a paper or plate label "Métronome de Maelzel" with a list of countries ("Paris, Amérique, Belgique, France, Hollande, Angleterre…") — this is the generic design label, not a maker's name; Seth Thomas examples add "Made in USA" or a Seth Thomas mark (Collectors Weekly discussion, unverified). Look for the maker's name on the label, the door, the base or the movement.
- **Bell:** a bell model has a lever or knob to select beats per bar and a small bell visible inside; titles say "with bell".
- **Model numbers:** Seth Thomas used numbers (e.g. "No. 7", mid-1960s, honey maple, per a Reverb listing title); Wittner "Maelzel" 800/810 series uses 80x for no-bell and 81x for bell models (e.g. 801M no bell, 816M with bell) (Reverb).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Wittner** (Isny, Germany, 1895–present): Maelzel-style wooden pyramids (Wittner "Maelzel" models 800/810 series with bell), Taktell (plastic, 1950s+); most common; vintage wood $30–70. Note: 800/810 series is still in current production (a black 816M with bell is a current model), so a Wittner wooden pyramid is often modern, not vintage — check for "West Germany" (pre-1990) on the label as an age clue (general knowledge, verify).
- **Seth Thomas** (Thomaston, CT): 1900s–60s metronomes "Metronome de Maelzel" with SETH THOMAS label; with bell more desirable; $50–120. Restored Seth Thomas bell metronomes in walnut and maple with brass trim are sold by restorers on Reverb.
- **Franz** (US): electric metronomes (1940s–70s) — $20–60, must be tested (electric = untested electronics rule).
- **Paquet / French 19th c.**: "Metronome de Maelzel, Paquet, Paris" with plaque; ornate; $100–300. Restored Paquet bell metronomes of c. 1900 in walnut/fruitwood with silver trim are offered on Reverb.
- **Bürgi** (Swiss): mantel-clock style; **Japanese (Nikko, Yamaha)**: low.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the label/plate: maker name (Seth Thomas, Paquet, Wittner, Franz) vs only "Métronome de Maelzel".
- Country: "Made in USA" (Seth Thomas), "Paris"/French plaque (Paquet), "West Germany" (Wittner pre-1990), "Germany" (current Wittner), "China"/"Taiwan" (modern).
- Bell: bell selector lever/knob and a visible bell = better tier.
- Case: solid hardwood with dovetailed or pegged base, brass feet/trim; vs veneered MDF or plastic.
- Scale: engraved or embossed brass scale (older/better) vs printed paper or plastic.
- Movement: brass plates and steel arbors; modern ones may use plastic parts and Phillips screws.
- Key and door present; pendulum weight slides and holds.

### Golden-egg tells
- 19th-c./c.1900 French Paquet "Métronome de Maelzel" with bell and plaque — ~2–4× a vintage Wittner ($100–300 vs $30–70) — French maker's plaque, silver/brass trim, fruitwood or walnut case.
- Early Seth Thomas with bell in walnut — $60–130 vs $35–80 no-bell — "Seth Thomas" or "Made in USA" label plus bell selector.
- Rare-wood or ornate cases (burl, inlay, carved) — top tier $100–300 (general) — figured grain, inlaid brass, carved top.
- "West Germany" vintage Wittner with bell vs current production — modest premium (no sold data) — country wording on the label; older paper scale.
- Franz electric (1940s–70s) — $20–60; only if tested working — cord, switch, Franz name plate.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid hardwood (mahogany, walnut) or veneer with dovetailed bases; brass scale and hardware; older ones have heavier mechanisms and hand-painted labels. Modern: plastic cases, printed scales, "Made in China".
- Restorers on Reverb sell "fully restored, serviced" vintage examples; refinished cases are common — original finish with wear is preferred by collectors.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Not ticking evenly ("limping"), missing key, missing door, broken pendulum, veneer lifting, cracked case. Mainspring repair costly — buy only ticking ones.
- Ask for a video at 60 and 120 with the bell set — uneven beat or a bell that does not strike is the common fault.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Wittner continuously produces classic designs; antique-style Chinese copies with "Maelzel" labels; key tells are plastic parts and modern screws.
- The "Métronome de Maelzel" label alone proves nothing about age or maker — it appears on modern Wittner and Chinese copies too.

## Market
Steady, modest demand from musicians and décor buyers; dozens monthly on eBay. Restored examples sell on Reverb to musicians.

## What sellers usually miss
French 19th-century makers and bell mechanisms; rare wood cases. The maker name hidden on the label inside the door.

## Shipping / handling
Shoebox size (~9×5×5"), 2–3 lb; $10–15. Secure pendulum with tape/foam.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, wood, ticking evenly (video), bell works, key included, scale legible.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker from the label and quote it (e.g. "Seth Thomas, Métronome de Maelzel, Made in USA"; "Paquet, Paris"), with the country and any model number. State bell or no bell, and that it ticks evenly across the range with the bell striking on the selected beat (video). Give the case wood and finish (original or refinished), height, and whether the key and door are present. Note that "Métronome de Maelzel" is the design name used by many makers, so the maker line is what dates it.
