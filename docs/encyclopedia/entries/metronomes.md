Buy rule (short): Buy only 19th-century French "Maelzel/Paquet" or early Seth Thomas with bell, working, at ≤ $30; pass on modern Wittner/Taktell and electric metronomes.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pyramid wooden case with a front door that slides off to reveal a pendulum rod with a sliding weight and a tempo scale ("Largo 40" to "Prestissimo 208"); wind key on side; optional bell (ring every 2, 3, 4, 6 beats). Maelzel's 1815 patent design is generic; "Metronome de Maelzel" appears on many makes. Paper tempo scale vs engraved brass scale; key wind on right side.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Wittner** (Isny, Germany, 1895–present): Maelzel-style wooden pyramids (Wittner "Maelzel" models 800/810 series with bell), Taktell (plastic, 1950s+); most common; vintage wood $30–70.
- **Seth Thomas** (Thomaston, CT): 1900s–60s metronomes "Metronome de Maelzel" with SETH THOMAS label; with bell more desirable; $50–120.
- **Franz** (US): electric metronomes (1940s–70s) — $20–60, must be tested (electric = untested electronics rule).
- **Paquet / French 19th c.**: "Metronome de Maelzel, Paquet, Paris" with plaque; ornate; $100–300.
- **Bürgi** (Swiss): mantel-clock style; **Japanese (Nikko, Yamaha)**: low.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid hardwood (mahogany, walnut) or veneer with dovetailed bases; brass scale and hardware; older ones have heavier mechanisms and hand-painted labels. Modern: plastic cases, printed scales, "Made in China".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Not ticking evenly ("limping"), missing key, missing door, broken pendulum, veneer lifting, cracked case. Mainspring repair costly — buy only ticking ones.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Wittner continuously produces classic designs; antique-style Chinese copies with "Maelzel" labels; key tells are plastic parts and modern screws.
