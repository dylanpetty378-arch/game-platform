## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Soapstone (steatite/pyrophyllite – "Shoushan", "Qingtian"): soft, waxy-looking stone, mottled browns, greens, reds, creams; carved into foo dog/lion pairs (one with ball = male, one with cub = female), vases with birds and flowers, landscape "mountain" groups, figures, seal chops. Hardstone: harder gemstones (agate, rose quartz, amethyst, crystal, carnelian, malachite, lapis, turquoise), often figures (Guanyin, immortals, birds, vases) with carved wooden stands. Jade: nephrite (oily, white-to-green, "mutton fat") or jadeite (vivid green, lavender, glassy).

## Makers, brands and variants
No brands. Periods: late Qing/Republic export (1890–1940s) better-carved, deep undercutting, natural colour variation; 1950s–80s PRC export (Friendship Store, "Made in China" labels) mid-quality; 1990s+ tourist and resin cast. Shoushan stone (Fujian) – esp. Tianhuang (yellow, very valuable), Furong; Qingtian (Zhejiang) – greener/creamier, cheaper. Hardstone carvings often sold at Gump's (San Francisco) and 1960s–70s Hong Kong export.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Soapstone: Mohs ~1–2.5 – scratches with a fingernail/steel; feels soapy, warmish, light-to-medium weight; polish is waxy. Good carving: deep undercut, crisp details, pierced openwork, colour used deliberately (e.g. red stone as flowers).
- Resin/composite fakes: uniform colour, mould lines, bubbles, very light, warm to the touch, casting seam on base.
- Jade vs soapstone/serpentine: jade (nephrite 6–6.5, jadeite 6.5–7) does not scratch with steel, is cold and heavy (SG ~2.95–3.35), rings when tapped (carefully); soapstone and serpentine ("new jade", bowenite) scratch. Dyed quartzite ("Malaysia jade") is bright uniform green. True ID needs gem testing (RI/SG/spectroscope) – professional.
- Hardstone: crystal clarity, polish quality, fitted carved hardwood stand (often original) increase value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Soapstone chips and breaks easily (ears, tails, finials); glue repairs common – check joins. Hardstone: chips at edges, glued breaks; stands missing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Heavily faked across the board: new soapstone artificially stained to look old, resin cast "soapstone", dyed/treated "jade" (B/C-jade = bleached/polymer-filled or dyed jadeite), glass "jade". Assume new unless age evidence is strong.

## Market
Large supply, soft demand for ordinary soapstone (sells slowly at $20–60). Strong demand from Chinese buyers for genuine top-tier items via auction houses. Hardstone figures sell steadily to decorators in 2–6 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
- Owners can't tell rose quartz/amethyst/agate from glass and under-price good hardstone carvings with stands.
- Conversely, they call everything green "jade" and over-price.
- A soapstone seal with a finely carved animal knob and Shoushan colour can be good; it's often listed as "stone stamp".

## Shipping / handling
Dense and fragile – heavy for size (1–5 lb). Wrap appendages, double-box; $10–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Material (say "believed soapstone/hardstone"), dimensions, weight, carving quality close-ups, any chips/repairs, stand included. Never claim jade without testing; say "untested".
