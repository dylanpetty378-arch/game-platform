## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Franklin Mint (Wawa/Franklin Center, PA, 1964–2000s) sold mail-order "limited edition" collectibles. Example 7928496771: the photo is a screenshot of an Etsy listing for "House of Faberge Carousel Seahorse" asking $99: gold-plated metal hippocampus with pale blue enamel saddle on a twisted brass pole and black base — part of the Franklin Mint / House of Fabergé "Fabergé Carousel" series (late 1980s–90s). The seller's photo is not the actual item.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **House of Fabergé** (licensed name, 1980s–90s): carousel animals, eggs, "Imperial" egg replicas — not Russian Fabergé.
- **Franklin Mint Precision Models**: 1:24 / 1:43 die-cast cars (Bentley, Mercedes, Duesenberg); 1:24 boxed with title paperwork sells best.
- **Sterling**: medals, ingots, plates and books — stamped "STERLING" or ".925" and "FM"; worth silver weight.
- Porcelain dolls, collector plates, pewter figurines, chess sets (chess sets with complete pieces $50–200).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
"24k gold electroplate" on base metal/zinc, enamel; marks "FM" or "Franklin Mint" on base. Sterling vs plated: hallmark "STERLING" plus weight.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing poles/bases, enamel chips, worn plating, missing COA/box.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked; but "Fabergé" naming misleads sellers into Russian-Fabergé prices.
