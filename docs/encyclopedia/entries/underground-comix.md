## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Small-press, adult, counterculture comic books 1968–c.1980 (the "comix" era; "newave" mini-comix after ~1978), mostly San Francisco/Berkeley (Apex Novelties, Print Mint, Rip Off Press, Last Gasp, Company & Sons) plus Kitchen Sink (Wisconsin). Standard comic size (6¾ × 10"), 24–52 pages, black-and-white interiors, glossy or card colour covers, saddle-stapled.
- Tells of the genre: hand-lettered covers, drug/sex/politics content, "Adults Only"/"For Adult Intellectuals Only" boxes, no Comics Code seal, cover prices 25¢–$1.50, a publisher name from the list above in the indicia (inside front cover or page 1, bottom).
- **The whole game is the printing.** The same cover was reprinted 10–21 times over 15 years with only the cover price and small back-cover/indicia changes. A photo of the front cover alone cannot price the book; you need the cover price, the back cover (printer statement) and the indicia.
- Title words sellers use: "Zap Comix", "R. Crumb", "underground comic", "head shop", "hippie comic", "1960s", "Freak Brothers", "Mr Natural", "Print Mint", "Last Gasp", "adult comic".

### Quick ID checklist
1. Publisher in the indicia (Apex / Print Mint / Rip Off / Last Gasp / Kitchen Sink / Company & Sons) and the year printed there.
2. Cover price — on a 1968–71 title, 25¢ or 35¢ is early; 50¢+ on Zap #0–#3 is a reprint.
3. Back cover: printer statement ("Printed by Charles Plymell" / "Printed by Don Donahue") on Zap #1; none = 3rd or later.
4. Page count and numbering (Zap #0: 24 numbered = 1st; 28 unnumbered = 2nd+).
5. Cover art details that changed between printings (Zap #0 "Z" hairline, spine scratch; Freak Brothers price colour, fence colour, cross-hatching).
6. Paper: newsprint toning (brown, brittle) is normal; pink interior paper on Freak Brothers = 9th/13th.
7. Condition: staples rusted? centrefold attached? spine split? water stains? writing? — underground comix were read hard, so FN is "good" for the genre.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Zap Comix #1, 1st printing — 25¢ cover, back cover "Printed by Charles Plymell"** — $4,000–7,000 raw VG/FN; CGC 8.0 $10,000–16,000; CGC 9.4 sold $26,000 (2010) vs $30–60 for the 35¢ Print Mint 3rd printing (~100×) — read the price box on the front cover (25¢) and the small type at the bottom of the back cover; slightly uneven red cover ink.
- **Zap Comix #1, 2nd printing — 35¢, back cover "Printed by Don Donahue"** — $500–900 raw VG, $2,200–3,500 CGC 8.0 (~15× the 3rd printing) — same 35¢ price as the 3rd; the only tell is the Donahue line on the back cover, so always ask for a back-cover photo on a 35¢ copy.
- **Zap Comix #0, 1st printing — 35¢, hairline in the bottom leg of the "Z", 24 numbered pages** — $300–800 raw (est) vs $10–25 for the 50¢–$2.95 printings (~20×) — zoom on the Z; count/see page numbers; back-cover upper-left lettering shows yellow ink.
- **Freak Brothers #1, 1st printing (Feb 1971, either orange or brown wall)** — $90–180 raw VG, CGC 8.0 $450–750 vs $5–15 for 50¢ 4th–10th printings (~10×) — no purple or blue price (price box treatment of the 1st), no cross-hatching on the billy clubs, 52 pages; compare against ComixJoint scans.
- **Snatch Comics #1–#2 (Apex 1968–69, digest-size, Crumb/Wilson)** — $300–800 raw (est) vs $20–40 later Print Mint reprints — Apex Novelties in the indicia; small digest format; 1st printing has no price on the cover of #1 (verify against ComixJoint before paying).
- **It Ain't Me, Babe Comix #1 (Last Gasp 1970)** — $200–500 raw, $1,000–1,700 CGC 8.0 (est) vs $20–40 later printings — 50¢ cover, Last Gasp Eco-Funnies 1970 indicia, cover with Wonder Woman/Sheena figures marching.
- **Head Comix (Viking 1968 hardcover with dust jacket) and Fritz the Cat (Ballantine 1969 1st)** — $150–400 vs $20–40 softcover reprints — hardcover with DJ, "Viking Press 1968" on the copyright page, first-printing statement.
- **Signed/sketched copies (Crumb signature)** — +$100–300 on a $20 book only if provenance (show, dealer COA, Heritage) — Crumb signatures are forged; most unsigned-looking "signed" eBay copies are worthless as signed.
- **CGC-slabbed early printings** — 2–3× raw at the same grade; underground books in 9.0+ are rare (only ~7 of ~145 graded Zap #1 1sts are 9.0+).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Newsprint interior, card or glossy cover, two staples. 1968–70 Apex/Print Mint covers are thin and show ink inconsistency; later Print Mint/Last Gasp printings have brighter, more even colour and whiter paper (and the higher price).
- Age toning: a 1968 copy with bright white interior pages is either a late printing or a rare well-stored copy — check the price before believing "first print".
- No Comics Code stamp, no UPC until the 1980s; a UPC box = 1980s+ reprint.
- Quality signs for the buyer: tight staples, no rust, flat spine, centrefold attached, no tape, no writing, no chipping at the edges.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Common: spine rolls and splits, rusty staples, chipped/brittle newsprint, water stains, tape repairs, store stamps, names written in, loose centrefolds, smoke/head-shop smell, foxing.
- Kills value: missing pages or centrefold, cut coupons/panels, mould, heavy water damage, tape on the cover, trimmed edges, restoration (colour touch, tear seals, cleaning) undisclosed — restored Zap #1s circulate; CGC labels restoration in purple.
- Acceptable (for the genre): light toning, minor spine stress, small tears at the staples — a FN 6.0 copy is a normal sellable copy.
- Ask: photo of the back cover and indicia, a photo of the centrefold open, a photo of the spine edge, "any tape, writing, cut pages, or restoration?"

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- The dominant "fake" is a misdescribed later printing sold as "1968 first print" — intentionally or not. Any seller who won't show the back cover and cover price is hiding a 3rd+ printing.
- Counterfeit Zap #1 2nd printings and undisclosed restorations are reported (MyComicsCollection); for anything over ~$500 buy only CGC/CBCS-slabbed or from Heritage/ComicConnect provenance.
- Modern reissues: Last Gasp reprints of Zap #0–#15 (1980s–2000s, $2.50–$4.95 covers), Fantagraphics *The Complete Zap Comix* box set (2014) and Zap #16, Knockabout (UK) editions, Kitchen Sink *Complete Crumb Comics* volumes — all legitimate books, all worth $5–30 each.
- Fake Crumb signatures are common.

## Market
- Narrow, loyal collector base (boomers, art-comics fans, Crumb collectors); little speculation; appreciation on slabbed keys ~8–12%/yr (MyComicsCollection). Heritage, ComicConnect and specialist dealers (ComixJoint, Lambiek-type) set the top; eBay carries the $5–300 band.
- eBay US: hundreds of underground comix sold per month across all titles (order of magnitude 500+), mostly $5–40; genuine first printings of keys appear a few times a month and sell in days. Readers' copies and later printings sit for months unless lotted.
- Best platform: eBay auction for verified early printings (bidders know the printings); Heritage for anything $1,000+. Lots of readers on eBay or local to comic shops.
- Seasonality: none strong; slight lift around Crumb exhibitions/documentary news.

## What sellers usually miss
- The printing. Estate and casual sellers list by cover ("Zap #1 1968") and price at $20–60 regardless; a 35¢ copy with "Printed by Don Donahue" is a $500+ book sitting under a $50 title — this is the edge.
- Zap #0 "Z" hairline and page numbering; Freak Brothers price-colour tells.
- That a run of 1970s Last Gasp/Kitchen Sink titles is worth more as a lot to a dealer than one at a time.
- Head Comix/Fritz hardcovers with dust jackets.

## Shipping / handling
- Flat, light: bag and board, sandwich between cardboard, rigid mailer or small box; ~$5–6 Ground Advantage / Media Mail does not apply (comics with ads do not qualify). Anything over $250: Priority with signature and insurance. Lots of 20+: ~5 lb, $12–18.
- Fragility 2/5 but corners and spine crush — never ship loose in a padded mailer.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Title: "Zap Comix #1 2nd printing Printed by Don Donahue 35¢ Apex 1968 R. Crumb underground comix FN" — printing and the identifying tell in the title.
- Photograph: front cover, back cover (full, legible printer line), indicia, centrefold open, spine edge, staples, any defects. State the grade honestly in comic terms (VG 4.0 / FN 6.0 / VF 8.0) and the page quality (off-white, tan, brittle).
- Keywords: underground comix, Crumb, Apex Novelties, Print Mint, first printing, Plymell, Donahue, Mr. Natural, Freak Brothers, Shelton, Rip Off Press, Last Gasp, head comix, 1968.

## Buy rule
Buy when: the printing is verified from the seller's photos (cover price + back cover + indicia) as a first printing of a 1968–72 key (or Zap #1 2nd printing), complete, no water damage/tape/cut pages, and the price is ≤ 35% of the raw sold band — e.g. Zap #1 2nd at ≤ $200, Zap #1 1st at any price ≤ $200 (obviously), Freak Brothers #1 1st at ≤ $40, Zap #2–#4 1st at ≤ $50, It Ain't Me Babe #1 at ≤ $70. Lots of 20+ undergrounds at ≤ $2/book are a buy only if 1968–72 titles are visible in the photos.
Pass when: cover price 50¢ or more on a 1968–69 title, 35¢ Zap #1 with no printer line (3rd printing, $30–60 — never clears $50 profit), any later printing of anything, water damage, missing centrefold, tape, trimmed, "signed" without provenance, or a seller who will not photograph the back cover. Anything over ~$500 needs a slab or a Heritage-grade provenance — say so. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (always does).
