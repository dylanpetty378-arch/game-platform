## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1950s-70s recreational scuba. The prized piece is the two-hose regulator (a round can on the tank valve with two corrugated hoses to a mouthpiece). Tanks are steel or (from 1971) aluminum cylinders, often galvanized with K or J valves, held by metal bands to a backplate/harness.

## Makers, brands and variants
U.S. Divers (Aqua-Lung, DA Aqua Master, Mistral), Healthways (Scubamaster), Voit (Viking regulator, masks), Dacor, Sportsways, Nemrod (Spain), Cressi (Italy). Hard-hat helmets: Morse, Miller-Dunn, DESCO - most offered are Indian replicas.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original regulators: chrome-plated brass cans with stamped names and serials. Tanks: stamped DOT/ICC and hydro dates on the neck; galvanized steel 72 cu ft common.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rotted hoses, corroded cans, out-of-hydro tanks (cannot be filled without testing). Never pressurize.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Brass hard-hat helmets are almost always reproductions. Regulators rarely faked.

## Market
Small but active collector community (vintage double-hose divers). Tanks themselves are commodity.

## What sellers usually miss
A regulator attached to an old tank or in a box of "old dive stuff".

## Shipping / handling
Steel tanks 25-35 lb; must be empty with valve removed or open; carriers restrict pressurized cylinders. $30-45.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Empty, valve open, hydro date, maker, display-only.

## Buy rule
Tanks alone fail profit math (heavy, ~$50-80 resale). Look for regulators.
