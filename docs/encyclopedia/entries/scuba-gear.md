Buy rule (short): Buy double-hose regulators and marked 1950s-60s US Divers/Voit/Healthways gear ≤ $50; pass on tanks alone.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1950s-70s recreational scuba. The prized piece is the two-hose regulator (a round can on the tank valve with two corrugated hoses to a mouthpiece). Tanks are steel or (from 1971) aluminum cylinders, often galvanized with K or J valves, held by metal bands to a backplate/harness.
More detail: the double-hose regulator is a disc-shaped chrome or plated can (two halves clamped by a band) that mounts on the tank valve by a yoke; two big corrugated rubber hoses (black, later yellow on some US Divers) run to a mouthpiece with a check-valve box. Single-hose regulators (a small first stage on the valve and one hose to a second stage in the mouth) took over in the 1960s–70s and are worth far less. A **J valve** has a reserve lever (a rod running down the tank side); a **K valve** is a plain on/off. Tanks are stamped on the shoulder with spec (ICC or DOT 3AA steel, 3AL aluminium), pressure, serial, maker and a row of hydro-test dates. Backpacks: aluminium or plastic plates with canvas or nylon straps and quick-release buckles (Voit, US Divers, Healthways stamped plates).
Words in titles: "aqualung", "double hose", "twin hose", "Mistral", "Aqua Master", "DAAM", "Royal Aqua Master", "Voit 50 Fathom", "Healthways Scubamaster", "Scuba Star", "Nemrod", "J valve".
Look-alikes: oxygen and welding cylinders (no scuba yoke valve), fire-extinguisher bodies, SCBA fire-fighter air packs (full-face mask with one hose and a regulator on the belt; low collector value), and brass "diving helmets" (decor reproductions).

## Makers, brands and variants
U.S. Divers (Aqua-Lung, DA Aqua Master, Mistral), Healthways (Scubamaster), Voit (Viking regulator, masks), Dacor, Sportways, Nemrod (Spain), Cressi (Italy). Hard-hat helmets: Morse, Miller-Dunn, DESCO - most offered are Indian replicas. More:
- **U.S. Divers / Aqua-Lung (Los Angeles; licensee of La Spirotechnique, France)**: CG-45 (first commercially sold US model; rare), DA "Navy Approved" and **DA Aqua-Master (DAAM)** (two-stage), **Royal Aqua-Master (RAM)** (top of line), **Mistral** (introduced 1958; single-stage, venturi-assisted; early units have a yellow nameplate with the "11201 West Pico Blvd" address, black hoses; later yellow hoses; early serials 100004–118953 per one dealer). Serial numbers on the can's nameplate date the unit by known year blocks (dealer database at vintagescubagear.com.au).
- **La Spirotechnique (France)**: the Cousteau–Gagnan originals; early French units are rarer in America.
- **Healthways (LA)**: SCUBA, SCUBA Deluxe, SCUBA Gold Label (highly collectible), Scubamaster, Scuba Star (single-hose, downstream lever). Healthways briefly had a "SCUBA Pro" division (1962 catalog) — the origin of Scubapro.
- **Voit**: 50 Fathom, Navy-Approved 50 Fathom, **Trieste II**, "Blue Voit" regulators (collectible); Voit masks and fins.
- **Nemrod (Spain) / Seamless Rubber (US)**: Snark II and Snark II Deluxe (early 1960s; sometimes marked only "Seamless"), Delta; sold at JC Penney.
- **Sherwood**: bought the piston regulator patent in 1958 and made regulators for Healthways, Nemrod-Seamless and others.
- **Dacor, Sportways (Waterlung)**: US makers; mid-value.
- **Chirana (Czechoslovakia)**: PL-40, PL-401 (Eastern bloc twin-hose; dated ID plate).
- **Hard-hat helmets**: genuine US Navy Mark V helmets by **DESCO, Morse, Miller-Dunn, Schrader** carry a maker's tag plate and a serial; most brass helmets for sale are modern Indian decor copies.

### Quick ID checklist
- Two corrugated hoses from a round can to the mouthpiece = double-hose (the money item); one hose = single-hose (low).
- Nameplate on the can: maker, model (DA / Royal / Mistral / 50 Fathom), address and serial number readable.
- Both can halves, clamp band, diaphragm, mouthpiece and hoses present.
- Corrosion check: inner can, horn openings, knife-edge, first-stage inlet threads.
- Hoses: cracked/sticky (dry rot) vs supple; original black or yellow vs replacement.
- Tank shoulder stamps: ICC/DOT 3AA, maker, last hydro date; valve type J (reserve rod) or K.
- Original box, manual or warranty card in the photo.
- Brass helmet: maker tag plate with serial, US Navy Mark V markings, heavy tinned-copper body vs shiny thin brass with fake tags.

### Golden-egg tells
- U.S. Divers CG-45, early La Spirotechnique, experimental Navy models, Voit Trieste II — dealer asks $1,500–4,000+ (vintagescubagear, retail) vs $20–80 for tanks — model name on the can plate, early serial, CG-45 shape.
- DA Aqua-Master, Royal Aqua-Master, Voit 50 Fathom, Mistral in working or complete condition — dealer asks $600–1,200 (retail); existing sold estimate $150–600 — "Aqua Master" or "Royal" plate, two-stage can, two hoses.
- New-old-stock regulator in original box with untouched chrome and matching hoses — dealer calls it museum-grade, $5,000+ asking (retail) — box and paperwork, unscratched chrome, no water staining.
- Healthways SCUBA Gold Label and Blue Voit regulators — called highly collectible by the dealer (no price given) — gold label on Healthways can; blue-finished Voit can.
- Damaged or non-working DAAMs and Healthways SCUBA units — dealer asks $250–500 — still 3–5× a bare tank.
- Genuine US Navy Mark V helmet (DESCO, Morse, Miller-Dunn, Schrader) with matching serials — far above any regulator; the existing entry's $1,000+ tier — maker tag plate, serial numbers stamped on bonnet and breastplate, heavy tinned copper. Most brass helmets are reproductions; treat as decor unless the tag and serials check out.
- Empty steel 72 with backpack and no regulator — the common version ($20–80 local).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original regulators: chrome-plated brass cans with stamped names and serials. Tanks: stamped DOT/ICC and hydro dates on the neck; galvanized steel 72 cu ft common. More: original hoses are thick corrugated rubber (black on early units, yellow on some later US Divers); mouthpieces are rubber with a check-valve box. Original chrome is bright and even; resilvering or replated cans look too new. Later single-hose regulators have small first stages and plastic second stages. Steel tanks: galvanized (dull grey spangled zinc) or painted; aluminium tanks (from 1971) are thicker-walled and lighter. Reproduction helmets are thin polished brass with copper, crude soldering, fake "US Navy" tags and no matching serials.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rotted hoses, corroded cans, out-of-hydro tanks (cannot be filled without testing). Never pressurize. More: saltwater corrosion (especially on gear from Florida, California and Hawaii) inside the can, at the horn openings, the knife-edge and the first-stage inlet threads; perished diaphragms; missing parts (cans, diaphragm, mouthpiece, hoses, clamps, yoke). Replacement hoses and diaphragms exist for US Divers; Healthways and Sportways diaphragms are hard to find; early CG-45 parts are very hard to source. Before diving, a rebuild (duckbill, diaphragm, cracking-effort test) is needed; sell as display or for rebuild.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Brass hard-hat helmets are almost always reproductions. Regulators rarely faked. Aqua Lung itself issued a **Mistral 2005 Limited Edition** (made in France, numbered, to mark 50 years since the original Mistral); a modern reissue, not a 1950s unit — check the plate. Modern twin-hose replicas (e.g. Argonaut Kraken, 2016) are new production.

## Market
Small but active collector community (vintage double-hose divers). Tanks themselves are commodity. Buyers: vintage-gear divers who rebuild and dive double-hose regulators, display collectors, and nautical decorators. Scubaboard's vintage forum and specialist dealers set prices.

## What sellers usually miss
A regulator attached to an old tank or in a box of "old dive stuff". Also the model nameplate and serial (DA vs Royal vs Mistral), and early features like the West Pico Blvd plate.

## Shipping / handling
Steel tanks 25-35 lb; must be empty with valve removed or open; carriers restrict pressurized cylinders. $30-45. Regulators alone ship easily (3–5 lb, $15–20); pad the can and don't kink the hoses.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Empty, valve open, hydro date, maker, display-only. For regulators: model, serial number, nameplate photo, both cans and inside, hoses, mouthpiece; say "not tested for diving, sold for display or rebuild".

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] [model] double-hose regulator, serial [#], [era from plate]. Complete with both can halves, clamp, [original/replacement] hoses and mouthpiece. Chrome [condition]; inside the can [clean / light corrosion]. Not serviced — sold for display or professional rebuild, not for diving as is. [Tank: empty, valve open, last hydro [date], display only.]"

## Buy rule
Tanks alone fail profit math (heavy, ~$50-80 resale). Look for regulators.
