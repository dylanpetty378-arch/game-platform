## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Clear glass gift pieces: bowls, trays, candy dishes, vases, tumblers, decanters. Three groups:
- **Modern giftware crystal** (1970s–2010s): Godinger (Shannon, Dublin), Mikasa, Gorham (Lady Anne, Althea), Lenox, Marquis by Waterford, Waterford, Cristal d'Arques (France, lead crystal, "Longchamp" etc.), Princess House (Highlights, Heritage), Fifth Avenue, Fifth Avenue Crystal, Bohemian/Polish cut crystal. Usually star-burst, diamond and fan cuts.
- **Pressed glass "imitation cut"** (1900s–60s): Anchor Hocking, Jeannette, Indiana Glass, Federal; vanity trays with starbursts, sometimes with a silvered underside or metal frame (like the tray in our sweep).
- **Early American Pattern Glass (EAPG)** (1850s–1915): pressed patterns by US Glass, Duncan, Heisey, Westmoreland, McKee, often with ruby stain or gold and enamel flowers (like the tumbler in our sweep).
- **American Brilliant Period cut glass** (1876–1917): Hawkes, Libbey, Dorflinger, Clark, Hoare; deep, sharp hand cuts, very heavy, often signed with acid stamps. Valuable.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Godinger** (NY, 1973–present), Shannon brand (Ireland-branded but made in Europe/Asia): low resale $5–25.
- **Waterford** (Ireland 1947–2009, then Slovenia/Czech/Hungary): acid-etched "Waterford" script (and seahorse on early pieces). Irish pieces (Lismore, Colleen, Alana) $20–150 each; vases and bowls $40–250.
- **Lenox / Gorham / Mikasa / Marquis**: etched or sticker; $10–60.
- **Cristal d'Arques / J.G. Durand**: $5–20.
- **Heisey** (Newark OH, 1896–1957): H-in-diamond mark; $15–200. **Fostoria**, **Cambridge**: see etched-glass-stemware.
- **US Glass (1891–1960s)** EAPG states patterns ("Pennsylvania", "Delaware", "Bohemian"/"Floradora" with rose-stained flowers) $15–60 per tumbler/goblet.
- **ABP cut glass**: Hawkes (trefoil with hawks), Libbey (sword logo), Hoare, Dorflinger (unsigned mostly). $80–1,500.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Lead crystal: heavy, rings when tapped, brilliant; cuts have sharp edges you can feel; polished base.
- Pressed glass: mould seams on corners, rounded "cut" edges, slightly wavy surface, lighter, dull thud.
- ABP: deep miter cuts, hobstars, very heavy, fine "orange peel" not present; signature faint (breathe on it).
- EAPG stain: ruby, amber or rose flash applied in bands or flower medallions; worn gold on rims is typical; check for chips on the pattern points.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on points and rims (ground down repairs leave flat rims), cloudy "sick" glass, scratches on bases, worn stain and gold, cracks radiating from chips, missing lids/stoppers. Silvered/mirrored bases flake (the tray in our sweep shows residue).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- EAPG reproductions (L.G. Wright, Westmoreland, Mosser, Summit) in clear and colours, with crisp moulding and wrong-feeling glass.
- Fake ABP: pressed "near cut" (Imperial "Nucut", Heisey) sold as cut glass; modern Asian/East-European cut crystal sold as "brilliant".
- Fake Waterford marks rare; "Waterford-style" listings common.

## Market
Huge supply, weak demand for modern giftware (thrift-store staple). Waterford and ABP have steady buyers. EAPG is soft (aging collector base) except rare patterns and colours.

## What sellers usually miss
- Acid-etched marks (Waterford, Hawkes, Libbey) on the base — need a base photo.
- ABP cut glass mixed in "crystal bowl" listings; feel weight.
- Reverse: sellers call pressed glass "antique crystal 1800s" (as in our sweep).

## Shipping / handling
Fragile and heavy. Small pieces $12–16; bowls/trays 3–6 lb $18–28 double-boxed.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker and pattern (Replacements name), dimensions, weight, mark photo, cut vs pressed, chip check.

## Buy rule
Shipping ~$15–25 each way kills anything under ~$100 sold. Buy only signed ABP cut glass or Waterford Irish vases/bowls at ≤ 25% of sold. Pass on pressed giftware and EAPG singles over $10. Passenger-seat fits all.
