Slug: hummel-figurines · Aliases: Hummels, Goebel figurines, Hummel lot, German figurines, bee mark figurines, MJ Hummel
Summary: common TMK-5/6/7 (1972–2000s) small figures $10–30; TMK-3/4 (1960–72) $20–60; TMK-1/2 crown and full-bee (1935–59) $40–200; large/rare/early or display pieces $150–1,500+; bulk lots average $10–25/figure

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glazed earthenware figurines of rosy-cheeked Bavarian children, based on drawings by Sister Maria Innocentia (Berta) Hummel, made by W. Goebel Porzellanfabrik from 1935. Matte pastel glaze, incised "M.I. Hummel" signature on the base, oval bases, model number ("Hum 1" to 2000s) incised underneath.

## Makers, brands and variants
Dating by trademark (TMK) on the underside:
- **TMK-1 Crown** (1935–49): crown with WG; "Germany" or "US Zone". Highest value.
- **TMK-2 Full Bee** (1950–59): large bee inside a V; incised/stamped.
- **TMK-3 Stylized Bee** (1957–72).
- **TMK-4 Three Line** (1964–72): bee + "© W. Goebel W. Germany" in 3 lines.
- **TMK-5 Last Bee** (1972–79).
- **TMK-6 Missing Bee** (1979–91).
- **TMK-7 Hidden Bee** (1991–99), **TMK-8** (2000s), later Manufaktur Rödental (2009–) and Hummel Manufaktur.
Also "US Zone"/"Western Germany" marks (1946–49) add value. Club exclusives, annual plates/bells (low value), Christmas ornaments.
Imitations: Japanese "Hummel-like" (Napco, Lefton, Arnart), Herbert Dubler (Ars Sacra, USA 1940s), Beswick-like; Hummel plates lower value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real Goebel: earthenware with velvety matte glaze, fine painted eyes, incised signature at base rear, model number, TMK mark, artist initials. Fakes/imitations: glossy, sticker "Japan", no incised signature, crude paint.
Size: model numbers with "/0", "/I", "/II" etc. indicate size; larger sizes worth more.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on hats, fingers, umbrella edges; restored breaks (check under UV); crazing; paint loss. Repaired pieces sell at 30–50% of perfect.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Asian knock-offs and resin copies; Goebel itself reissued older models in later TMKs. Dubler pieces are not Goebel (some collect them). "Hummel" name on non-Goebel items.

## Market
Very high supply, aging buyers; singles slow except early marks. Lots sell reasonably to resellers. International buyers (Germany) help.

## What sellers usually miss
Crown/full bee marks mixed into lots; US Zone stamps; large sizes (/I, /II, /III).

## Shipping / handling
Each figure 3–6", 0.3–1 lb; ship double-boxed with each piece wrapped; lot of 25 = ~15–20 lb, $25–40. Breakage risk moderate. A lot of 25 fits in one or two banker boxes – passenger seat OK.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph each base mark and model number, list TMK per figure, chips/repairs.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Singles (TMK-5+) never qualify. Lots: if ~25 figures can be bought for ≤ $100, resale ~$350–500 as a lot or split → passes. Crown/full-bee singles ≤ $20. Must confirm price is for whole lot.
