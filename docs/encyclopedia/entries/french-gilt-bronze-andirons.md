## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pairs of decorative fireplace fronts in Louis XV (rococo: asymmetric C-scrolls, acanthus leaves, rocaille), Louis XVI (urns, rams' heads, laurel swags, fluted columns) or Napoleon III style, with an iron log bar bolted to the back of each. Real 19th-century ones: gilt bronze (ormolu) or gilt brass, hand-chased detail (crisp leaf veins, matte vs burnished contrast), rough sand-cast backs with file marks, verdigris/green corrosion where the gilt is worn, 11–18" tall, heavy (6–12 lb each). 20th-century repros (1950s–80s Italian/Spanish/Virginia Metalcrafters): smooth cast backs, soft detail, bright lacquered brass, often no log bars. Titles sellers use: "antique brass andirons", "French style andirons", "fireplace dogs", "fire dogs", "brass fireplace decorations".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Styles**: Louis XV (rococo), Louis XVI (neoclassical; rams' heads, urns), Napoleon III (eclectic revival). Cherub and lion figural fronts sell well.
- **Signed makers**: Bouhon, Barbedienne – top tier, 1stDibs territory.
- **Repro makers**: Virginia Metalcrafters, 1950s–80s Italian/Spanish workshops.
- **With matching fender (chenets + barre de foyer) and tools**: multiplies value ($1,195 sold Oct 2026).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Gilt bronze (ormolu, mercury-gilt on older pieces) vs gilt brass vs lacquered brass. Quality: hand-chasing, matte/burnished contrast, sand-cast rough backs with file marks, heavy weight, wrought or cast iron log bars bolted with nuts. Cheap: smooth backs, soft blurry detail, uniform bright lacquer, light weight. Verdigris where gilt has worn and rough back casting are proof of age, not flaws.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing log bars (big value drop), single fronts, worn gilt (acceptable, even desirable as age proof), broken/re-soldered finials, replaced bolts, over-polished or re-lacquered surfaces (general knowledge, verify). Ask for height and a back photo.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large 20th-c. repro output in the same styles; mid-century Italian/Spanish copies and Virginia Metalcrafters pieces are the usual look-alikes. Tell by back casting, chasing and weight.

## What sellers usually miss
"Brass" vs gilt bronze, and the period. They never write "chenets", "ormolu", "Louis XV", "rococo" – those words are what buyers search. The verdigris and rough back casting that look like flaws are the age proof. Ask for height and a back photo.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy: a pair with bars 15–25 lb boxed, $50–70 UPS Ground; bars unbolt (two nuts) to pack flat. Sell on eBay or Chairish; 1stDibs if signed. Scrap value is irrelevant (gilt brass, not solid bronze weight).
