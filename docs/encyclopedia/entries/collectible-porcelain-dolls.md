## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Two unrelated markets:
1. **Modern porcelain collector dolls (1975–2010)**: mass-made in Taiwan and China, sold through QVC, Franklin Mint, Ashton-Drake, Knickerbocker, Seymour Mann, Gorham and drugstores. They have glossy or semi-matte painted faces with very pink cheeks, often "sleep" or set acrylic eyes, cloth bodies with porcelain lower limbs, satin and lace costumes, pink or red-curtain window boxes, a COA with an edition number in the thousands, and a stand. Pierrot/harlequin, bride, Victorian girl and musical (wind-up) dolls are the common themes. **Almost all are worth $5–40.**
2. **Antique bisque (1870–1930)**: German (Armand Marseille, Kestner, Simon & Halbig, Heubach, Kammer & Reinhardt) and French (Jumeau, Bru, SFBJ). They have matte, unglazed bisque heads with fine pores, real glass eyes (often with painted lashes), open mouths with tiny teeth, and mohair or human-hair wigs. Bodies are composition and wood ball-jointed, or kid leather. Marks are incised on the back of the head: "Germany 390 A 4 M", "S&H 1079", "Jumeau", "DEP". These sell for $75–3,000+.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Seymour Mann** (NY importer, 1980s–2000s): "Connoisseur Collection" Pierrots, clowns and Victorian girls. Tag on the body or a neck mark. $5–30.
- **Gorham** (1980s dolls by Gorham's giftware arm): "Doll of the Month", musical dolls such as Jillian, Allison and Ashley (1981–83, limited to 1,000–7,500), Susan Stone Aiken designs. $15–60; boxed with COA at the high end.
- **Effanbee** (US, 1910–): vinyl and porcelain. "Great Moments in Literature" (1984–85: Becky Thatcher, Huck Finn, Tom Sawyer), Legend Series. Mostly vinyl, $15–40.
- **Ashton-Drake Galleries** (1985–): porcelain and vinyl babies by named artists (Yolanda Bello, Cindy McClure, Waltraud Hanl). Mostly $15–60; certain lifelike babies $60–150.
- **Linda Rick "The Doll Maker"** (Precious Moments licensee; sold on HSN/QVC). Vinyl, porcelain and silicone-vinyl "I Love Baby / Baby Loves Me" babies with Bonnie Chyle. COA editions are limited to 150–500. Typical resale $25–80; certain limited silicone-vinyl babies $60–150 (estimate).
- **Bonnie Chyle**: reborn/lifelike artist; prototypes and full-silicone pieces fetch more.
- **Melissa Jane "Victorian Collection"**, "Genuine Porcelain Doll Victorian Collection" (Knickerbocker/Dandee-type boxed drugstore dolls, about 1994–2005): $5–20 boxed.
- **Dan-Dee / Ashley Belle, Heritage Mint, Alberon, Knightsbridge, Geppeddo/Lenox, Regency, Tuss, Walda, Danbury Mint (Shirley Temple)**: mostly $5–40. Danbury Mint Shirley Temple sometimes $40–80.
- **Antique**: Armand Marseille (1884–1950s; moulds 370 shoulder head, 390 socket head, both most common; googly 240/253 rare), J.D. Kestner, Simon & Halbig, Kammer & Reinhardt, Heubach (character faces), Jumeau and Bru (French, high value).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Antique bisque**: matte with tiny pores, crisp brows and feathered lashes, glass eyes set from inside the head, a pate (cork or cardboard) under the wig, and an incised mark at the neck. A mohair wig and a jointed composition body with a wooden ball-jointed limb set.
- **Modern porcelain**: glossy or satin finish, thick airbrushed colouring, a moulded neck mark like "© 1994 Knickerbocker" or a design number, acrylic eyes, and polyester lace with gold trim. Musical dolls have a wind-up key in the back.
- **Silicone/silicone-vinyl babies**: heavy, floppy, realistic veining, rooted hair, a COA from the artist. Full-silicone babies are much heavier than vinyl and command more.
- Original boxes and COAs help modern dolls a little ($5–15) and matter most for limited artist babies.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cracks or hairlines in porcelain (often hidden under hair at the crown or at the shoulder plate), chipped fingers, rubbed paint, faded or stained costumes, smoke odour, missing stands.
- Antique: replaced bodies or wigs, restrung elastic (acceptable), eyes reset, repainted bisque. Hairline cracks across the face cut value 50–80%.
- Silicone babies: tears, tackiness, stains and colour fade.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction "antique" bisque heads poured by hobbyists from 1970s–90s kits (Seeley's moulds) carry copied incised marks plus the hobbyist's name and year near the neck, sometimes scratched in. They are too smooth and uniform, with very white bisque.
- Chinese "antique style" dolls stamped with fake French marks.
- Modern porcelain sold as "antique" or "Victorian" in titles: "Victorian" usually means a 1990s costume style.

## Market
The modern collector-doll market collapsed after about 2005: tens of millions were sold as "investments", and estates are now flooding the market. Sell-through on eBay is low and prices hover around $10–25. Lifelike/reborn and silicone babies have an active niche market. Antique bisque has a steady but aging collector base; good examples sell well at specialist auctions.

## What sellers usually miss
- Most sellers overprice modern dolls (Lens "medians" of $40–175 are asks). The rare miss is an **antique bisque doll mixed into a lot of modern dolls**. Look for matte bisque, glass eyes, open mouths with teeth and a jointed composition body.
- Limited artist babies (Linda Rick/Bonnie Chyle silicone, low edition numbers) mixed into a lot of common dolls.
- Clothing and antique doll accessories (kid shoes, antique dresses) can be worth more than the modern doll.

## Shipping / handling
Modern dolls ship in their window boxes inside a larger box (16–24" dolls: box about 26×12×10", $18–30). Porcelain heads crack in transit: wrap the head separately in bubble wrap and immobilise it. Antique bisque must be double-boxed, with the head and hands padded. Silicone babies are heavy (4–8 lb); ship in a box with padding, never compressed.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the exact maker, doll name, edition number and the COA. Photograph the neck mark and body tag.
- Show close-ups of the face and hands for cracks or chips. Disclose odours and stains.
- Antique: give the incised mark, height, body type and eye type (sleep or set glass). Disclose any replaced wig or clothes.
- Silicone/lifelike: give the material (full silicone vs silicone-vinyl), weight and length.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price. Pass unless profit ≥ max($50, price). Tier A: profit ≥ 2× price.
- Modern porcelain collector dolls: median about $20, so never a buy at any price (shipping alone is $35+ each way for boxed dolls).
- Antique bisque: buy when photos show a matte bisque head, glass eyes and an incised German/French mark, the price is ≤ $40, and the doll is ≥ 14". Pass on cracked faces.
- Artist silicone babies: buy only with the COA visible, at ≤ $30, with a sold comp ≥ $120.
- All dolls fit a passenger seat. No authentication is needed for these tiers (high-end French bisque would need it: avoid).
