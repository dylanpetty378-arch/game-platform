## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lamps with shades made of many small pieces of coloured glass joined by lead came or copper foil and solder ("leaded"/"stained glass"), or bent "slag" glass panels (opaque streaky glass — caramel, green, purple) set in a cast-metal frame with pierced overlay.
- **Real Tiffany Studios (1893–1932)**: small irregular pieces following natural forms (dragonfly, wisteria, peony), "TIFFANY STUDIOS NEW YORK" stamped tag on the shade rim and base numbered; bronze base with rich patina. Almost never on Craigslist for under $200.
- **Antique panel lamps (1900–1930)**: Miller, Bradley & Hubbard, Salem, Moe Bridges, Handel: bent caramel/green slag panels behind cast white-metal or bronze filigree overlay, often marked on the base.
- **Hobbyist and 1970s copper-foil shades**: geometric grids of squares and rectangles, thick lumpy silver solder beads, swag ("Tiffany swag hanging lamp") shapes from 1970s kits and Dale Tiffany/Quoizel imports.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old work: lead came or copper foil with darkened patinated (brown/black or green) solder, tight even seams, glass with mottles/confetti/ripple, irregular organic shapes, heavy bronze base. Hobby/import: bright silver solder, beady uneven seams, mostly square/rectangular pieces, uniform streaky glass, light zinc or resin base. Shade aperture and fitter: antique leaded shades rest on a "spider" or "heat cap"; modern hobby shades use a vase-cap ring for a harp.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked pieces (common; replacement is a pro job), sagging/slumping shades (old leaded shades deform), broken solder seams, missing panels, rewired cords (expected), corroded sockets. Repairs make many shades worthless for Dylan (no repairs rule).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Tiffany Studios tags on Chinese reproductions are rampant; any "Tiffany" signature on a <$500 lamp is almost certainly fake. Handel reproduction tags too. Requires professional authentication for real Tiffany/Handel: outside Dylan's rules.

## Market
Loose modern shades sell slowly (6–12 weeks), local pickup favoured due to size/weight. Antique slag lamps sell steadily to Arts and Crafts collectors.

## What sellers usually miss
An antique cast-overlay slag lamp sold as "old lamp needs cord"; a Handel tag on a shade rim (under the lip); Pairpoint marks on bases. Sellers usually OVER-price modern Tiffany-style as "Tiffany".

## Shipping / handling
Leaded shades are heavy (5–15 lb) and fragile: custom double box, shade suspended, $25–45 ground. Breakage risk high.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Diameter, height, aperture size, weight, panel count, any cracked pieces, solder colour, whether it is hand-made hobby work or an import; never use "Tiffany" except "Tiffany-style".

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$30 to buyer − ~$30 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A $40 shade needs a ~$175 median: no hobby/modern shade qualifies. Buy only antique slag panel lamps with intact panels or signed Dale Tiffany/Meyda pieces at ≤ 20% of tier median, small enough for the passenger seat, local pickup preferred. No real Tiffany/Handel speculation (needs authentication).
