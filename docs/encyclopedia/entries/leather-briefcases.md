Buy rule (short): buy only marked Swaine Adeney/Brigg/Ghurka/Papworth/Made-in-USA Coach at ≤ $100, or unmarked English bridle-leather pieces at ≤ $40 in clean condition; designer authentication-heavy brands (Hermès, LV, Bottega) are excluded.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thick vegetable-tanned leather, saddle-stitched edges (double row of visible stitching), solid brass/nickel hardware, often a key lock clasp with maker name stamped on the lock plate ("Made in England", "Swaine Adeney", "Brady", "Papworth"). English school/briefcase satchels: flap with two pointed buckle straps, central lock, top handle. Generic: thin, glued, corrected-grain leather, plastic-feel.

## Makers, brands and variants
Swaine Adeney Brigg, Papworth, Brady, Mulberry (pre-2000), Hartmann (belting leather), Schlesinger Bros (NYC), Coach (made in NYC/USA pre-1990s), Ghurka, Filson, Saddleback (modern), Leather Satchel Co., unbranded English makers.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bridle/saddle leather (stiff, waxy, ages to honey patina); hand saddle-stitch; lock with brand stamp; heavy hardware. Old vs new: patinated edges, worn hand-sewn thread, brass not plated.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Scuffs (buyers accept), paint stains, cracked handle, broken buckle straps, missing key, mould/odour, dried-out leather (conditioning is light prep, not repair).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Indian/Pakistani "vintage style" satchels (Etsy) that look old; check for glued edges and uniform machine stitch.
