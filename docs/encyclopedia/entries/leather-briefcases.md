## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thick vegetable-tanned leather, saddle-stitched edges (double row of visible stitching), solid brass/nickel hardware, often a key lock clasp with maker name stamped on the lock plate ("Made in England", "Swaine Adeney", "Brady", "Papworth"). English school/briefcase satchels: flap with two pointed buckle straps, central lock, top handle. Generic: thin, glued, corrected-grain leather, plastic-feel.

Main shapes and their title words:
- **Flap briefcase / satchel** (one or two buckle straps, 1–3 gussets): "English satchel", "school bag", "double strap briefcase".
- **Frame bag / doctor's bag / Gladstone**: hinged metal frame mouth, often with a lock; "doctor bag", "lawyer bag".
- **Attaché** (hard or semi-rigid, boxy, combination or key locks): Hartmann belting leather, Swaine Adeney attaché cases.
- **Top-zip or messenger**: Coach, Ghurka, Filson; leather or twill with leather trim.

Where marks sit: lock plate or clasp face; blind-stamped (no colour) inside the flap or on the back panel; a cloth or leather label inside the main gusset; a hang tag (Coach, Hartmann); a creed patch (Coach); a numbered tag or stamp (Ghurka).

### Quick ID checklist
- Look for a maker stamp on the lock plate, under the flap, or on the inside back wall; ask for it if not shown.
- Edges: hand saddle stitch (slanted, evenly spaced, two rows) and burnished or painted edges = quality; raw, glued, rolled-plastic edges = cheap.
- Leather thickness at the flap edge: thick (≥ 3 mm) bridle/belting leather stands stiff; thin corrected-grain leather slumps and looks plasticky.
- Hardware: solid brass shows brass at wear points; plated hardware flakes to grey base metal.
- Coach: a leather creed patch with "Made in New York City" (earlier) or "Made in United States" (mid-1980s on) and a serial; brass hardware; glove-tanned cowhide with wrapped seams.
- Ghurka: chestnut leather or khaki/army twill with leather trim, brass hardware, a numbered model name (Express No. 2, Examiner No. 5) and a registration number.
- Hartmann: "Hartmann" on the lock, a leather Hartmann luggage tag, an impressed mark at a top corner, a cloth label inside.
- Smell and lining: ask about mildew/smoke odour and photos of the lining corners.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Swaine Adeney Brigg (London)**: bridle-leather briefcases and attachés (current named lines include the Westminster, Whitehall and Churchill attaché). Forum reports say SAB made briefcases for Brooks Brothers under the **Peal & Co.** label (unconfirmed); a Peal & Co. Brooks Brothers case can sell near SAB money (general knowledge, verify).
- **Papworth**: English bridle-leather briefcases; a forum poster reports some Papworth cases used second-grade leather (unconfirmed).
- **Brady**: English maker better known for fishing and game bags; Brady briefcases and satchels exist.
- **Mulberry (pre-2000)**: English-made, with a tree logo; older "Made in England" pieces are preferred.
- **Hartmann (Hartmann Trunk Co., USA)**: **belting leather** attachés and briefcases c.1960–1980, thick leather with stitched exterior, brass-buttoned leather fitted interiors, combination locks marked "Hartmann". Slim single-lock attachés are noted as scarcer.
- **Schlesinger Bros (NYC)**: US maker of leather briefcases (general knowledge, verify).
- **Coach (founded 1941 as Manhattan Leather Bags)**: US-made briefcases. The creed patch appeared in the mid-1970s (earlier bags carry only the logo). The "Made in New York City" stamp was phased out in the early 1980s in favour of "Made in the United States". Serials before 1994 follow no standard system, so they cannot be decoded; style numbers came later. Offshore production later lowers value. A US-made Coach flap briefcase in very good condition sold for $125 (Fedora Lounge thread, ~2025).
- **Ghurka**: founded 1975 by Marley and Linda Hodgson, using an old English tanning formula. Every design is numbered (Express No. 2 weekender, Examiner No. 5 briefcase, Expediter attaché, Market Tote No. 294). Each bag carries a registration number whose leading letters encode the production year (1978 onward). The **Marley Hodgson era (c.1975–2003)** is the most valued; Hodgson left after 2001 and sold in 2004. Later owners moved production to Asia; the company went through Chapter 11 in 2018 and was bought by a Cleveland group in 2019. Vintage Ghurka has strong demand in Japan.
- **Filson**: US twill and leather briefcases; older pieces with "C.C. Filson Co. Seattle" labels (general knowledge, verify).
- **Saddleback (modern)**, **Leather Satchel Co.**, unbranded English makers.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bridle/saddle leather (stiff, waxy, ages to honey patina); hand saddle-stitch; lock with brand stamp; heavy hardware. Old vs new: patinated edges, worn hand-sewn thread, brass not plated.
- **Bridle leather** is vegetable-tanned and stuffed with waxes and greases; a white "bloom" of wax on a stored English bag is normal and buffs off.
- **Belting leather** (Hartmann) is thick, firm cowhide originally made for machine belts; it holds a crisp shape.
- **Glove-tanned cowhide** (Coach) is supple, with seams wrapped in leather rather than raw-edged.
- **Ghurka** used aniline-dyed leather with hand-painted edges applied in several passes, plus heavy cotton twill.
- **Cheap production**: corrected-grain or bonded leather (uniform embossed grain, peeling surface), single-row machine stitch with plastic-looking thread, glued and painted raw edges, zinc hardware.
- **Old vs new**: brass wear to bare metal, softened handle wraps, thread worn in the stitch holes, older lock designs with keys; new "vintage style" pieces have perfect, uniform distressing.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Scuffs (buyers accept), paint stains, cracked handle, broken buckle straps, missing key, mould/odour, dried-out leather (conditioning is light prep, not repair). Also: torn or replaced linings, broken gusset stitching at the bottom corners, worn-through corners, failed combination locks (Hartmann attachés often come with an unknown combination and may not close perfectly), and replacement handles (new leather colour that does not match).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Indian/Pakistani "vintage style" satchels (Etsy) that look old; check for glued edges and uniform machine stitch. Coach and Ghurka copies exist: on Coach, check the creed wording and stitching around the patch; on Ghurka, check the model number and registration tag. Modern Ghurka reissues of original patterns (after 2019) are not vintage; check the registration year code.

## Market
Good demand from "dark academia"/heritage menswear buyers; 2–6 weeks. Vintage Ghurka has an active Japanese collector market.

## What sellers usually miss
Maker name inside the flap or on the lock plate; Coach bags with "Made in New York City" creed patch. Also the Ghurka model number and year code, and Hartmann "belting leather" (sellers list it simply as "old briefcase").

## Shipping / handling
16" × 12" × 4–6", 3–5 lb, $15–22.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Dimensions, maker mark, lock/key status, interior condition, smell, scuff photos.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] [model if known] in [bridle / belting / glove-tanned] leather, [made in England / New York City / USA], c.[decade] based on [creed patch wording / registration code / lock stamp]. Measures [W × H × D]. [Number] compartments, [buckles / lock] working, key [present / missing]. Honest wear: [scuffs, marks] shown in photos; lining [clean / worn]; no [odour / mildew]." Don't claim a date the mark cannot support (pre-1994 Coach serials do not decode).
