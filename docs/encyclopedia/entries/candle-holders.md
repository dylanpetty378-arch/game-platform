## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Any device that holds a candle: taper candlesticks (single or pairs), chambersticks (short with a finger-ring handle and drip pan), candelabra (multi-arm), pillar plates/prickets (a spike on a plate), votive holders, and floor/garden candle stands. Dating tells in photos:
- **Spike (pricket) on a stamped tin plate** = modern pillar-candle decor (1970s–present); example 7950325606 is a driftwood log with a stamped steel pricket plate.
- **Push-up rod** in the shaft of a brass candlestick = 18th–19th-century English/American; seam down the shaft (cast in two halves and brazed) = pre-1800s; solid-cast one-piece with a threaded tenon base = 19th–20th century.
- **Hand-hammered surfaces with visible planishing marks, rivets, and a stamped maker mark under the base** = Arts & Crafts (1900–1930).
- **Teak, cast iron, or enamelled steel with clean geometric forms** = Scandinavian/Danish modern 1955–1975. Dansk "Kobenstyle" and Quistgaard cast-iron "Tree" or "Lotus" candleholders with "DANSK DESIGNS … DENMARK" and "IHQ" marks.
- **Thin stamped copper/brass with chased lines, a tinned (silvery) inner wash, and an attached strap handle** = Middle-Eastern bazaar ware, 1940s–1970s (example 7937592260; "United Arab Republic" marks = Egypt/Syria 1958–1971).
- **Bent welded steel rod with powder-coat paint and a moulded glass "sun face"** = 1990s–2000s import garden décor (example 7982359308).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Arts & Crafts metal (top tier)**: Jarvie (Chicago, 1904–1917, cast brass/bronze candlesticks named with Greek letters, mark "Jarvie" script) $1,000–6,000 each; Roycroft (East Aurora NY, orb-and-cross mark) $150–800; Gustav Stickley (Craftsman Workshops, joiner's compass mark) $300–1,500; Dirk van Erp (San Francisco windmill mark) $1,500+; Old Mission Kopperkraft, Benedict Studios $60–250.
- **Silver**: Georg Jensen sterling (Denmark, crowned mark + design numbers) $500–5,000; American "weighted" sterling (Gorham, Towle, Duchin) – filled with pitch/plaster, only $20–80 a pair because the silver weight is tiny – a common seller overestimate.
- **Scandinavian modern**: Dansk (Quistgaard; cast iron, teak, enamel) $40–250; Kosta Boda / Orrefors / Holmegaard glass $30–150; Nambé (alloy, signed) $25–80; Arabia; Kay Bojesen; Stelton.
- **Colonial Revival brass**: Virginia Metalcrafters (marked, Colonial Williamsburg reproductions) $30–90 pair; Baldwin (Reading PA) $30–80 pair; Stiffel.
- **Elegant/Depression glass**: Fostoria, Heisey (diamond H mark), Cambridge, Imperial Candlewick, Fenton – $15–120 pair; covered in "depression-and-elegant-glass".
- **Pottery**: Fiesta bulb and tripod candleholders (see fiesta-ware), Roseville, Van Briggle, Weller $30–300.
- **Low/décor**: Partylite, Home Interiors, Yankee Candle, Pier 1, bazaar copper and brass, driftwood, garden stands – $5–30.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight: solid cast brass/bronze is heavy for its size; spun or stamped sheet is light and rings thin. Weighted sterling feels heavy but flexes at the thin skin and has a felt or cork-covered base.
- Hand-hammering: irregular facets and slight asymmetry = handmade; perfectly even dimpling = machine-pressed decor.
- Marks: always under the base or drip pan. A stamped maker mark + design number is the single best value tell.
- Patina: original dark brown patina on Arts & Crafts copper is valued; polishing to bright copper cuts value 30–50%.
- Tinned copper (silvery wash inside or worn through on outside) = utilitarian Middle-Eastern/Turkish/Indian ware, not American.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, split seams, bent shafts, missing bobeches/prisms on candelabra, re-soldered arms, broken pottery and glass chips (run a finger round the socket rims), wax damage (harmless), over-polishing, lacquer peeling on brass, re-drilled bases from lamp conversions (kills value).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Roycroft and Stickley marks are faked on modern hammered copper; check that the mark is crisp-stamped (not cast-in) and the forms match catalogue shapes.
- 18th-century English brass candlesticks heavily reproduced in India/Taiwan since the 1960s: no seam, bright brass, machine-turned threads.
- Jarvie has few fakes but many "Jarvie-style" Arts & Crafts candlesticks are unmarked and worth $40–150.
- Dansk reissued Quistgaard designs after 2000 – reissues carry modern country-of-origin marks (Thailand/China).

## Market
Huge supply of décor; low sell-through below $30. Collector demand is concentrated in Arts & Crafts, Danish modern and Georg Jensen. Seasonal bump Nov–Dec (holiday tables). Bulk garden stands are local pickup only.

## What sellers usually miss
- Unmarked-looking Arts & Crafts candlesticks whose mark is hidden under felt or the drip pan.
- Dansk cast iron candleholders sold as "old iron candle holder".
- Sellers more often over-estimate: driftwood, garden stands, bazaar copper and weighted sterling are routinely asked at 3–5× their sold value; Lens matches to Etsy/1stDibs asking prices make it worse.

## Shipping / handling
Pairs of brass/copper 2–6 lb, $12–20 boxed; glass and pottery need double boxing, $15–22. Driftwood is irregular and bulky. Garden stands are oversize – do not buy.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker as marked (Dansk, Quistgaard, Roycroft, Virginia Metalcrafters, Kosta Boda, Georg Jensen), material (brass, hammered copper, cast iron, glass), form (candlesticks pair, chamberstick), style (MCM, Arts & Crafts, Colonial), height, "pair". Junk: "hand hammered" without planishing marks, "sterling" on weighted, "antique" on Baldwin. `Dansk Jens Quistgaard Cast Iron Candle Holder Pair MCM Denmark 5 in Signed`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers; Arts & Crafts (Roycroft, Jarvie, Stickley) sells for more at Rago/LiveAuctioneers; décor and garden stands FB local only; list Nov–Dec for holiday tables.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (Dansk $40–150, Roycroft $175, weighted sterling pairs $50, VM/Baldwin brass pairs $30–90, décor $5–30), OBO, accept −20%, floor $10; cut after 30 days — below $30 sell-through is low.
- **Photos:** pair side-by-side; base marks (lift the felt or drip pan); socket close-up; planishing marks if hammered; patina or polish; dents and bends; height with ruler; pair on a scale.
- **State:** maker and mark, height, socket diameter (standard taper 7/8 in.), weight, metal, "hand-hammered" only with visible marks, polished or not, pair matched, dents, loose sockets by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** brass/copper pairs 2–6 lb $12–20; glass and pottery double-boxed $15–22; shoebox class; garden stands oversize, local only.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a signed [Dansk Quistgaard] [cast iron] pair, 1960s Denmark, [original finish]; signed Dansk pairs sell for $[40–150], while the unmarked import pairs are the $10 ones."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$15) − ship to Dylan (~$15) − price ≥ max($50, price). An item must sell for ≥ ~$95 to clear $50 at $0 cost, so only collectible and top tiers work. Buy: marked Arts & Crafts copper/brass (Roycroft, Stickley, Jarvie, Kopperkraft), Dansk/Quistgaard, Georg Jensen-style designer pieces with clear marks, at ≤ 1/3 of the sold median. Pass: driftwood, garden stands, souvenir/bazaar copper, weighted sterling, Partylite/Home Interiors, anything bigger than a passenger seat. Georg Jensen sterling may warrant authentication of marks – buy only with a clear hallmark photo. No repairs (bent arms, broken glass).
