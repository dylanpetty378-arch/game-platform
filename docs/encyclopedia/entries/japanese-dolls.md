## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Musha ningyo (gogatsu ningyo)**: warrior dolls displayed for Boys' Day (May 5): armor of silk brocade with lacquered "scales", kabuto helmet with kuwagata horns, bows/arrows/swords, often on a horse; cheeks plump with gofun (crushed shell) gesso faces and glass eyes.
- **Hina ningyo**: Girls' Day (March 3) Emperor/Empress dolls and court sets on tiered stands.
- **Ichimatsu**: child dolls with gofun skin, jointed body, real hair.
- **Gosho**: chubby white babies (Edo–Meiji), high value.
- **Hakata**: bisque/clay painted figures from Fukuoka, 1950s–80s souvenirs and fine-art ones.
- **Souvenir geisha/samurai** (1950s–90s): plastic or composition faces, synthetic brocade, glass cases with black lacquered base and gold-paper back; very common in US homes (occupation era to 1980s souvenirs).
- **Kokeshi**: turned wood dolls; traditional (dento) and creative (sosaku).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Yoshitoku** (Tokyo, since 1711), **Kyugetsu** (since 1835), **Mataro** (Kyoto) — tags or signed wooden fuda; mid-century pieces $150–600.
- Paper labels "Made in Japan", "Nishi" (Nishi & Co. importer) = export souvenir.
- Kokeshi artists: signatures on base (Sato Suigai, Kano Sansaku etc. for sosaku).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Gofun face: luminous, slightly translucent, layered white; fine crackle with age. Plastic face: uniform glossy, mold seam.
- Fabrics: real silk brocade with gold-wrapped thread vs. synthetic lamé.
- Details: real lacquered armor plates, metal fittings, glass eyes with natural set, real hair = quality. Paper armor, glued on gold foil, plastic accessories = souvenir.
- Glass display cases: the case adds little value but greatly increases shipping cost/risk.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Gofun cracks and chips (nose, fingers), insect damage to silk, faded fabrics, missing weapons/helmet ornaments, broken glass case panels. Moth damage common.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese-made "Japanese" dolls; souvenir dolls sold as "antique". Real antique dolls show hand-painted features, tissue paper stamped tags, and natural aging.

## Market
Slow, niche. Big Japan-sourced supply on eBay from Japanese sellers keeps prices low. Real antiques sell well at auction.

## What sellers usually miss
Gosho and ichimatsu dolls in plain "Japanese doll" listings; signed fuda tags; Meiji musha with lacquered armor.

## Shipping / handling
Dolls alone: $15–25 well-padded. With glass case: ship case separately or discard; risk high and size ~16×12×18 in.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Identify type (musha ningyo/hina/ichimatsu), face material (gofun vs plastic), fabrics, height, accessories present, era estimate, any tag/signature.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$25 ship out − ~$25 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Only gofun pre-war or signed dolls qualify; post-war souvenir dolls never do. Cased dolls fit a passenger seat but ship badly. No authentication needed (visual), but high ID difficulty.
