## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Scanners**: 1970s–80s crystal and early programmable scanners (Electra Bearcat BC101 1974–76, the first synthesised programmable scanner; Bearcat 210/220/250; Regency Touch; Realistic Pro-2004/2006 — the Pro-2004/2006 are the valued ones). Analog scanners are mostly obsolete in areas with digital trunked systems, which kills user demand.
- **Ham radios**: tube "boatanchors" (heavy, metal cabinets, plate/load tuning knobs = tube transmitter), 1970s hybrids (Kenwood TS-520, Yaesu FT-101), solid-state 1980s+ rigs, handhelds.
- Visible tells: brand plate, model, "Plate Tune/Ant Load" knobs (tube final), meter, crystals, mic with original connector, manuals.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Collins** (Cedar Rapids): S-line, KWM-2 — premium.
- **Drake** (Ohio): TR-4, R-4 series. **Hallicrafters** (Chicago): S-38, SX-series receivers. **Heathkit** (kits, quality varies with builder). **Swan**, **National**, **Hammarlund**.
- **Lafayette Radio Electronics** (NY, 1950s–80s): HA-series ham gear (HA-460 6-metre AM transceiver, HA-410 10 m, HA-800 receiver), mostly Japanese-made; modest value.
- **Kenwood/Trio**, **Yaesu**, **Icom**: dominant 1970s–today; solid-state rigs hold value.
- **Electra Bearcat** (Cumberland IN), later **Uniden Bearcat**; **Regency**, **Realistic (Radio Shack)**.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original knobs, intact meters, clean cabinets, original mic and manual add value. Modded or kit-built units vary widely.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dried electrolytic capacitors, burnt power resistors, weak tubes, drifting VFOs, dead displays/LEDs, broken dial cords, scratched cabinets. "Powers on" ≠ working. Repairs need a technician (outside Dylan's rules).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None; watch for parts radios sold as working.

## Market
Active ham buyers on eBay, QRZ swapmeet and hamfests; they pay for tested gear and steeply discount "untested". Old scanners have weak demand.

## What sellers usually miss
Estate-sale ham shacks sometimes include modern Kenwood/Yaesu/Icom gear priced like old radios; accessories (Collins mics, Heil, Bencher keys) are valuable small items.

## Shipping / handling
Tube gear 15–40 lb, fragile: $40–80 shipped, often damaged. Scanners 5–8 lb, $20–30.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model, serial, condition, tested functions (receive/transmit, power out), included accessories, manuals.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price ≥ max($50, price). Untested electronics are excluded by Dylan's rules. Only working modern handheld/mobile rigs with video proof and confirmed comps ≥ $200 at ≤ $60 qualify. Pass on scanners and boatanchors.
