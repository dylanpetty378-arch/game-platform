## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Scanners**: 1970s–80s crystal and early programmable scanners (Electra Bearcat BC101 1974–76, the first synthesised programmable scanner; Bearcat 210/220/250; Regency Touch; Realistic Pro-2004/2006 — the Pro-2004/2006 are the valued ones). Analog scanners are mostly obsolete in areas with digital trunked systems, which kills user demand.
  - **Crystal scanners** (Regency, early Bearcat III/IV, Realistic Pro-series of the early 70s) have a row of channel lamps and plug-in frequency crystals behind a door or under the lid; each channel needs its own crystal. Photo tell: a numbered strip of 4–16 small lamps and no keypad.
  - **Electra Bearcat 101** (Electra Co., Cumberland, Indiana; introduced late 1974/early 1975): 16 channels, a red-LED channel bank with individual lockout switches, chrome front bezel, built for car mounting with an AC version also made. Forum members recall a 1974 retail price of $399.99 at Lafayette (RadioReference). Electra was later bought by Uniden (c.1983 per forum recollection), so later "Uniden Bearcat" units are a different, much more common generation.
  - **Keypad scanners** (Bearcat 210/220/250, Regency Touch, Realistic Pro-2004/2005/2006): a numeric keypad and a red/green digital display = late 1970s–80s.
- **Ham radios**: tube "boatanchors" (heavy, metal cabinets, plate/load tuning knobs = tube transmitter), 1970s hybrids (Kenwood TS-520, Yaesu FT-101), solid-state 1980s+ rigs, handhelds.
  - Tube transmitter/transceiver tell: separate "PLATE" (tune) and "LOAD" knobs, a "GRID" or "DRIVE" control, and a meter switch for "Ip/Ig/PO". Receivers alone (Hallicrafters S-38/SX, Hammarlund HQ) have no plate/load controls and usually a slide-rule or round dial with a "Bandspread" knob.
  - Hybrid (1970s) tell: digital or analog VFO dial, tube final (still has plate/load), solid-state everything else — Kenwood TS-520/TS-820, Yaesu FT-101.
  - Solid-state (1980s+) tell: no plate/load knobs, digital frequency readout, often a "GENERAL COVERAGE" receiver.
- Visible tells: brand plate, model, "Plate Tune/Ant Load" knobs (tube final), meter, crystals, mic with original connector, manuals.
- **Lafayette HA-460** (the transceiver in the sweep): Japanese-made for Lafayette 1967–1970, 6-metre (50–52 MHz) AM only, ~12 W out (20 W input), runs on 117 VAC or 12 VDC, Hi-Z mic on a 4-pin connector, SO-239 antenna jack, 308×130×235 mm, 8.6 kg / 19 lb; launch price $149.95 (1967) falling to $109.95 (1969) (RigPix). Lafayette catalogue number 99-2579WX.
- Look-alikes: CB radios (23/40 channel, 27 MHz, "Channel" selector, no VFO) are often titled "ham radio"; shortwave broadcast portables (Zenith Trans-Oceanic, Sony ICF) are a different category; tube table radios ("AM/FM") are not ham gear.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Collins** (Cedar Rapids): S-line, KWM-2 — premium.
  - **KWM-2 / KWM-2A** HF SSB transceiver (from c.1959–60; Hug-a-Bug documents a serial 9XX unit with a January 1960 calibrator crystal and a 1963 unit at serial 12XXX). The KWM-2 drives a 30S-1 or 30L-1 amplifier without accessories; standard crystals covered the HF bands except part of 10 m (Hug-a-Bug).
  - **S-line** (75S-x receiver, 32S-x transmitter, 312B station control, 516F-2 power supply, 30L-1 amp). Emblem tells: **"winged emblem"** units are earlier; **"round emblem"** units are later and carry running improvements (Hug-a-Bug notes a late winged-emblem 75S-3B already having round-emblem upgrades). Collectors price winged-emblem, unmodified units higher (general knowledge, verify). The Collins Collectors Association keeps serial-number surveys per model (collinsradio.org).
  - Matching accessories (312B-4/5 console, 516F-2 supply, SM-2/SM-3 desk mics, MP-1 mobile supply) are sold separately and add value (general knowledge, verify).
- **Drake** (Ohio): TR-4, R-4 series. **Hallicrafters** (Chicago): S-38, SX-series receivers. **Heathkit** (kits, quality varies with builder). **Swan**, **National**, **Hammarlund**.
- **Lafayette Radio Electronics** (NY, 1950s–80s): HA-series ham gear (HA-460 6-metre AM transceiver, HA-410 10 m, HA-800 receiver), mostly Japanese-made; modest value.
- **Kenwood/Trio**, **Yaesu**, **Icom**: dominant 1970s–today; solid-state rigs hold value.
- **Electra Bearcat** (Cumberland IN), later **Uniden Bearcat**; **Regency**, **Realistic (Radio Shack)**.
- Value-moving nuances:
  - Collins: winged vs round emblem, serial range, unmodified ("never soldered") condition, matching station set.
  - Heathkit: builder quality (neat solder, no extra holes) — a sloppy build is a parts radio.
  - Scanners: Pro-2004/2005/2006 (wide coverage, moddable) are the only common 1980s scanners with user demand; crystal scanners are near-scrap unless boxed.
  - Any ham rig: original mic, power cord, manual and box add more than cosmetic cleaning.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the brand/model plate on the front panel; rear-panel serial plate on Collins and Drake.
- Plate/Load knobs present? → tube final (heavy, specialist repair).
- Digital readout and no plate/load → solid-state; look for Kenwood TS-/Yaesu FT-/Icom IC- prefix.
- Collins: winged emblem (earlier) or round emblem (later); note serial.
- Scanner: crystals and lamp strip = crystal scanner (low); keypad = programmable; "Electra" (not "Uniden") on a BC101 = 1974–76.
- Mic: original matching mic and connector (4-pin, 8-pin, Amphenol)?
- Back panel: any extra holes, non-original jacks, or cut wires = modified.
- Photo of it lit up and receiving is the minimum; transmit needs a power-meter reading.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original knobs, intact meters, clean cabinets, original mic and manual add value. Modded or kit-built units vary widely.
- Factory finishes: Collins grey wrinkle/"St. James grey" cabinets with aluminium panels; Hallicrafters black wrinkle; Drake two-tone grey; Kenwood/Yaesu 1970s champagne/grey panels (general knowledge, verify). Repainted cabinets (smooth paint over wrinkle) lower collector value.
- Chassis condition under the cabinet is the real quality tell: clean, unrusted, no scorch marks around power resistors or transformers.
- Modifications: extra holes, added jacks, replaced meters, "updated" capacitors. Collectors pay most for unmodified; users pay most for professionally recapped with documentation.
- Old vs new: date codes on electrolytic capacitors and tubes; inspection stamps; serial ranges (Collins).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dried electrolytic capacitors, burnt power resistors, weak tubes, drifting VFOs, dead displays/LEDs, broken dial cords, scratched cabinets. "Powers on" ≠ working. Repairs need a technician (outside Dylan's rules).
- Bearcat 101 specific: early units overheated and many were returned to Electra (fixed after about a year); burnt-out red LEDs common; bulging capacitors; sealed CPU and unavailable parts made factory repair impossible later (RadioReference forum).
- Hidden: transmit finals worn even when receive works; switching damage on band switches; missing crystals.
- Shipping damage: transformers tear loose inside tube gear; meters and glass dials crack.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None; watch for parts radios sold as working.
- Married stations (S-line pieces from different emblem eras or a non-matching supply) are common; check emblem and serial consistency.
- Reproduction knobs, dial glass and decals exist for Collins/Hallicrafters (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Active ham buyers on eBay, QRZ swapmeet and hamfests; they pay for tested gear and steeply discount "untested". Old scanners have weak demand.
- Collins buyers are a defined collector group (Collins Collectors Association) who check serials and emblem era.
- Parts buyers keep the market liquid for non-working units, but at parts prices.

## What sellers usually miss
Estate-sale ham shacks sometimes include modern Kenwood/Yaesu/Icom gear priced like old radios; accessories (Collins mics, Heil, Bencher keys) are valuable small items.
- Collins emblem era and serial, and whether matching S-line pieces are present.
- Crystals, manuals and original mics are sellable separately.

## Shipping / handling
Tube gear 15–40 lb, fragile: $40–80 shipped, often damaged. Scanners 5–8 lb, $20–30.
- Lafayette HA-460 alone is 19 lb unboxed (RigPix).
- Remove tubes or block them; double-box with 3 in of foam; tape knobs.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model, serial, condition, tested functions (receive/transmit, power out), included accessories, manuals.

### What to tell the buyer
State maker, model and serial; the era (e.g. "Lafayette HA-460, Japanese-made for Lafayette 1967–70, 6-metre AM, ~12 W out, 117 VAC or 12 VDC"; "Electra Bearcat 101, 16-channel programmable scanner, introduced 1974–75, pre-Uniden"). Say exactly what was tested and how ("powers on, receives local signals; transmit not tested") and never say "works" for a powers-on test. List included mic (with connector type), power cord, crystals and manual. For Collins, give the emblem type (winged/round) and whether it is unmodified. Note weight and that tube gear ships double-boxed.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price ≥ max($50, price). Untested electronics are excluded by Dylan's rules. Only working modern handheld/mobile rigs with video proof and confirmed comps ≥ $200 at ≤ $60 qualify. Pass on scanners and boatanchors.
