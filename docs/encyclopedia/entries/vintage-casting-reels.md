## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- 1930s–70s round-sided metal casting reels with a level-wind bar across the front, knurled end plates, and pearl or black handle knobs.
- The maker is stamped on the foot or end plate: Pflueger (Supreme, Akron, Nobby), Shakespeare (Criterion, Tournament), Langley, South Bend, Penn, Heddon, Ocean City, Bronson.
- The Great Lakes Products "Great Lakes" single-action trolling reel is a big spoked aluminium reel.
- Glass minnow traps: a gallon-size clear jar with a funnel neck moulded in. The ones embossed "Orvis" or "Joy" bring the most.
- Seller words: "vintage fishing reels lot", "old reels", "antique tackle".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Pflueger** (Akron OH): Supreme (the best mid-century casting reel; worth more in a leather case with wrench), Akron, Nobby, Medalist (fly; see fly-reels).
- **Shakespeare** (Kalamazoo): Criterion, Tournament; President (higher).
- **Langley** (San Diego): Streamlite.
- **Penn**: Peer.
- **Heddon**: 3-25 and other casting reels.
- **Meisselbach**: Tripart.
- **South Bend**: mid-range.
- **Bronson, Ocean City**: low.
- **Kentucky reels** (German-silver, hand-made, 1800s–early 1900s): B.F. Meek, B.C. Milam, J.F. & B.F. Meek, Talbot. Top tier: stop for anything engraved "Meek" or "Talbot".
- **ABU Ambassadeur 5000** red (Sweden, early serials) is top tier per this entry; see the note under Value ladder.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Nickel-plated brass, German silver and aluminium. Jewelled bearings and quadruple-multiplying gears in better reels (general knowledge, verify).
- Hand-made Kentucky reels: fine machining, a maker's name and town engraved, serial numbers.
- Leather cases, wrenches, oil and boxes add value. Pearl handle knobs are original on many better reels.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Seized gears, worn level-wind pawls, cracked pearl knobs, missing click buttons, heavy plating loss, dented pillars.
- Check that the spool turns and the level-wind tracks. Ask for photos of the foot stamping and end plates.
- Glass traps: chips on the funnel neck, cracks, missing wire bail or lid.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction glass minnow traps with "Orvis" embossing exist, and modern Asian casting reels are styled as vintage (general knowledge, verify).
- Married parts are common in lots.

## Market
Fishing-collectible buyers (NFLCC, ORCA members) and users. eBay is the main venue, plus Lang's auctions for high-end reels. Spring is the peak. Common reels are oversupplied (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Reel makers are only stamped on the foot or inside the end plate. A "lot of old reels" can hide a Pflueger Supreme or an Ambassadeur.
- Glass minnow traps are listed as "glass jar" or "minnow bottle" and are worth $40–80 by themselves.

## Shipping / handling
- Reels ~1 lb each; a 10-reel lot ships for $20–25. Glass minnow traps need double-boxing ($20). Resell individually on eBay; fees 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker, model, patent dates or serial stamped on the reel, whether the spool turns and the level-wind tracks, plating condition, case/wrench/box.
- Photograph the foot, both end plates, the handle and knobs, and the level-wind.
- Title keywords: "Vintage Pflueger Supreme Casting Reel with Case", "Antique Kentucky Reel Meek", "Orvis Glass Minnow Trap".

## Buy rule
Buy when: the lot price is ≤ $6/reel and at least one Pflueger Supreme / Ambassadeur / Heddon is visible; or a glass minnow trap is ≤ $25.
Pass when: the lot is plastic tackle boxes of modern lures; the seller already names Supreme/Ambassadeur.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
