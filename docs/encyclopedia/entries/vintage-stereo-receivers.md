## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1970s "monster" receivers: brushed-silver face, big tuning dial, wood case, weight 40–60 lb – Sansui G-series/9090/8080,
Pioneer SX-x50 (SX-1050/1250/1280), Marantz 22xx (blue-lit dial, gyro-touch tuning wheel), Kenwood KR-9600. 1979–85
"slim" silver receivers (Sansui R-30/50/70, Pioneer SX-5, Technics SA-xxx) are 20 lb, low watt, worth $80–150. Model number
is on the right of the dial glass or rear plate. Sellers write "vintage receiver", "Sansui receiver", "stereo",
"in storage". 1950s tube radio/record consoles are furniture, a separate (mostly low-value, local) market.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Marantz**: 2215/2230 decent; 2270/2285 collectible; 2325/2500/2600 top (general knowledge, verify 2325 tier).
- **Pioneer**: SX-650/750 decent; SX-1050/1250/1280 collectible; SX-1980 top.
- **Sansui**: 771/881 decent; 8080/9090/G-7700 collectible; G-33000 top; R-series slim junk tier.
- **Kenwood**: KR-6600 decent; KR-9600 collectible.
- **Technics** SA-series (slim junk–decent), **Yamaha CR** series, **Harman Kardon**, **Sherwood** (general knowledge,
  verify).
- **Lafayette**, **Realistic STA-xx** — junk tier.
- **McIntosh** — top tier.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Brushed-aluminium faceplates, glass dial, wood-veneer cases (original walnut vinyl or real wood). Weight is a quick
quality tell — monsters are 40–60 lb with big transformers. Original wood case, unbroken dial glass and working
meters/lamps add value. "Recapped" / serviced by a known tech raises price.

## Condition, damage and repairs
"Been in storage 20 years" = untested; capacitors/lamps usually need work, which Dylan does not do, so only tested units
qualify. Common faults: dead dial lamps, scratchy pots, one channel out, blown output transistors, broken dial string,
cracked faceplate glass, missing knobs. Ask for a video of it playing both channels and the dial lit.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No real fakes; watch for faceplate/knob swaps, LED lamp conversions (some buyers dislike) and units sold "serviced"
without proof (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Strong, active hi-fi collector market (Audiokarma, Audiogon, eBay, local). Tested monsters sell fast; untested sell
at a steep discount to repair techs. Local pickup sales avoid the shipping risk. Q4 holiday lift (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Model number – "Nice Sansui receiver" with one photo. Marantz 22xx are rarely mis-listed.

## Shipping / handling
Monster receivers 45–60 lb, double-box with foam – $60–90 and real damage risk; slim receivers 20 lb, $35. Resell on
eBay/Audiogon/Audiokarma, or locally.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State model, serial, tested status (both channels, AM/FM, phono, all lamps), service history (recap), wood case
condition, faceplate and glass condition. Photograph the front lit, the rear panel and serial, the inside if serviced.
Title: maker, model, "vintage stereo receiver", "tested working", "serviced"/"recapped", wattage.

## Buy rule
Buy when: tested working Marantz 22xx, Pioneer SX-x50 (1050+), Sansui 8080/9090/G-series ≤ $200. Pass when:
untested/"in storage", slim 1980s models, Lafayette/Realistic. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and
shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat (consoles do not).
