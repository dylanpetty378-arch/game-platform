## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1970s "monster" receivers: brushed-silver face, big tuning dial, wood case, weight 40–60 lb – Sansui G-series/9090/8080,
Pioneer SX-x50 (SX-1050/1250/1280), Marantz 22xx (blue-lit dial, gyro-touch tuning wheel), Kenwood KR-9600. 1979–85
"slim" silver receivers (Sansui R-30/50/70, Pioneer SX-5, Technics SA-xxx) are 20 lb, low watt, worth $80–150. Model number
is on the right of the dial glass or rear plate. Sellers write "vintage receiver", "Sansui receiver", "stereo",
"in storage". 1950s tube radio/record consoles are furniture, a separate (mostly low-value, local) market.

Reading the model number:
- **Marantz**: 20xx = smaller, 22xx = the main 1970s two-channel line, 23xx = bigger; within a series the last digits roughly equal watts per channel (2245 = 45 W, 2270 = 70 W, 2285B = 85 W, 2385 = 185 W; 2010 = 10 W, 2015 = 15 W) (Gear Patrol). A "B" suffix is a later revision (2285B 1977–80 has a silver-backed dial; the earlier 2285 a black dial).
- **Pioneer**: SX-x50 series (1976–77) is the classic silver-face generation; SX-x80 (1978–79, e.g. SX-980, SX-1980) is the top silver generation; higher number = more power (SX-980 = 80 W per channel; SX-1250 large, heavy with big transformer — Gear Patrol). Earlier SX-x2x/x3x units also exist (some gold/bronze faces — Audiokarma thread).
- **Sansui**: three/four-digit numbers (771, 881, 8080, 9090); "DB" suffix (8080DB, 9090DB, about 1977) adds Dolby FM; G-series (G-7700, G-9700, G-33000; ~1978–80) are the last big silver receivers; R-series are the slim 1980s units.
- **Kenwood**: KR-xxxx; KR-9600/KR-9400 are the big ones.
- **Quadraphonic** 4-channel receivers (Marantz 4xxx, Sansui QRX, Pioneer QX) — a separate niche (general knowledge, verify).

Look-alikes: 1980s black plastic "rack system" receivers, home-theatre AV receivers (1990s on, many inputs, remote), and integrated amplifiers (no tuner dial — e.g. Marantz 1060) or tuners (no speaker terminals).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Marantz**: 2215/2230 decent; 2270/2285 collectible; 2325/2500/2600 top (general knowledge, verify 2325 tier). Years: 2010 1972–73; 2245 1971–76; 2270 1971–75; 2285B 1977–80; 2385 1977–80 (Gear Patrol). Gear Patrol estimated used prices (retail/specialist, not eBay sold): 2010 ~$200+, 2245 ~$400+, 2270 ~$1,600+, 2285B ~$850+, 2385 ~$2,500+ (clean > $4,000).
- **Pioneer**: SX-650/750 decent; SX-1050/1250/1280 collectible; SX-1980 top. SX-1250 (1976) is large with a toroidal transformer and usually needs substantial restoration (Hudson Valley HiFi via Gear Patrol).
- **Sansui**: 771/881 decent; 8080/9090/G-7700 collectible; G-33000 top; R-series slim junk tier. 9090 rated 110 W; 9090DB/8080DB about a year later (~1977), overlapping production (Audiokarma).
- **Kenwood**: KR-6600 decent; KR-9600 collectible.
- **Technics** SA-series (slim junk–decent), **Yamaha CR** series, **Harman Kardon**, **Sherwood** (general knowledge,
  verify).
- **Lafayette**, **Realistic STA-xx** — junk tier.
- **McIntosh** — top tier (e.g. MAC 1700 hybrid receiver, 1967 — Gear Patrol).
- **Fisher** 1960s tube receivers (500C, 800C) — collectible but must be restored before use; modern wall voltage is higher than in the 1960s (Gear Patrol).

Dating: Sansui serials — 3rd digit = last digit of the year (decade from styling), 4th–5th digits = month, last four = sequence (e.g. 817062852 = June 1977 9090DB; Audiokarma forum, not every serial fits). Marantz and Pioneer serial schemes are discussed on Audiokarma; verify per model.

### Quick ID checklist
- Model number readable (dial glass, front panel or rear plate)? Without it, ask before anything else.
- Weight/size: monsters are 40–60 lb and deep; a 20 lb slim unit is the low tier.
- Dial lit in the photo (both meters and dial)? Dark dial = lamps out at minimum.
- Faceplate: brushed aluminium without pitting (pitting = damp basement storage — Gear Patrol); dial glass uncracked.
- All knobs present and matching (missing Marantz/Pioneer knobs are costly).
- Case: original wood/walnut-vinyl case present (Marantz wood cases sell separately — a 2270 walnut case was $189, Gear Patrol).
- Rear panel: all terminals, antenna connectors and AC outlets intact; serial plate present.
- Inside photo (if offered): dust, pet hair, corrosion, burnt parts or a hacked repair are red flags.
- Seller video showing both channels, all inputs and FM reception.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Brushed-aluminium faceplates, glass dial, wood-veneer cases (original walnut vinyl or real wood). Weight is a quick
quality tell — monsters are 40–60 lb with big transformers. Original wood case, unbroken dial glass and working
meters/lamps add value. "Recapped" / serviced by a known tech raises price.
More: Marantz look = blue dial lighting, brushed aluminium and serif fonts (Gear Patrol); Pioneer = blue-lit silver face with large signal/tuning meters; Sansui = black glass dial on silver with green/amber lighting (general knowledge, verify). LED lamp conversions are common and acceptable to many buyers but should be disclosed. A "restored" unit should come with a list of what was replaced (capacitors, lamps, dial cord, transistors).

## Condition, damage and repairs
"Been in storage 20 years" = untested; capacitors/lamps usually need work, which Dylan does not do, so only tested units
qualify. Common faults: dead dial lamps, scratchy pots, one channel out, blown output transistors, broken dial string,
cracked faceplate glass, missing knobs. Ask for a video of it playing both channels and the dial lit.
Tolerable: sticking buttons and slightly scratchy pots; avoid: a pot that cuts out either channel; dents and water stains suggest misuse; scratches are normal (Gear Patrol). Parts are widely available (LED lamps, capacitors, dial cords, faceplates, cabinets) and specialists service these units.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No real fakes; watch for faceplate/knob swaps, LED lamp conversions (some buyers dislike) and units sold "serviced"
without proof (general knowledge, verify). Replacement (non-original) wood cabinets are sold new and should be called out (Gear Patrol mentions replacement hardwood cabinets).

## Market
Strong, active hi-fi collector market (Audiokarma, Audiogon, eBay, local). Tested monsters sell fast; untested sell
at a steep discount to repair techs. Local pickup sales avoid the shipping risk. Q4 holiday lift (general knowledge, verify). Restored units sell at a premium through specialists and Reverb; the Marantz 2270 is Reverb's bestselling vintage receiver (Gear Patrol).

## What sellers usually miss
Model number – "Nice Sansui receiver" with one photo. Marantz 22xx are rarely mis-listed. Also: the original wood case (worth real money on its own), DB versions of Sansui, and recent service receipts.

## Shipping / handling
Monster receivers 45–60 lb, double-box with foam – $60–90 and real damage risk; slim receivers 20 lb, $35. Resell on
eBay/Audiogon/Audiokarma, or locally. Remove knobs or protect them; tape the wood case separately or pad the front so the faceplate and glass don't take the impact.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State model, serial, tested status (both channels, AM/FM, phono, all lamps), service history (recap), wood case
condition, faceplate and glass condition. Photograph the front lit, the rear panel and serial, the inside if serviced.
Title: maker, model, "vintage stereo receiver", "tested working", "serviced"/"recapped", wattage.

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker, model and serial (and the build date if decodable, e.g. Sansui serial), rated watts per channel, exactly what was tested (each input, both channels, FM/AM reception, phono, tape monitor, headphone jack, meters and lamps) and how. Say plainly whether it is original/unserviced or serviced, and list any work done (lamps to LED, recap, dial cord). Describe the faceplate (scratches, pitting), dial glass, knobs (all original?), and the case (original walnut vinyl/wood, replacement, or none). Include a video link of it playing. Keywords: vintage, stereo receiver, silver face, tested, 1970s, plus maker/model and watts.

## Buy rule
Buy when: tested working Marantz 22xx, Pioneer SX-x50 (1050+), Sansui 8080/9090/G-series ≤ $200. Pass when:
untested/"in storage", slim 1980s models, Lafayette/Realistic. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and
shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat (consoles do not).
