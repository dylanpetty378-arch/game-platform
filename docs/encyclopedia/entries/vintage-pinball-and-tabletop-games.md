## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Spring-plunger marble games played on an inclined board with pins/nails and scoring pockets – ancestors of coin-op pinball (1930s craze). Typical: 10–24 in long, wood or lithographed-tin board, glass or acetate cover (Poosh-M-Up), steel balls or marbles, plunger at bottom right, legs or a kickstand.
- **Poosh-M-Up** (Northwestern Products Co., St. Louis, 1930s–1960s): wood frame, bright printed paper/masonite playfield, glass cover, name in big letters ("Poosh-M-Up Jr.", "Big 4", "Slugger", "Double Track", "3-in-1"). Some had a "pin-ball" scoring dial.
- **Wolverine Supply & Mfg. Co.** (Pittsburgh, 1903–): lithographed tin pinballs, 1930s–1970s, colorful themes (space, cowboys, sports, "Pin Ball No. 99"-type numbering), wire legs, plastic covers on later ones.
- **Bagatelle**: older English/American parlor game, wooden board with pins and cups; tabletop versions 12–24 in; Victorian full-length boards are furniture-sized (pass).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Northwestern Products** – Poosh-M-Up line; earliest have rich lithographed paper and wooden plungers; later ones Masonite and plastic parts.
- **Wolverine** – tin pinballs, also "Sunny Andy"-era toys; marked "Wolverine Supply & Mfg Co Pittsburgh PA, Made in USA".
- **Louis Marx & Co.** – tin pinballs 1950s–60s (Marx logo), "Marx Pin Ball", character themes (e.g., cowboys, Popeye-type licenses – licensed ones worth more).
- **Durable Toy & Novelty** (New York), **J. Pressman**, **Lindstrom** (Bridgeport CT), **Chad Valley** (UK), **Ohio Art** – tin/wood games.
- Character/licensed (Disney, space, Western heroes) and baseball themes command premiums.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Value tells: bright unfaded litho, original glass cover intact, original plunger and spring, all balls/marbles, original box, scoring cards. Rust on tin, missing glass, replaced plungers (nails/rubber bands) drop it to $10–20. 1930s pieces: thicker wood, paper playfields, cast or turned plungers; 1960s+: plastic legs, plastic dome, thinner tin.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked/missing glass, faded/water-damaged playfield, bent pins, rusted tin, broken springs, missing balls, warped wood. No repairs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes. Modern "nostalgia" tin pinballs (Schylling, Made in China, 1990s–present) mimic Wolverine – look for "Schylling" or modern safety warnings. Bagatelle reproductions sold as decor.

## Market
Small but steady nostalgia market; decorators like the graphics. Moderate volume; common pieces take weeks. Q4 gift bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers rarely misprice badly; most ask $40–80 for common Poosh-M-Ups. Underpricing happens in toy/garage lots ("old marble game") where a boxed or licensed piece is hidden.

## Shipping / handling
Glass covers break – tape an X on glass, wrap, or remove and pack separately. Medium box, 3–6 lb, $15–22. Fits a passenger seat.

- **Title words:** maker + model name (Poosh-M-Up Big 4, Marx Pin Ball) + "tabletop pinball"/"bagatelle" + theme (baseball, space, character) + decade + "complete" + "tin litho". Junk: "marble game", "old toy", "pinball machine". Example: `Vintage 1950s Northwestern Poosh-M-Up Big 4 Tabletop Pinball Game Complete Tin Litho`.

- **Price position:** list at the sold median (common Poosh-M-Up Jr./Wolverine $25–60, Big 4/Marx licensed/space $50–100, boxed early or mint licensed $80–150); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) full overhead of the playfield; then plunger, back markings/maker label, glass edge-on for cracks, corners and legs, balls counted, box if present; short video of a ball shot and scoring.
- **State:** maker, model name, approximate decade, size, complete or missing parts (balls, plunger, glass), playfield graphics condition, glass intact, box, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 3–6 lb; tape an X on the glass or remove and pack it separately, playfield face-up with foam, single medium box with 2" padding, $15–22.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A [Poosh-M-Up Big 4 / Marx licensed] with all [N] balls and the glass intact in photo [4] — the plain bagatelles and Schylling repros are the $15 games; complete [model] examples sell for [$X]."
