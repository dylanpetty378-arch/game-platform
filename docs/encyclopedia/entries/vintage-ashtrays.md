## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Ashtrays were produced in enormous numbers from the 1920s to the 1980s by potteries, glasshouses and souvenir makers, and they were given away by restaurants, hotels, casinos, airlines and businesses. Almost all are worth $5–25. The money is in design-name pieces you can spot from the photo:
- **Fornasetti** (Milan): porcelain with black-and-white transfer prints (Lina Cavalieri face, suns, playing cards, architecture), gold rims, "FORNASETTI MILANO" on the back. $150–600.
- **Bitossi / Aldo Londi** (Italy): "Rimini Blu" deep blue glaze with incised geometric or fish and face motifs. $100–400.
- **Murano Sommerso / bullicante** (Seguso, Flavio Poli, Cenedese): heavy layered colour cased in clear glass, thick walls, often a geode or bowl form. Large signed or labelled pieces $60–300. Small 1970s souvenir "Murano" $15–40.
- **Higgins Glass** (Michael and Frances Higgins, Chicago): fused, laminated glass with a gold signature on the face ("higgins" lowercase script). $60–250.
- **Georges Briard:** gold-leaf and enamel glass, signed in gold. $25–80.
- **Lalique:** frosted crystal, acid-etched "Lalique France". $150–500. **Baccarat:** crystal, acid stamp of a circle with decanter, glass and goblet. $100–400+.
- **Royal Haeger, Raymor, Bitossi for Raymor** (labelled "Raymor Italy"), **Gambone**, **Jens Quistgaard**, **Arne Jacobsen** (Stelton cylinder): $40–200.
- **Figural or mechanical:** Art Deco bronze nudes (Armor Bronze, Frankart), Belmet "Rollodor" rolling ashtrays, push-button "Spin-o" chrome, Tiffany silver. $100–1,000+.
- **Low-end staples:** Snuf-A-Rette (National Porcelain, Trenton NJ), Shawnee, souvenir china (Japan, Czechoslovakia), milk glass, Anchor Hocking/Fire-King, and restaurant ironstone (Syracuse, Shenango, Buffalo, Mayer). Also onyx/alabaster from Pakistan, Mexico and Italy.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **National Porcelain Co. "Snuf-A-Rette"** (Trenton NJ, c.1930s–50s): rectangular ceramic safety ashtrays with eight "snuffer" cups along the sides. Colours: cobalt, sky blue, pink, green, beige, black, white. Many have railroad, hotel or company decals (Denver & Rio Grande Western etc.), which add $5–15. Mark on base "SNUF-A-RETTE / Pat. … / Made in USA".
- **Shawnee Pottery** (Zanesville OH, 1937–61): 1950s Kenwood/Lobster ware. Ashtrays are low ($10–25).
- **Fostoria, Heisey, Cambridge, Imperial** (US pressed and cut crystal): $10–40. Heisey "diamond H" mark adds some.
- **Fire-King, Anchor Hocking, Hazel Atlas**: milk glass, Jadeite, swedish modern. Jadeite ashtrays $25–60. Others $5–15.
- **Casino and hotel ashtrays:** 1950s–60s Las Vegas (Sands, Desert Inn, Flamingo, Dunes, Stardust) $20–60, closed casinos more. Modern casino ashtrays $5–15.
- **Restaurant and nightclub ashtrays** with an exchange-name phone number (TE 8-, PL 3-) date to before about 1965. Famous NYC/LA clubs (Stork Club, 21, Copacabana, El Morocco) sell $30–100. Ordinary diners sell $8–20.
- **Onyx / alabaster:** mostly Pakistani, Mexican and Italian (Volterra alabaster) tourist and gift-shop wares, 1960s–80s. $10–35 unless mounted in bronze or by a named maker.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight and thickness: art glass is heavy, with polished ground bases. Cheap pressed glass has mould seams and a rough base ring.
- Signatures: Higgins gold script on the surface, Fornasetti back stamp, Lalique and Baccarat acid marks on the base. Ask for a base photo.
- Glazes: Bitossi's deep "Rimini Blu" with incised lines is distinctive. Fornasetti's printed black transfers have fine engraved-style lines.
- Murano labels (foil "Murano Glass Made in Italy") are often lost. Heavy Sommerso with clean colour layering and a polished bottom is the tell.
- Onyx: banded green with honey or brown veins, cold to the touch, translucent at thin edges. Glass imitations show bubbles and mould lines.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips on rests and rims are the most common flaw and halve the value. Fleabites on art glass matter.
- Crazing and nicotine staining inside the crazing on ceramics (Shawnee, Snuf-A-Rette) can't be cleaned out completely.
- Cracks in onyx and alabaster along natural veins, and glued repairs on onyx (check the photos for a dark line).
- Worn gold on Briard, Fornasetti and souvenir pieces. Burn marks.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Fornasetti is still made today. Modern pieces are marked "Fornasetti Milano Made in Italy" with a current-style label and sell at retail ($150–250 new), so vintage carries only a modest premium. Knock-off "Fornasetti-style" Cavalieri face plates are common and unsigned.
- Chinese "Murano-style" glass is heavy but has sloppy colour and no polished base.
- Reproduction casino and Coca-Cola ashtrays exist.

## Market
Very high supply. Thousands of ashtrays are listed, and most common ones sell slowly at $8–20 or not at all. MCM design pieces (Bitossi, Fornasetti, Higgins, Murano) sell quickly to décor buyers on eBay, Chairish and Etsy. Casino and breweriana pieces have dedicated collectors. Smoking-related décor is not seasonal.

## What sellers usually miss
- **Higgins gold signature** on the top surface and **Bitossi incised Rimini Blu**. Both get listed as "MCM glass ashtray" or "blue pottery dish".
- **Fornasetti back stamps** on what looks like a "black and white dish with a face".
- **Heavy Sommerso Murano** listed as "glass ashtray" or "paperweight dish".
- **Exchange-name phone numbers** date restaurant pieces to before 1965. Famous clubs are worth more.
- The reverse: sellers over-price lots of common ashtrays ("antique pottery") and onyx ("soapstone", "jade").

## Shipping / handling
Ceramic and glass ashtrays are 0.5–3 lb. Double-box with 2" of padding: $10–14 for a single, $15–22 for a lot of 3–5. Heavy Murano and onyx are 2–5 lb, $14–20. Breakage risk is moderate. Chips on rests happen in transit, so wrap each one separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Name the maker and mark ("National Porcelain Co. Snuf-A-Rette, Trenton NJ, cobalt"), the dimensions, and the era with the reason (phone exchange, label, signature).
- Photograph the base, the mark, the rests up close, and any chips or crazing. State "no chips or cracks" only after checking under raking light.
- For advertising pieces, include the business name, address and city in the title (local-history buyers search for them).
- Selling as a lot is fine for common pieces, but price it at the sum of realistic singles minus about 20%.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$12) − shipping to Dylan (~$12) − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). That needs a resale of at least about $100 even on a $10 buy, which means the target is a designer or signed piece: Higgins, Fornasetti, Bitossi Rimini Blu, Lalique, Baccarat, large Sommerso Murano, bronze figural, or a 1950s famous-club or Vegas strip piece in a lot. Buy at ≤ 25% of estimated sold value when the signature or mark is visible or the form is unmistakable. Pass on lots of common ceramic and glass, onyx and alabaster, Snuf-A-Rette and Shawnee at seller "antique" prices. No authentication service needed, but ask for a base or mark photo before paying anything over $20.
