## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Two different markets share this name:
1. **Commercial fragrance bottles** (the juice is the value): Lanvin Arpège, Guerlain Shalimar/Mitsouko/L'Heure Bleue, Chanel No.5, Patou Joy, Caron Nuit de Noël, Schiaparelli Shocking, Dior Miss Dior, Nina Ricci L'Air du Temps (Lalique doves). Tells: maker label, "extrait"/"parfum" (strongest concentration), small 7.5–30 ml bottles with glass stopper or screw cap, a presentation box. Fill level and colour of the juice are visible in photos.
2. **Decorative vanity bottles** (the glass/metal is the value): Czech Bohemian cut crystal with intaglio-cut stoppers (1920s–30s), DeVilbiss and Irice atomizers, Hollywood Regency gold-tone "ormolu" filigree bottles (Matson, Stylebuilt, Globe, 1950s–60s), Lalique, Baccarat, Steuben, Moser.
Photo tells for value: frosted figural stopper with long dauber (Czech), acid-etched "Lalique France" or "R. Lalique" signature, "Made in Czechoslovakia" acid stamp on base or a metal tag, original silk tassel bulb (atomizers), sealed string-and-wax "baudruchage" on the neck of a commercial extrait.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Lanvin Arpège** (1927). Extrait in black ball "boule" bottle with gold Paul Iribe mother-and-daughter logo (1927–1990s) and, mid-century, a square clear flacon with octagonal black cap and gold label "Arpège Extrait de Lanvin Paris" (the 1960s–80s travel/boutique size). Box: cream with navy edges and logo. Old Arpège extrait is wanted by vintage-fragrance wearers.
- **Guerlain** Shalimar (1925, bat-wing "Baccarat" style stopper), Mitsouko, L'Heure Bleue (heart-shaped stopper), Jicky. Baccarat-made flacons marked "Baccarat" add value.
- **Chanel No.5** (1921). Vintage extracts with "No 5 Chanel Paris" label and sealed baudruche are the most counterfeited; 1950s–70s sealed sell well.
- **Caron**, **Patou Joy**, **Schiaparelli Shocking** (torso bottle by Leonor Fini), **Elizabeth Arden**, **Coty L'Aimant/Emeraude** (common, cheap).
- **Lalique** (René Lalique, signed "R. Lalique" pre-1945; "Lalique France" after 1945): Nina Ricci L'Air du Temps doves, Worth "Dans la Nuit", own-brand flacons.
- **Czech Bohemian** (Hoffmann, Morlee, Hermann Schlevogt; 1920s–30s): jewelled filigree collars, intaglio stoppers with nude figures, coloured cut glass, "Made in Czechoslovakia" acid stamp.
- **DeVilbiss** (Toledo OH, 1900s–60s) atomizers, often on Steuben or Fostoria blanks; marked "DeVilbiss" on the metal fitting or acid stamp.
- **Irice** (New York importer, 1940s–60s), **Matson** (1940s–60s, gold-plated ormolu over crystal with cast rose finials), **Stylebuilt**, **Globe**: Hollywood Regency vanity bottles. Matson often has a paper label or "Matson" stamp; most have a glass dauber.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Lead crystal rings, is heavy, and shows sharp, polished cut edges; pressed glass shows mould seams and rounded "cuts".
- Ormolu: real gold-plated cast brass has crisp casting; cheap pieces are stamped thin sheet with blurry detail and show green corrosion.
- Stoppers must match: ground-glass stopper with matching numbers etched on stopper and neck on Czech and French bottles.
- Fill level: evaporation lowers value; darkened juice is normal in old extraits (top notes fade) and buyers accept it if level is high.
- Old vs new: older boxes are paper-covered card with simple printing, no barcode (barcodes after ~1980). Ingredient lists on boxes appear after ~2000s.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips to stopper or neck, stuck stoppers, missing daubers, cloudy "sick" glass inside, cracked atomizer bulbs (dried rubber, almost always), worn gold on ormolu, broken filigree petals, empty or evaporated juice, label loss. Glued stoppers and replaced daubers are common.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Counterfeit Chanel No.5 and modern "vintage-style" bottles exist; check old-style box and label typography.
- Czech-style jewelled bottles are reproduced in quantity (2000s, often unmarked or with new foil labels). Real ones show wear on base and stopper.
- Reproduced "R. Lalique" signatures on moulded glass; Lalique authentication is specialist territory.
- Lots of new "Hollywood Regency" filigree bottles from China and Egypt sell as vintage.

## Market
Two buyer pools: vintage-fragrance wearers (pay for juice: full, sealed, old formula) and bottle collectors (pay for glass: Czech, Lalique, atomizers). Ormolu vanity bottles are plentiful and slow (weeks to months). Sealed pre-1980 extraits sell in days to weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
- A small, plain-looking square bottle with juice inside can be an old-formula extrait worth more than a fancy empty.
- Czech bottles sold as "pretty glass" lack the Czech stamp in the title; the stopper with an intaglio figure is the tell.
- Factice (dummy display) bottles in big sizes are worth more than the retail size.

## Shipping / handling
Liquid perfume is flammable (ORM-D/limited quantity): eBay ground shipping only, no air, under the USPS limits; ship upright, stopper taped, inside a sealed bag, double-boxed. Glass bottles $10–15 ground. Ormolu bottles: wrap the filigree and finial separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State concentration (extrait/parfum/EDT), size, fill level (photo against light), box condition, sealed or not, approximate decade from box/label. For décor bottles: dimensions, maker marks, stopper/dauber present, any chips. Say "vintage perfume is sold as a collectible; scent may have changed with age."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 shipping to buyer − ~$12 shipping to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). In practice: sealed or ≥ 80% full boxed pre-1980 extrait of a wanted name at ≤ $35; Czech intaglio-stopper or DeVilbiss/Steuben atomizer at ≤ $60 with clear photos of an intact stopper; giant factice ≤ $75. Pass on ormolu vanity bottles above $25, empty commercial bottles, and anything requiring Lalique authentication unless signed "R. Lalique" with provenance and priced like ordinary glass. All fit the passenger seat.
