## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
T-shirts printed in the 1980s–1990s for Japanese anime/live-action "tokusatsu" (special-effects hero) shows and films: Ultraman series (Tsuburaya Productions), Kamen Rider and Super Sentai (Toei), Godzilla (Toho), Akira, Ghost in the Shell, Neon Genesis Evangelion, Sailor Moon, Dragon Ball Z, Pokémon, Cowboy Bebop. Dating evidence in photos: (1) the neck tag, (2) the copyright line in the print ("©円谷プロ", "©TSUBURAYA PROD.", "©1995 Shirow Masamune/Kodansha", "©GAINAX", "©Bird Studio/Shueisha, Toei Animation"), (3) hem stitching (single row of stitching at sleeve and bottom hem is typical pre-~1996 US blanks), (4) the print feel (thick plastisol cracked with age vs. modern soft DTG).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Blanks (tags)**: US – Giant (the classic 90s licensed-movie/band blank, highly valued), Anvil, Hanes Beefy-T, Fruit of the Loom, Screen Stars, Tultex, Brockum, Winterland. Japanese – Printstar, United Athle, Glimmer, Cross & Stitch, Bandai/Banpresto tags; promo/event tees often on Printstar.
- **Ultraman/Tsuburaya**: Ultraman Tiga (1996), Dyna (1997), Gaia (1998) – Japanese event and kids' tees; 1998 Gaia tees with ©円谷プロ/毎日放送 copyright. Adult-size Japanese 90s tokusatsu tees are uncommon in the US.
- **Power Rangers (Saban 1993–95)**: US licensed, common; $30–100.
- **Akira**: Fashion Victim 1988 (US), 1990s "Akira" Streamline prints – the most bootlegged anime tee.
- **Ghost in the Shell**: 1995–96 Manga Entertainment/Fashion Victim promo – grail.
- **Evangelion**: 1990s Cospa/Gainax and US ADV promo – high.
- **Sailor Moon, DBZ, Pokémon (1999 Nintendo/Creatures/GAME FREAK)**: higher volume, lower price.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Real vintage: faded, slightly boxy cut, 100% cotton or 50/50, fabric thinned, print cracking consistent with fabric wear, tag font/age consistent with copyright year, single-stitch (pre-1996 US blanks) – but Japanese and later blanks are double-stitched even when genuine, so double stitch does not rule out 1998.
- Copyright year in the print is the strongest evidence; a 1998 Gaia print with ©円谷プロ and 1998-era tag is consistent.
- Size and measurements (pit-to-pit, length) drive price: L/XL are worth more than S/M; Japanese sizes run 1–2 sizes small.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Pit stains, collar stretch, holes, print cracking/peeling, fading. Light wear is accepted (even valued); holes and stains cut price 30–60%. Ask for measurements and tag photos.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Very high risk for Akira, GITS, Evangelion, Sailor Moon: bootlegs printed on genuine vintage blank tags (old Giant/Screen Stars tags) and modern "vintage-style" reprints (Hot Topic, Uniqlo UT, Crunchyroll, official retro reissues). Tells: copyright missing or wrong, print too crisp/soft-hand DTG, tag too clean, artwork from later home-video releases, cut too modern. Ultraman/Tokusatsu are less bootlegged than Akira.

## Market
Strong since ~2019 among streetwear buyers; fast for genuine 90s anime in L/XL (days to 2 weeks). Grailed and eBay both work; Depop for mid-tier. Ultraman/tokusatsu niche is smaller but under-supplied in the US.

## What sellers usually miss
- Thrift/estate sellers price any old tee at $5–15, regardless of title.
- Japanese event tees (Ultraman, Kamen Rider) look like generic kids' shirts but are scarce in the US.
- The print copyright year is right there and owners don't read it.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, light (6–8 oz), poly mailer, $5–6. No breakage risk.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Copyright line photo, tag photo, measurements (pit-to-pit, length), single/double stitch, flaws. Title: year + show + "vintage tee" + tag + size, e.g. "Vintage 1998 Ultraman Gaia T-Shirt Tsuburaya Japan Size L".
