## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small animals built from faceted, polished clear glass beads and rods glued together (ears, legs, tails). The Austrian maker Swarovski started its Silver Crystal line in 1976 with the mouse; thousands of lookalikes followed.
- **Swarovski tells:** very bright, crisp facets with high lead/optical clarity; black **crystal** eyes (faceted jet stones), not paint; an etched logo under the base or on a facet: block "SC" (1976–88), swan (1989 on); often a silver-coloured metal tail or whisker (mice, cats). Original box (blue or later white) and certificate.
- **Lookalike tells:** **painted** eyes and red nose (as in the example), cloudier glass, rough glue joints, gold cord hangers, bubbles.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Swarovski Silver Crystal** (Wattens, Austria, 1976 on); **Swarovski Crystal Society (SCS)** annual editions (1987 on); Kris Bear series (1990s–2000s).
- **Crystal World** (USA/Japan, 1980s on), **Crystal Temptations**, **Hoya** (Japan), **Goebel crystal**.
- Luxury: **Lalique** (frosted/clear moulded, signed "Lalique France"), **Baccarat** (signed, often colourless or coloured moulded), **Daum** (pâte de verre).
- Generic: Chinese/Taiwanese faceted-glass ornaments, often on gold string.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lead crystal rings and shows strong rainbow fire. Swarovski joins are nearly invisible. Moulded Lalique/Baccarat are a separate style (not faceted beads).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chipped facets, lost ears/tails, yellowed glue, scratches. Lost parts are often re-glued — check joints. Swarovski without box/certificate sells for 30–50% less.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit Swarovski with fake swan logos exist, especially for popular pieces. Swarovski reissued many designs, so date by logo style. Treat "Swarovski style" titles as lookalikes.

## Market
Very large supply, slow sales except for retired/rare Swarovski. Collectors know the catalogue numbers.

## What sellers usually miss
Owners call every crystal animal "Swarovski". The undervalued case is the reverse: an estate seller listing a boxed early Swarovski as "crystal mouse" for $10.

## Shipping / handling
Tiny but fragile; $6–9 in a padded box (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Swarovski" only if logo-marked, line (Silver Crystal/SCS Annual Edition/Kris Bear), animal, catalogue number, "retired", "with box and certificate"; Lalique/Baccarat with "signed". Junk: "Swarovski style", "crystal glass animal", "rare". `Swarovski Silver Crystal Retired Mouse SC Logo 7631 with Box COA`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (collectors search catalogue numbers); generic and Crystal World pieces lotted on FB local.
- **Price position:** common Swarovski at the sold median ($15–80, box/cert required for the top of it), OBO; SCS annual at $153 median and retired SC-logo at p75, firm 3 weeks; generic $3–12 lotted. Cut 10% at 45 days.
- **Photos:** first: piece on black cloth, lit for fire; then logo (swan/SC) close-up, catalogue number on box, certificate, every facet edge and ear/tail tip, glue joints, ruler.
- **State:** maker and logo type, line, catalogue number, size, box and certificate present, chips/lost parts/yellowed glue/re-glued joints by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** tiny but fragile; padded box, each piece wrapped, $6–9.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Swarovski [mouse 7631] with the [block-letter SC] logo, retired [pre-1989], boxed with certificate — unboxed pieces sell 30–50% lower and the lookalikes go for $10."
