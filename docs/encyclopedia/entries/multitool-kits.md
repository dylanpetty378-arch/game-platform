## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Folding pliers with tools stowed in the handles (Leatherman-style butterfly-open, or Gerber-style slide-out jaws), often sold in gift
kits with a knife, flashlight or bit set in a case. Generic promo kits: black-coated pot-metal or cheap stainless pliers, no brand or
a sponsor logo, egg-crate foam case. Name brands carry the maker's logo stamped or etched on the handle and model name on the tool.

Where the marks sit: Leatherman — logo and "Portland, Oregon U.S.A." stamped on the handle or tang; model name often on the handle;
Gerber — logo on the handle, "Portland, Oregon" on older USA-made tools (general knowledge); Victorinox — cross-and-shield logo on the
handle, "Victorinox Swiss Made" on the tang; SOG — logo on handle.

Title words: "Leatherman PST", "Wave", "Super Tool", "Charge Ti", "Gerber Multi-Plier 600", "SwissTool", "multi tool lot".
Look-alikes: store brands (Ozark Trail, Husky, Stanley, Craftsman, Coast) built on the Leatherman pattern — $5–25.

### Quick ID checklist
- Brand logo stamped/etched on the handle; "Portland, Oregon U.S.A." on Leatherman.
- Model name visible (PST, Wave, Charge, Super Tool, Juice, Squirt, Skeletool, Micra).
- Handle material: plain stainless, black oxide, coloured aluminium (Juice/Squirt), **titanium scales** (Charge Ti/XTi/TTi — grey matte inserts).
- Outside-accessible blades (Wave, Charge) vs all-inside tools (PST, Super Tool) — helps date generation.
- Original sheath (leather on early PST/Super Tool; nylon later) and original box.
- Pliers jaws and cutters: closed alignment, no chips; ask for a photo with all tools open.
- Generic kit tells: no logo, sponsor print, black coating everywhere, egg-crate foam case.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Leatherman Charge Ti / XTi (2004–08)** — roughly 2–3× a common Wave (general knowledge, verify) — titanium scale inserts on the handles, "Charge" and "Ti/XTi" marked; XTi adds a different blade.
- **Original PST (1983–2004) with leather sheath and box** — ~2× a loose worn PST (general knowledge) — smooth flat handles with no outside blades, leather sheath stamped Leatherman, early box.
- **Limited / one-year editions** (PST 35th Anniversary 2018, Super Tool 300 30th Anniversary 2013, Wave limited editions 2003/2008/2009/2018, Charge+ Tom Sachs NASA 2021, Juice Pro Costco 2002–03) — premium over the standard model, highest when boxed — anniversary etching or special markings on the handle; box with edition wording.
- **Retired Juice colours (2001–06) and Squirt P4/S4/E4 (2002–10)** — small premium to collectors, several Squirts in a lot can clear the rule (general knowledge) — coloured aluminium handles; "Juice" or "Squirt" on the scale.
- **Wave Original (1998–2004)** vs Wave New — collector preference, modest premium — earlier Wave has non-locking/different outside blades and no "+" in the name.
- **Victorinox SwissTool / SwissChamp complete with pouch** — mid tier over generic — Victorinox cross-shield on the handle.
- Promo/Ozark Trail kits — **not** golden eggs: $7–22 sold on Terapeak.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real Leatherman/Victorinox: 420HC or similar stainless, crisp stamped logos, tight pivots that open smoothly, flush tool tips, sharp
well-ground cutters. Cheap production: pot-metal or thin sheet handles, uneven gaps, gritty or wobbly pivots, black coating chipping off,
blurred logos. Old vs new: early Leathermans are plain stainless with fewer outside tools; trademark wording on tools and boxes changes
year to year on genuine product, so it is not a fake tell (Multitool.org).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust, loose pivots. Also: chipped or nicked wire cutters, broken screwdriver tips, bent tools, sharpened-down blades, missing bits from
bit kits, missing sheath. Leatherman's warranty means some users have tools replaced — a heavily used tool is still worth something
to users. Ask: all tools open in one photo, pliers closed tight, any play in the pivots.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Leatherman/Victorinox exist and have hit eBay in waves (Multitool.org). Tells from a side-by-side real vs fake Tread: real screws
are black only on the head and neck with bare threads, fake screws are black all the way down; real bottle-opener icon is crisp, fake
looks like a blob; real scribe is an uncoated insert, fake is moulded and coated; real box print is centred and the tool sits snugly in
dense foam, fake foam is looser with a wrong-shaped cutout; on the Tread specifically the fake clasp had two ball detents vs one.
Not reliable: weight (1 g differences), barcodes, trademark wording, wear, and price alone — though a new "$312 list" tool for $50 is a
red flag.

## Market
High volume. Leatherman users and collectors buy year-round on eBay; Q4 gift bump. Lots of TSA-confiscated tools sell in bulk, which
caps prices for common used models.

## What sellers usually miss
Discontinued Leatherman models (Charge Ti/XTi, original PST, Juice colours, Squirt), anniversary editions, original leather sheaths and boxes.

## Shipping / handling
Small, $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model, condition. State the exact model and generation, handle material, every tool present (photo open), pivot condition, cutter
condition, sheath/box included.

### What to tell the buyer
"Genuine Leatherman __, stamped Portland, Oregon U.S.A. on the handle. This model was made __–__ (retired). All __ tools present
and photographed open; pliers close tight with no gap, wire cutters unchipped, pivots firm. Comes with the original __ sheath/box.
Light use marks, no rust."

## Buy rule
Generic sets never clear. Buy name-brand lots at ≤ ~$15 per tool or a retired/limited Leatherman ≤ ~$25 with box/sheath. Profit must be
≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
