## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Edmund Astroscan**: a red plastic, bowling-ball-shaped 4¼" reflector that sits in a three-point base, 1976–2010s. Complete means the base, RKE eyepiece, peep sight, strap and cap.
- **Celestron C5/C8 orange tube**: Schmidt-Cassegrains from the 1970s–80s with an orange tube on a fork mount. $250–500.
- **Meade/Celestron black department-store refractors** on tripods: $20–60.
- **Questar 3.5"**: a small chrome and black Maksutov on a built-in fork, with a leather case. $1,500+.
- Sellers usually write "telescope", often with the brand.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Edmund Scientific**: Astroscan 2001; later Astroscan Plus.
- **Celestron**: orange-tube C5/C8 (1970s–80s, collectible "user" scopes); NexStar 4SE/6SE/8SE (modern, steady used market); PowerSeeker (junk).
- **Meade**: ETX-90 Maksutov; LX200 (heavy); 60mm department-store refractors (junk).
- **Questar** (New Hope PA): 3.5" and 7". Premium; holds value.
- **Unitron** (1950s–70s Japanese-made refractors, white tubes, wooden cases): collectible (general knowledge, verify).
- **Criterion RV-6 Dynascope** (1960s 6" reflector, heavy pier mount).
- **Takahashi, Astro-Physics, TeleVue**: premium apochromats; Astro-Physics has waiting lists.
- **Tasco, Bushnell**: department-store level.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Optics are the value. Mirror or corrector coatings should be clean and unpitted; refractor lenses should be free of fungus and separation.
- Accessory cases with eyepieces (Plössl, Nagler, Orthoscopic) add value. TeleVue eyepieces are $100+ each by themselves (general knowledge, verify).
- Plastic focusers and 0.965" eyepieces = department-store quality. 1.25"/2" eyepieces and metal focusers = serious instruments.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Tarnished or peeling mirror coatings, fungus in lenses, a cracked corrector plate, missing finder or eyepieces, and dead electronics on GoTo mounts.
- Ask for a photo down the tube and of the corrector or objective lens with a flashlight.
- Collimation can be off after shipping (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- No fakes to speak of. Look out for decorative brass "nautical" telescopes sold as antiques and for rebranded Chinese optics (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Astronomy forums (Cloudy Nights classifieds, AstroMart) and eBay. Buyers are hobbyists who know prices. Fall and winter (long nights) and before Christmas are peak. Big scopes are local-only (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Astroscans are usually named, because the shape is unique.
- Vintage orange C8s and Unitron refractors get listed as "old telescope".
- Mirror coating condition matters: ask for a photo down the tube.

## Shipping / handling
- Astroscan: 12 lb with base and awkward, $35–45 to ship, double-boxed.
- C8s: 40+ lb, local only.
- Questar: small, about 7 lb in its case; ship insured (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + model + aperture + type (refractor, SCT, Maksutov) + "complete" + "with eyepieces/mount/case" + "vintage"/"orange tube". Junk: "old telescope", "stargazing", "powerful". Example: `Edmund Scientific Astroscan 2001 Telescope Complete with Base Eyepieces Tested`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for Astroscan/ETX/NexStar; Cloudy Nights classifieds and AstroMart for C8, Unitron and Questar at the same price with no fee; FB local for 40+ lb scopes; fall–winter and pre-Christmas peak.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (Astroscan complete $150–250, ETX-90/NexStar 4SE $150–250, orange C8 $300–500, Questar 3.5 $1,500–3,500); accept −10%, floor −20%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) full scope on its mount/base; then down the tube with a flashlight (mirror/corrector coating), serial plate, focuser, every eyepiece and accessory laid out, case, mount controls; video of the drive/electronics running if it has them.
- **State:** aperture, focal length, type, maker, model, serial, optics condition (coating, dust, fungus), collimation if known, every eyepiece and accessory, mount type, electronics working, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** Astroscan 12 lb and awkward, double-boxed, $35–45; Questar ~7 lb in case, insured; C8/RV-6 40+ lb, local, fits a back seat with the tripod in the trunk.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "An [Astroscan 2001 / orange-tube C8 / Questar 3.5], complete with [eyepieces, base, case], optics clean under a flashlight in photo [2] — the department-store 60mm refractors are the $40 scopes; complete [model] sets sell for [$X]."

## Buy rule
Buy when: an Astroscan complete is ≤ $70; a Questar, Unitron or C8 is at any price under $200.
Pass when: department-store refractors; an Astroscan over $100.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (an Astroscan does; a C8 on its fork and tripod generally doesn't).
