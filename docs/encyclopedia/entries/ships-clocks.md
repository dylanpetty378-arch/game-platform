## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Round brass case (4–8 in dial) with a hinged bezel, designed to bolt to a bulkhead; 8-day or 7-day mechanical movement, often with ship's-bell strike (strikes 1–8 bells per four-hour watch). Dial: Arabic 1–12 with inner 13–24, sometimes "Ship's Bell" text, winding holes, and a STR/SIL (strike/silent) lever and F/S (fast/slow) regulator at the top.
Key identifiers on the dial: maker name; "German Movt" or "Made in W. Germany" = later Seth Thomas (Hermle/Franz Hermle movement, 1970s–90s); "U.S. Navy" or "U.S. Maritime Commission", serial numbers on the dial, Chelsea's "Chelsea Clock Co. Boston" with serial number.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Chelsea Clock Co.** (Boston/Chelsea MA, 1897–): top tier; serial number on dial and movement dates the clock. Ship's bell models command most.
- **Seth Thomas** (Thomaston CT): American-made 1900s–1960s Navy and commercial ship clocks (No. 10 and No. 17 movements); 1970s–90s "Corsair" (E537-000 etc.) and "Helmsman" with German Hermle movements — common.
- **Waltham** and **Hamilton**: WWII navy clocks.
- **Howard Miller**, **Salem**, **Maritime**: decorative quartz/German-movement.
- **Wempe** (Germany), **Schatz** (Germany, Royal Mariner), **Sestrel** (UK).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Heavy cast brass case, thick bevelled glass, silvered dial (old) vs. printed aluminium (newer), key-wound with one or two arbors. Navy ones are often bakelite (WWII Chelsea/Seth Thomas Mk I deck clocks) or brass with black dials. Check if the movement is marked "Seth Thomas Thomaston" (American) vs. "Franz Hermle" (German).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Not running (needs cleaning ~$150–300 — counts as repair), broken mainspring, strike out of sequence, replaced dials, polished-off lacquer, missing keys, cracked glass. Service history matters.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian/Chinese brass "Chelsea" or "Navy" decor clocks with quartz movements are common and look convincing from the front: check for a battery compartment and winding holes. Re-dialled clocks with fake "U.S. Navy" text.

## Market
Steady; nautical decor and boaters; Chelsea has a very strong collector base; Corsairs are plentiful.

## What sellers usually miss
Chelsea serial numbers (dates early clocks); "U.S. Navy" or Maritime Commission markings on dial or case; American-made Seth Thomas movements.

## Shipping / handling
