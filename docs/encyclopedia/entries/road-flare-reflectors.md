## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Folding stands holding two round red reflectors (one above the other), placed on the road behind a broken-down truck. US rules from the 1950s–60s required trucks to carry three, so they were sold in sets of three in a metal box, often with red cloth flags.
- **Double red plastic reflectors** in chrome rims on a stamped steel base that folds flat (1960s–80s). The example is this type: three units, plus a steel "reflector kit" box and red flags with rods.
- **Glass-jewel reflectors** (1930s–50s) have faceted glass lenses — more collectible.
- **Smudge pots / flare pots** (Toledo Torch, Dietz, Handlan) are kerosene pots; separate collector market.

## Makers, brands and variants
Stratilite (Stratton), Anthes, Fleet-Lite, Kiddie, Signal-Stat, Dietz, Handlan. Many unmarked.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original box with label, all three units, unbroken lenses, working folding hinges. Plastic lenses craze and fade.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked lenses, rusted bases, missing pins, missing box. Not repairable meaningfully.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern triangle reflectors replaced these after 1974 FMCSA changes; no fakes.
