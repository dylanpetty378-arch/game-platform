## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast-aluminium/iron head with a glass or plastic dome, coin slot, dial showing minutes, a key lock on the back or side. Makers cast on the head: Duncan (Duncan-Miller, "Duncan 60/70/76" are 1960s–80s), Park-O-Meter (POM, Oklahoma, 1935 originator; "Park-O-Meter" script), Rockwell/Rhodes, M.H. Rhodes "Mark-Time", Dual (two-head). Early = 1930s–50s single dial, rounded "penny/nickel" slots, "Model M" POM. Value: original key + working mechanism + unmodified; lamp conversions (drilled, wired) are common decor and sell for less than an original meter. Pole-mounted with the original pipe adds a little. Sellers' words: "parking meter lamp", "old parking meter", "man cave".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Park-O-Meter (POM)** (Oklahoma City, 1935 originator): "Park-O-Meter" script; 1935–40 first generation top tier ($400–800); "Model M" 1930s–50s collectible; 1940s–50s single head working $150–300.
- **Duncan / Duncan-Miller**: 1950s Duncan-Miller collectible; Duncan 60/70/76 (1960s–80s) decent ($80–140 with key).
- **Rockwell / M.H. Rhodes "Mark-Time"**: collectible tier.
- **Dual** two-head meters on a pole: collectible.
- **Rare makers**: Karpark, Mi-Co — top tier.
- **1980s–90s electronic** Duncan/POM: junk tier.
- **Lamp conversions**: decor, $75–175, priced as lamps (~$75–125) regardless of head age.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast aluminium or iron housings, glass domes on early meters (plastic later), painted finishes (often grey, green, silver). Originality tells: unmodified (no drill holes for wiring), original paint, working coin mechanism, original key, period coin slot (penny/nickel). Collector meters keep the "violation" flag and dial intact (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
No key (locks often need a locksmith), jammed mechanisms, cracked or yellowed domes, heavy rust/corrosion, repainting, drilled lamp conversions (permanently lower value). Ask: does it take coins and run down? Is the key included? Any drilling?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Decorative reproduction meters and meter-shaped banks exist (general knowledge, verify). Replacement domes and new-old-stock parts (e.g. Duncan Miller "police will not turn handle" tags) are sold separately and can be used to dress up meters. Lamp conversions marketed as "rare" or "custom" by dealers.

## Market
Niche decor/man-cave and Americana collector market; moderate volume on eBay, better margins locally because of weight (general knowledge, verify). Gift-season demand (Father's Day, Christmas) plausible (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Maker/era; working lock with key; whether it still takes coins. Conversions are priced as lamps (~$75–125) regardless of the head's age.

## Shipping / handling
Heads 10–20 lb; lamp conversions 15–25 lb; $30–50 shipping. eBay ~13%. Margins thin – mostly a local flip, not a ship-in item. Full meters on poles are long and may not fit the size rule.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model, era, coin denominations accepted, working status (coins accepted, timer runs), key included, dome condition, original paint vs repaint, unmodified vs lamp conversion, height/weight, pole included. Photograph maker casting, dial face, key and lock, back, any drilling. Title keywords: "Vintage 1950s Duncan Miller Parking Meter with Key Working", "Park-O-Meter Model M".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1930s–50s POM/Duncan-Miller/Rhodes head, working with key, ≤ $60. Pass when: lamp conversions over $40, 1970s+ Duncan over $50. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (heads do; full pole-mounted meters may not — those are excluded).
