## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Precisely milled 5 cm beechwood cubes with tunnels and grooves; stacked to make marble runs. Box: tan/grey with white grid and lowercase "cuboro" wordmark (1990s–2000s boxes) or newer white/photo boxes. Each cube is unpainted pale beech with very sharp edges and a perfect fit.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Cuboro AG (Switzerland, Matthias Etter, from 1985)**: Basis (54 cubes, the starter), Standard (older name for same core set, 54 pieces), Standard 32, Cugolino (smaller kid line), Tricky Ways, Metro, Duo, Profi, Plus, Multi, Kuboro. Cubes are interchangeable across sets.
- **Naef** (Swiss) distributed early sets.
- **Quadrilla** (Hape) and **HABA Kugelbahn**: cheaper wooden rail systems, lower resale.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid untreated beech, CNC-milled, 5 cm cubes. Knock-offs (Chinese "marble cube" sets) use lighter wood, rougher tunnels, off-size cubes, and printed brand-less boxes.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing cubes (most important), crayon/marker, chewed corners, water swelling (cubes stop fitting), missing marbles (cheap to replace). Box wear matters little.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese generic clones exist; they do not carry the cuboro mark burned/printed and do not fit Cuboro cubes precisely.

## Market
Niche but steady (Montessori/homeschool and puzzle buyers); maybe 20–50 eBay US sales/month. Complete sets sell in 1–3 weeks at fair price.

## What sellers usually miss
Unboxed bags of plain wooden cubes with grooves are often Cuboro — buyers pay per cube (~$4–6 each).

## Shipping / handling
Basis set ≈ 6–7 lb, box 12×10×4 in.; Ground $15–20. Robust.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph all cubes laid out in a grid with a count, show the cuboro stamp, list set name and number, note any marker or swelling.
