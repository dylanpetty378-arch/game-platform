## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Parts pulled from US cruiser, middleweight and muscle bikes (1930s–70s) and European road bikes (1960s–80s). Most value is in **Schwinn** and in a handful of supplier brands. Many tells are stamped, so the photo has to show them:
- **Schwinn stamps and dates:** Schwinn-approved parts carry "Schwinn" on chainrings, cranks, stems, bars, rims ("Schwinn S-7", "S-2") and fenders (some). Handlebars and stems have a two-letter or number date code. Saddles: "Schwinn Approved" with Persons or Mesinger on the pan.
- **Sting-Ray / Krate parts (1963–79):** "Slik" rear tyre, Solo Polo or "Bucket" banana seats with a Schwinn script, springer forks with a chrome spring, Stik shifter (Shift-Stik) with an orange/red knob, sissy bars, high-rise "ape hanger" bars stamped Schwinn with a date code, 16" or 20" "S" rims.
- **Middleweight (1950s–60s, Jaguar, Corvette, Panther, Hornet, Wasp):** chrome full fenders, tank with horn or light, carrier rack, "drop-stand", Persons or Mesinger 2-spring saddles with studs.
- **Balloon-tire (1930s–50s):** Phantom and Black Phantom tanks, chrome springer forks, Delta and Seiss headlights, Torrington pedals, horn tanks. Prewar Elgin, Colson and Shelby parts also sell high.
- **Accessories:** Stewart-Warner and Sears/Elgin speedometers (1950s chrome round dial, cable drive) $50–200. 1970s–90s Japanese/Taiwanese speedos (Dorcy, Sunlite, Cateye) $10–25.
- **Road bike:** Campagnolo Nuovo/Super Record derailleurs, hubs and cranks (the "Campagnolo" script, patent numbers), Brooks Professional saddles (copper rivets, "Brooks Professional" stamp), Cinelli bars and stems, Mafac and Universal brakes.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Schwinn (Arnold, Schwinn & Co., Chicago, 1895–1993):** the main collector brand. Muscle-bike Krates (Orange, Lemon, Apple, Pea Picker, Cotton Picker, Grey Ghost) are the most valuable 1960s–70s parts sources.
- **Persons Majestic Mfg.** (Worcester MA) and **Mesinger** (NY): saddles, often with "studs" or rivets around the skirt (1940s–60s). Troxel (Ohio) is mid- and low-grade.
- **Bendix:** 2-speed "kickback" hubs (yellow band 1965+, blue band, red band), coaster hubs. Automatic 2-speed $80–200.
- **New Departure, Morrow:** coaster hubs ($20–60).
- **Stewart-Warner / Hopalong Cassidy / Sears:** 1950s speedometers.
- **Dorcy:** 1970s–90s Japanese import accessories (lights, speedometers). Low value.
- **Campagnolo (Vicenza):** Record and Super Record, 1960s–80s. **Simplex, Huret, Suntour** (Cyclone, Superbe) are mid.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Chrome: original 1950s–70s Schwinn chrome is thick and bright. Pitting or rust bubbles cut value roughly 50%. Re-chromed parts are worth less to purists.
- Saddles: original "studded" vinyl or leather covers with the maker's stamp on the pan underside. Cracked tops and rusted springs are common. A reupholstered saddle sells as a "user", not a collector piece.
- Paint: Schwinn colours (Coppertone, Flamboyant Red, Radiant Blue) are distinctive. Original paint with the correct decals beats repaint.
- Date codes: two letters on Schwinn bars and stems (for example "DE" = a 1960s year/month code), and serial numbers on the left rear dropout for bikes.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rust and pitting on chrome fenders, rims and bars. Dents and creases in fenders. Broken fender-brace mounts.
- Saddles: split covers, dents (like the dip in the 2026-10 black saddle), rusted springs.
- Speedometers: broken glass, missing cable and drive gear, frozen needles. They need testing (Dylan's no-untested-electronics rule doesn't strictly apply since they're mechanical, but parts sold "untested" sell low).
- Bendix hubs: worn internals. Sold "as is" they're priced as parts.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction Sting-Ray seats, sissy bars, Krate springers, Slik tyres, decals and grips are made in Taiwan and China. They are lighter, with sharper vinyl graphics and no Schwinn date codes.
- Schwinn itself reissued the Sting-Ray and Krate-style bikes (2000s, Pacific-era Schwinn), with cheaper chrome and "Schwinn" stickers rather than stamps.
- Reproduction Phantom tanks and balloon-tire headlights exist and are sold as "vintage style".

## Market
There's an active community on TheCABE, Ratrod Bikes and Facebook groups, and eBay sells hundreds of Schwinn parts a month. Krate and Sting-Ray parts sell fast. Middleweight chrome is slower. Spring and summer are a little stronger. Buyers are knowledgeable and check date codes and stamps.

## What sellers usually miss
- **Stamps and date codes** on bars and stems, and **maker names under saddles**. Sellers list "old handlebars" or "old seat" without them.
- **Bendix automatic 2-speed hubs** (yellow, blue or red band on the hub) inside a "kids bike wheel".
- **Campagnolo and Brooks Professional** on garage-sale road bikes.
- The reverse: sellers see a Lens hit for a complete bike ($1,000+) and price single parts as if they were the bike. Middleweight fenders are a $60–120 item, not a $1,350 one.

## Shipping / handling
- Speedometers, hubs, derailleurs: 1–2 lb, $8–12.
- Saddles: 2–3 lb, $12–15.
- Handlebars with stem: an awkward 24–30" box, $18–25.
- Fender pairs (26"): a 30x14x8 box, $25–35 each way, with a high dent risk.
- Whole bikes: freight or bike-box, $60–120. Local only for Dylan.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Photograph stamps, date codes, the underside of the saddle and the hub band colour. Name the model years it fits ("fits 1960s Schwinn middleweight Jaguar, Corvette").
- Grade chrome honestly (pitting, rust, dents) with close-ups.
- Say whether it's original, reproduction or re-chromed.
- List on eBay with "Schwinn" plus the model in the title. Cross-post to the TheCABE classifieds for high-value parts.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer − shipping to Dylan − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). Buy only when a Schwinn stamp, a saddle maker's mark, a Bendix band or Campagnolo script is visible (or the seller confirms it with a photo), the part is small (no fender sets or frames unless resale is over $150), and the price is ≤ 25% of the estimated sold value. Pass on generic bars, saddles, speedometers and lights, anything rusted or "needs restoration", and single parts priced off whole-bike Lens matches. No authentication service is needed, but ask for a stamp photo.
