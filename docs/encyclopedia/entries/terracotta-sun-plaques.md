## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Round wall plaques with a human face in a sun disk with rays, made of terracotta (unglazed or painted), glazed stoneware, or resin. Main origins: Metepec (State of Mexico) painted clay suns (bright acrylic paint); Talavera glazed suns (Puebla, Dolores Hidalgo); Southwest/US studio stoneware (1970s–90s); Italian/Spanish majolica; garden-center imports (China, Mexico, 1990s–2010s).

## Makers, brands and variants
Metepec workshops (Soteno family for Trees of Life; various sun makers), Talavera Uriarte (Puebla, marked), US studio potters (signed incised initials), Pier 1 / Pottery Barn imports (labels), Sunset/Southwest decor 1990s.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-built vs slip-cast (seams, uniform walls = cast); glaze quality; signature incised on the back; age patina vs painted "distressing". Resin = light, molded detail, hollow back.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, flaking paint, efflorescence (white salt) from outdoor exposure, cracked hangers, glue repairs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mass-produced "aged" finish imports sold as antique. Rarely worth faking.
