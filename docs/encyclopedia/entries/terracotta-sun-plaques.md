## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Round wall plaques with a human face in a sun disk with rays, made of terracotta (unglazed or painted), glazed stoneware, or resin. Main origins: Metepec (State of Mexico) painted clay suns (bright acrylic paint); Talavera glazed suns (Puebla, Dolores Hidalgo); Southwest/US studio stoneware (1970s–90s); Italian/Spanish majolica; garden-center imports (China, Mexico, 1990s–2010s).

Metepec "soles y lunas" are bright decorative plaques for interior and exterior walls; suns usually have smiling faces, and moons are often shown in phases with a flirtatious look, red lips and flowers. Metepec started making decorative pieces (rather than only utilitarian ware) in the 1950s (Wikipedia). Paired sun-and-moon (eclipse) faces belong to the same family.

Photo routine: front (face style, paint vs glaze), **back** (signature, workshop stamp, "Mexico", paper labels, raw clay colour), edge (wall thickness), hanger (wire through holes, moulded hole, rope).

## Makers, brands and variants
Metepec workshops (Soteno family for Trees of Life; various sun makers), Talavera Uriarte (Puebla, marked), US studio potters (signed incised initials), Pier 1 / Pottery Barn imports (labels), Sunset/Southwest decor 1990s.
- **Metepec**: family workshops in the town do every stage including firing; clays are red, sandy yellow and black from nearby Octotitlán, San Felipe Tlamimilolpan and Tlacotepec; the traditional shaping method is coiling. Known families: Soteno (trees of life; Alfonso Soteno honoured 2012), Ortega (five generations; patriarch Odilón a master potter). A national pottery contest (Concurso Nacional de Alfarería y Cerámica) runs each May; trees of life have a collective trademark with holograms and registration numbers (Wikipedia). Suns are commonly unsigned or signed by hand on the back (general knowledge, verify).
- **Uriarte Talavera** (Puebla, founded 1824): from 1922 pieces were signed with decorative initials of Ysauro Uriarte Martínez and Enrique Luis Ventosa; today pieces carry a serial number and usually the artisan's signature; c. 1994 Uriarte was among the first workshops eligible for the government certificate of authenticity (Wikipedia). Vintage Ysauro Uriarte pieces are offered by dealers (district.net).
- **"Talavera-style"** suns from Dolores Hidalgo (Guanajuato) and elsewhere: the certified Talavera designation is tied to Puebla/Tlaxcala workshops, so Dolores Hidalgo suns are Talavera-style majolica, not certified Talavera (general knowledge, verify). Many new ones are sold by importers (Novica, GlobeIn) at retail.
- **Italian mid-century** (Bitossi, sold in the US by Raymor; Alvino Bagni designs) made figural wall plaques; a Bitossi/Raymor sun face would sit in the mid-century tier (Chairish shows Raymor/Bitossi plaques; sun-specific examples not verified).
- **US studio/Southwest** stoneware suns: incised initials or full signature on the back, 1970s–90s.

### Quick ID checklist
- Back photo: signature, workshop name, "Hecho en México", serial number, hologram, paper label (Pier 1, Pottery Barn, "Made in China").
- Paint on raw clay (Metepec, often bright and matte) vs glossy glaze on a cream base (Talavera/majolica) vs moulded resin (very light, hollow).
- Talavera palette: only black, cobalt/blue, yellow, green, red, sometimes deep rose on an off-white crazed glaze; neon or pastel colours = not Talavera.
- Hand-built (uneven rays, finger marks, varied thickness) vs slip-cast (seams, identical rays, uniform thin wall).
- Size: diameter over ~18" and heavy hand-built = workshop/folk-art piece; 10–14" = typical import.
- Outdoor wear: efflorescence (white salt bloom), flaking paint, moss.
- Mid-century Italian: "Italy" incised/painted with a number, Raymor or Bitossi label.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Signed Uriarte (Puebla) Talavera sun, especially vintage with Uriarte initials** — ~2–3× a generic garden sun (top of the $25–80 Talavera tier vs $10–30; vintage Uriarte dealer stock on district.net, verify) — Uriarte mark/serial on the back, restricted Talavera palette, crazed cream glaze.
- **Large Metepec workshop sun by a known family (Soteno, Ortega) or contest piece** — ~4–15× a garden-center sun ($100–400 vs $10–30) — big hand-built clay with elaborate layered rays and figures, artist signature on the back.
- **Italian mid-century majolica sun (Bitossi/Raymor or similar)** — ~4–10× ($100–400 tier) — "Italy" plus number, Raymor label, crisp mid-century stylised face.
- **Signed US studio stoneware sun (1970s–90s)** — ~2–4× ($30–120 vs $10–30) — incised signature or initials, high-fire stoneware glaze, heavy.
- **Matched sun-and-moon pair or eclipse face by a named maker** — premium over a single sun (magnitude unverified) — both pieces with the same signature.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-built vs slip-cast (seams, uniform walls = cast); glaze quality; signature incised on the back; age patina vs painted "distressing". Resin = light, molded detail, hollow back. Authentic Talavera: fine Puebla clays, high-fired, tin-and-lead glaze, off-white to slightly yellow ground with the design slightly fused into the glaze, which crazes; limited palette (Uriarte Talavera, Wikipedia). Metepec suns: low-fired clay, typically painted after firing (general knowledge, verify), so paint chips and flakes rather than glaze chipping. Old vs new: dust and smoke darkening in recesses, old wire hangers, hand-lettered signatures, faded paint versus sharp new colour and printed stickers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips, flaking paint, efflorescence (white salt) from outdoor exposure, cracked hangers, glue repairs. Broken rays reattached with glue show as a line across a ray and colour mismatch; repainted areas are brighter than the rest. Ask for back and edge photos and whether it has been outdoors.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mass-produced "aged" finish imports sold as antique. Rarely worth faking. New "Talavera-style" suns are sold widely by importers; Metepec trees of life face Chinese copies (Wikipedia), and the same risk applies loosely to suns.

## Market
Low demand, decor buyers; slow (weeks–months).

## What sellers usually miss
Little — most are priced above value. The exceptions are a Uriarte mark or serial on the back and a Metepec family signature, both only visible in a back photo.

## Shipping / handling
10–16" diameter, 2–5 lb, fragile; double-box $15–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Diameter, material, glaze, signature/marks, condition, hanging method.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the diameter and thickness, weight, and what it is made of (painted low-fired clay, glazed majolica/Talavera, stoneware or resin). Quote any mark on the back exactly and photograph it; say "Talavera" only for a Puebla-certified mark such as Uriarte, otherwise "Talavera-style" or "Mexican painted clay". State origin only from a mark or label, the hanging method, and every chip, flake or repair. Note if it has been used outdoors.
