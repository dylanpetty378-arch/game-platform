## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Matching vanity items: hand mirror, hairbrush, clothes brush, comb, buffer, powder jar, hair receiver (jar with a hole in the lid), pin tray, cuticle tools, perfume bottles.
- **Celluloid/French ivory** (1910s–40s): cream with faux grain lines, often monogrammed; "Ivory Pyralin" or "Du Pont Pyralin" stamped near the handle; smells of camphor when rubbed.
- **Sterling**: "Sterling" mark, maker hallmarks, repoussé florals, cherubs or Art Nouveau maidens (Unger Bros., William B. Kerr), monograms; hollow handles, often dented.
- **Guilloché enamel** (1920s–30s): translucent enamel over engine-turned silver-plate or sterling, often pink, blue, green with painted roses.
- **Porcelain**: Nippon, Bavaria, Limoges blanks often painted by amateur china painters (1900–30s); hair receivers, trays, hatpin holders. Professional factory decoration is crisper and marked.

## Makers, brands and variants
DuPont (Pyralin, Ivory Pyralin, 1915–40s), Celluloid Co. (Arlington NJ), Fiberloid, Viscoloid; Gorham, Unger Bros., William B. Kerr, International Silver, Webster, Towle, Wallace (sterling); Apollo Studios (NY, 1920s silver-plate and enamel); Matson and Stylebuilt (1950s ormolu sets).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Celluloid shows grain lines, yellows, and can crack or "off-gas"; Bakelite is heavier; modern plastic has no grain.
- Sterling: check hallmarks; weighted (filled) handles carry less silver.
- China painting: amateur work has uneven gold and cramped florals; factory work has stamped backmarks.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks (porcelain hairline cracks kill value — the porcelain example here has a through crack), celluloid crazing and warping, bristle loss in brushes (buyers accept), dents in sterling, worn enamel, de-silvered mirror glass.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction Art Nouveau sterling-look sets in plate; new "Victorian" painted porcelain from China; celluloid is rarely faked.

## Market
Steady, modest. Sterling and guilloché sell in weeks; celluloid and porcelain singles are slow.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling-backed mirrors sold as "silver colour" without checking marks; guilloché sets described as "pretty vanity set".

## Shipping / handling
Mirrors and porcelain fragile; $10–15 boxed. All fit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Material and marks, number of pieces, damage close-ups, weight for sterling, monograms.
