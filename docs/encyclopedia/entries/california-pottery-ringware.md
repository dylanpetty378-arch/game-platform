## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1930s–40s Los Angeles solid-colour kitchenware. Bauer "Ringware" mixing bowls: heavy, flared, with raised concentric rings on the outside (and three inside rings on early ones). Colours: orange-red, jade green, cobalt, yellow, ivory, black (black and burgundy are the scarce ones). Nesting mixing bowl numbers (bigger number = smaller bowl): #9 (1 gal, 10–11"), #12 (9"), #18 (8"), #24 (7"), #30 (6"), #36 (5"). Mark on bottom is often just an impressed number or "Bauer Los Angeles"; many are unmarked, which is why sellers just write "California pottery" or "orange bowl". Pacific Hostessware and Garden City look similar; Pacific is marked "Pacific" in a circle, Garden City is usually unmarked with sharper rings. Look-alikes: Fiesta (Homer Laughlin, Ohio – rings on rims, ink/impressed "Fiesta" mark), Vernon/Metlox/Gladding McBean (Franciscan "El Patio") solid colours (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **J.A. Bauer Pottery, Los Angeles (1909–1962)**: Ringware introduced c.1932 – the reference line. Mixing bowls, "beehive" nesting bowls (butterscotch/other colours), pitchers, plates, carafes, cookie jars. Early bowls with inside rings are the premium version. Bauer also made the separate "Monterey" and "Brusche/Al Fresco" lines (general knowledge, verify).
- **Pacific Clay Products (Pacific Hostessware, 1932–c.1942)**: similar ringed solid-colour ware, marked "Pacific" / "Pacific Made in USA" in a circle; generally a bit below Bauer.
- **Garden City Pottery (San Jose)**: usually unmarked, sharper rings; lower collector demand.
- Colours: orange-red, jade, yellow, cobalt, ivory common to mid; black, burgundy, delph blue, chartreuse and others scarce (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Heavy earthenware with thick, glossy single-colour glaze; unglazed foot ring shows a pinkish/buff clay body. Period pieces show foot-ring wear and light crazing; glaze pools in the rings. Check for repaint/cold paint on scarce colours (the old entry's warning: "verify colour, not repaint"). Weight is a tell – originals are heavy and thick-walled (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips are the common damage and knock about 50% off; crazing is tolerated. Look for hairlines (ring a tap test), glued breaks, filled chips (glossy patch, slightly different shade under raking light) and stained crazing. Ask for close-ups of the rim all round and the foot ring.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Bauer Pottery Company of Los Angeles reissued ringware shapes in the 2000s (newer, often marked differently and with crisper, lighter bodies) (general knowledge, verify). Modern "Fiesta" and generic ringed bowls get mis-titled as Bauer. Unmarked pieces need size and shape matching to the catalog numbers.

## Market
Steady but thin: about a dozen orange ringware mixing bowls sold in Terapeak's window. Buyers are California-pottery collectors, mid-century kitchen decorators and Fiesta crossover buyers. Asking prices on eBay run well above what actually sells. Sells on eBay and Etsy; local antique malls in California price high. No strong seasonality (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The word "Bauer" (and the size number). Listings titled "California pottery bowl", "orange mixing bowl", "Fiesta-style bowl" are the ones to open. Chips on the rim knock 50% off; crazing is tolerated.

## Shipping / handling
A #9 bowl is ~5 lb; double-box, $18–25 to ship. Breakage is the main risk. Nesting sets ship nested with foam between.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title with "Bauer Ringware", the bowl number and colour, and diameter (e.g. "Bauer Ringware #9 Mixing Bowl Orange-Red 10.75in Los Angeles Pottery"). State rim condition explicitly, crazing, and whether it has inside rings. Photograph the impressed mark/number, foot ring, rim all round and inside. Give diameter and height.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a #9/#12 ringware bowl in a good colour is under $25, or two or more bowls for under $40 – and given the Terapeak median of $38, only buy single orange bowls when nearly free; scarce colours (black, burgundy) and multi-bowl lots are what actually clear. Pass when: chipped, or already titled "Bauer" at $50+. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (all bowls do).
