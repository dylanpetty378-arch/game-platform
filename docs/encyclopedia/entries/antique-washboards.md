## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wooden frame (pine or poplar) with a ribbed rubbing surface and a header board printed with the maker and number. Surface types: galvanized zinc (grey crimped metal), brass (yellow), glass (clear ribbed, sometimes blue/cobalt), enamel/porcelain (white or blue speckle), all-wood ribs (oldest). Lingerie/pail size boards are smaller (about 8 × 18 in); family size about 12.5 × 24 in.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **National Washboard Co. (Chicago, Saginaw, Memphis; c.1890s–1960s)**: header "National Washboard Co." with model number — No. 701/703 "Zinc King" (701 family, 703 lingerie), 511 Glass King, 860 Brass King, "Bear Brand", "Silver King". Header listing "Chicago, Saginaw, Memphis" dates to roughly 1920s–50s (general knowledge, verify).
- **Columbus Washboard Co. (Logan, Ohio, 1895–present)**: Dubl Handi, Maid-Rite, Crystal; still making boards today, so "vintage" Dubl Handi/Maid-Rite overlap with new ones (comps show new-old-stock Dubl Handi $19.99).
- **Others**: Bear Brand, Atlas, Northern Queen, Sunnyline, Victory (WWII glass because metal was rationed).
- **Advertising boards**: soap-brand headers (Fels-Naptha, Octagon) sell better.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Galvanized zinc = most common, lowest value. Brass and glass = mid. Cobalt glass, enamelware and wood-rib = best.
- Old vs new: old boards have soft worn wood, square nails or rust stains, faded stencilling. New Columbus boards have crisp fresh lettering and bright wood; reproduction "primitive" decor boards are thin.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Split frames, loose ribs, cracked glass, rust holes in zinc; heavily rusted boards still sell as decor. Glass surface cracks kill value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Columbus Washboard Co. still makes Dubl Handi and Maid-Rite; decor-store fake "antique" boards with distressed paint and invented brand names exist.

## Market
Steady, slow decor market. Huge supply at estate sales for $5–20. Shipping (24 in long) eats margin.

## What sellers usually miss
Glass colour (blue/cobalt glass boards are worth several times clear) and enamel surfaces; also soap-advertising headers.

## Shipping / handling
About 24 × 12.5 × 2 in, 2–4 lb; $15–25 shipped. Glass boards need heavy padding.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + model + surface + "washboard": "National Washboard Co No. 860 Brass King", "Glass King 511", "cobalt glass washboard", "Columbus Dubl Handi"; Junk: "laundry decor", "rustic", "primitive", "antique" on a new Dubl Handi. Example: `National Washboard Co No. 860 Brass King Washboard Chicago Saginaw Memphis Vintage`.
- **Where:** local FB for common zinc boards (supply is huge at $5–20; 24" box eats margin); eBay BIN with offers only for glass colour, brass, enamel or soap-advertising headers.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (Zinc King $14–32; Brass King $20, p75 $29; Maid-Rite $48; cobalt/enamel/early wood $60–200), accept −15%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after a month.
- **Photos:** first photo front straight-on with the header readable; then header macro (maker, model, cities), surface close-up (zinc/brass/glass/enamel), back, frame joints for splits, loose ribs, glass for cracks, ruler for size.
- **State:** maker, model number, surface type, size, frame condition, rust holes/cracked glass/loose ribs by photo number, "working board or decor", current-production Columbus check.
- **Pack / haul:** 24×12.5×2", 2–4 lb; flat box, glass boards heavily padded both faces; $15–25. Fits a passenger seat.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Brass King 860] with the three-city header — the 1920s–50s National board, not the Columbus one still made today; frame tight, surface uncracked; the zinc ones are [$15–30], brass and glass [$30–60]."

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$18 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price. Zinc King median $19 → net $16 − $33 = loss. **Pass on all galvanized boards.** Cobalt glass/enamel (~$90) at ≤ $10 barely clears $50. Practically: pass unless rare surface and nearly free.
