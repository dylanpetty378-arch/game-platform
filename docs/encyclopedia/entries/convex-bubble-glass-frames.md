## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Wood (walnut, mahogany-grain painted, oak) or gesso-on-wood frames with domed (convex) glass, sold door-to-door c. 1890–1925 with enlarged "crayon" (charcoal/pastel over photo) portraits of family members, or devotional prints.
- Shapes: oval (most common), arched-top, rectangular with oval opening. Gesso frames have applied floral corners and gilt or bronze paint.
- Size: typically 16×20 to 20×24", so they are large items.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Mostly unbranded; portrait companies (Chicago Portrait Co., Pan-American Portrait Co.) sometimes stamped backing paper.
- Devotional prints (Mater Dolorosa, Sacred Heart) often printed by Catholic publishers.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Original wavy, slightly greenish convex glass; intact backing paper; square nails.
- Painted-grain frames are cheaper than solid walnut.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cracked/missing convex glass (replacements cost $30–80), loose gesso ornaments, faded/foxed portraits, broken corners.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Modern convex frames exist (import decor) but are uncommon; lighter and painted.
