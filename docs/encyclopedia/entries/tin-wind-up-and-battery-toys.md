## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Lithographed tin bodies, with a wind-up key on the side or a battery box underneath.
- Makers print their name or logo on the body or base:
  - US: MARX ("Louis Marx & Co", 1930s–70s), Chein, Unique Art, Wolverine.
  - Japan 1950s–60s: Alps, Masudaya (TM), Nomura (TN), Yonezawa (Y), Horikawa (SH), Bandai, and Linemar (Marx's Japan line).
  - Germany: Schuco, Lehmann, Arnold.
  - Taiwan, Hong Kong and China 1970s+: low value.
- The plush-over-tin battery toys ("drummer", "bear": Funny Drummer tiger, Happy Drummer, Musical Bear) are 1960s Japan or Taiwan and sell $60–150 working.
- Robots and space toys (Horikawa, Nomura, Masudaya) are where the money is.
- Seller words: "tin toy", "wind up toy", "old mechanical toy", "battery toy".

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Lithographed tinplate with tabs folded through slots, a clockwork spring motor, celluloid or vinyl faces on later toys, and battery boxes with spring contacts.
- Old vs new: the 1980s+ Schylling and Chinese reproductions use thinner, brighter litho, plastic gears and "Made in China" (general knowledge, verify).
- An original box is often worth as much as the toy.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rust, scratched or faded litho, broken springs, missing keys, corroded battery contacts and torn plush.
- Repainting or retouched litho kills value. Wind-ups usually still run; battery toys often don't.
- Dylan's rule: no untested electronics. Ask for a video for battery toys.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Schylling and other modern reissues of Marx and Lehmann designs; Chinese reproduction robots (Robby, Planet robots).
- Fake boxes (colour-copied) for rare robots (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Collectors are mostly older, with strong demand for robots and space toys from US and Japanese buyers. eBay, plus Morphy, Bertoia and Milestone auctions for top pieces. Q4 is peak. Common Marx is softening as its buyers age (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Maker marks (TN/TM/SH/Y logos) and the country; whether it works (wind-ups usually do; battery boxes corrode).
- Boxes double the price.
- Robots and character pieces (Popeye, Disney Linemar) are the big misses.

## Shipping / handling
- 1–3 lb; pack carefully; $12–20. eBay ~13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker and mark, country, size, wind-up or battery, working (with a video), every action it performs, litho condition, rust, original box and its condition.
- Photograph the mark, every side, the base and battery box, and the key.
- Title keywords: "Vintage Marx Tin Wind Up", "1950s Japan Tin Litho Battery Operated", "Nomura TN Robot", "Linemar".

## Buy rule
Buy when: Japan/Marx/Schuco marked, working, at ≤ 35% of sold value; or any robot/space toy at ≤ $100.
Pass when: Taiwan/China 1970s+; non-working battery toys; priced at ≥ 60% of sold.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
