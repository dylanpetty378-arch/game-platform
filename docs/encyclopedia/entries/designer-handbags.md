## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Handbags split into three groups for Dylan:
1. **Luxury labels** (Louis Vuitton, Gucci, Chanel, Hermès, Prada, Fendi, Burberry, Céline, Dior, Bottega Veneta, YSL). Prices are high, but so are counterfeits. Resale depends on authentication (date codes, heat stamps, stitching counts, hardware engraving), and eBay routes $500+ bags through its Authenticity Guarantee. **Dylan's rule: out of scope. Pass.**
2. **Mid-market and mall brands** (Coach modern, Michael Kors, Kate Spade, Calvin Klein, Guess, Nine West, Liz Claiborne, Fossil, Mondani, Guy Laroche licensed bags). Plenty of supply and low prices: most sell $10–50.
3. **Vintage non-authentication collectibles.** This is where an owner who doesn't know what they have creates the edge:
   - **1950s Lucite box purses:** hard acrylic boxes in tortoise, pearlized, confetti or clear-with-rhinestones, with brass hinges and clasps. Makers: Llewellyn (Rialto label), Wilardy, Patricia of Miami, Maxim, Gilli, Myles Originals, Florida Handbags. $80–400, with carved or rhinestone versions more.
   - **Whiting & Davis mesh bags** (1900s–80s): metal mesh with an enamel-printed design. 1920s Art Deco painted mesh $100–400. 1970s–80s plain gold mesh $20–60.
   - **Enid Collins of Texas** (1959–1970s): painted and jewelled wooden box bags and canvas bucket bags with "ec" signature. $40–200.
   - **Real alligator and crocodile** (1940s–60s): look for hornback (raised bony scales) or tile-pattern belly scales, umbilical scar, a leather-lined interior, and brass frame clasps. Makers Lucille de Paris, Saks, Nettie Rosenstein. $60–300 (estimate). Embossed cowhide "croc print" or patent vinyl "croc" (very common) is worth $10–30. Exotic skins can have CITES/state restrictions on shipping (California bans the sale of some species). Check before listing.
   - **Bakelite** handle/frame bags, beaded Victorian/Flapper bags, petit-point tapestry bags, Judith Leiber (Leiber needs authentication; pass).
- **Vintage Coach** (1960s–1990s "Made in New York City" / "Made in USA" with serial creed): known among resellers. Sold $40–200. The creed patch with a serial is visible in photos, but fakes are good, so treat as borderline: only buy when the creed and the glove-tanned leather are clearly shown and the price is ≤ $20.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Burberry:** classic "Haymarket check" (beige/black/red/white) canvas with black leather trim. "Nova Check" (same palette, an earlier name). Fakes are everywhere. A real bag needs authentication: pass.
- **Coach:** Bonnie Cashin era (1962–74, turnlock "Cashin carry" pieces), 1970s–90s glove-tanned "Made in New York City" / "Made in United States" creed with serial numbers. 2000s "Made in China" logo jacquard bags are low ($15–40).
- **Dooney & Bourke** All Weather Leather 1980s–90s: $40–120.
- **Calvin Klein:** 2010s–present department-store CK-monogram bags. $15–40.
- **Mondani (New York):** department-store mid-price leather, 1990s–2010s. $10–30.
- **Guy Laroche:** licensed handbags. Low ($15–40).
- **Lucite makers:** Llewellyn Inc. / "Rialto" label (NYC, 1950s), Wilardy Originals, Patricia of Miami, Maxim, Gilli Originals, Myles Originals, Dorset Rex, Charles S. Kahn.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Real alligator:** irregular scale sizes, a natural scale pattern that doesn't repeat, sometimes small pores in the scales, and the edges show a cut skin. Embossed croc repeats its pattern every few inches and the scales are flat.
- **Lucite:** heavy, thick (1/8"+), with smooth polished edges, brass hinges, and a maker's stamp on the inside frame or lid. Later plastic (1960s+) is thinner and moulded with seams.
- **Leather quality:** a full-grain leather interior with turned edges. Coated canvas or PVC "leather" has a bonded backing visible at the edge.
- **Hardware:** solid brass with crisp engraving vs plated zinc with bubbling.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Corner wear, handle cracking, broken straps, stains, pen marks, an odour (smoke, mildew), a torn lining.
- Lucite: cracks (especially at the hinges), crazing, yellowing on clear pieces, missing feet or clasp.
- Mesh: broken or missing links, a torn lining, a bent frame.
- Alligator: dry, flaking skin between scales, broken frame clasps.
- Restoration (re-dyeing, re-lining) lowers collector value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Luxury brands have a counterfeit rate high enough that buyers and platforms demand authentication. This is the reason they're out of scope.
- Coach has 1990s–2000s fakes with mis-stamped creeds and wrong fonts.
- Modern Lucite box bags (2010s retro trend) are thinner and often marked "Made in China". Vintage-style reproductions of Whiting & Davis exist.
- "Croc embossed" listings are often called "crocodile" by sellers.

## Market
This is a huge, fast market on Poshmark, eBay, Mercari and The RealReal. Mid-market bags are saturated and slow. Lucite and vintage-novelty bags sell to a collector niche within weeks at good prices. Luxury moves fast but only with authentication. There's a slight Q4 bump for evening and Lucite bags.

## What sellers usually miss
- **Lucite box purses listed as "old plastic purse"**, especially in estate lots.
- **Real alligator listed as "croc purse"**: look for the hornback and irregular scales.
- **Enid Collins** listed as "painted wooden purse" (look for the "ec" signature on the side).
- **Painted Whiting & Davis mesh** listed as "metal chain purse".
- The reverse: sellers attach luxury names to look-alike or licensed bags ("Coach" for a Calvin Klein hobo, "Burberry" without a visible label), and Lens matches luxury listings to mall bags (the Mondani and Coach Ergo confusion).

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb. Ship in a box (not a poly mailer), stuffed with tissue to hold the shape. $10–14. Lucite needs double-boxing with corners padded: $14–18. Exotic skins: check the destination state's laws (California) and avoid international shipping (CITES).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Give the maker, the material (genuine leather, alligator, Lucite, mesh), the era, the measurements (L x H x D, strap drop), and the interior label and stamps.
- Photograph all sides, the corners, the interior, the label, the hardware, the base and the strap.
- Disclose odour, stains and lining issues.
- Never write a brand name in the title unless the label is in the photos. Use "unbranded" or "attributed" honestly.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$12) − shipping to Dylan (~$12) − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). **Luxury labels (LV, Gucci, Chanel, Burberry, Prada, Fendi, Hermès, Dior, YSL, Judith Leiber) are out of scope because they need professional authentication. Always pass, at any price.** Buy only 1950s Lucite box purses (maker stamp or thick brass-hinged construction visible) ≤ $40, real hornback alligator ≤ $40, Enid Collins ≤ $20, or 1920s painted Whiting & Davis mesh ≤ $40. Pass on all mall and mid-market brands and on croc-embossed and patent bags.
