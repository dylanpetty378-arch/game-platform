## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
This is a reading guide for coin lots, jars, folders and "old money" boxes. Read **silver-certificates-and-coins** for paper money, Morgan grading tiers and the common-coin price floor; **world-banknotes** for foreign paper; **transit-tokens** for fare tokens; **precious-metal-marks-reading-guide** for melt arithmetic.

**What each mark means:** **CC** Carson City 1870–93 (all CC coins carry a premium); **O** New Orleans 1838–61 and 1879–1909; **S** San Francisco 1854– (proofs since 1968); **D** Dahlonega (gold only, 1838–61) or Denver (1906–); **C** Charlotte (gold, 1838–61); **P** Philadelphia (war nickels, then 1979/80 on); **W** West Point (gold/silver bullion and commemoratives 1984–, 1996-W dime, 2019–20 W quarters). No mark = Philadelphia before 1980.

**What it looks like in a typical listing photo:** a heap of coins photographed from above; the mint mark is 1–2 mm and unreadable unless the seller photographs single coins. Ask for date and mint-mark close-ups for every coin you'd pay a premium for. A Whitman folder with filled holes is the best tell that someone pulled keys (look at the empty holes: an empty 1909-S VDB hole is the norm).

**Recognition rules:** gold is non-magnetic, deep yellow, small and heavy for size; a sovereign's 22K is slightly reddish. Silver coins ring when balanced on a fingertip and tapped; slide off a tilted magnet slowly. Read the edge (reeded vs plain) and the weight on a gram scale.

### Tokens (general knowledge, verify unless cited)
- **Civil War tokens (1861–64):** private one-cent tokens struck because government cents were hoarded; ~25 million in 7,000–8,000 varieties; types are **patriotic** (flags, cannons, the Monitor, Dix "shoot him on the spot"), **store cards** (merchant name and town) and **sutler tokens** (regiment + sutler; rarest). Mostly copper/bronze, also brass, nickel, white metal, silver. Catalogued by Fuld numbers with rarity R-1 (5,000+ known) to R-10 (unique). Banned by law in 1864; owning them was never illegal (Wikipedia). Size ≈ cent (19 mm). Values: common patriotic $15–40, common store cards $20–80, scarce towns, R-7+ and sutlers $200–2,000+. Look-alikes: Hard Times tokens (1833–44, larger, political), modern copies (cast, soft, sometimes marked COPY).
- **So-called dollars:** dollar-size (≈38 mm) medals for expositions and events (1876 Centennial, 1893 Columbian, 1901 Pan-American, 1904 Louisiana Purchase, 1915 Panama-Pacific, 1926 Sesquicentennial), Bryan money (1896 satire), and commemorations. Catalogued by Hibler-Kappen (HK) numbers. Base-metal pieces $20–150; silver and gold strikes, rare HK numbers, and high grade $300–5,000+. Often sold as "old medal", "coin", "token".
- Transit tokens: see transit-tokens.

### Proof sets, mint sets and slabs (general knowledge, verify)
- **Proof sets:** 1936–42 (rare; $1,000+ sets), **1950–64 silver proof sets** (cellophane or flat pack; 1950 $400+, 1952–64 ~$30–60 each; silver content 0.6148 oz ≈ $36.89 melt), 1965–67 Special Mint Sets (no proofs; SMS) $10–20, 1968– clad proof sets $5–15, 1992– silver proof sets $25–60 (more for 1999, 2008), Prestige sets. Error proofs: 1968-S, 1970-S and 1983-S no-S dimes, 1990 no-S cent — the best hidden value in a box of proof sets; look in the dime and cent windows for a missing S.
- **Mint sets:** 1947–58 double sets (original cardboard) $50–1,000+; 1959–64 $20–40; modern $5–20.
- **Slabs:** **PCGS, NGC** (top), CAC sticker (premium), ANACS, ICG (lower). Any other "slab" (e.g. "Gem Uncirculated" holders from home-shopping sellers, unknown "certified" labels) is a plastic box, not a grade. Check the cert number on the grader's site and match the photo; counterfeit PCGS holders with fake coins exist (NGC forum cites fake 1885-CC Morgans in fake MS65 PCGS holders).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Gold coin in a "foreign coin lot"** — a sovereign ≈ $989, a 20-franc Rooster ≈ $784, Mexican 50 pesos ≈ $5,064 melt, in lots sold at $20–100 (~10–100×). Photo tell: a small, bright yellow coin among copper and nickel; St George and dragon, a rooster, or a winged angel.
- **War nickels and 40% Kennedys in "nickel" and "half" rolls** — $3.38 and $8.88 each vs face (~65× and ~18× face). Tell: big P/D/S above Monticello; Kennedy dated 1965–70.
- **1909-S VDB, 1914-D, 1931-S, 1922 no-D in a wheat-cent folder or jar** — $85–2,500+ each vs 3–10¢ (~1,000×+). Tell: S/D under the date; VDB on the reverse rim.
- **1916-D dime, 1942/1 dime, 1932-D/S quarter in "silver coin lot"** — $100–1,200 vs $4–11 melt (~10–250×). Tell: tiny D left of the fasces; overdate; mint mark below the quarter's wreath.
- **1955 doubled die in a cent jar** — $1,200–3,800 circulated-to-MS63 (CPG, Heritage 2026).
- **CC Morgans in a "silver dollar lot"** — $300–1,500+ vs $46 melt (~7–30×). Tell: CC under the wreath.
- **No-S proof dime or cent** in a proof-set box — $500–3,000+ vs $5–15 per set.
- **Civil War sutler token or rare store card in a "token lot"** — $200–2,000 vs $1–5 per token.
- **Silver or gold so-called dollar** sold as a "medal" — $300+ vs $20.

### Quick ID checklist
- Sort the lot by date and country before reading any title: anything US dated ≤ 1964 (dimes–dollars) = silver; Kennedy 1965–70 = 40%; nickels 1942–45 with a big mint mark = 35%.
- Count and weigh: $ face × $42.90 for worn 90%; grams × fineness for world silver and gold.
- Look for anything yellow and small: sovereign, 20 francs, Mexican or US gold.
- Ask for date and mint-mark close-ups of any key-date candidate; compare the mint mark's style and field for added letters.
- Doubling: rounded and separated = doubled die; flat shelf = machine doubling.
- Proof sets: check every dime and cent window for the S.
- Slabs: PCGS/NGC only; verify the cert number online; check the holder's edges and label print quality.
- Magnet and weight on anything bullion-like or high-value: genuine silver and gold are non-magnetic.
- Never clean coins; a cleaned key date loses 50%+.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Original surface vs cleaned:** cleaned coins show hairline scratches across the fields, an unnatural bright or dull grey color, and residue in recesses; graders net-grade them ("details"), cutting value 30–70%.
- **Toning:** natural rainbow toning on Morgans and proof sets can add value; artificial toning (heated, chemical) is garish and penalized.
- **Proof vs business strike:** proofs have mirror fields and frosted devices; prooflike Morgans command premiums.
- **Grading terms:** G (Good, design outlines only), VG, F, VF (major details clear), XF, AU (slight wear on high points), MS60–70 (no wear). Key dates move most with grade.
- **Metal tests:** silver ring, magnet slide, edge stripe (clad), weight (Morgan 26.73 g, 38.1 mm; Trade 27.22 g).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Holes, solder (jewelry use), bends, corrosion, cleaning, whizzing (wire-brushed fake luster) and tooled dates all cut collector value to near melt. Coins mounted in bezels may have been soldered — ask. Corroded cents in jars are mostly worth face; key dates still sell in poor grades. Bulk silver is condition-insensitive (priced by weight).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Chinese copies of Trade dollars and Morgans** (also Seated, Bust, Flowing Hair, Draped Bust, gold, Silver Eagles and Pandas): sold by the bag on marketplaces and from "found in grandpa's attic" listings. Tells (NGC forum and general knowledge): wrong date size, shape and placement; tilted or oversize mint marks; mushy design details (Liberty's face, eagle's breast feathers, uneven denticles, wrong letter forms); lack of normal bag marks on a supposed business strike; wrong luster. Weight alone no longer proves much because many fakes now match it (NGC forum); combine weight, diameter, thickness, magnet and the ring test.
- **Impossible dates:** Morgans are not dated 1905–1920 or 1922+ (except modern 2021+ US Mint issues); business-strike 1895 Morgans don't exist; Trade dollars 1879–83 are proof-only and 1884 (10 known) and 1885 (5 known) are museum coins — any "1884 Trade dollar" is fake (general knowledge, verify).
- **Altered dates and added mint marks:** 1909-S VDB, 1914-D, 1916-D, 1932-D/S, 1893-S, CC Morgans. Look for tool marks around the mint mark, and the mint mark's font for that year.
- **Copper-plated 1943 steel cents** and "1943 copper" altered from 1948.
- **Fake slabs** (PCGS and NGC look-alikes); fake bullion (tungsten-cored or plated gold bars; "silver clad"/"silver layered" "999 fine silver plated" commemoratives — no silver).
- **Restrikes:** Mexican 50 pesos and 2/2.5/5/10/20 pesos gold (dated 1945–47) are official restrikes, still full gold; Austrian 1915 ducats and 1780 Maria Theresa thalers are official restrikes; their value is metal.
- **Replica tokens:** cast Civil War tokens and modern "Bryan money" souvenirs.

## Market
Coins are the most liquid collectibles: melt sells same-day to any coin shop or bullion dealer at 90–100% of melt; key dates sell quickly on eBay, through Heritage/GreatCollections, and to local dealers. Bulk lots attract many bidders, so cheap estate lots are where the edge is (sellers price by face or by "old coins"). Gold and silver at record levels (October 2026) make dealers aggressive buyers; slabbed keys hold value. Seasonality small; coin shows and ANA (August) lift activity.

## What sellers usually miss
- The mint mark entirely ("1916 dime" with a tiny D).
- War nickels and 40% Kennedys mixed in with clad.
- Gold coins among foreign change ("brass coins").
- VDB initials and overdates (1942/1).
- No-S proofs in proof-set boxes.
- Civil War tokens called "old pennies".
- The reverse error: sellers list Chinese fakes, cleaned coins and machine doubling as rare and over-ask.

## Shipping / handling
Light and small: coin flips or 2×2s in a padded envelope or small box, $5–6; insure anything over $100, USPS Registered for high value; do not label "coins" or "gold" on the outside. Bulk silver is heavy (1,000 g ≈ 2.2 lb) — flat-rate box. Never clean or polish.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State date, mint mark and denomination, metal and fineness, weight, grade (or "raw, ungraded" with honest description), any cleaning or damage, and the variety attribution (e.g. FS-101). Photograph obverse and reverse straight on with even light, plus date and mint-mark close-ups and the edge. For slabs, show the cert number. Title keywords: year + mint mark + series + variety + grade ("1916-D Mercury Dime G4 Key Date"), "90% silver", "PCGS" or "NGC".

## Buy rule
Buy when: silver lots at ≤ ~70% of melt (resold at melt to a dealer that still leaves Dylan's profit only on larger lots — check the arithmetic) or ≤ ~43% of a realistic eBay sold price; gold coins at ≤ ~40–45% of melt unless resold at melt quickly; a key date or variety visible in a sharp photo at ≤ 30% of the comp for its grade, ideally slabbed PCGS/NGC with a verified cert. Pass when: no date or mint-mark photos, cleaned or holed keys priced as originals, unknown "certified" holders, bags of bright Morgans or Trade dollars from overseas or "attic finds", any impossible date. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Always fits a passenger seat.
