## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thin borosilicate ("Pyrex") lampworked bottles made in Cairo workshops since the 1970s and sold to tourists and through import shops. Tells: very thin clear or coloured glass, gold-painted bands and leaf/floral decoration, etched (sand-blasted or wheel-cut) floral bands, tall "flame" or "teardrop" stoppers with ribbing, spiral foot rings, sizes 3–12". Often sold in sets. They are décor, not antiques, and are still produced today.

## Makers, brands and variants
No named makers. Variants: clear with gold; colour-tinted (cobalt, ruby, green, amethyst, amber) with gold; black-painted with gold (common 1990s–2000s); miniatures 2–4"; large display 10–14". Similar look: Czech or Murano miniatures (heavier, better), Indian/Chinese imitations (thicker, cheaper gold).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Borosilicate glass is extremely thin and light; gold is a fired-on lustre that rubs off. Better pieces have even wall thickness and crisp etching. Older (1970s–80s) ones show worn gold; new ones are bright.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken stopper tips (very common, they're thin as paper), chipped feet, worn gold, cracked necks, stoppers glued in.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
The whole category is souvenir production; "antique Egyptian" claims are almost always wrong. Real ancient Egyptian glass (core-formed, opaque, small) is a completely different, museum-grade field and needs authentication.

## Market
Saturated: new ones sell for $3–8 each on Amazon/eBay from importers. Vintage carries no premium.

## What sellers usually miss
Nothing; sellers usually overvalue them as "hand-blown vintage art glass".

## Shipping / handling
Extremely fragile; each bottle wrapped separately, stopper removed and wrapped separately, double-boxed. Breakage rate is high.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Describe as "hand-blown Egyptian Pyrex glass perfume bottle, souvenir/décor", heights, colours, any broken tips.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price ≥ max($50, price). Fails at any realistic price. Pass. Not worth the time.
