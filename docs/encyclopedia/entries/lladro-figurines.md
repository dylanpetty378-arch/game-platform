## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Spanish porcelain figures (Valencia) in pale blue/grey/tan matte or glossy glazes, elongated faces, often with flowers. Backstamp on the base: blue "LLADRÓ" with the bellflower logo and "Made in Spain" (1974+), or impressed early marks; **NAO** (Lladró's budget line – same look, blue "NAO" mark) is worth 20–30% of a Lladró; **Daisa** on the base is the design-copyright line, not a maker. Model number often incised/printed on the base. Sellers write "Lladro angel", "Spanish porcelain figurine". Value is in retired limited editions, large groups, and perfect condition – chipped flower petals or fingers kill value. Look-alikes: Zaphir, Rex, Casades, Tengra (other Valencian makers, lower value).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Lladró** (founded 1953 by the Lladró brothers; general knowledge, verify) – core line, glossy and matte ("mate") finishes, Gres (stoneware, earthy tones) line.
- **NAO** – Lladró's secondary line; 20–30% of Lladró value.
- **Zaphir** – earlier Lladró-related line (general knowledge, verify).
- High-value: limited editions (Cinderella's Arrival, Flamenco Flair, 18th Century Coach), large groups (carriages, Three Kings, Gypsy Dancers), Utopia/Elite pieces.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hard-paste porcelain; hand-made flowers (individual petals), fine fingers; glossy vs matte glaze. Genuine: crisp backstamp, model number, quality modelling; Lladró blue backstamp evolved over decades (dating by mark; general knowledge, verify). Ground-off or scratched-out mark = factory second ("seconds" sold in Spain), worth much less (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chipped flower petals, fingers, lace and thin protrusions; restored pieces (look for slight colour/glaze mismatch, UV light shows repairs), glued breaks, missing parts (umbrellas, canes). Ask for closeups of hands, flowers and the base, and whether the original box is present (adds 20–30%).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese copies with fake Lladró marks exist; poorer modelling and print. Seconds with ground marks. NAO sold as Lladró. (general knowledge, verify)

## Market
Large supply from estates (1980s–90s gift buying); demand softer than at peak – common pieces sell slowly, large groups and limited editions still sell. Buyers: collectors, replacement seekers (Replacements Ltd). Nov–Dec and Mother's Day bumps. (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
That NAO ≠ Lladró; that a "lot of Lladro" sometimes contains one large retired group worth the whole lot. Boxes add 20–30%.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile; double box, $20–35 insured. eBay 13%. Photograph every finger and petal before shipping.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State model number and name, Lladró vs NAO, glossy/matte, height, retired year if known, condition (no chips/repairs), box. Photograph the backstamp, every delicate part closeup, box. Title keywords: "Lladro #1030 Don Quixote retired", "mint in box", "Made in Spain".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 12"+ groups or boxed retired pieces under $100; lots under $5/piece with large pieces visible. Pass when: small angels/children over $30 each, any chips, NAO at Lladró prices. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat.
