## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-thrown or hand-built ceramics by named artists, typically signed (incised signature, stamp or chop) on the base. Tells: thrown rings inside, fingermarks, considered glazes (crawled, crystalline, ash, volcanic), weight appropriate to stoneware.
- **Thrown pieces:** spiral throwing rings inside the neck, a trimmed foot ring with tool chatter or a cut-off swirl, slight asymmetry, glaze that pools and breaks over edges.
- **Hand-built/sculptural:** slab or coil joins, carved or combed texture, applied clay (e.g. extruded "hair"), often unglazed iron-rich clay contrasted with a glazed area.
- **Noel Osheroff / Robert Maxwell & David Cressey circle (1970s California)**: sculptural mother-and-child and figure groups in unglazed red/brown clay with textured hair, set on blue or earth-glazed cylindrical bases; incised signature on base (general knowledge, verify). A documented Osheroff mother-and-child is credited "for Robert Maxwell Studio", signed, c. 12.5 in tall, ceramic on a solid walnut base, dated by the seller to c. 1970s (1stDibs).
- **Hans Hedberg (Biot, France)**: faience fruit (apples, eggs) and vases with bright glazes.
- **Decor imports**: moulded, faux-patina painted finishes (rust red over grey with metallic flecks), applied wire spirals — not studio pottery.
- **Slip-cast "art pottery" (Haeger, Royal Haeger, Red Wing, Van Briggle, McCoy)** is factory production, not studio pottery: crisp mould seams, identical forms, moulded mark. Some (Van Briggle, Rookwood) have their own markets.

## Makers, brands and variants
See heading; check signatures against mark databases (e.g. potterymarks/makers'-mark guides).
- **Robert Maxwell (California) and David Cressey — Earthgender:** stoneware collaboration (late 1960s–1970s); hand-carved, iron-rich brown unglazed clay with relief motifs, planters, tall vessels and figural sculpture; signed on the base. Maxwell also produced under his own studio name, and some pieces are Maxwell-only, not Earthgender (Chairish listing makes this distinction). Asking prices on 1stDibs/Chairish for Maxwell planters run into the high hundreds ($875 asking for one planter), which are retail asks, not sold comps.
- **Noel Osheroff:** sculptor making figural pieces (mother-and-child groups) for Robert Maxwell Studio; signed. Values track Maxwell/Earthgender interest.
- **Hans Hedberg (1917–2007):** Swedish, studied in Faenza 1947–48, own studio and gas kiln in Biot (French Riviera) from 1949. Known for faience fruits and eggs (apples, pears, eggs, figs) in naturalistic, often mottled glazes, from small to near 1 m garden pieces. Signed "HHg" (often with "Biot"). Bukowskis sold a c. 15.5 cm blue/green spotted faience egg (on metal stand) for SEK 15,000 hammer on 2023-11-15 (estimate SEK 8–10,000).
- **Gustavsberg (Sweden) — Berndt Friberg, Stig Lindberg:** Friberg small thrown stoneware vases with hare's-fur and celadon-like glazes, incised "Friberg" with the Gustavsberg hand mark and a year-letter code; Lindberg broader range (stoneware, faience) with Gustavsberg hand and "Stig L." (general knowledge, verify).
- **Natzler (Gertrud threw, Otto glazed):** thin-walled bowls and bottles, crater/volcanic and reduction glazes; signed "NATZLER" incised; high fake/attribution risk at that value (general knowledge, verify).
- **Beatrice Wood:** lustre glazes, folk-figurative pieces; signed "Beato" (general knowledge, verify).
- **Peter Voulkos, Jerry Rothman:** Otis-era abstract expressionist stoneware; big values, frequent forged signatures.
- **Warren MacKenzie / Minnesota potters:** functional stoneware, often only a small chop or no mark; modest per-piece values but steady demand (general knowledge, verify).
- **Edwin & Mary Scheier; Toshiko Takaezu (closed forms with a rattle inside); Brother Thomas Bezanson (porcelain, copper-red and iron glazes):** recognised American names with collector markets (general knowledge, verify).
- **Lucie Rie, Hans Coper (UK):** impressed "LR" and "HC" seals; tens of thousands at auction, heavily scrutinised.
- What moves value inside a maker: sculptural/figural over plain vessels, size, signature legibility, a documented glaze or series, and original base or stand.

### Quick ID checklist
- Photo of the **base**: incised signature, chop/seal, studio stamp, paper label? Read it letter by letter.
- Thrown rings inside / trimmed foot vs crisp mould seams (slip-cast factory).
- Glaze vs paint: real glaze is glassy and fused, paint sits on top and scratches; metallic flecks and faux rust = decor.
- Unglazed areas show real clay colour (iron-rich red/brown for Maxwell circle) rather than painted brown.
- "Made in China/Mexico" paper label or felt pad covering the base = decor.
- Sculptural figure on a walnut or glazed cylinder base, textured clay hair → check Osheroff/Maxwell.
- Faience fruit/egg with mottled glaze → look for "HHg"/"Biot".
- Chips or restoration at rims, noses, fingertips, foot ring.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real studio work has glazed foot treatment, signed bases, purposeful glaze; decor pieces have felt pads, paper labels "Made in China/Mexico", paint not glaze.
- **Stoneware** (high-fired, dense, rings when tapped, often grey/buff/brown body); **porcelain** (white, translucent where thin); **earthenware/faience** (soft, porous, chips show light-coloured body, usually tin-glazed white under colour).
- Quality: even wall thickness for the size, considered weight (not leaden), glaze breaks crisply over texture, clean foot.
- Cheap production: heavy, uniform walls from slip casting; pinholes all over a cheap glaze; painted finishes; foam-filled resin "ceramic".
- Old vs new: base dirt and wear on the foot ring, oxidised unglazed clay, period labels (gallery stickers from the 1960s–70s); fresh bright bases and uniform "aging" suggest new or decorator pieces.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips and restorations reduce value strongly; firing flaws inherent are acceptable.
- Common: rim chips, foot ring flakes, broken fingertips/noses on figures, hairline cracks from the rim, crazing on earthenware.
- Restorations show as matte or slightly different-coloured patches, overly glossy areas, or fluorescence under UV; ask for a raking-light photo of rims and extremities.
- Kiln flaws (glaze skips, small firing cracks in the base, kiln grit) made at the time are acceptable and should be described, not hidden.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Forged signatures on high-end names (Natzler, Voulkos); attributed "style of" listings.
- Lens or "similar to" matches to famous names are not attributions; insist on a legible signature that matches published examples.
- Decorator copies of Earthgender-type carved planters and of Scandinavian "fat lava" or hare's-fur glazes exist; check signature and clay body.

## Market
Collector-driven, sporadic, high variance; strong for mid-century California names.
- Buyers: mid-century design collectors and decorators (1stDibs/Chairish retail is high; eBay and regional auction realise less). Hedberg sells best in Scandinavian auction houses.

## What sellers usually miss
Signature identity; sculptural studio pieces sold as "decorative statue".
- The Robert Maxwell Studio connection on Osheroff figures; "HHg" on a faience apple sold as "ceramic fruit".

## Shipping / handling
1–6 lb, fragile; double-box; $15–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Signature close-up, dimensions, attribution with reference, condition under light.
- Title pattern: "<Maker> <form> <clay/glaze> Studio Pottery <decade> California/Swedish Signed".

### What to tell the buyer
Give the maker as read from the signature and show the signature photo; if the attribution rests on style plus signature, say so. For an Osheroff figure: "Noel Osheroff, sculpted for Robert Maxwell Studio, California, c. 1970s; signed; unglazed clay with glazed base" (match the actual base). For Hedberg: "Hans Hedberg (1917–2007), Biot, France; faience; signed HHg". State height and width, and describe every chip, repair and kiln flaw with a close-up. Do not call a piece Earthgender unless it is marked as such; Maxwell made pieces under his own studio too.
