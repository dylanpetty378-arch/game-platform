## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Folded printed tri-fold or rack brochures, guidebooks and maps distributed at attractions. Date clues: logos (SeaWorld's Shamu/"wave" logo variants), attractions advertised (SeaWorld Orlando "Penguin Encounter" opened 1987), prices, phone numbers without area codes (pre-1960s), ZIP codes (post-1963).
- Disneyland ticket books date themselves by coupon letters: "Value Books" with A–C coupons began within three months of the July 1955 opening; D coupons were added in 1956 and E coupons in June 1959; coupons ended at Disneyland by June 1982. Walt Disney World's Magic Kingdom used the same system from 1971 (Wikipedia). A book with only A–C coupons is 1955–56; one with A–D is 1956–59; A–E is 1959–82.
- Coupon face values (Wikipedia): A 10¢, B 15¢, C 25¢, D 50¢, E 85¢ — printed prices on the book also help date it.
- World's Fair official guides are mass-market paperbacks with a 25¢ cover price (1933 Century of Progress, 1939 New York "World of Tomorrow"); the 1939 guide went through several editions, and the edition is stated in the book (EBTH lot listed a first and a third edition).
- Railroad timetables come in two kinds: public (passenger) fold-up pocket timetables with graphic covers, and larger employee timetables (about 8 × 14 in, "horseblankets") meant for internal use (Collectors Weekly).
- Airline timetables: small folded schedules with route maps and fares; pre-war (e.g. American Airways 1933) and early Pan Am "Clipper" items are the collected ones (listing titles).

## Makers, brands and variants
SeaWorld (San Diego 1964, Ohio 1970, Orlando 1973, San Antonio 1988); Walt Disney World (1971–); Disneyland (1955–); Busch Gardens; Cypress Gardens; Silver Springs; Marineland; Knott's Berry Farm; World's Fairs (1933 Chicago, 1939/1964 NY); railroads and airlines (Pan Am, TWA).

### Quick ID checklist
- Which attraction and what year? Check opening dates (SeaWorld parks above; Disneyland 1955; WDW 1971) and advertised rides.
- Disney ticket book: which coupon letters are present (A–C, A–D or A–E)? Are coupons still attached?
- ZIP code present (post-1963)? Area code in the phone number?
- World's Fair guide: "Official Guide Book" on the cover, edition statement inside, 25¢ cover price.
- Timetable: public pocket timetable vs employee "horseblanket"; date printed on the cover.
- Printing: period offset with visible dot screen and aged paper vs crisp modern inkjet on bright white paper.
- Condition: writing, tape, tears, water stains, missing coupons or panels.

### Golden-egg tells
- 1955–56 Disneyland items (A–C-only ticket books, opening-year guides and maps) — $200–2,000 vs $3–15 for 1980s rack brochures — only A, B, C coupons; 1955 dates; early attraction lineup.
- 1950s–60s Disneyland guidebooks, ticket books and early maps — $25–200 (Terapeak "1955 Disneyland guide" median $62, p75 $94) — A–D or A–E books, pre-ZIP addresses, early ride names.
- 1939 New York World's Fair first-edition Official Guide Book — above later editions (no comp this run; general knowledge, verify) — edition statement on the title or copyright page.
- Pre-war airline timetables (American Airways 1933, early Pan Am Clipper) — upper part of the $10–100 band (general knowledge, verify) — airline name before mergers, propeller aircraft, 1930s dates.
- Railroad public timetables with strong graphic covers from named streamliners — upper part of $10–100 (general knowledge, verify) — colourful covers, train names.
- Not golden eggs: 1980s–90s SeaWorld, Busch and Disney World rack brochures (Terapeak SeaWorld median $3.49); 1939 World's Fair general brochures (Terapeak median $14).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original printing (offset, period colours) vs. reprints/scans on modern paper. Crisp folds, no writing, no tape.
- Period paper tones evenly with age at edges; modern copies are bright white and the colours sit on the surface (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Folds, creases, stains, tears, sun fade, writing. Most value is condition-driven for mid-tier items.
- World's Fair guides commonly have edge wear, loose covers, creasing and browned pages (EBTH lot); price those at the low end.
- Ticket books: missing coupons reduce value; a full unused book is the premium state (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Disney items and early tickets are reproduced; theme-park rack brochures are not worth faking.
- Disney itself has issued commemorative reproductions of early tickets and maps (general knowledge, verify); check paper and printing.

## Market
High volume, low prices; lots sell to nostalgia buyers. Single 1980s brochures take months. Railroad timetables are a popular, affordable collecting niche (Collectors Weekly).

## What sellers usually miss
Early Disneyland items in "old brochure" lots; airline/railroad timetables with graphic covers; the coupon letters that date a Disney ticket book.

## Shipping / handling
Flat rigid mailer, $4–6.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Date it by attractions/logos, give dimensions and condition, photograph all panels.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the attraction or issuer, the date and how it was dated (opening year of an advertised ride, ZIP code, coupon letters, edition statement), dimensions, number of pages or panels, and condition including folds, writing, tape and fading. For ticket books, list exactly which coupons remain. Say it ships flat in a rigid mailer.

## Buy rule
Single brochure can never clear $50; pass. Buy lots only when they contain pre-1970 Disney/World's Fair items with sold median ≥ $100 at ≤ $30.
