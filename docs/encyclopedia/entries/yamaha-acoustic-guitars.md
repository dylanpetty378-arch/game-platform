## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Yamaha headstock is a narrow three-point crown with the tuning-fork logo. Tells for the valuable lines: **A-series**
(A1M/A1R/A3M/A3R/A5) = dreadnought or concert with a single cutaway, tortoise pickguard, onboard SRT preamp on the upper
bout, often "Vintage Natural" amber finish; **FG red-label** (1966–72, "Nippon Gakki" red label inside soundhole) = 1960s
Japanese dreadnought; **L-series** (LL/LS/LJ 6/16/26) = high-end solid wood with a wider headstock; **FG-180/FG-300** =
early Japan FGs. Sellers write "Yamaha guitar", "acoustic guitar with case". The model is printed on the label inside
the soundhole.

More detail:
- **Where the identifiers are**: the soundhole label carries the model and often the serial. Serial numbers may also be stamped on the neck block (visible through the soundhole) or on the back of the headstock. Taiwan-made guitars sometimes say "Made in Taiwan" on the back of the headstock (YamahaMusicians).
- **A-series model code** (Bananas): A = A-series; C = concert body (no C means dreadnought, e.g. AC3M vs A3M); 1/3/5 = tier; M = mahogany back and sides; R = rosewood. The A4K/AC4K was a limited edition in all-solid Hawaiian koa.
- **Red-label FG**: a red label reading "Nippon Gakki" in the soundhole, a plain dreadnought with no cutaway, and a simple headstock with the tuning-fork logo. Models seen include FG-75, FG-110, FG-140, FG-150, FG-180, FG-230 (12-string) and FG-300 (TDPRI; Reverb).
- **Look-alikes**: Yamaha's current F310/F325/FG800 look like the vintage FGs from across the room. The label colour and text and the serial format sort them out.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **FG red label** (1966–72, Japan, "Nippon Gakki"): FG-180, FG-140, FG-300 etc. — collectible.
- **FG tan/green label** (1970s–80s, Taiwan/Japan): FG-335/340/375 — decent.
- **FG current** (FG700/FG800, F310/F325 retail lines) — junk tier (current retail).
- **A-series** (A1M/A1R/A3M/A3R/A5): solid top, SRT preamp — decent to collectible.
- **L-series** (LL/LS/LJ 6/16/26/36/56): solid wood, high end; LL36/LL56 hand-built top.
- **FG-1500**, 1970s **L-10/L-12** — top.
- **APX500/FGX** — decent; **C40** classical and **Eterna** beginner — junk.
- Suffix letters: M = mahogany back/sides, R = rosewood; ARE = Yamaha's torrefied-top process (general knowledge, verify).

Label-era corrections and detail (TDPRI owners' club; YamahaMusicians):
- **Green label** (c.1966–67): early Japan-market FGs, sharing the serial pool with red labels. They are *not* a 1970s–80s Taiwan label as the line above implies. Green-label and very early red-label FGs have a truss-rod cover reading "Reinforced Neck".
- **Red label** ("Nippon Gakki", Japan): Yamaha dates the first US imports to 1968. An FG-150 with an internal date stamp of 17 March 1967 shows that some are earlier. US-bound Japanese production is reported to have stopped around fall 1971, with later guitars made in Taiwan (Kaohsiung). A red label without "Nippon Gakki" is reported to mean Taiwan-made (YamahaMusicians).
- **Later 1970s labels** (orange, black, tan/brown) cover the FG-x35/x40/x45/x75 generations from Japan and Taiwan (general knowledge, verify by label photo).
- **A-series tiers** (Bananas):
  - **A1**: solid Sitka spruce top, laminate back and sides, simplified preamp, no A.R.E. MSRP from $815.
  - **A3**: solid back and sides, A.R.E. top, SRT2 preamp, die-cast tuners, hard bag; made in China.
  - **A5**: solid Sitka top with A.R.E. and scalloped bracing, solid mahogany or rosewood back and sides, ebony board and bridge, TUSQ nut and saddle, open-gear Gotoh tuners, SRT2 preamp, hardshell case; made in Japan.
- **L-series**: Yamaha's solid-wood line (LL = dreadnought-type "original jumbo", LS = small body, LJ = medium jumbo); higher numbers mean more solid wood and hand work (Yamaha L-series brochure 2014; general knowledge, verify per model).

### Quick ID checklist
- Get a clear photo of the soundhole label. It shows the model, label colour, "Nippon Gakki" or not, and often the serial.
- Red label with "Nippon Gakki" means Japan, roughly 1967–71. A red label without it, or "Made in Taiwan" on the headstock back, means Taiwan.
- On a vintage FG, check the truss-rod cover for "Reinforced Neck", which marks a green-label or very early red-label guitar.
- A cutaway, upper-bout preamp and amber "vintage natural" finish mean A-series. Read the A1/A3/A5 and M/R code on the label; an A3 or A5 is worth far more than an A1.
- Check for a hard case vs gig bag. The A5 shipped with a hardshell case and the A3 with a hard bag (Bananas).
- Take a side view of the action at the 12th fret and look for a bulge behind the bridge. High action and bowed necks are common on red labels.
- Check the bridge edges for lifting and the top around the soundhole and below the bridge for cracks.
- Look for a battery-compartment photo on electric models to check for corrosion.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid tops (spruce/cedar) vs laminate; solid back/sides on L-series high models. Red-label FGs have laminate bodies but
are prized for era and tone. Original tuners, bone nut/saddle, and the SRT preamp working on A-series. Finish checking on
1970s guitars is normal.
- **Solid vs laminate check**: look at the soundhole edge. A solid top shows the same grain continuing through the edge. A laminate shows plies or a grain that doesn't match inside. The A1 is solid-top over laminate; the A3 and A5 are solid throughout (Bananas).
- **A.R.E.** (Acoustic Resonance Enhancement) is a Yamaha top-aging process, on the A3 and A5 but not the A1 (Bananas).
- **Red-label construction**: all-laminate bodies, which is why they cost less than their reputation suggests (YamahaMusicians). Their appeal is era and sound.
- **Dating by serial** (GuitarInsite; YamahaMusicians):
  - 8-digit serials on early Japanese FGs begin with the year. 6–7-digit neck-block numbers are sequential across Yamaha production.
  - Some red labels carry an internal date stamp in Japanese Showa era years (Showa 1 = 1926, so Showa 42 = 1967).
  - 1969–84 six-digit format: two-digit year, two-digit month, two-digit serial.
  - From 2004, a seven-character code: two year letters, a month letter, three digits, and a factory letter. Example: QPM044C = June 2009, where H=1 … P=9, Q=0, and the month letters are H=Jan … P=Sep, X=Oct, Y=Nov, Z=Dec.
  - Use YamahaMusicians' serial checker to confirm.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Top cracks, bridge lifting, belly bulge, high action needing a neck reset, fret wear, dead preamp/battery corrosion,
cracked pickguard. "Needs work" or cracked = pass. Ask for photos of the soundhole label, bridge edges, neck heel, and a
side view of the action.
- **Red labels**: bowed necks and high action are common, and repair can cost more than the guitar is worth (YamahaMusicians). Owners of FG-140/150/180s report flat intonation of about 10 cents because the saddle cannot move far enough. A neck reset or bridge work may be needed (TDPRI).
- **Repairs visible in photos**: cleats or glue lines inside the body under top cracks, a shaved bridge (thin, with very little saddle showing), a replacement saddle, a non-original pickguard, and extra holes from added pickups.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeits are rare at this price; mislabelled models (a later FG sold as "red label") are the risk — confirm the label
colour and text (general knowledge, verify).
- A Taiwan red label (without "Nippon Gakki") sold as Japan "Nippon Gakki" is the main misdescription. European average for a Nippon Gakki FG-180 is about €380, with Taiwan versions somewhat lower (YamahaMusicians).
- Yamaha has issued "FG Red Label" reissues (FG3, FS3, FGX5 and similar, 2020s) with a modern red label. They are new-production guitars, not 1960s (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Players (beginner to intermediate upgrading) and Japanese-vintage collectors (red labels). Liquid on Reverb, eBay and
local; acoustic-electrics with cases sell fastest. Q4 holidays and spring lift guitar sales (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The model name (on the label inside the soundhole) and that A-series has electronics and a solid top. A cutaway + preamp
+ amber "vintage natural" finish is the A-series tell from across the room. A hard case adds $60–100 of resale.
Also: A3 and A5 are all-solid (A5 made in Japan) while A1 has laminate back and sides; a concert-body "AC" model is still A-series; and "Reinforced Neck" truss-rod covers mark early FGs.

## Shipping / handling
Dreadnought in hard case ~15 lb, 48×20×8 box: $50–80 ground, double-box. Resell on Reverb (~8%) or eBay (13%). Loosen
strings for shipping. Selling locally avoids shipping.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State model (label photo), serial and year, country, top wood (solid/laminate), electronics working, action/neck
condition, cracks/repairs, case included. Photograph label, headstock front/back, top, back, sides, bridge, and
electronics. Title: "Yamaha", model, solid top, "acoustic electric", year/label colour, "with hard case".

### What to tell the buyer
Give the exact model from the label (e.g. "Yamaha A3M, dreadnought cutaway, mahogany") and the serial with its decoded date. Give the country: Japan (Nippon Gakki) or Taiwan for vintage, or as the label says for modern. State the woods accurately: "solid Sitka spruce top, laminate back and sides" for an A1, or "all solid" for an A3 or A5. Name the electronics (SRT2 on A3 and A5) and say whether they were tested through an amp. Give the action at the 12th fret in mm if possible and state any neck reset, bridge work, cracks or cleats. For red labels, say "Nippon Gakki red label, c.1967–71" only when the label says Nippon Gakki. Say what case is included and whether the strings were loosened for shipping.

## Buy rule
Buy when: A-series (any) with case under $120; A3/A5 or L-series under $200; red-label FG under $120. Pass when:
F3xx/FG7xx/FG8xx at any price (retail goods); "needs work" or cracked. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees
and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat (a cased dreadnought does).
