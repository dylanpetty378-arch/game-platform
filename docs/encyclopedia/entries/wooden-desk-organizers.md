## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **1980s tambour organizers**: oak-veneer particleboard ends with a roll-top tambour of wood slats glued on fabric; built for floppy disks, CDs or cassettes (e.g. "The Byte Box" 2101 dual rolltop CD/disk holder). Low value.
- **Pine/maple mail organizers and cubbies** (1980s–2000s): clear-lacquered knotty pine, small knob drawers, cork boards. Low value.
- **Victorian/Edwardian stationery boxes**: walnut, oak or mahogany with slanted fronts, pigeonholes for envelopes, brass hinges and plaques.
- **Danish modern**: solid teak/rosewood, crisp joinery, labels (Dansk Designs, Digsmed, Made in Denmark).

## Makers, brands and variants
The Byte Box, Eldon, Rolodex (later plastic), Lane, Bombay Co. (1990s mahogany-look, $20–60), Dansk (Jens Quistgaard teak, $60–200), Shaker reproductions, Victorian unbranded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid wood vs veneered particleboard (look at edges/backs), dovetails vs staples, brass hardware vs plated, maker labels. Old shellac finish with patina vs modern polyurethane.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken tambours (slats detached from canvas), veneer chips, water rings, missing knobs, cracked panels.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Bombay Company and Pier 1 "antique-style" stationery boxes look Victorian but are 1990s Asian-made (MDF or rubberwood).
