## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1930s–1960s decorative serving ware made of aluminum, sold as affordable "silver look" giftware. Hand-hammered texture with embossed (chased) floral, fruit and leaf borders; Bakelite or wood finials; Pyrex glass liners in casserole holders and chafing dishes.
Marks underneath: "Everlast Forged Aluminum", "Continental Hand Wrought 1093", "Buenilum Hand Wrought" (with wire-twist handles), "Rodney Kent" (ribbon-tied tulip handles), "Wendell August Forge" (deep relief scenes, often with a forge stamp), "Arthur Armour" (hammered with anvil logo), "Kensington" (Alcoa, 1930s modern), "Cromwell", "Farberware", "World Hand Forged".

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-wrought: visible hammer facets, deep chased decoration, heavier gauge. Spun/stamped: thin, uniform, machined lines. Artistic forge pieces have deep repoussé scenes (pine cones, ducks, sailboats). Pyrex liners add utility value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Oxidation/pitting, dents, missing liners/burners, wobbly legs, broken Bakelite finials, dishwasher darkening.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Wendell August continues production (newer pieces lower than early forge pieces). Little faking otherwise.
