## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Table-top AM or AM/FM radios from roughly the 1930s–50s with moulded phenolic ("bakelite") cabinets, usually brown or black, sometimes ivory-painted or urea plastic in white/ivory; a dial window with station numbers, two or three knobs, a cloth or slotted grille. Model numbers are on a paper label or stamp on the back or underside of the chassis (e.g. Zenith H725 – a 1950s AM/FM bakelite table set). Sellers write "tube radio", "bakelite radio", "vintage Zenith radio", "works". Look-alikes: 1970s–90s reproduction "nostalgia" radios (Crosley, Thomas, Spartus) with modern solid-state guts (all general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
All general knowledge, verify:
- **Zenith** – H725 and similar AM/FM table sets ($70–130 working); Trans-Oceanic portables are a separate, higher-value line.
- **Philco, RCA, Emerson, Motorola, Arvin, Admiral, General Electric** – common brown/black bakelite table sets; mostly low value.
- **Catalin** (cast phenolic in marbled yellow, red, green, blue – Emerson, Fada, Addison, Sentinel) – the collector tier, often hundreds to thousands depending on colour and cracks.
- **Art Deco / designer sets** (e.g. Emerson Patriot, Sparton) – higher value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
General knowledge, verify: bakelite is dense, opaque, usually brown/black; it does not melt with a hot pin. Catalin is translucent-ish, brighter, often marbled, and ages from white toward butterscotch. Painted ivory cabinets often show paint chipping. Original knobs, back cover, and dial glass matter. Old wax/paper capacitors in a set that "plays" are near end of life.

## Condition, damage and repairs
General knowledge, verify: cabinet cracks (especially around corners and handles), missing or wrong knobs, missing back, cracked dial glass. Electrically: dried capacitors, bad tubes, hum, dead speaker; plugging in an unrestored set can damage it. Catalin shrinks and cracks; cracks cut value sharply. Recapped/restored sets sell higher – ask what was done.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
General knowledge, verify: reproduction "nostalgia" radios (Crosley, Thomas, Spartus) styled like old cathedral/bakelite sets; replacement reproduction knobs and backs; Catalin cabinets with repaired cracks or re-polished/reproduced colours.

## Market
General knowledge, verify: steady but soft market for common brown sets (decor and hobby restorers); strong collector market for Catalin and Art Deco designs. eBay and local pickup; restored-and-working sets sell much better than untested.

## What sellers usually miss
unknown – research needed. Likely candidates (general knowledge, verify): Catalin listed as "plastic radio", designer models listed by brand only, and whether a set has been recapped.

## Shipping / handling
General knowledge, verify: 5–10 lb packed; bakelite cracks if dropped – double-box with foam; ~$20–30 to ship.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State brand and model number (from the label), bands (AM/FM), working status and any restoration (recap, new tubes), cabinet cracks/chips, original knobs and back. Photograph front, back with label, chassis, dial, and any cracks. Keywords: brand + model + "tube radio" + "bakelite" + "working" + decade.

## Buy rule
Buy when: unknown until researched – only a working, restored set well under $40, or a coloured Catalin set (verify). Pass when: brown bakelite > $40 and anything untested. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (table radios do; console radios are bigger and excluded).
