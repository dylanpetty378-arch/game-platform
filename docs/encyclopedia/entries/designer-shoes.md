## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Women's heels and pumps with a recognisable maker or exotic leather. Photo tells:
- **Vintage (1930s–60s) American luxury**: gold-stamped insole script ("Julianelli", "Delman", "I. Miller", "Palter DeLiso", "Herbert Levine", "Saks Fifth Avenue", "Bergdorf Goodman"), leather sole with a fine waist, slender almond toe, small stacked or covered heel, narrow widths written as 7½ AAA, 8N. Store names (Mandel's, Neiman Marcus) often on the insole.
- **Genuine alligator/crocodile**: large irregular rectangular "tiles" that vary in size and shape across the vamp, each with soft creases between; on belly-cut skins the tiles get smaller toward the sides. Glazed shine with natural colour variation. Embossed cowhide shows a perfectly repeating pattern with sharp, shallow edges.
- **Lizard** (teju, ring lizard, karung): small even scales in rows, often glazed burgundy, navy or black; 1970s–80s Italian ankle-strap sandals.
- **Modern luxury**: Christian Louboutin (red lacquered sole, insole "Christian Louboutin Paris" in gold script; size on the sole as "36½"), Manolo Blahnik (insole script "Manolo Blahnik", made in Italy), Jimmy Choo ("Jimmy Choo London").

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Julianelli** (Mabel and Charles Julianelli, New York, 1930s–70s): Coty Award winner; rounded toes, quality alligator and suede; in museum collections. Typical resale $40–120.
- **Delman / Roger Vivier for Delman / Dior** (1950s): Vivier pieces are high ($300–1,000+).
- **Herbert Levine** (1948–75): sculptural heels, "Spring-o-lator" mules, Kabuki; $100–500.
- **I. Miller, Palter DeLiso, Andrew Geller, Mademoiselle, Johansen, Naturalizer** (descending quality): $15–80.
- **Ferragamo**: 1930s–50s handmade = high; 1980s–2000s Vara pumps $40–120.
- **Italian 1970s–80s boutique** (Cleo-Bottier, Bruno Magli, Charles Jourdan): $20–80.
- **Christian Louboutin** (1991–): used $80–300 depending on model (So Kate, Pigalle, Iriza sell best); older square-toe 2000s slides at the low end.
- **Manolo Blahnik**, **Jimmy Choo**: used $60–250.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Leather sole and leather lining, hand-lasted, nailed heel lift; quality vintage has a fine "waist" and hand-stitched details.
- Exotic: real skins show tile/scale variation, pores near the flank, and natural creasing; embossed leather has identical repeats.
- Age: vintage sizing is narrow; insole gold stamping wear; heel tips worn or replaced.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Worn insoles (foot imprints), scuffed toes, heel tip wear, peeling exotic scales (dry, lifting tiles), cracked patent, mould in old leather, smell. Resoles and heel-tip replacements are fine; re-dyed exotics drop value. Red soles worn to black reduce Louboutin value 30–50%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Louboutin is among the most counterfeited shoes: wrong font on the insole (the C and L shapes), red sole too orange or painted, poor box/dustbag. eBay's Authenticity Guarantee does not cover women's heels, so buyers dispute often. Requires professional authentication = outside Dylan's rules.
- Manolo and Choo are faked too.
- Vintage American shoes are not faked; the risk there is embossed-leather "alligator".

## Market
Vintage shoes are a niche: size and width dictate whether anything sells (narrow AAA and sizes under 6 or over 10 sit). Exotic skins have extra friction (eBay bans international shipment of many exotic-skin items; California restricts sales of alligator and crocodile products). Louboutin resale is liquid but dominated by authentication-backed platforms (The RealReal, Vestiaire, Poshmark Authentication).

## What sellers usually miss
- The insole script and store name on old pumps sold as "vintage heels".
- That glazed tiles are real alligator (often listed as "leather pumps").
- Usually the reverse for luxury labels: sellers know Louboutin is valuable.

## Shipping / handling
Light (2–3 lb boxed). Stuff toes, wrap heels, ship in a shoebox inside a mailer. $10–14 each way. No international shipping for exotic skins; check California restrictions before shipping alligator there.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give insole length in inches, width, heel height, marked size; photograph soles, insoles, heels and any scale lifting; say "genuine alligator" only if tiles vary. Vintage sizing runs small; say so.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 to buyer − ~$12 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Modern luxury labels (Louboutin, Manolo, Choo, Gucci, Prada): need professional authentication → **pass** (Dylan's rule).
- Vintage designer pumps: a $90 sale nets ~$54 before price, so buy only at ≤ $20 (Tier A at ≤ $10). Only sizes 7–9, B or M width, minimal wear.
- Herbert Levine / Roger Vivier / 1930s–50s Ferragamo: buy at ≤ $60 with clear insole photos.
- Fits passenger seat: always.
