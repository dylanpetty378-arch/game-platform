## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Kraut cutters/slaw boards**: rectangular board with 1–3 diagonal steel blades and often a sliding box hopper; 1880s–1950s; common, often Pennsylvania/Midwest made, some with heart cut-outs (folk, more valuable).
- **Cone sieves (chinois)**: aluminum cone with perforations, wooden pestle, three-leg wire stand. Wear-Ever No. 8 (most common), No. 475/480/462; Mirro. 1930s–60s.
- **Butter molds/stamps**: carved wood with designs (cows, sheep, eagles, wheat) — eagles and animals sell well.
- **Dough bowls**: hand-hewn oblong wooden bowls; burl bowls are high value.
- **Apple peelers**: cast iron with patents (Reading Hardware 1870s+, Goodell "White Mountain", Lockey & Howland).

## Makers, brands and variants
Wear-Ever (Aluminum Cooking Utensil Co., New Kensington PA, Alcoa subsidiary), Mirro (Manitowoc WI), Enterprise Mfg (Philadelphia: pitters, grinders), Reading Hardware, Goodell Co., Griswold/Wagner (cast iron), unsigned regional woodworkers.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-forged blades with rivets, square nails, old patina and wear = older; stamped steel with machine screws = later. Folk decoration (hearts, carved crests) adds big value. Aluminum: marks embossed on handle/rim; pitting reduces value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Splits in boards, rusted blades, missing hoppers, warped bowls, cracks repaired with staples (older repairs can add character), aluminum dents and missing pestles.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
New "farmhouse" dough bowls (Indonesian/Mexican mango wood, distressed), reproduction butter molds, and aged kraut cutters. Look for authentic wear and tool marks.

## Market
Steady decor demand but low prices; 2–6 weeks to sell common pieces.

## What sellers usually miss
Folk-art decorated cutters (hearts, dates), carved eagle butter stamps, burl bowls. Usually they over-price ordinary pieces ("antique").

## Shipping / handling
Kraut cutters 2–5 lb but long (to 36"); sieves with stands are awkward. $15–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** item (kraut cutter, cone sieve, butter stamp, dough bowl) + maker (Wear-Ever No 8, Enterprise) + size in inches + "wood"/"aluminum" + "antique"/"primitive" + "farmhouse". Junk: "rustic decor", "old kitchen", "boho". Example: `Antique 36 Inch Wooden Kraut Cabbage Slaw Cutter Primitive Farmhouse Board`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (steady decor demand, 2–6 weeks); Etsy at the same price reaches decor buyers; FB local for long cutters that cost $25 to ship.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (12" cutters ~$17–25, 36" cutters ~$50, Wear-Ever sieve ~$25–30, carved butter stamps/hewn bowls $60–300); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) full piece straight-on in daylight; then blade/carving close-up, maker mark, underside, end grain or patina, any cracks/worm holes, tape measure for length.
- **State:** dimensions, blade count, maker marks, completeness (hopper, pestle, stand), wood type if known, wear and cracks by photo number, age estimate stated as estimate.
- **Pack / haul:** 2–5 lb; cutters to 36" need a long box (40×8×6), sieves with stand immobilized in a 14×14×10, 3–6 lb class, $15–25; single box with 2" padding.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A [36-inch / folk-decorated] [kraut cutter / butter stamp], not the modern farmhouse-decor copy — the [heart carving / Enterprise mark] is what collectors pay for; comparable pieces have sold for [$X]."
