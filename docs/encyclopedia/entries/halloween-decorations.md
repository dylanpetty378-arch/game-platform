## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **German papier-mâché/composition (1910–1939)**: jack-o'-lanterns with paper insert faces (thin tissue eyes/mouth), cats, devils, veggie-people; candy containers; stamped or labelled "Germany". Hand-painted, often worn, very light.
- **US pulp (1940s–50s)**: molded pressed-paper JOLs, cats and witches, thicker and grayer pulp inside, cut-out faces with paper inserts; bail handles of wire.
- **Beistle and Dennison paper**: embossed die-cut cardboard (cats, witches, owls, skeletons) with "Beistle Co." mark, honeycomb tissue, table centerpieces; Dennison "Bogie Book" items.
- **Hard plastic (1950s)**: Rosbro, E. Rosen, Tico candy containers and witches on motorcycles, pumpkin-head figures, rack toys.
- **Blow molds (1960s–2000s)**: Empire, Union Products (Featherstone), General Foam, Carolina Enterprises, Don Featherstone — hollow plastic light-ups. Union Products and Empire marks molded in.
- **Modern (1990s–now)**: Paper Magic Group (licensed party/lighted decor), Gemmy inflatables, Dollar-store foam "carvable" pumpkins, Department 56 villages, Bethany Lowe folk-art reproductions.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **German (pre-1940)**: unnamed cottage industries in Thuringia/Sonneberg; stamps "Germany" or "Made in Germany". JOL lanterns $150–600; figural cat/devil heads $300–1,200; candy-container figures $200–2,000.
- **Beistle** (Shippensburg, PA, 1900–): die-cuts $15–200; rare 1930s centerpieces $200+.
- **Dennison** (Framingham MA): crepe-paper costumes, Bogie Books $30–150.
- **US pulp**: unmarked or "Pulp Products"; JOLs $60–300; black cats $150–400.
- **Rosbro / Rosen / Tico** hard plastic: witches, cats on pumpkins, $20–150.
- **Blow molds**: Empire (1960s–2000s), Union Products (1970s–2006), General Foam (2000s). Typical $20–80; scarce figures (Empire Frankenstein, Union witch on pumpkin, Empire scarecrow) $100–250.
- **Paper Magic Group** (1990s–2000s): ceramic/resin light-up JOLs and licensed decor; $10–40.
- **Bethany Lowe** (2000s folk-art paper clay) $25–100 — collectible but modern.
- **Ceramic JOLs**: 1970s–80s Atlantic Mold / Holland Mold hobbyist ceramics $20–80 (taller, lit with bulbs).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight: German composition is light and chalky; US pulp is fibrous and gray inside; modern resin is heavy; foam pumpkins are very light and spongy (often called "funkins").
- Paper inserts in eyes on pulp/papier-mâché lanterns = early; open cut-outs painted yellow = foam/resin modern.
- Look at the bottom: molded "Union Products Leominster MA", "Empire", "Paper Magic Group", date code (1998, etc.), or "Germany".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paper inserts are often replaced (lowers value 20–40%). Papier-mâché flakes and cracks; heavy repainting kills value. Blow molds fade and crack; missing light cords/bulb sockets. Foam pumpkins chip at cut edges.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Big repro market: modern papier-mâché "vintage-style" JOLs (Bethany Lowe, Ragon House, TJ Maxx), new pulp from Asia, reproduction German figures and Beistle reissues (Beistle reissued many 1930s die-cuts in the 1990s–2000s, marked with later copyright). Check for "Germany" stamp, paper insert wear, and old paint patina; new paint is uniform and bright.

## Market
Strongly seasonal: peak August–October 25. After Halloween prices drop 30–50%. Sell-through high in season for vintage paper and pulp.

## What sellers usually miss
Old "Germany" papier-mâché pieces in grandma's box of decorations; Beistle die-cuts mixed with modern paper; 1950s hard-plastic candy containers mistaken for dollar-store toys.

## Shipping / handling
Light but fragile (paper and pulp crush). Box in box with soft fill; $10–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Identify era and maker, note any replaced paper inserts or repaint, show the base and inside, measure height.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 ship out − ~$15 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Practically: German or US pulp/papier-mâché pieces under $60, and Beistle die-cut lots with marks. Buy before mid-October to sell into the season, otherwise hold 11 months. Fits a passenger seat. No authentication.
