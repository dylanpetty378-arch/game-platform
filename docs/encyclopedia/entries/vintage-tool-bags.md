## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wide-mouth canvas bag with a steel-jaw frame that opens like a doctor's bag, leather handles, leather strap/buckle closure, riveted leather bottom. Klein stamps "Mathias Klein & Sons Chicago" or "Klein Tools" on leather tabs/handle; Bell System bags have bell logo; railroads stencil initials.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Mathias Klein & Sons (pre-1950s mark)** → **Klein Tools** (later). Model 5102 series canvas bags still in production — modern ones have little value.
- **Buckingham**, **Bashlin**, **Kunz**: lineman's gear makers.
- **Bell System / Western Electric**, **railroads (Pennsylvania RR, Santa Fe)**: marked bags have crossover collector value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: heavy duck canvas, copper rivets, hand-stitched leather, brass/steel hardware, "Chicago" stamping. New Klein: nylon or bright canvas, plastic tags.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rotted canvas at corners and frame edges, torn seams, dry cracked handles, mildew, broken frame hinges. Collectors accept patina but not holes.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None meaningful; modern Klein bags look similar but cleaner and with modern tags.

## Market
Modest; rustic-decor and workwear buyers. 3–6 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
Faint "Mathias Klein & Sons" stamps on the leather tabs; railroad initials stenciled on the bottom.

## Shipping / handling
3–5 lb, soft, ships in a box or poly bag $12–18. Low risk.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
