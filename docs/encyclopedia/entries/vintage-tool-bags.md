## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wide-mouth canvas bag with a steel-jaw frame that opens like a doctor's bag, leather handles, leather strap/buckle closure, riveted leather bottom. Klein stamps "Mathias Klein & Sons Chicago" or "Klein Tools" on leather tabs/handle; Bell System bags have bell logo; railroads stencil initials.
Other forms:
- **Lineman's nut-and-bolt bag**: round or rectangular leather bucket bag with a stiff rim, hung from the belt or a handline; Buckingham and Bashlin make these (Buckingham still sells a "Heritage" leather line, so check for modern tags).
- **Zippered canvas tool bag** (Klein 5102-20 larger, 5139 smaller): rectangular, leather or vinyl bottom, zipper top.
- **Carpenter's/plumber's bag**: open tote, canvas or leather, wooden or leather handles.
Words sellers use: "lineman", "Bell System", "Western Electric", "railroad", "Klein Chicago", "doctor style", "industrial".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Mathias Klein & Sons (pre-1950s mark)** → **Klein Tools** (later). Model 5102 series canvas bags still in production — modern ones have little value.
- **Buckingham**, **Bashlin**, **Kunz**: lineman's gear makers. Bashlin (Grove City, PA) has made climbing and safety gear for over 90 years; Buckingham still produces leather lineman bags (Heritage line), so a "Buckingham" stamp alone does not mean vintage.
- **Bell System / Western Electric**, **railroads (Pennsylvania RR, Santa Fe)**: marked bags have crossover collector value.
- **Klein model numbers**: 5102-20 (larger zippered canvas bag) and 5139 (smaller). Collectors date 5139s by the zipper pull: leather pull = older, nylon rope pull = newer (Garage Journal). Zippers by YKK appear on later bags.
- Klein hand tools carry quarter/year date codes before 1972 (e.g. "/_\ MNE" = Q1 1964 per collector decoding); bags generally do not, so date bags by mark wording, materials and hardware.

### Quick ID checklist
- Find the stamp: leather tabs, handle ends, frame or bottom — "Mathias Klein & Sons Chicago", "M. Klein & Sons", "Klein Tools", Bell logo, railroad initials.
- "Mathias Klein & Sons" wording = older than "Klein Tools" wording.
- Hardware: copper rivets, brass/steel buckles and a steel-jaw frame (older) vs plastic tags, nylon webbing (newer).
- Zipper pull on a Klein 5139: leather (older) vs nylon rope (newer).
- Canvas: heavy duck with faded, even wear vs bright orange/olive new canvas or nylon.
- Corners and frame edges: torn or rotted canvas there is the common fatal flaw.
- Bottom: riveted leather bottom intact and not cracked through.

### Golden-egg tells
- Bell System / Western Electric marked lineman's bag — ~$60–150 vs $20–45 for an unmarked worn bag (entry tiers, verify) — bell logo stamped or stencilled on leather or canvas.
- Railroad-marked bag (Pennsylvania RR, Santa Fe etc.) — ~$60–150, crossover railroadiana demand — railroad initials stencilled or stamped, often on the bottom.
- Early all-leather tool bag or early Mathias Klein & Sons leather bag — ~$80–200 — full leather body, "Mathias Klein & Sons Chicago" stamp, copper rivets.
- Clean marked Mathias Klein canvas frame bag — ~$50–120 vs $20–45 unmarked — legible stamp, sound canvas, working frame.
- Modern Klein 5102/5139 — the common version, little value — nylon or bright canvas, plastic tags, YKK zipper with nylon pull.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: heavy duck canvas, copper rivets, hand-stitched leather, brass/steel hardware, "Chicago" stamping. New Klein: nylon or bright canvas, plastic tags.
- Leather: old harness leather darkens, rounds at the edges and cracks on the fold lines; new leather is bright, uniformly dyed, with sharp edges.
- Canvas: faded olive/khaki duck with even soft wear; repaired seams show newer, brighter thread.
- Frame: a hinged steel jaw that still opens and locks is a plus; broken hinges are hard to fix.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rotted canvas at corners and frame edges, torn seams, dry cracked handles, mildew, broken frame hinges. Collectors accept patina but not holes.
- Ask about smell (mildew), whether the frame opens and closes, and for photos of all four corners and the bottom.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None meaningful; modern Klein bags look similar but cleaner and with modern tags. Buckingham Heritage bags are new production in a vintage style.

## Market
Modest; rustic-decor and workwear buyers. 3–6 weeks. Bell System and railroad bags also draw telephone and railroadiana collectors.

## What sellers usually miss
Faint "Mathias Klein & Sons" stamps on the leather tabs; railroad initials stenciled on the bottom.

## Shipping / handling
3–5 lb, soft, ships in a box or poly bag $12–18. Low risk.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Close-ups of stamps, interior, any holes; dimensions.

### What to tell the buyer
Quote every mark exactly as stamped (maker, city, model number, company or railroad initials) and photograph it. Give length, height and opening width; say whether the frame opens and locks. Describe the canvas and leather honestly, listing tears, rot and mildew, and photograph the corners and bottom. Date it by evidence ("Mathias Klein & Sons Chicago stamp, copper rivets") rather than guesswork.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship − ship − price. Marked clean Klein median ~$80 → $70 − $30 − price; even at $20 profit is only $20 < $50. Effectively pass unless Bell System/railroad bag with comps ≥ $150 at ≤ $25.
