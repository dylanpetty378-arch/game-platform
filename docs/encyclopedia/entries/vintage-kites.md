Buy rule (short): Buy unopened 1950s–70s Hi-Flier paper advertising/character kites under ~$20; pass modern nylon kites.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Vintage paper kites (Hi-Flier, Decatur IL 1920s–1980s) come rolled with sticks, printed advertising (Coca-Cola, Pepsi, 7-Up, Bazooka, Archie, Batman, Star Trek, Space Age); Gayla (Houston) plastic kites 1960s–80s; Peter Powell stunt kites (UK 1970s). The example: Go Fly a Kite "Cloud Critters" nylon bear/dog kite with header card — 2000s, low tier.

## Makers, brands and variants
Hi-Flier (most collectible), Gayla, Spectra Star, Go Fly a Kite (1970s–2010s, now Toy Smith-owned brand), Peter Powell, Shaw Creations (made novelty paper goods).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Paper kites: newsprint-ish printed paper, wood sticks, rubber band/string bundles; NOS still rolled with band. Nylon ripstop = modern.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tears, brittle paper, stained, sticks missing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Hi-Flier reproductions exist (modern reprints marked).
