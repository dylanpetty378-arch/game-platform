Buy rule (short): Buy unopened 1950s–70s Hi-Flier paper advertising/character kites (or barn-door/box kites) under ~$20; pass modern nylon kites.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Vintage paper kites came rolled with their sticks, printed with the design, often with a rubber band and string bundle. Hi-Flier
(Decatur, Illinois) made paper **bow (diamond) kites** in three sizes — small 30 in (vertical stick 29¼ in, bow 23¾ in), medium 36 in,
large 42 in — plus **box kites** and flat three-stick **barn-door kites** (Junkbox). Plastic Hi-Flier bow and delta kites followed; by 1977
nearly all were plastic, though small paper kites appear as late as 1987 (Antique Trader, Junkbox). Gayla (Houston, 1961–) made plastic
delta/keel kites, later ripstop nylon (Wikipedia). Peter Powell stunt kites (UK 1970s). The example: Go Fly a Kite "Cloud Critters"
nylon bear/dog kite with header card — 2000s, low tier.
Title words: "Hi-Flier paper kite NOS", "rolled", "premium kite", "Gayla", "Spectra Star", "1970s kite".
Look-alikes: modern nylon character kites on header cards; licensed Hi-Flier-branded toys (the brand was licensed onto marbles and yo-yos
after the factory closed — Antique Trader), which are not Decatur kites.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hi-Flier Manufacturing Co., Decatur, IL** — Harvey A. Sellers began c. 1921–22; bought Arthur W. Cash's bow-kite patent, patented a
  barn-door kite and later a box kite; 20,000 kites/day by 1928; peak in the 1960s (~200 workers, big promotional business, ~20 million kites
  a year); bought by Damon Corp. in 1971; Decatur closed 1981, production moved to Damon's Estes division in Penrose, Colorado (Antique Trader).
  Designs: Playmates of the Clouds (most common small), American Beauty, Big Ben, Big Boy ("Tailless Dancing Kite"), Space Warp, Strat-O-Flier,
  Cosmic, Rainbow, Little Ace, Dandy (barn-door), Orbiteer, Pegasus and "Color Glow" plastics (late 1960s), Goof-Proof and Daredevil/Hustler
  (1977), Big Bruno (1976, artist Harry Gans) (Antique Trader, Junkbox).
  Advertising/premium sponsors: Jif, Sinclair, Texaco, Studebaker, Burger King, AC spark plugs, Big Boy (1973 Fan Club kite), Delco, Pepsi,
  Dutch Boy (plastic), Chex (1971 "psychedelic" set and 1972 "energy" set) (Antique Trader, Junkbox).
  Licensed: Snoopy Astronaut kite 1969, made by Hi-Flier for Determined Productions (CollectPeanuts).
- **Gayla Industries** (Houston, 1961–) — plastic keel deltas, later nylon and windsocks (Wikipedia); 1980s NOS cards (e.g., "Lil Hoot" 1981, eBay title).
- **Spectra Star** — 1970s–80s nylon/plastic kites and windsocks (e.g., 1979 25-ft "Ride the Wind" dragon, eBay title; licensed GoBots windsocks).
- **TopFlite, Alox** — dime-store paper kites sold at Walgreens and Kresge (Antique Trader).
- Go Fly a Kite (1970s–2010s nylon), Peter Powell, Hayes, Shaw Creations (novelty paper goods).

### Quick ID checklist
- Maker name on the sail: Hi-Flier (and logo style), Gayla, TopFlite, Alox, Spectra Star.
- Paper vs plastic vs ripstop nylon (nylon = modern, low tier).
- Still **rolled and banded** with sticks (NOS) or assembled/flown?
- **Printed retail price** on the kite: 10¢/15¢ (earlier) → 49¢ (mid-1970s) (Junkbox).
- Sponsor or character art (gas, food, cereal, Peanuts, superheroes).
- Kite type: bow (diamond), box, barn-door (three-stick flat), delta.
- Logo: early biplane logo = rare early Hi-Flier; Damon-era logo resembles the Estes logo (Junkbox).
- Note the 1923 patent date printed on bow kites does **not** date the kite (most are late 1950s+).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Hi-Flier barn-door kites** (Little Ace, Dandy, Rainbow) — rarest early type, top of the $40–200 band (general knowledge) — flat kite with three crossed sticks, Hi-Flier name.
- **Early biplane-logo Hi-Flier** — rare early kites, premium over 1960s–70s paper (Junkbox) — small biplane in the logo.
- **Advertising/premium paper kites** (Texaco, Sinclair, Studebaker, Burger King, Jif, AC, Delco, Big Boy 1973) — $40–200 NOS tier (old entry) vs $10–25 modern — sponsor logo printed on a 30 in paper bow kite; still rolled.
- **Chex 1971 "psychedelic" and 1972 "energy" sets** — sought as full sets (Junkbox: "reasonable prices") — Chex branding, matching series art.
- **Licensed character kites** (1969 Snoopy Astronaut by Hi-Flier for Determined Productions; Batman/space themes) — character-collector premium (CollectPeanuts calls the Snoopy "rare"; no price) — copyright line and Determined Productions credit.
- **Hi-Flier box kite, assembled and sound** — ~$50 (Junkbox, anecdotal) — green-and-white paper with jet/helicopter art.
- **TopFlite "Man in the Moon"** — $300+ cited (Junkbox, anecdotal; verify) — TopFlite name with moon-face art.
- Nylon 1990s–2000s character kites (Go Fly a Kite, etc.) — not golden eggs; $10–25.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Paper kites: newsprint-ish printed paper, wood sticks, rubber band/string bundles; NOS still rolled with band. Nylon ripstop = modern.
Early plastic bow kites have **metal grommets** in the sail; later ones are simply punched (Antique Trader). Old paper browns and
becomes brittle at the folds; sticks are unfinished softwood. Printed prices and catalogue designs date a kite better than the patent line.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tears, brittle paper, stained, sticks missing. Also tape repairs, water stains from storage, crushed rolls, sun-faded colour, missing
bow string. NOS and unrolled-but-complete kites are the collectable state; flown kites are decor value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Hi-Flier reproductions are said to exist (old entry; not confirmed by the sources consulted — verify). Post-1981 licensed "Hi-Flier"-branded
toys and Estes-era kites are not Decatur production. Modern "vintage style" paper kites are printed on bright white paper with modern fonts.

## Market
Seasonal (spring); collectors for Hi-Flier, advertising (gas/oil, cereal) and character (Peanuts, superheroes) cross-collectors.

## What sellers usually miss
Advertising Hi-Flier kites in garage lots; printed retail prices that date a kite; sponsor names on rolled kites (not visible until unrolled
— ask for the printed side).

## Shipping / handling
Long thin tube/box; $10–15. Never unroll brittle NOS kites to photograph them flat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, character, sealed status, size. Add sponsor, printed price, stick count/length, and condition of paper at the ends of the roll.

### What to tell the buyer
"Hi-Flier (Decatur, Illinois) paper bow kite, __ in size, new old stock — still rolled with its original sticks and band. Printed with
__ advertising and a __¢ retail price, which places it around __. Paper is __ (supple/brittle at edges) with no tears found.
Hi-Flier was North America's largest kite maker until the Decatur plant closed in 1981."

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): only Hi-Flier advertising/character NOS (or barn-door/box). Fits.
