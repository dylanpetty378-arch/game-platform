## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Flatware and serving pieces with a thin electroplated silver layer over nickel silver (EPNS) or brass, or solid silver (sterling 925; Continental 800/835). Ornate pierced pastry/cake servers, tongs, and fish/asparagus servers are favourites of sellers' "antique silver" titles.
Marks to read: "1847 Rogers Bros", "IS" (International Silver), "Wm Rogers", "Community", "Holmes & Edwards (inlaid)", "EPNS", "A1", "Silverplate", "Christofle" (with weight number), "WMF 90" (German), "Alpacca"/"Alpaca" (nickel silver, little silver), Italian "800" with a lozenge/star mark and province number (post-1935 fasces or post-1968 star), "Sterling"/"925".
pid 7961449380: blade of an ornate Italian-style pastry/petit-four server or tong, with repoussé/cast pierced scrollwork, birds and floral motifs, plain pointed blade; worn-through spots on the plain blade suggest plate (seller says silver-plated). Many such Italian/Portuguese pieces are actually 800 silver — the seller's title says plated; need a mark photo.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Plate wears through to yellowish (brass) or grey-white (nickel) on high spots and blade backs; sterling/800 never shows a different base colour. Weight: a sterling serving spoon ~60–80 g; plated similar but marks distinguish. Crisp, deep die-striking and hand chasing = better quality; soft blurry casting = later copies. Monograms reduce value for plate, less for sterling.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Plate loss (resilvering costs more than value), bent tines, dents, pitting from dishwasher, loose handles on hollow-handled knives, bent pierced blades, repaired tong springs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
False "800"/"925" stamps on Asian silverplate; "German Silver"/"Nickel Silver"/"Alpaca" are no silver at all. Modern Italian "Made in Italy" silverplate servers sold as antique.

## Market
Very high supply; plated pieces sell slowly and cheap. Sell-through for single plated servers ~25–35%. Silver (800/925) moves fast on weight.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling/800 pieces titled "silver plated"; Christofle marks; Victorian figural pieces; patterns with active replacement demand (e.g. 1847 Rogers "Charter Oak", "Vintage"; Community "Morning Star" sets).

## Shipping / handling
Light, flat: padded envelope or small box $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the mark close-up, give length and weight, state plated vs. sterling, note wear-through spots, mention pattern name if known.
