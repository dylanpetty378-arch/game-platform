## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tall barrel-shaped Afro-Cuban hand drum (quinto ~11", conga ~11.75", tumba ~12.5" head; ~28–30" tall). Shells are stave-built wood (oak, ash, Siam oak) or fiberglass; tuning by side-plate lugs and hooks with a rim.
Tells for value:
- **Valje** (Tom Flores, Los Angeles, late 1950s–70s; later San Francisco and LP): thick red-oak staves; early drums have **scored (grooved) interior staves**; about 31" tall (LA) or about 30" (SF); "Valje of San Francisco" decal on SF drums; extremely collectible. (Corrected: the earlier entry named "Ubaldo Rivera, NYC"; the forum history names Tom Flores of Los Angeles.)
- **Gon Bops** (Mariano Bobadilla, Los Angeles, 1954–1980s): "Gon Bops" decal, distinctive rounded side plates and center band, heads tacked or tuned; classic red/brown finishes.
- **LP** (from 1964, Martin Cohen): metal LP badge or decal (older brass badge reads "Latin Percussion – LP – Garfield, New Jersey" with "M. Cohen"), Comfort Curve rims (late 1980s on), side-plates with LP stamped.
- **Mexican/Tijuana**: thin staves, crude hand-cut side plates, bright stain, painted designs, tacked or bolted heads; cheap.
The example: stave shell with reddish stain, crude hooked rods passing through hand-cut L-plates, a riveted metal center band, worn rawhide head; no visible badge.

### Quick ID checklist
- Badge or decal: Gon Bops, Valje (or "Valje of San Francisco"), LP brass badge (Garfield NJ = older), Matador, Meinl, Toca; no badge = check construction.
- Inside the shell (ask for a photo): scored interior staves = early LA/SF Valje; smooth Thai-oak interior = later LP-era; pencil or marker numbers inside LP shells.
- Height: ~31" (LA Valje), ~30" (SF Valje), 28–30" typical; sizes between standard diameters suggest handmade drums.
- Rims: straight "old style" rims (pre-late-1980s LP) vs Comfort Curve rims (later).
- Hardware: crude hand-cut L-plates and bent rods = Tijuana; "Valje" inscribed on side plates, handle and rods = LP-era Valje; bolts per side plate (three on the later Armando Peraza cherry series vs two earlier).
- Staves: thick, tight joints with no gaps vs thin staves with glue lines and gaps.
- Head: intact rawhide with no tears; even tuning across lugs.
- Bottom: rubber/plastic floor tray (later Valje/LP) vs plain shell edge.

## Makers, brands and variants
Gon Bops, Valje, LP (USA-made 1970s), Junior/Matador, Meinl, Toca, Pearl, Remo (fiberglass), Cuban workshop drums (rare in US).
- **Gon Bops**: founded 1954 by Mariano Bobadilla (Mexican-American, born in Guadalajara, a band-instrument repairman and trumpet player) in a garage in downtown Los Angeles. The leading US Latin-percussion maker into the 1970s (used by Mongo Santamaría and Poncho Sanchez; played at Woodstock 1969). Bobadilla kept production in the US when rivals moved overseas and closed the company in the 1980s. DW (Don Lombardi) bought the name in 2001; SABIAN bought it in 2010. Original Bobadilla-era drums are the collectable ones.
- **Valje**: built by Tom Flores at 3312 and then 3314 Sunset Blvd, Los Angeles (the 3314 address drums are said to be earliest). In the early 1980s Flores sold the name and shop to Haight Ashbury Music in San Francisco, where Akbar Moghaddam and Octavio Ruiz built drums for about two years until a fire. LP then bought the rights and made Valje drums in Thailand from Thai oak (hardware inscribed "Valje", no interior scoring), later an Armando Peraza cherry-wood series. SF-made drums are the rarest; Flores later designed Resolution drums with his son Ralph.
- **LP (Latin Percussion)**: founded 1964 by Martin Cohen; the older brass badge names Garfield, New Jersey. Fiberglass shells (hand-laid) and wood (e.g. 1980s rosewood) were made; Patato model (Carlos "Patato" Valdés signature) fiberglass drums continue today. Matador is LP's mid line.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Thick tight staves with no gaps = quality; thin, gapped staves with visible glue = cheap. Original heads, matching hardware, maker badge.
- Woods: red oak (early Valje), ash and oak (Gon Bops, LP), Siam/Thai oak (later LP and LP-era Valje), cherry (Peraza series), fiberglass (LP, Remo).
- Old vs new: older drums have straight rims, brass badges, hand-finished interiors and patina on hardware; newer imports have uniform lacquer, Comfort Curve rims and stamped logos.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Split staves, broken head, rusted hardware, cracked rims. Heads cost $40–70 and require skill to mount — Dylan's no-repair rule. Also: re-glued staves (dark glue lines inside), refinished shells (removes decals and value), mismatched replacement hardware, stripped tuning lugs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Gon Bops decals on Mexican drums exist; modern Gon Bops reissues (DW 2001–2010, then SABIAN from 2010) are separate. (Corrected: the earlier entry dated DW reissues to the 2010s; DW owned the brand from 2001 and SABIAN from 2010.) LP-made Thai-oak Valjes are legitimate but worth less than LA/SF drums; early LP-era Valjes reportedly carry no LP marking.

## Market
Reverb and local; vintage Gon Bops/Valje sell fast; unbranded sells slowly.

## What sellers usually miss
Missing decal Gon Bops sold as "Mexican conga"; Valje shells unrecognised. Interior stave scoring (early Valje) and the Garfield NJ LP badge are also missed.

## Shipping / handling
~32×15×15, 15–20 lb: $60–100 per drum. Fits passenger seat upright.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Head diameter, height, badge photos, staves, any cracks, sound clip.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] [quinto / conga / tumba], [head diameter] head, [height] tall, [wood / fiberglass] shell, c.[era] based on [badge text / rim style / interior scoring]. Staves are [tight, no splits]; head is [original rawhide / replaced], [tunes up / untested]; hardware [complete, original]. Photos show the badge, inside of the shell and every side plate." Don't call an unbadged drum Gon Bops or Valje; say "construction similar to".
