## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tall barrel-shaped Afro-Cuban hand drum (quinto ~11", conga ~11.75", tumba ~12.5" head; ~28–30" tall). Shells are stave-built wood (oak, ash, Siam oak) or fiberglass; tuning by side-plate lugs and hooks with a rim.
Tells for value:
- **Valje** (Ubaldo Rivera "Valje", NYC/ California): thick staves, characteristic flat stamped side-plates, cord "Valje" sticker; extremely collectible.
- **Gon Bops** (Mariano Bobadilla, Los Angeles, 1954–1990s): "Gon Bops" decal, distinctive rounded side plates and center band, heads tacked or tuned; classic red/brown finishes.
- **LP** (from 1964, Martin Cohen): metal LP badge or decal, Comfort Curve rims (later), side-plates with LP stamped.
- **Mexican/Tijuana**: thin staves, crude hand-cut side plates, bright stain, painted designs, tacked or bolted heads; cheap.
The example: stave shell with reddish stain, crude hooked rods passing through hand-cut L-plates, a riveted metal center band, worn rawhide head; no visible badge.

## Makers, brands and variants
Gon Bops, Valje, LP (USA-made 1970s), Junior/Matador, Meinl, Toca, Pearl, Remo (fiberglass), Cuban workshop drums (rare in US).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Thick tight staves with no gaps = quality; thin, gapped staves with visible glue = cheap. Original heads, matching hardware, maker badge.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Split staves, broken head, rusted hardware, cracked rims. Heads cost $40–70 and require skill to mount — Dylan's no-repair rule.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Gon Bops decals on Mexican drums exist; modern Gon Bops reissues (2010s, Drum Workshop) are separate.
