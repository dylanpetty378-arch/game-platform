## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
The bottom mark is a reversed "RP" monogram with flames around it. One flame was added per year from 1886 to 1900; after that a Roman numeral below gives the year (XIV = 1914, etc.). The shape number and a size letter sit next to it. Artist-signed, hand-painted pieces (initials incised) are the valuable ones. Moulded "production" pieces are common and mostly $40–150: matte glazes, florals, bookends, paperweights, candy dishes. Look-alikes include other Ohio art pottery (Weller, Roseville, Owens) with similar matte glazes; the RP flame mark is the tell.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Artist-decorated lines** (c.1880s–1930s): Standard glaze (brown/amber underglaze slip painting), Iris (pale grey-to-white grounds), Vellum (matte translucent, often landscapes/scenics), Sea Green, Aerial Blue (general knowledge on line traits, verify). Artist-signed pieces in these lines are $400–3,000.
- **Top artists**: Kataro Shirayamadani and Albert/Edward Valentien. Large Vellum scenics are $5,000+ at auction. Others include Carl Schmidt, Sara Sax, Ed Diers and Lenore Asbury (general knowledge, verify).
- **Production ware** (1910s–1960s): moulded matte vases, bookends (rook bookends), elephant and animal paperweights, trays, candy dishes. 1920s–30s pieces are $75–200; 1940s–60s pieces are $25–60.
- Later and revival period: the company closed in 1967. The name and moulds were later revived and new Rookwood has been made in Cincinnati since the 2000s (general knowledge, verify). Check the date mark.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Hand-painted pieces show brushwork under the glaze, an incised artist monogram near the base mark, and depth in the glaze.
- Production pieces have crisp moulded relief and an even matte glaze, with no artist initials.
- The date numeral and flame count date the piece.
- An "X" or wheel-ground "X" mark means a factory second, which lowers value (general knowledge, verify).
- Crazing is normal in Standard glaze.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips, hairlines and restored rims cut value sharply. On artist pieces, professional restoration may only show under blacklight.
- Glaze crazing on Standard glaze is expected and only mildly negative.
- Ask for a clear photo of the base mark, the artist initials, and the rim and foot under raking light. Ask about any "X" mark.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
New Rookwood (2000s revival) production exists. Some pieces use old moulds, so check the date marks (general knowledge, verify). Fake marks on unmarked Ohio pottery are uncommon but possible. Artist signatures are the area to verify on anything expensive.

## Market
Established collector base. Humler & Nolan (Cincinnati) and Rago handle the top end. Production pieces sell steadily on eBay to Arts & Crafts and Ohio pottery buyers. Prices for common production ware have softened over the long term (general knowledge, verify). No strong seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
The date numeral and whether the piece is artist-signed. Most sellers in Ohio do know the Rookwood name. The arbitrage is outside Ohio, where it is listed as a "pottery dish with flame mark".

## Shipping / handling
2–5 lb, double-box, $15–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: "Rookwood Pottery [year] [shape no.] [glaze/line] [form] [artist if signed]". Decode the year from flames or numerals and state it. Give the height and condition (chips, crazing, X mark). Photograph the full base mark, the artist monogram and every side.

## Buy rule
Buy when: an artist-signed piece is under $150, or a production piece is under $30. Pass when: a production piece is priced at $90. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
