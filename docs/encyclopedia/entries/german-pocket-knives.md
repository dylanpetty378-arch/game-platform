## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Folding knives from Solingen, Germany. Puma (founded 1769, Lauterjung family; the firm marked its 240th anniversary with a "Jubiläumsmesser") is the most collected. Typical Puma traits: rounded bolsters, pinned handle scales (stag, jacaranda, white-tail stag, or green/black synthetic), a six-digit model number stamped on the blade (e.g. "230365", "230265"; older fixed blades use three-digit numbers such as "959", "970", "965"), "PUMA" with the cat-head logo, and "Solingen Germany" or "PUMA-WERK SOLINGEN".

Example 7950989573: lockback with clip-point black-coated blade, olive-green checkered synthetic scales with round cat-head medallion, steel bolsters and pins. It is a Puma military/utility lockback of the 230 series family. Terapeak shows a NOS NIB 1994 Puma 230365 sold at $172.99 (n=1) and boxed Puma lockbacks median ~$150 (2026-10-13 pull).

**Dating a Puma (corrected in this revision):** Puma does not use a dot or symbol code. It stamps a numeric **control number** that encodes the year and quarter of manufacture. Example: "22072" on a Puma Waidmesser was dated by a dealer to Q2 1970. Coding began in **October 1964**, so a Puma with no control number probably predates it ("pre-64", which collectors pay more for). The number sits on the **lock bar of lockbacks** (visible on the spine side when closed), on the **ricasso of fixed blades**, and sometimes on a **liner inside the blade well**. Collector charts (PumaKnifeman, forum charts covering 1964–2012) decode it. Puma 4 Star lockbacks have the code on the side of the lock bar.

Title words sellers use: "Puma Germany", "Solingen", "lockback", "stag", "Sergeant", "Commando", "Master", "Hunter's Pal", "Duke", "Prince", "White Hunter", "Game Warden", "Plainsman", "Cape Buffalo".

Look-alikes: Puma IP and Puma SGB (modern, cheaper), Pakistani/Chinese counterfeit Pumas, generic "Solingen" stamped knives (the word "Solingen" alone, with no maker, often means a cheap distributor brand), Spanish lockbacks with similar green checkered scales.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Puma Werk (Lauterjung & Sohn, Solingen)**: 1769–present. Stamp "PUMA [cat] GERMANY" or "PUMA-WERK SOLINGEN" + model number. **Puma IP** stands for **"International Production"** (Puma's own description: designed in Solingen, made by partner factories abroad to Puma specs; earlier Spain, later China). It is low value, and forum members report newer Puma-branded models made in Spain. **Puma SGB** (Solingen, 2010s) is mid value.
  - **230 series military/utility lockbacks** (the series most often seen on Craigslist): 230265 **Sergeant** (stainless, black gunstock-type scales), 230270 Sergeant variant, 230370 **Commando** (black gun-blued blade, 3¾" blade, about 5" closed; part of Puma's military series), 230245 **Sportec**, 230 **Master** (also in green). Checkered olive/black synthetic scales with a cat medallion are typical of this family.
  - **Lockbacks with natural materials**: Duke, Earl, Prince, Hunter's Pal, White Tail, Cape Buffalo, Medici, Pony, Cadet 460, 4 Star / Mini 4 Star (early 1980s on). Stag versions beat synthetic.
  - **Fixed blades**: White Hunter, Game Warden / Plainsman 970, Deer Hunter 965, Bowie, 959 (first made 1963), Waidmesser with saw/gut-hook/needle tools. These are the high-dollar Pumas.
- **Böker (Heinr. Böker & Co., Solingen)**: Tree Brand stamp (tree logo). Also US Böker (Böker USA) and Argentina (Böker Arbolito). Check the country on the tang; Solingen pieces are worth more than Argentine or modern Chinese "Böker Plus/Magnum" lines (general knowledge, verify).
- **J.A. Henckels (Zwillingswerk)**: twin logo, slipjoints and lockbacks. Older pieces with pearl or stag scales are the collectable ones.
- **Hen & Rooster** (Bertram, later Frost): rooster logo; later Frost imports from Germany/Spain. Check the stamp.
- **Hubertus, Linder, Herder, Otter, Carl Schlieper (Eye Brand)**: lower value but collectible.

### Quick ID checklist
- Tang/blade stamp reads "PUMA … GERMANY" or "PUMA-WERK SOLINGEN", not "PUMA IP", "Spain" or "China".
- A six-digit model number is stamped (230xxx = military/utility lockback family). No model number at all on a "Puma" is a counterfeit flag.
- A numeric control number on the lock bar (lockbacks) or ricasso (fixed blades) dates it. No number on a genuine German Puma suggests pre-October 1964.
- Scales: natural stag (irregular bark texture, colour variation) vs moulded synthetic (repeating pattern, seams). Stag sells higher.
- Cat-head medallion present and crisp; pins flush; liners tight with no gaps.
- Blade profile: factory swedge and full belly vs a narrowed, re-ground blade (sharpening loss).
- Original box, paper "Puma" tag/brochure (often tied with green string) and leather pouch present.
- Lock-up: no vertical play in the photo or video (230-series knives can develop play with use).

### Golden-egg tells
- Pre-1964 Puma White Hunter 6377 (no control number) — ~2× ($192 dealer listing; $2,000 asks for unused stag examples) vs a 1970s White Hunter at ~$135 — "PUMA 6377 WHITE HUNTER" on the ricasso with NO five-digit control number, stag scales, original card/sheath (https://napoleonexhibit.com/store/Vintage-Puma-White-Hunter-6377-Pre-1964-w-Homemade-Sheath_166842432212.html)
- NOS boxed German Puma lockbacks (230365 NIB 1994 $172.99; boxed median ~$150) — ~1.5–2× vs loose at $90 — matching model number on the box label and blade, green-string paper tag, leather pouch (entry comp)
- Puma Game Warden 970 / Plainsman fixed blades — ~1.5× ($135 median, $170 p75) vs lockbacks — "GAME WARDEN" etch on the blade, stag handle, brass guard (entry comp)
- Puma Medici lockback — rarer in the US (never exported) — "MEDICI" model name on the blade, slim stag lockback; collectors seek it (https://bladeforums.com/threads/my-vintage-puma-collection.821762)
- Steel-etch dating: "Genuine Pumaster Steel" (1977–80) and "Super Keen Cutting Steel" (1968–84) etches — era markers that beat unlabelled 1990s blades — etched slogan on the blade flat alongside the model number (https://bladeforums.com/threads/my-vintage-puma-collection.821762)
- Natural sambar stag scales with a silver monogram shield — ~1.5× vs synthetic — irregular bark texture, colour variation, inset oval shield (https://bladeforums.com/threads/my-vintage-puma-collection.821762)
- "Puma IP" / "Spain" / "China" tang — 0.3× ($15–50) vs German — the letters "IP" or a non-German country on the tang; pass (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
German-made: crisp stamping, tight fitting liners, even walk-and-talk, carbon or high-carbon stainless steel. Imports: laser-etched logos, loose tolerances, "Made in China/Spain" or "IP". Stag scales from older production (natural antler) beat synthetic. Original boxes with paper "Puma" brochure and leather pouch add 30–60%.
- Black-coated (gun-blued or coated) blades on 230-series military knives show silver wear at the edge and tip; a fully silver blade on a model that should be black has been stripped or buffed.
- Old vs new: control numbers and pre-64 absence of numbers; older paper tags and boxes; 1990s and later Pumas may show "Puma IP" or "SGB" branding. Newer Pumas often have laser-etched rather than stamped marks (general knowledge, verify).
- Puma 230-series lockbacks cannot easily be taken apart; a knife with mismatched screws instead of pins has been opened.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Sharpened-down blades (profile changes), broken tips, lock play, rust pitting, cracked stag, missing medallion, buffed/polished blades (removes etch). Also: blade play developing even with light use (reported on the 230370 Commando), worn black coating, missing pouch (often advertised with the knife but absent), and damaged or crushed boxes (still add value).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit Puma White Hunter and Puma folder copies from Pakistan/China exist (wrong logo font, no model number, "Puma" without Germany). Puma IP is legitimate but cheap; sellers routinely omit "IP" from titles. Married parts: a genuine box with the wrong model knife; check that the box label number matches the blade number.

## Market
Strong collector base; eBay sells German Pumas within 1–3 weeks. Knife buyers scrutinize tang stamps. Pre-64 (no control number) and boxed NOS pieces draw the strongest bids; Puma IP sits.

## What sellers usually miss
"IP" vs Germany distinction (sellers both under- and over-price); Puma control numbers (date codes); the original box and catalogue paper; stag vs synthetic. Also the model name (Sergeant, Commando, Master), which buyers search by, and pre-64 status.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket size; padded box $5–8. Ships legally domestically (folding knives fine via USPS).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph both sides of tang stamp, model number, date code (control number on the lock bar), lock-up, edge; state sharpened or factory edge; include box/papers.

### What to tell the buyer
"Genuine Puma made in Solingen, Germany (not a Puma IP import), model [230265 Sergeant / etc.], stamped [mark]. The control number [xxxxx] on the lock bar dates it to [year/quarter] (Puma began control numbers in October 1964). Scales are [stag / checkered synthetic] with the cat-head medallion. Lock-up is [tight / slight play], the edge is [factory / lightly sharpened], and the coating shows [wear]. Comes with [box / paper tag / pouch]." State only what the photos show; give the closed length and blade length.
