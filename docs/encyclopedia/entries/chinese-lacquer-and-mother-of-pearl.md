## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glossy black (sometimes red or brown) lacquered wood or MDF panel with raised, carved pieces of mother-of-pearl shell, dyed shell and sometimes bone or soapstone glued on to form birds on blossoming branches, peonies, cranes, geisha/ladies, landscapes and pagodas. Rims often have gilt hand- or stencil-painted borders (lattice, scroll, key-fret). Round plaques of 18–36" with a cast "ruyi"/meander hanger at top, and tall rectangular panels sold in sets of 2 or 4 (Four Seasons), are the classic 1970s–90s souvenir/decor form sold through Hong Kong, Taiwan and later PRC export houses and import stores (Pier 1, Bombay, military PX shops in Asia).
Photo tells: thick dimensional shell pieces with visible glue lines; dyed green/pink shell leaves; very even glossy black surface (polyurethane/spray lacquer, not hand-layered urushi); perfectly regular gilt border = stencilled production.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Unbranded export production (by far the bulk): Hong Kong and Taiwan 1960s–80s; PRC (Fujian, Yangzhou, Fuzhou) 1980s–2000s. Paper labels read "Made in Hong Kong", "Made in Taiwan Republic of China", or "Made in China"; many labels lost. ROC/Taiwan labels date roughly 1960s–80s.
- Forms: round wall plaques; rectangular wall panels (sets of 4 Seasons birds/flowers or "Four Beauties"); folding 4–6 panel screens; jewellery boxes and tables (Asian furniture, too big).
- Higher end: Yangzhou "baibao qian" (hundred-treasure inlay) with hardstone, jade, coral, ivory-substitute; Japanese "raden" thin-shell inlay (very fine, flush, iridescent rather than raised); Korean najeon chilgi (flush, iridescent abalone/MOP on black lacquer, boxes and cabinets) — Korean najeon boxes do sell well ($80–300 for good small boxes).
- Antique Chinese (Qing) lacquer with inlay: rare on Craigslist; requires expert ID.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Decor grade: MDF or plywood substrate, spray black finish, thick carved freshwater/oyster shell, dyed elements, stencilled gilt. Feels light; back is plain painted board with a staple-on hanger.
- Better grade: solid wood, multi-layer lacquer with depth, finely carved shell with engraved detail (feather lines, petal veins), soapstone/hardstone faces or flowers, hand-painted borders with irregularities.
- Raised (applied) shell = decorative export; flush, thin, iridescent shell laid into the lacquer (raden/najeon) = higher craft and typically higher value.
- Age tells: old lacquer shows fine crackle ("cracking like snake skin"), wear to edges with wood visible, old hand-forged hangers; new pieces have pristine glossy surfaces and modern screws.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Missing shell petals/feathers (very common; check the photo for blank glue spots), lifted pieces, yellowed glue.
- Scratches and crazing in the black finish; sun fade to brown; water damage swelling MDF on bottom edge.
- Repairs: re-glued shell (visible glue blobs), black marker touch-ups on rim. Large round plaques are often warped.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
The whole decor category is mass production, so "fake" mainly means a 1990s–2020s piece being sold as "antique" or "Qing". Anything round with birds/peony on glossy black is 20th-century export unless proven otherwise. New pieces are still sold today on Amazon/Etsy, so retail asks of $200–400 are not comps.

## Market
Steady but soft demand from decor and Chinoiserie buyers; slow on eBay because of size and shipping. Most realistic outlet is local (Facebook Marketplace) or Etsy. Est. eBay sold ~30–80/month across all forms, sell-through modest, 30–60 days typical.

## What sellers usually miss
Sellers overrate: they read Etsy asks of $300+. Undervalued cases are the opposite: Korean najeon boxes called "Chinese jewelry box", and genuinely old fine-inlay pieces listed as generic "Asian decor".

## Shipping / handling
Round 30–36" plaque ships oversize: double-box with rigid board, corners protected; $35–60 to buyer. Shell pieces snap off in transit; wrap face with foam sheet, never bubble wrap pressure on raised shell.

## Buy rule
Dylan's rule: profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A clean 30–36" plaque sells ~$80–120 but ships $40–60 each way and exceeds a passenger seat at the large size → effectively never a buy. Buy only: (a) Korean najeon or Japanese raden small boxes (flush iridescent inlay) ≤ $40, or (b) small panels ≤ 18" with fine engraved shell and hardstone ≤ $25. No authentication-dependent "Qing" claims.
