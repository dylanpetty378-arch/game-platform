## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Small (5–6½") dolls with painted faces and mohair wigs in elaborate costume**, sold by series (Mother Goose, Operetta, Days of the Week, Months, Seasons, Flower Girls, Around the World, Sports, Family, Big & Little Sister, Masquerade, Bridal).
- **Bisque era (1936–1947)**: one-piece painted bisque body and head, jointed arms; legs either **jointed** (earlier, c.1936–41) or **frozen** (moulded with the body, c.1941–47). Painted eyes, often a **side-glancing** look; painted shoes (black or silver). Mark on the back: "STORY BOOK DOLL USA" or "STORYBOOK USA"; earliest babies marked "Made in Japan 87/88"; Oriental "Made in Japan 1148".
- **Hard-plastic era (1947/48–c.1955)**: same size and costumes but in lighter, warmer, slightly shiny hard plastic; painted eyes first, then **sleep eyes**; marked "STORYBOOK DOLLS USA" or "NANCY ANN STORYBOOK DOLLS". Tap test: bisque clinks like china, plastic clicks.
- **Muffie (1953–56)**: 8" hard plastic, sleep eyes with painted upper lashes, straight-leg or bent-knee walker; competitor to Vogue Ginny. **Debbie** 10" (1955–57). **Lori Ann** 7½" vinyl (1957–58). **Style Show** 17–18" hard plastic, saran wig, elaborate gowns (1950s). **Miss Nancy Ann** 10½" high-heel teen (1959+).
- Packaging: small boxes in pale blue, pink, gold, red with white dots, or white with coloured dots; a named gold foil wrist tag; a booklet of available dolls. Box end labels give the number and name.
- Look-alikes: Madame Alexander-kins and Vogue Ginny (8", sleep eyes; see madame-alexander-dolls), Japanese "occupied" bisque minis, Daisy Dolls ("DAISY DOLLS"), Kerr & Hinz ("K & H USA").

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Nancy Ann Storybook Doll Co.** (Nancy Ann Abbott, San Francisco, 1936–1971). Bisque made in the US after the earliest Japanese imports; huge production in the 1940s, so ordinary dolls are plentiful.
- **Series value order (estimate)**: common — Mother Goose, Days of the Week, Months, Seasons; better — Operetta, Masquerade, Around the World, Sports, Family; best — Topsy and Eva (c.1938), early "Judy Ann", early Japanese-made babies and Oriental.
- **Hard-plastic follow-ons**: Muffie (with large tagged wardrobe), Debbie, Style Show (NASS), Miss Nancy Ann/Little Miss Nancy Ann, Aline (1973, Hong Kong).

### Quick ID checklist
1. Height: 5–6½" (Storybook), 8" (Muffie), 10" (Debbie/Miss Nancy Ann), 17–18" (Style Show).
2. Material: bisque (matte, cool, chips) or hard plastic (smooth, light, shiny).
3. Legs: jointed (separate, can swing) vs frozen (moulded to the body).
4. Eyes: painted (often side-glance) vs sleep eyes.
5. Back mark: "STORY BOOK DOLL USA", "JUDY ANN USA", "Made in Japan", "NANCY ANN".
6. Extras: gold wrist tag with name, box with dots, booklet, hang tags.

### Golden-egg tells
- **"Judy Ann" marked bisque (c.1936–37)** — ~5–10× a common frozen-leg doll ($100–300, estimate) — back mark reads JUDY ANN instead of STORY BOOK; early, sometimes cruder face; ask for a back-mark photo.
- **Topsy (Black doll, c.1938) and the Topsy/Eva pair** — ~8–20× ($150–400 each, estimate) — dark-brown bisque with black mohair wig in tufts or braids; original white box with coloured dots and gold wrist tag lift it further.
- **Jointed-leg bisque with silver painted shoes (pre-1941)** — ~2–3× frozen-leg ($30–60 vs $10–20, estimate) — gap at the hip, legs can sit apart; silver or black painted slippers.
- **Mint in original dot box with wrist tag and booklet** — ~3–4× the loose doll ($40–90 vs $10–25, estimate) — box with white dots on blue/pink/red, gold foil tag on the wrist, the booklet in the box.
- **Style Show 17–18" hard-plastic gowned dolls** — ~10–30× a small doll ($250–700, estimate) — tall, glued saran wig, sleep eyes, flat feet, floor-length net or taffeta gowns with hats.
- **Muffie 8" in tagged outfits or boxed, especially early straight-leg walkers** — ~3–8× a small loose doll ($60–200, estimate) — 8" with painted upper lashes (not moulded), "Nancy Ann" on the back, tagged clothes.
- **Early Japanese-made bisque babies and the 1938 Oriental (Around the World)** — ~3–5× ($50–120, estimate) — "Made in Japan 87/88" or "1148" on the back.
- No premium: loose frozen-leg Mother Goose/Days of the Week dolls with faded, split skirts and no tags — $8–20.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Bisque: matte, slightly gritty surface; paint wears off the face and shoes first. Hard plastic: smoother, lighter, often a pinkish tint; seams along limbs.
- Original costumes are taffeta, net and cotton with lace, often with a gold paper sticker or tag; replaced clothes, hot-glue and synthetic lace are later.
- Mohair wigs that are unplayed, with the original ribbon, add value; frizzy or cut hair reduces it.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Bisque: broken or reglued arms (rubber band stringing perishes), chips on hands, face paint rubbed, crazing of the paint.
- Hard plastic: broken arm hooks, sleep eyes stuck or cloudy, fading.
- Clothes: fading on one side (displayed in sun), shattered taffeta, missing hats. Taffeta shattering is the most common value killer.
- Ask: back-mark photo, close-up of legs (jointed/frozen) and of the face, any box/tag.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few outright fakes. Risks are married outfits on the wrong doll, Japanese "occupied" lookalikes, and later hard-plastic dolls described as bisque. Boxes and wrist tags get swapped between dolls — match the box number/name to the costume.

## Market
Doll collecting is aging and soft; plain Storybook dolls are plentiful because production was enormous. Buyers are doll collectors filling series, and people buying a "birthday month" or "day of the week" doll as a gift. eBay and Etsy; Theriault's for big collections. Slow: weeks to months for single loose dolls, faster for boxed/tagged.

## What sellers usually miss
- The back mark (most never turn the doll over); Judy Ann and Japanese marks are worth multiples.
- Jointed versus frozen legs.
- Boxes and wrist tags stored separately in the same estate.
- That an "old doll lot" often mixes Nancy Ann with Madame Alexander-kins and Vogue Ginny, which are worth more (see madame-alexander-dolls).

## Shipping / handling
Each doll ~0.25 lb; a lot of 9–12 ships at 3–5 lb in a 12×12×8 box for $12–18. Wrap each doll separately in tissue; bisque arms and hands chip. Don't compress taffeta skirts.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give: height, bisque or hard plastic, jointed or frozen legs, painted or sleep eyes, back mark (photo), series and doll name (from the wrist tag/box), clothing condition (fading, shattering), box and tag presence. Title: "Nancy Ann Storybook doll bisque jointed legs [name] [series] 1940s wrist tag box".

## Buy rule
Buy when: a lot works out to ≤ $5 per doll and the photos show tags, boxes, jointed legs, Topsy/Judy Ann, Muffie or a Style Show doll, and profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$15 to buyer − ~$15 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Pass when: loose frozen-leg dolls with faded/shattered clothes over ~$8 each, single common dolls, or anything priced at "book value". Fits a passenger seat easily.
