## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast brass or bronze small doors from post office lock-box banks, removed when post offices modernized (1960s–90s). Each has a box number, a small window (glass), and a combination dial (Keyless Lock Co. / Yale "dial" type: two dials with letters A–J) or a keyhole. Fronts often show an eagle with shield, stars, or Victorian scrollwork; numbers stamped or on a small plate. Hobbyists convert them into coin banks with a wooden box behind. Postal scales: Triner "Official Postal Scale" (Chicago) with brass pan and painted dial; Pelouze (Chicago) letter scales; Hanson; earlier balance-beam post office scales.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Yale & Towne Mfg. Co.** (Stamford CT): many patterns 1870s–1960s; marked "Yale & Towne" on back, patent dates.
- **Keyless Lock Co.** (Indianapolis): the common dial "keyless" doors with eagle, 1900s–1950s.
- **Corbin, Eagle Lock Co., Salsbury Industries** (later modern aluminum doors, 1970s+, low value).
- **Size/number**: #1 (small, ~5×3"), #2, #3 (larger); larger doors and "Victorian ornate" early types (1880s–1910s) bring more.
- **Triner Scale & Mfg.** (Chicago, 1897–1970s): "Official Postal Scale" counter scales; **Pelouze** (spring letter scales, 1890s–1970s); **Hanson** (1888–); balance beam scales by Fairbanks.
- **Other postal**: cast-iron letter boxes, porcelain "U.S. Mail" signs (fake-heavy), USPS badges, mail-carrier bags, stamp vending machines.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original doors: heavy cast bronze/brass, mellow patina, crisp eagle detail, back with lock mechanism and hinge pin, glass intact. Reproductions: lighter, softer detail, sold as "bank kits" (new doors made ~1990s–present, often marked "Made in Taiwan" or no maker marks). Modern Salsbury doors: aluminum, no ornament, low value. Scales: original paint dials with rate charts dated by postal rates (e.g. 3¢ first-class = 1932–58, 4¢ = 1958–63) — the rate chart dates it.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing or cracked glass, missing combination/without known combo, missing hinge pins, broken dials, polished-off patina (some buyers like polished), paint-filled numbers. Scales: rusted pans, faded dials, missing weights, non-accurate springs.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
New-made "PO box door" bank kits are everywhere (craft stores, eBay, ~$20–30 new). Reproduction porcelain US Mail signs are common. Fake "post office" scales in brass from India.

## Market
Consistent but low-priced; dozens sold monthly on eBay; crafters buy lots for banks. Mostly a volume, low-margin item.

## What sellers usually miss
Early Victorian-pattern doors listed as just "brass mailbox door"; lots with matching sequence numbers; combination info stamped inside.

## Shipping / handling
Doors are small and heavy (~1–2 lb each); USPS Ground Advantage $6–10 for one, flat-rate box for lots. Scales $15–25. Wrap glass.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "post office box door" + maker (Yale & Towne, Keyless Lock Co.) + "brass" + "eagle" + size number (#1/#2/#3) + "antique" + "combination" if known; lots: "lot of 10". Junk: "mailbox", "bank" (unless built), "steampunk". Example: `Antique Yale & Towne Brass Post Office Box Door #2 Eagle Combination Known`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (dozens sold monthly; crafters buy lots); singles are volume/low-margin, so lots of 5–10 are the listing to make. Nothing beats eBay.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (single eagle brass door $25–45, lots of 10 $200–350, ornate Victorian/large #3 $50–150, Triner scales $30–80); accept −10%, floor −20%; OBO; cut after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) door face straight-on; then back with maker stamp and patent dates, dial/combination stamp inside, glass window close-up, edge for thickness, ruler for size; lots laid out in a grid with numbers visible.
- **State:** maker, size number, box number, combination known and working or not, glass intact, measurements, original removed door (not a kit), count and matching sequence for lots, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 1–2 lb each, wrap glass in bubble, small box, USPS Ground Advantage $6–10 single; lots in a medium flat-rate box; scales 5–10 lb, $15–25.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Original [Yale & Towne / Keyless Lock] brass door pulled from a [1900s–1950s] post office, combination [stamped/working], not a reproduction kit — matched-number lots like this sell for [$X] and single eagle doors go for $25–45 each."
