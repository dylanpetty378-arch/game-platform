## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Pipe clamps**: cast fixtures (Pony/Jorgensen "No. 50", orange or red) that slide on 1/2" or 3/4" black/galvanised pipe. Value is in the fixtures, not the pipe.
- **Bar clamps / F-clamps**: steel bar with a sliding jaw (Jorgensen, Pony, Bessey "GZ", Stanley, Record).
- **Parallel jaw clamps**: Bessey K-Body (red/black), Jorgensen Cabinet Master (orange), Jet. Sold new $50–80 each; used sets are the best value in this category.
- **Hand screws**: two maple or beech jaws with two screws. Antique versions have **wooden screws** (pre-~1920, collectible as primitives); later ones have steel threaded spindles with wooden handles (Jorgensen "Adjustable Clamp Co. Chicago", still made, user value).
- **C-clamps**: cast/forged; old drop-forged Wetzler, Armstrong, Cincinnati Tool, Brown & Sharpe sell modestly; "deep-throat" and big ones are heavy.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Adjustable Clamp Co.** (Chicago, 1903–; brands Jorgensen and Pony): hand screws stamped "Jorgensen" on jaw, Pony pipe fixtures, Cabinet Master parallel clamps.
- **Bessey** (Germany, 1889–): K-Body Revo, GZ/GS bar clamps, tradesman clamps; red/black.
- **Record** (Sheffield, UK) T-bar sash clamps (No. 135/136), heavy and long.
- **Stanley** No. 1-xxx bar clamps; **Brink & Cotton**, **Hargrave** (Cincinnati), **Wetzler** (NY) C-clamps.
- **Antique wooden hand screws**: unmarked or with owner's initials; some Hammacher Schlemmer, Sargent. Wooden screw threads are hand-cut or turned.
- Specialty: violin/luthier clamps, bookbinding presses, veneer presses, miter-frame clamps (small, pocket-sized and sometimes $20–60).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Cast fixtures: crisp casting and a maker name cast in = old/quality; plain unbranded fixtures = Harbor Freight-type.
- Hand screws: hard maple jaws with chamfered ends; old ones show patina and darkened wood. Steel spindles with "Jorgensen" stamp = 1920s+ user; wooden spindles = 19th c.
- Parallel clamps: check for a straight bar and intact plastic jaw pads.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent bars, stripped threads, cracked jaws (hand screws), seized clutches on pipe fixtures, missing jaw pads, rust. Users accept surface rust; bent bars are junk.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None meaningful. Decorative "antique" hand screws are sometimes new import décor; new wood is pale and sharp-edged with machine threads.

## Market
Mostly local market. Clamps are heavy and long, so eBay demand is for small specialty clamps and antique wooden hand screws (décor). Long clamps and pipe clamps sell locally on Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist at $5–15 each.

## What sellers usually miss
- Parallel clamps (K-Body, Cabinet Master) sold for "$10 each" in garage clearouts: new retail $50–80.
- All-wood hand screws are décor pieces worth more than steel-spindle ones.

## Shipping / handling
Pipe and bar clamps over 36" are not shippable economically and do not fit the passenger seat rule. Hand screws 3–8 lb ship $12–20. Sets of parallel clamps ship $40+ (oversized).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give jaw length, maximum opening, maker stamps, thread type (wood vs steel), straightness of bar, and whether jaws close parallel.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping both ways − price ≥ max($50, price). Clamps almost never meet it shipped. Buy only: sets of 4+ K-Body/Cabinet Master ≤ 36" long at ≤ $15 each, or several all-wood hand screws together at ≤ $10 each. Pass on pipe clamps, long bars and single clamps.
