## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Western Electric 500** (1949–1980s): rounded desk set, finger-wheel dial with letters under clear holes; date stamped under base and inside handset ("10-63"). Bell System property.
- **Western Electric 302** (1937–1954): smaller, taller "Lucy" phone, metal (early) or thermoplastic body, dial on a sloped front; values rise for metal, ivory, red, green.
- **WE 554/2554 wall, Princess (701/2702), Trimline**, 2500 Touch-Tone.
- **Automatic Electric (GTE) Type 80/90** (1950s–70s): squarer than WE 500 with a flat rectangular dial plate behind the finger wheel, often brown/beige; "Automatic Electric" or "GTE" on the base.
- **Candlestick** (1900s–1920s), **wooden crank wall phones** (Kellogg, Stromberg-Carlson, Western Electric).
- **Novelty**: Coca-Cola bottle and disc phones (1980s–90s), Mickey Mouse (American Telecommunications, 1976), Snoopy & Woodstock, Garfield (1980s, Tyco), hamburger, football, lips phones.

## Makers, brands and variants
Western Electric (Bell System), Automatic Electric (GTE, Chicago), ITT/Kellogg, Stromberg-Carlson, North Electric (Ericofon "cobra" 1950s–70s $60–200), Northern Electric (Canada), Siemens (Germany). Licensed novelty phones: Coca-Cola (1990s licensed by Telemania and others), Tyco Garfield, Columbia Telecommunications.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Original Bell System handset cord (coiled, cloth for 1950s) and a modular-converted line cord; date codes on base and handset matching = originality.
- Rare colors: pink, turquoise, light blue, red, yellow, green (WE 500 colors from 1954). "Gold" retirement phones.
- Heavy metal base = WE 500/302; lightweight plastic body with no metal = later reproduction or novelty.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Discolored plastic (beige yellows), cracked cases, worn dial number cards, non-working ringers, missing finger stops, frayed cords. Rotary phones still work on most landlines (pulse dialing) and VoIP adapters — testable by the seller.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern "retro" reproductions (Crosley, Mid-Century Modern) look like 302s and 500s but are light and have push-buttons hidden in rotary-style dials; China-made "candlestick" phones abound. Look for the Western Electric/Bell System markings and date codes.

## Market
Steady decor/prop demand; colored phones sell in 1–3 weeks. Common brown/beige phones sit.

## What sellers usually miss
302s in a box of "old phones"; candlesticks missing parts still sell well; Ericofons; matching date codes.

## Shipping / handling
3–6 lb, $15–20; handset can crack the cradle if not padded.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model, color, date code, whether it rings and dials (tested on landline), cord type, original or modular conversion.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 ship out − ~$15 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Practically only 302s, candlesticks, crank wall phones and rare-color 500s at ≤ $40. Fits a passenger seat. No authentication needed.
