## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Mink**: short dense glossy guard hair, visible vertical "let-out" stripes on full coats (narrow strips sewn diagonally). Colors: ranch (dark brown), mahogany, pastel (light brown), tourmaline/silver-blue, black (Blackglama). Female pelts are softer and lighter.
- **Critter/animal stoles**: whole pelts with heads, feet and tails, clip-mouths joined in a chain (usually 2–6 mink, marten or sable), 1930s–50s.
- **Fox**: long fluffy guard hair (red, silver, blue, white, crystal). **Sable**: long, silky, brown with silver hairs. **Marten**: like sable but coarser, slightly longer, often with buff throat patch. **Persian lamb (karakul)**: tight curled black or brown, low value. **Raccoon**: banded long hair (1920s raccoon collegiate coats have value). **Beaver/muskrat/nutria**: sheared dense.
- **Faux**: knit or woven backing visible at seams; perfectly uniform stripes printed or dyed in; dense acrylic pile. Labels: Borgana (Collins & Aikman), Glenoit, Tissavel (France), "Silver Candalon" style.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Furriers' labels add trust (Revillon Frères, Maximilian, Evans, Ben Kahn, Alixandre, Christian Dior Fourrure, Neiman Marcus/Saks/I. Magnin/Marshall Field fur salons, Bergdorf Goodman). Ben Kahn/Maximilian/Revillon coats can be $300–1,000.
- Blackglama label = top US ranch mink.
- Faux: Borgana (1950s–60s), Collins & Aikman "Candalon", Glenoit, Sasson faux (1980s), modern Dennis Basso, Apparis (modern). Vintage faux sells $30–100; 1960s mod/designer faux $80–250.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Leather (skin side) must be soft and pliable; a dry "paper crackle" when squeezed or tears near seams = dry rot, unsellable.
- Full skins vs pieced: cheap coats are made from paws, tails or "piece plates" (visible patchwork). Let-out strips = higher quality.
- Weight: female mink coat 5–7 lb; long faux coat 3–5 lb.
- Lining: monogrammed silk, hand-sewn = quality; torn linings are common and cheap to replace.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dry rot (fatal), shedding/bald spots at cuffs and collar, odor (mothball, smoke), oxidation (black/brown fur turning reddish), torn linings, broken hook-and-eye closures, moth damage. Stoles often lose eyes/claws.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Faux sold as real (and vice-versa): check backing with a pin — real fur has leather; faux has fabric. Dyed rabbit sold as mink/chinchilla; dyed muskrat sold as "Hudson seal". Use the FTC-required label (post-1952) that names the animal and country.

## Market
Soft, slow and seasonal (Oct–Feb). Huge supply of mink coats from estates; buyers are costume, vintage fashion, crafters ("fur teddy bear" makers buy damaged mink for $30–60). Some states/cities restrict new fur sales (California AB44 bans sale of new fur products; used/vintage is exempt) — check before listing. eBay allows non-endangered furs; no export of some species.

## What sellers usually miss
Sable or lynx called "mink"; designer furrier labels; they over-price ordinary mink coats ("paid $5,000") far more often than under-price. Faux Borgana often assumed real.

## Shipping / handling
Fold loosely in a garment box or large poly mailer with tissue; 4–8 lb, $15–25. Low damage risk; odor management is the main issue.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State species from label, color, length, chest and sleeve measurements, leather condition (supple, no tears), lining condition, odor, and furrier label. Note it's vintage (pre-ban for CA buyers).

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 ship out − ~$20 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Only full-length female mink, sable, lynx or chinchilla at ≤ $60, with seller confirming supple leather; pass stoles, Persian lamb, rabbit and faux under typical pricing. Coats fold into a passenger seat. No authentication needed except species — rely on the label.
