## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Chord zither ("guitar-zither", "mandolin-harp"):** flat trapezoidal box with a slanted pin row, a round soundhole with a paper rosette, chord string groups on the left, melody strings across, and a paper note chart under the strings. Usually an eagle, landscape or flowers decal. Sold door-to-door 1890s–1930s with numbered song sheets.
- **Ukelin (1920s–1960s):** long narrow coffin-shaped box, two soundholes, 16 melody strings bowed with a violin bow plus 4 groups of chord strings strummed; sold door-to-door by International Musical Corp. (Hoboken/Jersey City NJ) and later Oahu Publishing (Cleveland).
- **Bell Harp (John Gill / Bell Harp Co., Fall River MA, c. 1890s–1920s):** small zither with a sound box that was swung while playing to make a vibrato; label inside soundhole.
- **Marxophone:** chord zither with spring-steel hammers on a keyboard bar.
- **Concert zither (Alpine):** fretboard on one side with metal frets, 30–40 open strings, guitar-shaped or rounded body, German/Austrian makers.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **International Musical Corp. / Oahu / Manhattan Musical** – ukelins; label "Ukelin" on the bar; usually black or dark brown finish with gold stencil rosettes.
- **Phonoharp Co. (Boston), Columbia (Phonoharp brand), Menzenhauer & Schmidt (NJ), Oscar Schmidt (Jersey City)** – chord zithers; decals with eagles, angels or ships; paper labels inside the soundhole.
- **Marxochime Colony (NY)** – Marxophone, Hawaiian Art Co. "Violin-Uke" and Pianolin.
- **Bell Harp Co.** – small trapezoidal with label; earlier ones are better made.
- **Concert zithers:** Kiendl, Tiefenbrunner, Hopf; scroll-shaped Alpine "harp zithers".
- **Modern bowed psalteries** (1970s–present, hobby makers) – triangular, bowed; $40–150.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Spruce or pine tops, maple/birch sides, painted or stained finish, steel strings, hitch pins and tuning pins.
- Better: intact paper labels/rosettes, crisp decal, clean finish, all tuning pins present, flat top.
- Low: lifted or sunken top, missing pins, rusted strings, broken bridges, glued-on replacement bottoms.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Tops crack and sink under string tension; bottoms separate; bridges crack; strings rust and break; tuning pins strip.
- Most surviving examples need restringing (needs a tuning wrench and a set of strings, ~$30–60) — this counts as a repair under Dylan's rules.
- Missing bow (ukelin), missing tuning hammer, missing song cards and case reduce value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- No meaningful fakes. Modern lap harps (Music Maker, Hearthsong) and Chinese-made zithers are new products, sold cheaply.
- Autoharps (Oscar Schmidt, with chord-bar buttons) are a different, more playable instrument with their own market.

## Market
- Large supply; many listings, slow sales. Wall-decor buyers drive the low end. Sell-through low (≈20–30%). Weeks to months to sell.

## What sellers usually miss
- They often overprice because Lens/eBay asking prices are high; very few sell above $100.
- The rare valuable variant is the Marxophone or a complete Alpine concert zither in its fitted case, not the decal chord zither.

## Shipping / handling
- Long, flat, 20–30" boxes, 5–10 lb; $20–35 to ship. String tension makes tops fragile; slacken strings slightly and protect the pin rows.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** correct type ("ukelin", "chord zither", "Marxophone", "Bell Harp", "concert zither" — not "autoharp"), maker from the label (Phonoharp, Oscar Schmidt, International Musical Corp), decade, "with bow case song cards", "decal". Junk: "harp", "antique instrument", "rare", "autoharp". `Ukelin International Musical Corp 1920s Complete with Bow Case Song Cards`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (sell-through 20–30%; weeks to months); wall-decor buyers on FB local for cracked zithers.
- **Price position:** chord zithers at the sold median ($40–90), OBO, accept −20%; complete ukelins/Bell Harps $80–175; Marxophone/concert zithers $100–400, accept −15%; cut 15% at 45 days. Cracked at $20–50 as decor.
- **Photos:** first: instrument flat, top straight-on; then label through the soundhole, decal/rosette, pin rows (count the missing), bridge, top in raking light for sinking/cracks, back seam, bow, case, song cards.
- **State:** type named correctly, maker from the label, size, strings/pins present or missing, cracks and top condition, "sold as display / needs restringing" unless playable, accessories included.
- **Pack / haul:** 20–30" flat box, 5–10 lb, strings slackened, pin rows padded, $20–35; fits a passenger seat.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Complete [ukelin] with bow, case and the original song cards, flat top and all pins — most survivors are cracked wall decor at $30; a complete playable one is the $80–175 piece."

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$25) − ship to Dylan (~$25) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- With typical sold medians of $50–120, profit is rarely reachable: a complete ukelin at $150 resale allows max buy ≈ $30. Most need restringing (a repair) → pass.
