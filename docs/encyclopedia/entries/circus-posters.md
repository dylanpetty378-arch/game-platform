## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Advertising posters printed for touring circuses. Formats: one-sheet (~28 × 41–42 in), half-sheet (~28 × 21 in), window cards (~14 × 22 in, cardstock, with blank top for town/date), multi-sheet billboards (huge, rarely complete). Originals usually show: a **printer imprint** in a bottom margin ("Strobridge Litho Co., Cincinnati & New York", "Erie Litho & Ptg Co.", "Courier Co. Buffalo", "Donaldson Litho", "Tribune Show Print", "Enquirer Job Printing"); fold lines (posters shipped folded); slight paper toning; sometimes a date/town snipe pasted on. Reprints: on bright white or heavy glossy stock, sized for frames (18 × 24, 24 × 36), no imprint or "© (modern year)", "Reproduced by…", even dot pattern everywhere.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey** (combined 1919; ended 2017, revived 2023). Earlier separate Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey shows (pre-1919) posters are the most valuable.
- **Strobridge Lithographing Co.** (Cincinnati) – the great stone-litho printer for Barnum & Bailey/Ringling into the 1940s. Imprint is a strong value tell.
- **Erie Litho**, **Courier Co.**, **Donaldson** (Newport KY), **Morgan Litho** (Cleveland), **Riverside Print** – other show printers.
- **Clyde Beatty Circus**, **Cole Bros. Circus**, and the merged **Clyde Beatty–Cole Bros.** (1957 on): many 1940s–70s window cards and one-sheets, often letterpress/"show print" style with bold type plus animal imagery; mid-value.
- Others: Hagenbeck-Wallace, Sells-Floto, Al G. Barnes, King Bros., Mills Bros., Hunt Bros., Carson & Barnes.
- **Ringling's own later reprints** and museum-shop reproductions (Circus World Museum, Ringling Museum Sarasota) of famous 1930s–40s images – sold as posters, often 1970s–2000s.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Stone lithograph (pre-~1935): rich, layered colors, grainy crayon texture under a loupe, no regular dot screen.
- Offset lithograph (1940s+): halftone dots/rosettes visible under a loupe; still original if the paper, imprint and size are right.
- Linen-backed: original posters are often mounted on linen by conservators – a sign of value, raises price.
- Window cards: heavy cardboard, often with town and date printed in a blank band.
- Photo tells: printer imprint text, fold creases in a grid, uneven toning, small edge tears = probably original. Perfect flat glossy paper, shrink-wrap, frame-shop size = reprint.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tears along folds, losses at fold intersections, water staining, tape stains, fading of reds, trimmed margins (kills imprint). Restoration/linen backing is professional work ($150–400) – not for Dylan.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reprints far outnumber originals. Common traps: 1970s Ringling "nostalgia" reprints of 1930s–40s designs (offset, sometimes with a small modern credit line), bookstore/museum prints, and "aged" decor prints. Check imprint, dimensions, dot pattern, paper. Deliberate forgeries are uncommon at this price level.

## Market
Steady niche: circus collectors (CHS – Circus Historical Society), decorators, and poster collectors. Originals sell in days to weeks when priced right; reprints sit. Moderate eBay volume.

## What sellers usually miss
Estate sellers can't tell original from reprint either way – sometimes an old folded poster from an attic is listed as "old circus print $40". The imprint line and fold lines are the tells. Window cards with a filled-in town/date are originals by definition.

## Shipping / handling
Ship rolled in a 3–4 in diameter tube (never fold further); framed pieces are awkward – remove from frame if possible. ~$12–20 ground. Rolled posters easily fit a passenger seat; large frames may not.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State show, year (if known), printer imprint, format and exact dimensions, printing method, folds/tears, linen-backed or not, and that it is an original (only if imprint/paper confirm). Photograph imprint and a loupe close-up.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship (~$15) − ship to Dylan (~$15) − price ≥ max($50, price). A 1960s–80s original at $100 median needs price ≤ ~$27; a 1930s–50s original at $250 median works up to ~$90–$100. Require photos of the bottom margin (imprint) and fold lines. Pass on anything framed under glass that can't be inspected, anything at a standard frame size without an imprint, and anything needing restoration.
