## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Doulton & Co. made stoneware at Lambeth (London, 1815–1956) and earthenware/bone china at Burslem (Staffordshire, 1877–). "Royal" was granted 1901. Collectible non-figurine lines:
- **Seriesware** (1890s–1960s): printed-and-hand-coloured scenes or florals on plates, jugs, trays, with a "D" pattern number (e.g. D5xxx). Includes Dickens, Shakespeare, Gibson Girls, Old English Scenes, and florals on mottled grounds.
- **Flambé / Sung / Chinese Jade / Titanian**: red-veined high-fired glazes (flambé), very collectible.
- **Lambeth stoneware**: salt-glazed brown/blue art pottery signed with incised artist monograms (Hannah Barlow, George Tinworth, Frank Butler, Eliza Simmance).
- **Dinnerware**: hundreds of bone china patterns (e.g. Larchmont, Tiara, Old Colony) — low.
In photos: lion-and-crown over four interlinked D's in a circle ("Royal Doulton England") on the base; impressed date marks on earthenware.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Backstamps: Lambeth "Doulton Lambeth" with rosette (1869–1956); Burslem "Doulton Burslem" (1882–1902); "Royal Doulton" lion-crown (1902–), "Made in England" added c. 1930; "Bone China" added; small numbers beside the lion-crown give the year after 1928 (number + 1927 = year).
- Florals on cobalt-and-gold mottled ground, wavy-rim bowls and slice trays — 1930s–40s seriesware.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Seriesware: printed outline then hand-coloured; heavy gloss glaze; raised white enamel dots on flower centres = higher effort. Flambé: deep red with black veining, "Flambé" in red on base. Lambeth: salt-glazed stoneware, incised decoration, artist monogram and assistant marks.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim chips and flea bites, crazing (common on 1930s earthenware), gilt wear on rims, utensil scratches, hairline cracks (ring test, hold to light). Restored rims show as a dull patch under raking light or UV.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Flambé has some Chinese fakes with printed red marks. Seriesware not much faked. Royal Doulton reissued some popular lines in the 1980s–2000s with modern backstamps.

## Market
Seriesware sells steadily in US/UK but prices have fallen since 2005. Expect weeks. Flambé moves quickly when priced right.

## What sellers usually miss
- Flambé or Titanian pieces sold as "red vase".
- Lambeth stoneware listed as "brown crock" — check for incised artist monograms.
- Small year numbers next to the backstamp.

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb; double box with 2 in of padding each way, $12–18.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the D pattern number, backstamp period, size, and state crazing, gilt wear and chips explicitly.
