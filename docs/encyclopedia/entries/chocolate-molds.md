## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Hinged/clamp (two-part) figural molds**: two mirror-image halves of tinned steel (or nickel-plated) joined by a hinge or held by separate clips, making a hollow 3-D figure. Heavy, grey-silver tin finish with a dull patina.
- **Flat or tray molds**: a single sheet with several shallow figures or bars in a row; often a frame. 3-section bar trays and egg trays are low value.
- **Display molds**: 15"–40" tall rabbits, Santas and bears made for shop windows; huge, rare.
- **Pewter ice-cream molds**: small (3–5") heavy pewter hinged molds of fruit, Santa, flags, fraternal emblems; marked "E & Co NY" or "S & Co" with a number.
- **Marks**: stamped into the metal along the flange/edge: maker name and city ("ANTON REICHE DRESDEN", "T.C. WEYGANDT CO. NEW YORK", "E & CO. N.Y." or "EPPELSHEIMER & CO N.Y.", "LETANG FILS PARIS", "VORMENFABRIEK TILBURG HOLLAND"), a 4–6 digit model number, and often "GERMANY" or "Made in Germany". Importer stamps (Weygandt, "I.C.W." of NYC) appear on German molds.
- Craigslist sellers call any heavy metal mold "cast iron"; a cast-iron Griswold lamb or rabbit is a cake mold (a different type).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Anton Reiche (Dresden, c.1870–1950s)** — the giant of the trade; tens of thousands of designs, numbered; Santas, rabbits, animals, characters. Highest prices.
- **T.C. Weygandt Co. (New York)** — US importer of Reiche and other German molds; Weygandt stamp + Germany.
- **Eppelsheimer & Co. (New York, c.1880–1940s)** — American maker; "E & Co" or full name with number; rabbits, Santas, animals (rhino, elephant), pre-WWII display molds.
- **Letang Fils (Paris)** — fine French work; stamped "LETANG Fils" with a 4-digit number; elegant figures, hens, fish.
- **Vormenfabriek (Tilburg, Netherlands)** — Dutch, 20th century, common in Europe.
- **Dehaeck (Belgium/Brussels)**, **H. Walter (Berlin)**, **Sommet, Cadot (France)** — less common, collected.
- **Pewter ice-cream mold makers**: Eppelsheimer (E&Co), Schall & Co., Krauss — small figural pewter molds.

### Quick ID checklist
1. Hinged/clipped figure or flat tray?
2. Height (15"+ = display mold).
3. Stamped name/city/number on the flange.
4. Subject: Santa/Father Christmas, character, uncommon animal vs plain sitting rabbit.
5. Tin coating intact, crisp detail, hinge and clips present, rust level.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Display molds 15"+ by Reiche or Eppelsheimer** — ~20–100× a small rabbit ($500–3,000; two 38" Reiche molds made $11,000 and $15,000 at Leonard Auction 2024) — huge hinged mold, often needs two hands to hold, stamped number, multiple clamp clips.
- **Father Christmas / Belsnickel / Santa with a toy sack, 10"+** — ~10–30× ($1,700–3,000 at Milestone: Reiche Father Christmas pair $3,000, Reiche 21" Santa $2,200, Dehaeck 20" Father Christmas $1,700) — long robe, hood, sack or tree, deep detail.
- **Uncommon animals (standing elephant, rhinoceros, fish, horse and rider)** — ~10–40× (16" elephant with separate ear molds $3,300 at Milestone) — anything that isn't a rabbit, chick or egg.
- **Character molds (Mickey Mouse, Bugs Bunny, Popeye, Felix)** — ~3–10× ($100–400, estimate) — copyright lines like "Walt Disney" or "Warner Bros." stamped on the back.
- **Eppelsheimer or Reiche multi-figure hinged trays (4–6 rabbits/Santas)** — ~3–5× a single small mold ($150–250 retail; Replacements asks $249.95 for Reiche #26814) — a row of figures with an importer stamp like "I.C.W. NYC GERMANY Patented".
- **Pewter ice-cream molds (E&Co, S&Co) of fraternal, patriotic or holiday subjects** — ~2–5× a common fruit mold ($40–150, estimate) — heavy dark pewter, hinged, with a number.
- No premium: 3-section bar trays, egg-shaped trays, 1960s+ plastic molds, unmarked thin tin copies — $5–30.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old molds are heavy tinned steel or nickel-plated steel with crisp detail; the inside polished to release chocolate. The outside shows a dark grey patina and maker stamps.
- Hinged molds have riveted hinges and separate spring clips; missing clips cut value by maybe 10–20%.
- Plastic (polycarbonate) molds date from the 1950s on and are of little interest.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust pitting inside (kills function; reduces decor value), worn tin plating, dents, bent flanges, broken hinges, missing clips, separated halves (a half-mold is worth ~25% of a pair). Ask for both halves open, the interior, and the stamp.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Decor reproductions are lighter, thinner tin with soft detail and usually no maker name or number. Some recast copies of famous Reiche Santas exist; compare weight and crispness, and look for genuine stamped (not cast-in) numbers. Griswold cast-iron rabbit/lamb cake molds have been reproduced (see the separate cast-iron entries).

## Market
Strong Easter (Feb–Apr) and Christmas (Oct–Dec) seasonality. Buyers: holiday collectors, decorators, a few working chocolatiers. eBay for small molds; auctions (Milestone, Bertoia, Leonard via LiveAuctioneers) for display molds. Small rabbits are common and slow; anything big or unusual sells quickly.

## What sellers usually miss
The stamped maker and number on the flange; that a Santa or unusual animal is worth many times a rabbit; and that oversized molds are display molds. They also lump valuable figurals in with bar trays and call everything "cast iron".

## Shipping / handling
Small molds 0.5–1.5 lb, $8–12 to ship; 12–15" molds 2–4 lb, $15–20; display molds 5–15 lb with big boxes, $30–60. Clip the halves together and wrap; flanges bend if dropped.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "antique" + maker (Anton Reiche / Eppelsheimer / Weygandt / Letang) + "chocolate mold" + subject + mold number + height + "hinged" + Germany/NY. Junk: "cast iron", "candy tray", "cute bunny". `Antique Anton Reiche Dresden Chocolate Mold #8821 Santa Claus 12in Hinged`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for molds under 15" (list by Feb for Easter, Oct for Christmas); LiveAuctioneers (Milestone/Bertoia) for 15"+ display molds, Father Christmas and rare animals, which beat eBay.
- **Price position:** at the sold median for the subject/size, accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days or after the holiday passes.
- **Photos:** closed mold standing on white; flange stamp with maker and number; both halves open showing interior tinning; hinge and clips; back; next to a ruler.
- **State:** maker, number, height, subject, both halves/hinge/clips present, interior rust or pitting, tin coating, decor only (not food-safe, may contain lead), flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** shoebox, small 0.5–1.5 lb $8–12; 12–15" 2–4 lb $15–20; display 5–15 lb double-boxed $30–60; clip halves together, pad flanges, no loose play.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Maker] #[number] [subject] mold, [height], [1920s–30s], stamped on the flange with hinge and clips intact; plain rabbits of this size sell for [$] and [Santas/animals] bring [$]."

## Buy rule
Buy when: hinged figural mold with a stamped maker/number ≤ $30, 12"+ Santa/character/rare animal ≤ $60, or a 15"+ display mold ≤ $100, and profit = resale × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Pass when: flat bar/egg trays, plastic, unmarked thin tin, single halves, heavy rust. Display molds over ~30" may not fit the passenger seat — check size.
