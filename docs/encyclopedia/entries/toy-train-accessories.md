## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Tinplate and bakelite trackside pieces:
  - silver lattice tower with a red/orange top (Lionel 394/494 rotary beacon)
  - black semaphores with red/green lenses (Lionel 151)
  - crossbuck crossing signals on a black base (Lionel 154)
  - gateman shed (Lionel 45/145)
  - water tower (Lionel 138)
  - street lamps (Lionel 64/71)
- Marx lithographed crossing gates and signals use thinner tin and brighter litho.
- Noma "Lites" electric stations come in a brown Noma box.
- Seller words: "vintage train accessories", "Lionel pieces", "train set parts".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Lionel postwar (1945–69)**: numbered accessories, often stamped "Lionel" on the base. Operating accessories (coal elevator 97, log loader 164, bascule bridge 313, transfer table 350, switch tower 445, freight station 356) carry the most value. Prewar Lionel (1920s–30s, Standard gauge) is a separate, higher market (general knowledge, verify).
- **Marx**: tin litho, cheaper, lower value.
- **Noma**: lighted stations, collectible when boxed.
- **Plasticville** (Bachmann): plastic buildings, low value.
- **Transformers**: Lionel ZW and KW (big, valuable if tested); 1033/1044 (small, low).
- Modern Lionel (1970+, "6-" catalogue prefix) is a different market. Example: 1990 6-18808 SD-18 locomotive $70–120; postwar 2343 F3 sets $300+.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Postwar Lionel: heavy-gauge painted steel, die-cast bases, bakelite, with "Lionel" and the number stamped or embossed.
- Original boxes (orange and blue postwar boxes) add a lot (general knowledge, verify).
- Postwar-era pieces are heavier than modern reissues (Lionel reissued many accessories in the 1980s+), which have "6-" prefixed numbers.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rust, chipped paint, missing bulbs or lenses, broken wires, seized motors on operating pieces.
- Repainted pieces are worth less. Operating accessories must be tested.
- Transformers: frayed cords and untested condition. Only tested transformers sell well.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Lionel's own modern reissues of postwar accessories, and aftermarket reproduction parts and boxes (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
A big, steady hobby with an older buyer base. Peak is Nov–Jan (holiday layouts), and train shows run in the fall and winter. Prices for common postwar pieces have softened; operating accessories and boxed items hold up (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Pieces are sold as box lots "as-is". The 394 beacon, water towers and operating accessories in the lot carry most of the value.
- Noma stations in the original box are scarce ($40–80).
- Lionel transformers only sell if tested (ZW/KW $80–150; 1033/1044 $15–30).

## Shipping / handling
- A 25-piece lot is ~20 lb in two boxes, $40–60 to ship in. It sells best split into 10–15 eBay lots, which takes a lot of listing time. Fees 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Catalogue number, postwar or modern, tested working, complete (bulbs, lenses, wires, controller), box condition.
- Photograph the number on the base, every side, and the box flaps.
- Title keywords: "Lionel Postwar 394 Rotary Beacon", "Lionel 97 Coal Elevator Tested", "O Gauge".

## Buy rule
Buy when: the lot price is ≤ $4/piece and at least one 394 beacon, water tower or operating accessory is visible; or any Lionel 313/350/97/164 is under $80.
