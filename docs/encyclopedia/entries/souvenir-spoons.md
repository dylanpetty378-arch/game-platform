## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **US souvenir spoon craze 1889–1920s**: sterling teaspoons/coffee spoons with figural handles (cities, states, Indians, Salem witch, world's fairs), engraved bowls ("Niagara Falls"). Gorham's 1891 Salem witch spoon by Daniel Low started it.
- **European enamel spoons** (Norway — David Andersen, J. Tostrup; Denmark; Netherlands; Austria) in 800/830/925 silver with guilloché or plique enamel.
- **Salt spoons**: tiny (2–3 in) spoons for open salt cellars; sterling or plate.
- **Figural cast brass/bronze mini spoons** (Italy, India, 1950s–80s): crusty "antique look" handles with cherubs, animals, grapes; base metal.
- **Modern collector spoons**: plated with enamel crest tops, sold in racks (1960s–2000s) — near worthless.

## Makers, brands and variants
Gorham (lion-anchor-G), Watson & Newell, Durgin, Shepard Mfg, Mechanics Sterling, Paye & Baker, Charles M. Robbins, Wallace, Mayer Bros (Seattle, Native American motif), Joseph Mayer, Daniel Low (retailer, Salem). Norway: David Andersen, Marius Hammer, J. Tostrup.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling: "Sterling" or "925" stamp, crisp chasing, soft patina; plated: "EPNS", "Silver Plate", "A1", wear through to brass. Brass: yellow under worn gilt, heavy cast lumps, green verdigris in recesses — not silver.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent bowls, worn engraving, enamel chips (big impact), monogram removal, plate wear.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern cast copies of figural sterling (often in plated base metal); fake "sterling" stamps on Asian items; 1970s reissues of Salem witch spoons.

## Market
High volume; sterling sells reliably (melt floor); plated/brass sells slowly in lots.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling spoons mixed into plated lots; Norwegian enamel (800/830S marks) listed as "pretty spoon"; Native American figural sterling.

## Shipping / handling
Very light, padded envelope or small box, $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph hallmarks, give weight in grams for sterling, describe motif and city/state, note monograms.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$6 ship to buyer − ~$6 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Sterling lots bought below melt are the only routine win; figural sterling at resale ≥ $120 for ≤ $35. Brass/plate never qualifies.

## Field notes (learned in triage)
- 2026-10-13 (redo): Italian figural-finial demitasse spoons (Rome she-wolf, Venice lion, etc.) are often 800 silver even when the seller says plated. The back stamp ("800" vs "Italy" only) settles it.
- 2026-10-14: The "Rolex collector spoon" sellers talk up is the Bucherer of Switzerland (Rolex dealer) giveaway, usually Interlaken/Luzern with a bear finial in a red-lined black case; very common, ~$15-25. It is not a Rolex-made item.
