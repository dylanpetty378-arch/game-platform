## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small lidded porcelain boxes with hinged brass clasps (Limoges) or lift-off lids (gift ware). Limoges: hand-painted, figural shapes (shoes, animals, food), brass hinge with clasp, mark underneath "Limoges France Peint Main" plus decorator (Rochard, Artoria, Chamart, Beauté, Parry Vieille). Gift ware: white glazed hearts with applied porcelain roses, gold trim, fabric ruffles, wind-up music movement under the base.
- **Mark wording seen on genuine pieces**: "Rochard, Peint-Main, Limoges, France"; "Artoria Peint Main Limoges France" plus extra characters; "Limoges France PV Marque Déposée Peint Main" (1stdibs listings). "Peint main" = hand painted; "Marque déposée" = registered trademark. Rochard boxes are also signed by the painter (Lifelong Collectibles). Hunker notes the "Limoges" and "France" wording is typically painted in green on the base.
- **Shapes** collectors look for: figural novelties (houses, champagne bottles, coffee makers, ice-cream carts, animals, shoes, handbags) and themed boxes with a tiny loose accessory inside (e.g. a removable bone in a Rochard pug box).
- **English enamel boxes** are enamel on copper, not porcelain: 18th-century Bilston/Battersea patch boxes (with a mirror), snuff boxes and animal bonbonnières, typically no more than about 2" (Homes & Antiques); modern Halcyon Days, Crummles and Bilston & Battersea Enamels boxes copy the form with mottoes and dates.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Limoges “Peint Main”** (1980s–present): Rochard, Artoria, Chamart, La Gloriette, Beauté. Retired/limited models rise. Rochard still sells new boxes with a certificate of authenticity and green-and-gold gift box (e.g. 2.25" × 1" × 2" pug box retail $350) (Lifelong Collectibles) — the box and certificate help resale.
- **Halcyon Days / Crummles / Bilston & Battersea**: English enamel on copper.
- **Antique English enamels** (Homes & Antiques): painted copper enamels made commercially from the 1740s (Battersea first), Bilston-type from about the 1750s in south Staffordshire, faded by the 1830s; Bilston pieces carry no identifying marks; a small box can have 17+ components; mottoes are affectionate, saucy or in misspelled French. Rarity drives price: spaniel and bird bonbonnières are common; frogs, rabbits, cherubs, lemons, melons and gherkins sell for several times more (c.1780 frog bonbonnière offered at £3,500; c.1780 patch box £185 — dealer prices).
- **Herend** (Hungary), **Royal Crown Derby** (Imari), **Wedgwood jasperware** — quality boxes.
- **Capodimonte / Italian porcelain flowers** — mostly modern gift ware.
- **Heritage House, San Francisco Music Box Co., Formalities by Baum Bros, Lefton** — 1980s–2000s gift ware; minimal resale.

### Quick ID checklist
- Turn it over: "Limoges France" plus "Peint Main" and a decorator name (Rochard, Artoria, Chamart, Beauté, PV); painter initials or a signature are a plus.
- Hinge and clasp: a crisp, well-fitted metal hinge and a clasp that snaps shut = Limoges. Glued, loose or crooked hardware = import copy.
- Painting: visible brushwork and fine detail. Identical blurry printed edges = decal.
- "Limoges" without "France", or "Limoges China", = a warning sign (see Fakes).
- Look inside for a loose accessory (charm, bone, tiny bottle) and check that it is present. A missing piece cuts value.
- Enamel box: the lid edge shows copper. Is there a motto or date? Halcyon Days / Crummles / Bilston & Battersea name on the base = 20th-century. Unmarked with crude mounts may be antique or a Samson copy.
- Gift ware: applied roses, fabric ruffle, wind-up key under the base, foil "made in China/Taiwan" sticker = pass tier.
- Check applied petals, the lid rim and the hinge pins under zoom.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-painted (brushstrokes, slight variation) vs transfer decals. Fine hinge work = Limoges. Gift ware: heavy, thick white porcelain, fabric/lace additions, paper "made in China/Taiwan" stickers.
- A sturdy, well-fitted clasp that closes securely and fine brushwork with smooth finish are the authenticity tells retailers cite (Limoges Boutique).
- Enamel: powdered glass fused onto copper, each colour fired separately; old enamels show fine crazing and wear at edges; 19th-century Samson copies have noticeably poorer hinges and mounts (Homes & Antiques).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken petals on applied flowers (very common), hinge loose, chips at lid rim, fabric yellowing, music movement not playing.
- Enamel boxes: chips and cracks to the enamel ("bruising") over the copper are common; damaged boxes are unappealing to collectors, though skilled restoration is accepted by some (Homes & Antiques).
- Missing removable accessory or original box/certificate on a modern Limoges reduces resale.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese "Limoges-style" boxes with fake "Limoges" marks (no "France", wrong hinge, poor painting) are common.
- Reproductions are openly sold as decor (Hunker); a lookalike with no decorator name and a weak clasp is the usual fake.
- Samson of Paris (mid-19th c.) copied Bilston enamel designs, sometimes exactly (Homes & Antiques).

## Market
Limoges boxes sell steadily (hundreds/month) with moderate prices; gift ware hardly sells. 1stdibs dealer asking prices for "Limoges France Peint Main" pieces run $236–750 (asking, not sold) — don't price from dealer sites.

## What sellers usually miss
Estate lots of small boxes often hide Limoges Peint Main pieces among gift ware; turn every box over. Also: enamel-on-copper English boxes mixed into "porcelain" lots, and the tiny removable accessory still inside a Limoges box.

## Shipping / handling
Small, fragile; 0.3–1 lb each; $6–10 per box with bubble wrap; double box for applied flowers. Tape the clasp shut with low-tack tape and bag any loose accessory separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Underside mark close-up, hinge working, any chips, dimensions; list painter initials if present.

### What to tell the buyer
Quote the base mark exactly as written, for example "Limoges France – Peint Main – Rochard" with painter initials. "Peint main" means hand painted in French. Give the dimensions in inches, say whether the hinge and clasp close firmly, and say whether any removable accessory, the original box or the certificate is included. Describe chips to the paint or porcelain at the lid rim. For an enamel box, say "enamel on copper", give the maker on the base (Halcyon Days, Crummles, Bilston & Battersea) or state that it is unmarked, quote any motto, and describe any enamel chips. Do not call a box "antique Bilston" unless a specialist has confirmed it, because 19th-century and modern copies are common.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship − ship − price. Single Limoges at $45 median → ~$15 margin at best: pass singles. Lots of 5+ Limoges at ≤ $40 total can work (lot $150 → $130 − $25 − $40 = $65). Gift-ware boxes: always pass.
