## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass pressed in iron moulds, made in America c.1840–1915 ("EAPG"), followed by "near-cut" imitations of brilliant cut glass c.1905–1930, and lots of later reissues and look-alikes.
- **Flint era (c.1840–1870)**: lead glass; heavy, rings when tapped, slightly grey-clear; patterns like Ashburton, Bellflower, Horn of Plenty, Thumbprint, Comet, Lacy Sandwich. Pontil scars on early lacy pieces.
- **Non-flint EAPG (1870–1910)**: soda-lime, lighter; hundreds of patterns (Daisy and Button, Westward Ho, Lion, Three Face, Thousand Eye, Manhattan, Bull's Eye); ruby-stained and amber-stained souvenir pieces ("Mother 1904 World's Fair").
- **Near-cut (1905–1930)**: pressed imitations of cut glass with hobstars, fans and crosshatching; McKee "Prescut", Imperial "Nucut", Cambridge "Near Cut", Heisey. Crisp sawtooth rims, mould seams through the pattern. Large punch bowls on separate bases are common.
- **Later reissues and look-alikes (1930s–1980s)**: Anchor Hocking, Indiana, L.E. Smith, Jeannette, Federal "Pioneer" and 1970s–80s gift punch sets; thinner and sharper-edged but lacking wear.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **US Glass Co.** (1891 merger of 18 Pittsburgh-area factories; "States" pattern series: Manhattan, Texas, Kentucky, etc.).
- **McKee & Brothers** (Jeannette PA): "Prescut" trademark moulded in script on some pieces (c.1904–1920s).
- **Imperial Glass** (Bellaire OH): "Nucut" trademark; later Imperial reissues marked IG.
- **A.H. Heisey** (Newark OH 1896–1957): diamond-H mark moulded on many pieces after 1901; Colonial, Punty Band, Fancy Loop. Heisey near-cut punch bowls $100–300.
- **Cambridge Glass**: "Near Cut" moulded mark; Inverted Thistle, Buzz Star.
- **Fostoria**, **Duncan & Miller**, **Tarentum**, **Higbee** (bee mark), **Westmoreland**, **Dalzell, Gilmore & Leighton**, **Adams**, **Bryce**, **Gillinder**, **Boston & Sandwich Glass Co.** (lacy flint).
- **Reissue lines**: L.E. Smith Moon & Star, Westmoreland English Hobnail, Indiana Glass Sandwich, Anchor Hocking Manhattan (1938–43 depression pattern, not the EAPG Manhattan) and Early American Prescut ("EAPC", 1960–1999, the cheap hobstar pattern often confused with McKee Prescut).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Flint (lead) glass: heavy, bell tone, clear with slight grey; under UV often fluoresces dull; worth far more than non-flint.
- Wear: genuine old pieces show fine scratches on the foot rim and base; reissues are crisp with no foot wear.
- Moulded marks: Heisey diamond-H on the stem or centre; "Prescut", "Nucut", "Near Cut" scripts.
- Stain/flash: ruby or amber flashing is original if worn on high points and engraved through; modern reissues are often fully coloured glass instead.
- Clarity: sick/cloudy glass (etched inside) is permanent damage.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Flea-bite chips on sawtooth rims and points (near-universal on near-cut), cracks from thermal shock, cloudy "sick glass", ground rims to remove chips, missing lids and mismatched bases on punch bowls and compotes. Damage drops value 50–80%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Many popular EAPG patterns were reproduced from the 1930s on (Westmoreland, Fenton, L.E. Smith, Imperial, Summit Art Glass, AA Importing): Daisy and Button, Moon and Star, Bellflower, Three Face, Lion, Westward Ho, Thousand Eye, Cherry Thumbprint. Tells: too crisp, no foot wear, slightly off-colour clarity, odd colours unknown in the original pattern. Anchor Hocking "Early American Prescut" (star-of-David centre, 1960–99) is the most common near-cut look-alike, worth $5–25.

## Market
Oversupplied; demand from older collectors is shrinking; prices fell steadily since the 1990s. Clear near-cut is the slowest category; flint glass, coloured EAPG and marked Heisey still sell.

## What sellers usually miss
- Flint-era heavy goblets and spoon holders bundled into "old glass" lots.
- Heisey diamond-H on a "pressed glass bowl".
- The reverse is common: punch bowls and near-cut priced as "antique cut glass" at $100+ (Lens matches retail asks).

## Shipping / handling
Punch bowl + base is large (12–14" diameter, 10–15 lb boxed) → $25–40 each way; bowl and base must be boxed separately with 3" padding. Small pieces $10–15.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the pattern and maker only with a source (EAPGS, Metz); state flint vs non-flint; photograph rims under light for flea-bites; say "pressed glass" (never "cut glass"). Give dimensions.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Clear near-cut punch bowls/bowls/sugars: pass at any price (shipping eats resale).
- Flint-era heavy pieces in known patterns or rare colour: buy at ≤ $25 when resale ≥ $120.
- Heisey marked near-cut punch set complete with cups: buy at ≤ $40 if resale ≥ $200 and local pickup is possible.
- Size: small pieces yes; punch bowls are borderline but fit a passenger seat.
