## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-sewing tools of the 1850s–1950s: decorative scissors with figural/cast handles (stork, eagle, bust medallions, Art Nouveau women), sterling or gold thimbles, needle cases (sterling, ivory, bone, celluloid, figural brass), tape measures (celluloid figural pigs/fish/cars, brass), chatelaines and etuis.
**D. Peres Solingen** (Daniel Peres, Solingen, Germany, est. 1792): high-quality scissors and knives; decorative scissors with cast medallion busts (classical heads, historical figures) or embossed gilt handles; mark "D. Peres Solingen" with a fish/crown-type logo on the blade (verify logo). Ornate D. Peres scissors appear frequently; most sell modestly.
Thimbles: sterling marked "Sterling" / maker symbol inside (Simons Bros "S" in shield, Ketcham & McDougall "K&McD" or anchor, Webster "W", Stern "SB", Waite-Thresher "W.T. Co."); size number; gold thimbles marked "10K/14K" inside the band; panel or scenic bands = higher value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Scissors**: D. Peres, J.A. Henckels (twin mark), Gebr. Weyersberg, Wiss (US), Unger Bros/Gorham/Webster (sterling handles, 1890s–1910s), Napoleon/ Clauss (US), stork scissors (since 19th c., still made — common).
- **Thimbles**: Simons Bros (Philadelphia), Ketcham & McDougall (NY), Webster Co., Stern Bros, Goldsmith Stern, Waite-Thresher, Gabler (Germany, marked "800"), Charles Horner (UK, Dorcas steel-core), James Fenton; Meissen/porcelain modern collectibles low.
- **Needle cases/etuis**: sterling (Unger, Gorham), Victorian bone/vegetable ivory, celluloid advertising; figural German brass.
- **Tape measures**: German/Japanese figural celluloid (pigs, fruit, Kewpies), brass novelty — $30–150 for good figurals.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling: hallmarks; gold: karat stamps, color, weight. Scissors: forged steel blades, crisp casting, gold plating on handles; cheap modern ones are stamped, light, marked "Italy/China/Pakistan". Old vs new thimbles: older Simons Bros have deeper, hand-chased bands; modern collector thimbles are souvenir-type.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Thimbles: holes worn through the top, dents, splits (repaired seams). Scissors: loose rivet, rust pitting, worn plating. Needle cases: missing caps, dents, cracks in celluloid/bone. Ivory pieces: legal restrictions (avoid selling ivory across state lines; CITES/US rules).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern stork scissors and "Victorian style" figural scissors are reproduced en masse; Chinese "sterling" thimbles not actually silver; reproduction chatelaines. Test sterling with mark checks and magnet.

## Market
Collector base is older and shrinking but steady; gold/sterling move on metal value. Thimble lots sell well on eBay. Figural scissors sell slowly unless sterling.

## What sellers usually miss
Gold thimbles mixed in sewing-box lots; sterling marks inside thimbles; sterling-handled scissors listed as "brass"; early German figural tape measures.

## Shipping / handling
Tiny; $5–6 envelope/box. Scissors need blade protection.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker marks, metal content (tested), size, weight in grams for gold/silver, damage (holes in thimble tops), length of scissors. Avoid ivory listings.
