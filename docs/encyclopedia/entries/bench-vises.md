## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wilton "bullet" machinist vise: round cylindrical body (looks like a bullet), hex nut on the front of the dynamic jaw, date stamped under the front jaw/key (month-year, e.g. "10-93"), "WILTON Chicago" or "Schiller Park, Ill." cast on the body (Chicago = 1941–57 earliest/most prized, Schiller Park = 1958–2000s, "Made in China" = pass). Models: 101/300S/400S/500S (3", 3.5", 4", 5" jaws), 825 "Toddler" 2.5", 9300/9400 swivel. Other money vises: Prentiss (Brooklyn, 1900s), Reed (Erie PA), Charles Parker (Meriden CT), Columbian/Athol/Starrett D-series, Yost, Morgan, Will-Burt Versa-Vise, and leg/post vises for blacksmiths. Cheap: Sears/Craftsman cast (China/Taiwan), Harbor Freight, Irwin, any red generic vise with "SEARS" cast in. Sellers' words: "machinist vise", "bench vise", "old vise".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Wilton** – bullet 101/300S/400S/500S, 825 Toddler, 9300/9400 swivel; Chicago (1941–57) > Schiller Park (1958–2000s) > China.
- **Charles Parker** (Meriden CT), **Prentiss** (Brooklyn), **Reed** (Erie PA) – collectible, especially large sizes.
- **Columbian, Athol, Starrett D-series, Yost, Morgan** – decent to collectible.
- **Will-Burt Versa-Vise** – collectible (rotating multi-position).
- **Leg/post vises** – blacksmith market, heavy.
- **Sears/Craftsman** – pre-1970 USA decent; post-1980 import junk. **Harbor Freight/Central Forge, Irwin, generic red** – junk.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Wilton bullets: ductile iron/steel bodies, precision-fit sliding jaw, replaceable serrated jaw inserts. Quality USA vises: forged or high-grade cast with crisp lettering, tight slide, smooth screw. Imports: rough castings, play in the slide, thin jaws. Date stamp under the jaw dates a Wilton. Original paint vs repaint: restored examples sell well if done properly (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked or brazed body (often at the base or jaw), bent handle, worn screw/nut with slop, chewed jaws (replaceable), missing swivel lock. Welded repairs kill collector value. Ask for photos under the jaw (date stamp, casting text) and of the base (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
China-made Wilton-branded vises (later production) – check casting text. Listing/photo mismatches occur (a "Wilton" title with a Sears vise in the photo, as seen in sweep).

## Market
Strong, steady demand from machinists, hobbyists and restorers; Wilton bullet ~43 sales in the aggregation window (period unspecified). Local sale avoids heavy shipping; eBay reaches collectors for top pieces (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
"Wilton" in the title is usually enough for the seller to know. The arbitrage is a bullet vise titled "machinist vise" or "old vise" with no brand, or a Chicago-marked one priced as a Schiller Park. Also Parker/Prentiss/Reed names that mean nothing to most sellers.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy: 4" bullet ~40 lb, 5" ~60 lb. Ship UPS ground in a double box or on a half-pallet: $40–70 each way. This kills most of the margin, so only flag at ≤ 35% of sold value.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model, jaw width, opening capacity, swivel/lock function, date stamp, casting location (Chicago/Schiller Park), slop in the slide, jaw condition, any repairs. Photograph casting text, date stamp, jaws, base, and screw. Keywords: maker + model + jaw width + "bullet vise"/"machinist vise" + "USA" + date.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a real Wilton bullet (Chicago or Schiller Park), Parker, Prentiss, or Versa-Vise at ≤ $90, or any 5"+ bullet at ≤ $150. Pass when: the photo shows "SEARS"/import casting or the listing is at ≥ 50% of sold value. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (~$100 total for a 4"). Must fit a passenger seat (bench vises do; large leg/post vises may not).
