## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Rectangular lithographed steel boxes (and dome-top "workman" variants) with a handle and latch, usually with a matching
steel/glass thermos clipped inside the lid. The maker name (Aladdin Industries, Nashville; King-Seeley Thermos; ADCO
Liberty) and copyright year of the licensed character are printed along the bottom edge or rim of the litho and on
the thermos cup. Vinyl boxes are cardboard covered in printed vinyl with a stitched/heat-sealed seam. 1990s boxes are
moulded plastic or vinyl. Sellers write "old metal lunch box", "vintage lunchbox with thermos", the character name.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Metal boxes (Aladdin, Thermos/King-Seeley, ADCO) with matching steel/glass thermos are the collectible tier:
  1950s–60s Westerns (Roy Rogers, Lone Ranger), Disney, Beatles, Star Trek 1968, Jetsons.
- Vinyl boxes 1960s–70s (Barbie, Monkees) $50–200 if not cracked.
- 1980s metal (Dukes of Hazzard, Pac-Man, ET) $30–80; 1985 Rambo was the last metal.
- 1990s plastic/vinyl (Godzilla 1998, Jurassic Park, Power Rangers) $15–40 with thermos.
- Top rarities: 1954 Superman, Toppie, 1930s Mickey Mouse (Geuder, Paeschke & Frey) — auction pieces.
- Dome-top boxes and boxes with embossed (3-D) lithography generally bring more than flat ones of the same era
  (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lithographed tin-plated steel; embossing started ~1962 (general knowledge, verify). Original thermos should match the
box's character and maker; glass-lined thermoses are more fragile (glass liner often broken — rattles). Bright,
unfaded litho with intact gloss and no paint loss is the quality tell. Vinyl: no splits at the seams/handle rivets,
no mould.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Condition rules everything: rust, dents, missing thermos cut 50–70%. Common damage: rust on the bottom and inside,
paint rubs on edges, dents, broken latch/handle, names written in marker, cracked or split vinyl, broken thermos
glass. Touched-up paint and clear-coat over rust show as glossy patches in photos. Ask for photos of the inside,
bottom rim and thermos.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Licensed reproductions and later reissues exist (e.g. 1990s–2000s retro tin boxes of Star Wars, Beatles, Hopalong
Cassidy; Vandor and similar novelty tins) — they lack the period maker mark and have modern copyright lines
(general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Collectors of pop-culture and character merchandise (nostalgia buyers 40–70) plus decorators. Common titles sell
steadily on eBay; rare ones go to auction (Hake's, Heritage). Prices for mid-tier boxes have softened as the
boomer collector base ages (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The thermos (worth 30–50% of the set) and the 1950s–60s dates. "Old metal lunch box" titles with a visible
Western/space theme are the ones.

## Shipping / handling
2–3 lb, $10–14. Wrap the thermos separately (glass liners).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the character, maker and copyright year (photo of the litho rim), whether the matching thermos is included and
its cup, and condition of all four sides, the inside and bottom. Mention rust, dents and marker. Title: maker,
character, year, "metal lunch box", "with thermos".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1950s–60s metal box with thermos under $60, or a 1960s vinyl under $25. Pass when: 1990s boxes at any price
(ceiling $40). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat.
