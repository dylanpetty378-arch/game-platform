## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Platinotype (later gelatin/collotype) photographs hand-coloured with watercolour by colourists in Nutting's Framingham, MA studio (c. 1900–1941), mounted on a mat with the title pencilled at lower left under the image and "Wallace Nutting" signed (by staff) at lower right. Original frames are narrow gilt or wood. Competitors (Davidson, Sawyer, Thompson) used the same format with their own signatures.
Value drivers: subject (interior > exterior; unusual > common), size, colour quality, condition (mat toning, foxing, fading), original backing paper with Nutting label.

### Golden-egg tells
- Children with animals / rare figural titles (e.g. "Elizabeth and Her Pets", child with St Bernard and puppies) — $6,325 sold (Ivankovich, second-highest Nutting picture ever) vs $25–60 common exterior (~100×) — a child or dog as the main subject; any animal close-up; title pencilled lower left under the image, "Wallace Nutting" lower right on the mat (https://www.antiquesandthearts.com/ivankovich-sells-second-highest-ever-wallace-nutting-picture)
- Men at work / genre outdoor scenes (e.g. "Shoeing the Gray Mare", blacksmith with white horse) — $4,180 sold Ivankovich 2016 vs $25–60 (~70×) — a man doing a task (smith, farmer, mower), a horse, a barn; "With Compliments – Wallace Nutting" inscriptions add more (https://www.antiquetrader.com/auctions/wallace-nutting-photo-commands-4180)
- Rare-title interiors and figural interiors ("Mrs Maloney" $3,300; "A Peep Down the Street" $1,760; "Better Than Mowing" $1,210; "Six Bullseye Door, Leonard House" $660) — $660–3,300 sold vs $25–60 (~15–50×) — colonial room, often a woman at a hearth/spinning wheel or a doorway view; men inside an interior is the rarer variant (https://www.antiquesandthearts.com/ivankovich-sells-second-highest-ever-wallace-nutting-picture)
- Foreign scenes (Italy, England, Ireland: e.g. "Wine Carrier, Ravello") — $770–1,100 sold vs $25–60 (~20×) — Mediterranean stone walls, figures carrying jars, thatched cottages; title naming a foreign town (https://www.antiquetrader.com/auctions/wallace-nutting-photo-commands-4180)
- Fred Thompson hand-coloured seashore/figure scenes ("Toiler of the Sea" $1,980; "The Clam Digger" $1,100; "A Daughter of Wadsworth" $1,045) — $1,000–2,000 sold vs $15–60 typical Thompson (~20×) — Maine coast figures, signature "Thompson" lower right on the mat; usually unnoticed because sellers only know the Nutting name (https://www.antiquetrader.com/auctions/wallace-nutting-photo-commands-4180)
- Snow scenes and cows/sheep pastorals ("Pasture Dell" $1,980) — $400–2,000 sold (titled snow scene $400 McInnis Apr 2026) vs $25–60 (~10–30×) — white snow on the ground or animals in the field; winter titles are uncommon in a sea of blossoms (https://appraisily.com/artist/wallace-nutting/)
- Charles Higgins / other Nutting-like figural scenes (e.g. "A Hurried Departure", stagecoach) — up to $440 sold vs $15–50 (~10×) — "C. Higgins" pencil signature, action/period-costume subject (https://www.antiquesandthearts.com/ivankovich-sells-second-highest-ever-wallace-nutting-picture)
- Common: blossom lanes, birch streams, apple-tree exteriors, any 1930s printed-signature process print — ~1× ($10–70, median ~$50) — pink/white blossoms over a road, small (≤ 10×12") toned mat; beware: process prints show a halftone dot screen and a printed (not pencilled) signature (https://appraisily.com/artist/wallace-nutting/)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real hand-coloured photo: under magnification, continuous photographic tone (no dot screen) with soft watercolour brushing that doesn't perfectly match edges. Process print: halftone dot pattern, printed signature. Colour fading toward blue-grey indicates light exposure; vivid colour is better.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Mat toning/burn, foxing, water stains, faded colour, torn backing, replaced mats (huge value hit — the signature is on the mat), trimmed images, glass broken.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
1930s Nutting "process prints" (machine printed) sold as hand-coloured; modern scans/inkjets of popular titles; added fake pencil signatures on other studios' prints.

## Market
Mature, declining collector base; huge supply. Sell-through ~30%, slow (1–2 months) unless uncommon subject.

## What sellers usually miss
Rare subjects (interiors with men, cows, sheep, seascapes, snow) priced like common blossoms; Nutting furniture-label items; also original catalogue numbers on backs.

## Shipping / handling
Framed under glass: box with corner protectors, glass taped, $12–20. Small sizes fit easily.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title (read the pencil), overall/image size, colour condition, mat toning/foxing, frame original or not, back label photo.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$15 − ~$15 − price ≥ max($50, price). Common exteriors (~$40–60) never clear. Buy uncommon subjects with comps ≥ $150 at ≤ $40.
