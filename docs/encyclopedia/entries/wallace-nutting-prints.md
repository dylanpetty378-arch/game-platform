## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Platinotype (later gelatin/collotype) photographs hand-coloured with watercolour by colourists in Nutting's Framingham, MA studio (c. 1900–1941), mounted on a mat with the title pencilled at lower left under the image and "Wallace Nutting" signed (by staff) at lower right. Original frames are narrow gilt or wood. Competitors (Davidson, Sawyer, Thompson) used the same format with their own signatures.
Value drivers: subject (interior > exterior; unusual > common), size, colour quality, condition (mat toning, foxing, fading), original backing paper with Nutting label.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real hand-coloured photo: under magnification, continuous photographic tone (no dot screen) with soft watercolour brushing that doesn't perfectly match edges. Process print: halftone dot pattern, printed signature. Colour fading toward blue-grey indicates light exposure; vivid colour is better.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Mat toning/burn, foxing, water stains, faded colour, torn backing, replaced mats (huge value hit — the signature is on the mat), trimmed images, glass broken.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
1930s Nutting "process prints" (machine printed) sold as hand-coloured; modern scans/inkjets of popular titles; added fake pencil signatures on other studios' prints.

## Market
Mature, declining collector base; huge supply. Sell-through ~30%, slow (1–2 months) unless uncommon subject.

## What sellers usually miss
Rare subjects (interiors with men, cows, sheep, seascapes, snow) priced like common blossoms; Nutting furniture-label items; also original catalogue numbers on backs.

## Shipping / handling
Framed under glass: box with corner protectors, glass taped, $12–20. Small sizes fit easily.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title (read the pencil), overall/image size, colour condition, mat toning/foxing, frame original or not, back label photo.
