## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A spool-on-a-string toy. Three collector eras, each identifiable from a photo:
- **Wooden (1928–c.1965)**: one-piece turned maple or two halves on a wooden axle; logo hot-stamped in gold or silver foil ("Duncan O-Boy", "Duncan Gold Seal", "Cheerio 77", "Royal Tournament", "Hi-Ker", "Festival"). Rhinestone/"jeweled" sides on 1950s Duncan Tournaments and Cheerio. Painted stripes, sometimes faded. Look for the stamped word "Tournament", "Genuine Duncan", "Official" and the Duncan "bow tie"/oval logo. Wood grain visible at chips.
- **Early plastic (c.1955–1985)**: Duncan Imperial (solid colour, foil-stamped "Duncan Imperial" with the "sunburst", 1955 on; glow-in-the-dark "Glow Imperial" 1970s–90s), Duncan Butterfly (1957 on, flared halves), Duncan "Wheels" (tyre graphic, 1980s–90s), Festival/Royal plastic. Thick injection plastic, metal axle pin, often "Made in USA, Flambeau" moulded inside (Flambeau Plastics, Baraboo WI, made Duncan yo-yos from 1968).
- **Ball-bearing / clutch era (1990–2005)**: Yomega Brain (1993, centrifugal clutch, "Brain" on cap), Yomega Fireball (fixed axle, flame cap), X-Brain, Yomega "Raider"/"Hyper Raider" (Bandai co-branded 1998–99), Yomega "Firestorm"/"Fire Storm Xcel", Playmaxx ProYo, Spintastics, Duncan Freehand/Speed Beetle/Raider. Clear or translucent coloured plastic, removable caps, stickers. Hugely produced in the 1998–99 yo-yo craze.
- **Boutique metal (2003 on)**: one-piece machined aluminium/titanium, anodised, engraved logos (YoYoFactory, One Drop, CLYW, Spyy, YoYoJam, Duncan "Metal Drifter", General Yo, Turning Point). Recognised by bright anodised metal and wide "H" or "W" profiles.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Duncan** (Donald F. Duncan bought Pedro Flores's company 1929/1932; Flambeau acquired the brand 1968): wooden O-Boy, Gold Seal, Tournament, Beginner, Satellite, "Genuine Duncan Butterfly"; plastic Imperial (1955–), Butterfly (1957–), Midget, Mardi Gras; 1990s Freehand, Wheels, Raider, Hayabusa; 2000s Metal Drifter, Throwback.
- **Flores Yo-Yo** (Pedro Flores, Santa Barbara, 1928–1932) – the first US mass-made yo-yo; very rare, $300–1,500+ (general knowledge, verify).
- **Cheerio** (Canada/US 1940s–60s, Coca-Cola and Pepsi promotional demonstrators): wooden "Cheerio 77", "Cheerio Tournament", jeweled; soda-logo Cheerio/Duncan promos $40–150.
- **Royal** (Royal Tops Mfg, NY, 1930s–80s): wooden "Royal Tournament", "Royal Professional".
- **Hummingbird, Hi-Ker, Goody, Festival (Festival Toys), Alox, Whirl King, Chico** – mid-century wood/plastic, mostly $10–40.
- **Tom Kuhn** (1980s–90s, "No Jive 3-in-1", "SB-2" wooden/aluminium) – $30–120.
- **Yomega** (1993–): Brain, X-Brain, Fireball, Firestorm/Xcel, Raider, Hyper Raider (Bandai), Saturn, Ultra Glow. Mass-market; $2–8 loose.
- **Playmaxx / ProYo** (Playmaxx ProYo II and III with "Roller axle", 1990s) – $5–20.
- **Boutique**: Spyy, CLYW (Caribou Lodge), One Drop, YoYoFactory, YoYoJam (Hspin), Henrys (Germany), Sakura, What Did You Expect – metal throws $30–300 sold.
- **Advertising/promo yo-yos**: Coca-Cola, Pepsi, 7-Up, Kool-Aid; Duncan "Coca-Cola" jeweled wood $50–200.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old wood: dull, worn foil stamp, wood glows amber, hand-painted stripes, axle often a wooden dowel; two-piece glued halves. Reproductions (Duncan "Throwback", 1990s Duncan wood re-issues) are bright with crisp foil and modern copyright lines on card.
- Plastic Imperial dating: older (1950s–60s) have a solid metal axle pin visible as a dot at centre and heavier plastic; 1970s+ "Flambeau" inside.
- Yomega dating: Hyper Raider "Yomega-Bandai 1998 Made in USA"; Yomega products moulded "Fall River MA".
- Quality tell for boutique: one-piece metal, weight 60–70 g, rim-weighted, serial/engraving.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing or swapped strings (minor), cracked plastic halves, missing caps/stickers on Yomegas, scratched clear plastic, faded foil on wood, chipped wood edges, warped halves (wobble). Original string and original card/box add strongly. Loose 1990s plastic is "player grade" – collectors do not pay for it.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Duncan reissued wooden "Throwback"/"Retro" Butterflies and Tournaments (2000s–2010s) – look for modern packaging or a © date. Chinese counterfeits of One Drop/YoYoFactory exist (cheap anodising, no engraving depth). Soda-brand "Coca-Cola" yo-yos were reissued in the 1990s.

## Market
Large supply of 1990s plastic (millions sold in the 1998–99 craze); slow, cheap market for loose lots, a few lots per day on eBay at $15–40. Wooden pre-1960 and boutique metal sell steadily to collectors. Yo-yo forums (YoYoExpert, r/Throwers BST) are better than eBay for boutique metal.

## What sellers usually miss
- A single 1930s–50s wooden Duncan/Cheerio with jewels or gold-foil "Tournament" in a junk drawer lot is the money piece; owners lump it with plastic.
- Old store display cards/boxes (dozen carded Imperials) are worth far more than loose.
- Boutique metal throws look like "a metal yo-yo" to non-throwers – CLYW/One Drop can be $60–150.

## Shipping / handling
Tiny and robust. A 10-lot ships in a small flat-rate or ground-advantage box, 1–2 lb, $8–12. Wooden singles in a padded mailer $5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + model + material + era: "Duncan Tournament wood 1950s", "Cheerio 77 jeweled", "Duncan Imperial sealed card 1960s", "CLYW [model]"; lots: "Yomega Duncan yo-yo lot 10 1990s"; Junk: "toy", "spinning", "vintage" on 1990s plastic. Example: `1950s Duncan Tournament Wooden Yo-Yo Gold Foil Seal Original String Vintage`.
- **Where:** eBay for wood singles and 1990s nostalgia lots; YoYoExpert / r/Throwers BST (no fee) beats eBay for boutique metal (CLYW, One Drop, YoYoFactory).
- **Price position:** at the sold median (1990s plastic $15–40 per 10-lot, median collection $43; carded 1950s–60s Imperial/Butterfly $10–40; wood $15–60; jeweled/soda promo $40–200; boutique metal $30–200), accept −15%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo both halves side by side; then the logo/foil seal macro, axle gap, edges (chips), string, card/box front and back, a spin video for metal, lot laid out in a grid.
- **State:** maker, model, era for each piece, wood/plastic/metal, strings original, cracks/wobble/chips by photo number, card/box, loose 1990s plastic sold as player grade, reissue check (Throwback/Retro © date).
- **Pack / haul:** tiny and robust; wood singles in a padded mailer ($5), a 10-lot in a small box 1–2 lb ($8–12).
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Duncan Tournament], wood with the [gold foil] seal still legible — the pre-Flambeau 1950s one, not the 2000s Throwback reissue; legible-logo wood runs [$15–60], jeweled and soda promos more."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$10 shipping to buyer − ~$10 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A 1990s plastic lot (resale $25–40) never clears it at any realistic asking price – pass. Buy only when a photo shows pre-1965 wood with legible foil logo/jewels, sealed vintage carded yo-yos, or identifiable boutique metal, and the total price is ≤ 25–30% of the summed ladder value. Fits passenger seat always. No authentication needed.
