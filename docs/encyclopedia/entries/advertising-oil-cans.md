## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tin cans (1 qt, 1 gal, 5 qt, 5/10/25 lb grease pails) with lithographed brand graphics; porcelain-enamel or tin signs; glass-globe gas pump tops. Tells:
- **Graphics with an image** (Indian head – Archer, Mohawk, Red Indian; Sinclair dinosaur; Polly Gas parrot; Flying A; Frontier buffalo; Musgo; Harbor Petroleum) are worth 3–10x plain-text cans (Pennzoil, Quaker State, Havoline, Valvoline, Texaco – $15–60).
- **Archer Lubricants (Omaha, NE)**: Indian archer graphic; quart cans $40–120, 5-lb/10-lb/25-lb grease pails $100–300 depending on graphic version and condition; paper-label versions and salesman sample kits $100–250.
- **Condition grades**: rust, dents, fading knock 50–80% off; "full" or "NOS" adds a little; 25 lb pails are shipping-expensive.
- **Signs**: porcelain originals (Shell shield, Texaco, Mobil Pegasus, Sinclair) $500–5,000+ – thick, heavy, chipped edges show black steel under glass-like enamel; **plastic/lexan 1970s–2000s gas station signs** (like a 5 ft Shell plastic panel) $50–200 local only; **"metal reproduction advertising"** (thin, printed, Chinese) $5–20 regardless of the $150 ask; tin tacker/embossed originals $100–800.
- **Globes**: 1-piece glass $1,000+, gill-body/plastic-body 1950s $300–800, reproductions $50–100.
See also the `advertising-signs` entry for non-petroleum signs.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Image brands (premium)**: Archer (Omaha), Mohawk, Red Indian, Sinclair (dinosaur), Polly Gas (parrot), Flying A, Frontier (buffalo), Musgo, Harbor Petroleum, Marathon (runner).
- **Plain-text brands (common)**: Pennzoil, Quaker State, Havoline, Valvoline, Texaco – $15–60.
- **Formats**: quart, gallon, 5-qt, half-gallon and "rocker" cans (rare formats bring more), 5/10/25 lb grease pails, salesman sample kits.
- **Small advertising tins** (e.g. 1926 Nokorode soldering paste, $10) and STP oil-treatment cans are low tier.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Lithographed tin (printed directly on metal) on originals vs paper labels on some early/variant cans. Porcelain = fused glass on steel, chips show black steel with a shelf edge; tin signs scratch to silver; reproductions are thin, uniformly printed, often with artificial "rust" printed on and fresh-looking holes (general knowledge, verify). Seams: older quart cans have soldered seams; later ones are crimped/rolled (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust, dents and fading knock 50–80% off. Repainting/touch-up of graphics and clear-coating are common and reduce value (general knowledge, verify). For signs, rust-through and added holes hurt; porcelain chips at mounting holes are expected and acceptable. Ask for close-ups of graphics, seams, and the bottom.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction tin signs are everywhere ("metal reproduction advertising", thin, Chinese) – $5–20. Reproduction globes $50–100. Fake porcelain signs (often from India) exist with artificially aged chips (general knowledge, verify): look for uniform, too-perfect color, thin steel, and chips without the layered shelf. Never buy repro.

## Market
Strong collector base (petroliana, "man cave" decorators); regular sales on eBay; top pieces go through Morphy and Richmond Auctions. Graphic cans sell readily; plain cans are slow (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
"Old oil can $10" with an Indian/animal graphic; grease pails used as garage bins. Porcelain vs tin vs plastic signs – sellers say "metal sign" for all three. Reproduction sellers inflate ("$150 metal reproduction advertising") – never buy repro.

## Shipping / handling
Quart cans 1 lb ($10); 25 lb pails 5–8 lb empty, oversize ($25–40 each way); signs over 24 in are freight. Sell graphic cans on eBay (13%); big signs/globes through petroliana auctions (Morphy, Richmond).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** brand + "motor oil can"/"grease pail" + size (qt/gal/5 lb/25 lb) + graphic ("Indian", "dinosaur", "parrot") + city + "petroliana" + "gas station"; Junk: "old can", "metal sign" (say porcelain/tin), "reproduction" (never sell repro). Example: `Archer Lubricants 25 lb Grease Pail Indian Graphic Omaha Petroliana Gas Station Can`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for graphic cans and pump plates (petroliana and man-cave buyers); big porcelain signs and globes to Morphy/Richmond; plain-text cans local or lot. eBay for cans; auction beats it only at the top.
- **Price position:** at the sold median (graphic quart cans $50–150; Archer/Mohawk/Polly pails and pump plates $150–600, pump plate median $67; plain-text $15–60), accept −15%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo the main graphic panel square-on; then all sides, seams, top and lid, base, inside if open, rust/dents at an angle, any touch-up under raking light, ruler for size.
- **State:** brand, size, city of the oil company if printed, full/empty, graphic condition grade, rust/dents/fading by photo number, touch-up or clear-coat stated, repro check.
- **Pack / haul:** quart cans 1 lb, small box ($10); 25 lb pails 5–8 lb empty, oversize, lid taped and contents removed ($25–40); signs over 24" are freight/local.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Archer] [25 lb] pail with the Indian graphic, [Omaha] on the label — the image brand collectors chase; graphic [85%], no touch-up, empty and clean; these sell [$150–300] against [$15–60] for Pennzoil."

## Buy rule
Buy when: graphic quart/5-lb cans under $30; Archer/Mohawk/Polly pails under $60; porcelain sign under $150 if the photo shows enamel chips to steel (not paint). Pass when: plain-text cans over $15, any reproduction, plastic signs, pails over $100 unless mint. Signs or globes bigger than a passenger seat are excluded by the size rule. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
