## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1978–1990 table and floor lamps: black or brass metal with frosted glass discs, cones or fins; halogen bulb under a dome with a dimmer. Key designs:
- **Robert Sonneman for George Kovacs "Saturn"**: stacked frosted-glass rings/dome on two thin black columns, round black marble or metal base, about 22–23" tall.
- Sonneman **"Flying Saucer"** floor lamp.
- **Koch & Lowy** brass and glass desk lamps.
- **Memphis**-style Ettore Sottsass or Michele De Lucchi lamps (Artemide) are a tier above.

Sellers write "space-age lamp", "80s modern lamp", "UFO lamp" and never the designer. Look for a Kovacs/Sonneman sticker or foil label under the base. Look-alikes: 1990s big-box halogen torchieres and Asian-made copies of the Saturn form, which have plastic diffusers and lightweight bases (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **George Kovacs Lighting (NYC)**: the main distributor of Sonneman designs and other postmodern lamps from the 1970s on; paper/foil label under the base (general knowledge, verify). Saturn is the key model: $250–450 eBay sold.
- **Robert Sonneman**: designer; Sonneman-signed pieces and floor lamps (Flying Saucer) sit in the $250–500 band. His later company, Sonneman Lighting, still makes lamps, so check the period (general knowledge, verify).
- **Koch & Lowy (NYC)**: brass and glass desk and floor lamps, $80–200.
- **Artemide / Memphis (Sottsass "Tahiti", De Lucchi "Oceanic")**: $800–3,000. Artemide-made lamps carry Artemide labels or moulded marks. Memphis designs were reissued, so check the date (general knowledge, verify).
- Unmarked Kovacs-style tiered lamps: $80–200 if the form is right but there is no label.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Period pieces have a heavy marble or cast-metal base (10–15 lb on the Saturn), real frosted glass rings (cold, heavy, with ground edges), an enamelled steel column and a rotary or slide dimmer on the cord or base.
- Cheap copies have plastic or acrylic diffusers, a light stamped base, and a UL sticker from a 1990s importer (general knowledge, verify).
- An original label under the base is the best tell. Old halogen sockets (bi-pin, often 12V transformer or 120V T3) are period-correct (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common damage: chips to the glass rings, a missing dimmer knob, cracks in the marble base. Chipped or missing rings are the value killer, because replacement glass is hard to find. Halogen fixtures can be rewired and buyers accept "works". Ask for a photo lit up, close-ups of every ring edge and of the base underside/label. Ask whether all rings are present (count them against reference photos).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Saturn-style knock-offs and current Sonneman Lighting production exist. Memphis/Artemide designs have been reissued and later issues are worth less than period pieces (general knowledge, verify). A missing label is not proof of a copy, but it pushes the lamp down to the "unmarked Kovacs" tier.

## Market
Bought by decorators and mid-century/postmodern collectors. The 1980s postmodern look has been in demand since the late 2010s (general knowledge, verify). It sells steadily but not fast on eBay. 1stDibs and Chairish get higher prices but sell slowly. Local pickup avoids freight risk. No real seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
The designer attribution. Also that halogen fixtures can be rewired, so buyers accept "works". Condition tells: chips to glass rings, missing dimmer knob, marble base cracks.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy (marble base 10–15 lb) and glass: $40–60 ground, double-boxed, rings wrapped separately. eBay 13%. High risk of breakage, so ask the seller to ship rings and base in separate boxes.
