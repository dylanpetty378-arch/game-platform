## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Fish decoys: 5–12" carved wood fish (some to 14–17"), weighted belly (poured lead), metal fins, line tie on the back. They are lowered through a hole in the ice and "swum" on a jigging stick to draw pike/muskie within spearing range. Used in MN/WI/MI/ND, upstate New York and southern Canada; the practice is Native American in origin (a 1762 fur-trader journal near Sault Ste. Marie describes lead-filled wooden decoys used with a barbed spear) (Wikipedia).
- Old ones (1900–50s) have worn paint, hand-cut tin or copper fins, folk-art character; named carvers (Oscar Peterson, Hans Janner, Bud Stewart, Lake Chautauqua) bring big money. New ones by living carvers are decor ($15–40).
- History that dates pieces: Michigan outlawed ice spearing c.1910 (except some Native use); when the ban ended around the start of the Depression, spearing boomed and factories (e.g. South Bend) made mass-produced decoys, now also collected (fishdecoy.com history).
- Species seen: trout, perch, sucker, pike, bass, shad, bluegill/sunfish, walleye, muskie, plus frogs, polliwogs and minnows. Odd species (frog, polliwog, sucker, shad) and large sizes are worth more than a plain generic "fish".
- "Floaters" are unweighted and held down by a separate weight; decoys with hooks ("cheaters") are banned in Minnesota but legal in Michigan (Wikipedia).
- Duck decoys: Mason Factory (glass eyes, premier grade), Ward Bros, Crowell = auction grade; 1950s–70s Herter's, Victor, Animal Trap (Pascagoula) cork/papier-mâché = $20–60.
- Sellers write "fish decoy", "wooden fish", "old duck decoy". Look-alikes: decorative wall fish (no belly weight, no line tie, hanging hook), fishing lures (have hook hardware/lips), and modern "rustic" carvings with faux crackle.
For duck decoys see the fuller `duck-decoys` entry; its tiers differ (it puts Victor/Animal Trap at $40–300).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Oscar Peterson** (1894–1951, Cadillac MI; wilderness guide; carved ~50 years): top tier fish decoys, $2,000–6,000 for a trout at Guyette & Deeter. Estimated output 10,000–15,000 decoys, about 10% surviving (fishdecoy.com). Hallmark "Cadillac style": streamlined but abstract bodies, curved, lively shapes. Periods (Wikipedia): I 1900–1919 (most primitive, rarest); II 1920–24 (more defined carving and paint); III 1925–34 (most creative: bold colours, blending and feathering of paint, detailed carving); IV 1935–44 (simpler, mainly spearing decoys); V 1945–51 (almost all spearing decoys, simpler paint). Eyes vary by species: inset glass taxidermy eyes (brown/brook trout), brass carpet-tack or upholstery-tack eyes (polliwog, pike), carved/painted eyes (sucker, rainbow, perch), nailhead eyes; metal fins. Record c.1930 brown trout $18,700 (Sotheby's 1991); a plaque reportedly $100,000 (Maine Antique Digest 2010). Widely copied and counterfeited, and living carvers make "honest copies".
- **Hans Janner Sr.** (1880–1963, Mt. Clemens MI, used on Lake St. Clair): his son estimated no more than ~150 survive. Guyette & Deeter: 17"+ fish with carved mouth and gills, four weights, tri-colour dot painting $168,000 (world record fish decoy); 15" walleye $84,000; 12" rock bass $69,000. Inset glass eyes on a c.1930 bass. **Augie Janner** (Mt. Clemens, c.1940) used sign-reflector eyes.
- **Other Mt. Clemens / Lake St. Clair carvers**: Abraham DeHate (brass tack eyes), Yock Meldrum (charred-wood and painted shad), Larry Peltier, Ted Vandenbossche (16" muskie with copper fins, tack eyes and inserted metal teeth $51,000), Gordon Charbeneau, Andy Trombley, Gordon "Pecore" Fox, Tom Schroeder (Wikipedia; Antiques and the Arts).
- **Bud Stewart** (Michigan): collectible-to-top (general knowledge on locations, verify). Terapeak shows typical eBay sales ~$68–233.
- **Lake Chautauqua NY** decoys and **Harry Seymour** (NY, c.1890, painted trout/sunfish with metal fins, painted leather tail, tack eyes; $15,600 at G&D): collectible.
- **Factory decoys** (Depression era, e.g. South Bend 6" with glass-bead eyes): collected, mid-range (fishdecoy.com).
- **Anonymous 1930s–60s folk fish** (MN/WI/MI): decent, $50–150. An unattributed 12" perch made $44,400 at G&D against a $6,000 high estimate, so quality anonymous pieces can surprise.
- **Contemporary carvers / "Made in North Dakota" hobby pieces**: decor, $15–40.
- **Bru-ell** spearing decoys: seen 2026-10-13 via Lens, asking-price median $85; maker age and status not yet researched (verify).
- **Duck decoys**: Mason (standard grade collectible, premier top), Crowell, Ward Bros (top); Herter's, Victor, Animal Trap (Pascagoula) cork/papier-mâché $20–60.

### Quick ID checklist
- Is there a poured lead weight in the belly and a line-tie (screw eye, staple or wire) on the back? No weight + hanging hook = wall decor.
- Fins hand-cut from tin, copper or sheet brass and nailed/slotted in (old) vs identical stamped fins (factory/modern).
- Eyes: tacks, nailheads, inset glass or sign reflectors suggest old; plastic doll eyes or perfectly round painted dots suggest new.
- Paint: genuine crazing, flaking down to wood and wear on the high points and around the line tie vs uniform "crackle" or sanded-through edges.
- Underside: grime and oxidised lead vs clean bright lead or new wood.
- Shape: Peterson "Cadillac style" streamlined/curved body; Mt. Clemens pieces with carved gills, multiple weights, dot painting.
- Species and size: frogs, polliwogs, suckers, muskie, large (12"+) pieces are the valuable outliers.
- Any signature or "made in …" stamp: modern hobby carvers sign and date; most old working decoys are unsigned.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-carved softwood (pine, cedar, basswood; also walnut and charred wood on some Mt. Clemens/Native pieces), hand-cut tin or copper fins nailed in, poured lead belly weights, tack, nailhead or glass eyes, crazed/worn paint, line-tie screw eye or staple on the back. Leather tails on some 19th-c. NY pieces. New: smooth bandsawn bodies, bright acrylic paint, factory fins, clean undersides (general knowledge, verify). Quality tells on old pieces: balance (it was made to swim level), deliberate carved gills and mouth, layered paint with blending/feathering (Peterson period III), dot or scale painting, glitter or sparkle on some Depression-era pieces (general knowledge, verify). Wear on a working decoy concentrates at the line tie, nose and fin edges; faked wear is even all over.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing fins, chipped paint, repainted bodies, replaced line ties. Original paint drives value as with duck decoys (general knowledge, verify). Replaced fins cut value but not fatally (the $44,400 perch had probable replaced front fins). Ask for: belly photo, close-ups of fin slots (fresh wood or new nails = replaced), raking-light photo of paint (overpaint sits glossy over cracks), and whether any weight was added or removed.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Artificially aged "antique" fish decoys and carvings in the style of Peterson are common; real wear vs faux distressing is the key judgment (general knowledge, verify). Peterson is documented as "widely copied and counterfeited", and contemporary carvers make "honest copies" that later lose their signature or label (Wikipedia; fishdecoy.com). Attributions need an auction house (Guyette & Deeter, Copley) or an experienced collector.

## Market
Folk-art and fishing-collectible buyers on eBay; strongest interest in the Upper Midwest; winter ice-fishing season may help (general knowledge, verify). Named pieces go to Guyette & Deeter. Michigan (Mt. Clemens, Cadillac) material is the deepest-pocketed segment.

## What sellers usually miss
Age (real wear vs faux), carver attribution, lead-weighted bellies; "Made in North Dakota" by a local hobby carver is modern. Unusual species (frog, polliwog, sucker), carved gills, multiple weights and tack/glass eyes are tells sellers rarely mention.

## Shipping / handling
<2 lb, $8–10 each way. eBay to folk-art buyers. Wrap fins so they don't bend.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State length, species, wood, fin material, eye type, belly weight, paint originality, region/carver if known. Photograph both sides, the belly, the back line tie and any signature. Title: "Vintage folk art fish spearing decoy, [length]in, tin fins, lead belly, [region]".

### What to tell the buyer
"Hand-carved wooden fish spearing decoy, [length]", [species], with [hand-cut tin/copper] fins, poured lead belly weight and original line tie. Eyes are [tack/glass/painted]. Paint shows honest age wear and crazing consistent with use through the ice; no repaint seen [or note any touch-up]. Fish decoys were swum on a jig stick through the ice to draw pike and muskie for spearing, a tradition in Michigan, Minnesota, Wisconsin and upstate New York. Attribution: [unattributed / style of …]; not represented as a named carver unless documented." Never write "Oscar Peterson" for an unpapered piece; say "Cadillac style" only if it fits.

## Buy rule
Buy when: visibly old (crazed paint, tin fins, lead belly) fish decoy ≤ $30, or any decoy a seller attributes to Peterson/Stewart/Mason ≤ $100. Pass when: new carvings sold as decor. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
