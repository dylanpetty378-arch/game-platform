## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Fish decoys: 5–12" carved wood fish, weighted belly (lead), metal fins, line tie on the back — used through the ice in MN/WI/MI/ND. Old ones (1900–50s) have worn paint, hand-cut tin fins, folk-art character; named carvers (Oscar Peterson, Hans Janner, Bud Stewart, Lake Chautauqua) bring big money. New ones by living carvers are decor ($15–40). Duck decoys: Mason Factory (glass eyes, premier grade), Ward Bros, Crowell = auction grade; 1950s–70s Herter's, Victor, Animal Trap (Pascagoula) cork/papier-mâché = $20–60. Sellers write "fish decoy", "wooden fish", "old duck decoy".
For duck decoys see the fuller `duck-decoys` entry; its tiers differ (it puts Victor/Animal Trap at $40–300).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Oscar Peterson** (Cadillac MI): top tier fish decoys, $2,000–6,000 for a trout at Guyette & Deeter.
- **Hans Janner** (Mt. Clemens MI) and **Bud Stewart** (Michigan): collectible-to-top (general knowledge on locations, verify).
- **Lake Chautauqua NY** decoys: collectible.
- **Anonymous 1930s–60s folk fish** (MN/WI/MI): decent, $50–150.
- **Contemporary carvers / "Made in North Dakota" hobby pieces**: decor, $15–40.
- **Bru-ell** spearing decoys: seen 2026-10-13 via Lens, asking-price median $85; maker age and status not yet researched (verify).
- **Duck decoys**: Mason (standard grade collectible, premier top), Crowell, Ward Bros (top); Herter's, Victor, Animal Trap (Pascagoula) cork/papier-mâché $20–60.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-carved softwood, hand-cut tin or copper fins nailed in, poured lead belly weights, tack or glass eyes, crazed/worn paint, line-tie screw eye or staple on the back. New: smooth bandsawn bodies, bright acrylic paint, factory fins, clean undersides (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing fins, chipped paint, repainted bodies, replaced line ties. Original paint drives value as with duck decoys (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Artificially aged "antique" fish decoys and carvings in the style of Peterson are common; real wear vs faux distressing is the key judgment (general knowledge, verify). Attributions need an auction house.

## Market
Folk-art and fishing-collectible buyers on eBay; strongest interest in the Upper Midwest; winter ice-fishing season may help (general knowledge, verify). Named pieces go to Guyette & Deeter.

## What sellers usually miss
Age (real wear vs faux), carver attribution, lead-weighted bellies; "Made in North Dakota" by a local hobby carver is modern.

## Shipping / handling
<2 lb, $8–10 each way. eBay to folk-art buyers.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State length, species, wood, fin material, eye type, belly weight, paint originality, region/carver if known. Photograph both sides, the belly, the back line tie and any signature. Title: "Vintage folk art fish spearing decoy, [length]in, tin fins, lead belly, [region]".

## Buy rule
Buy when: visibly old (crazed paint, tin fins, lead belly) fish decoy ≤ $30, or any decoy a seller attributes to Peterson/Stewart/Mason ≤ $100. Pass when: new carvings sold as decor. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
