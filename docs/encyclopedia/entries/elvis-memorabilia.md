## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Everything from 1950s fan merchandise to the endless memorial items after his 1977 death. The valuable core is the 1956 Elvis Presley Enterprises (EPE) licensed line (marked "© 1956 Elvis Presley Enterprises"), original concert programs, tickets and early RCA records. Life-size statues are 1990s-2010s resin/fiberglass decor.

By late 1956 hundreds of Elvis items were sold in department stores, drugstores and music shops: bubble-gum cards, pin-back buttons, handkerchiefs, dolls, guitars, billfolds, photo albums (Mountain States Collector), plus pillows ("Love Me Tender", "Personally Yours, Elvis Presley"), charm bracelets, jackets and shoes (Rockhurst lots). Items marked "Elvis Presley Enterprises" with a 1956 or 1957 copyright date are the desirable ones.

Dating by the trademark line:
- **"© 1956/1957 Elvis Presley Enterprises"** (or "EPE"): the original fan merchandise.
- **"Boxcar" (Boxcar Enterprises) trademark**: 1974–1981 (Mountain States Collector) — 1970s concert-era souvenirs.
- **"Elvis Presley Enterprises, Inc." with later dates / Graceland**: the EPE name reverted to Graceland in 1982 — anything with a later EPE date is post-death licensed product. Modern goods also show Authentic Brands Group (ABG) licensing.

Records: Sun 45s and 78s (1954–55, five releases) are the top tier; RCA reissues and later RCA albums are mostly cheap except picture sleeves and complete first pressings.

## Makers, brands and variants
EPE 1956 licensees (Teen-Age Lipstick, charm bracelets, scarves, Magnolia shoes, record cases), McCormick Distilling decanters (1978-80s, music boxes), Bradford Exchange plates, Hallmark ornaments, Vandor, Franklin Mint, Graceland souvenirs, life-size statues by various decor makers.

Detail by line:
- **1956–57 EPE merchandise**: pillows (13 × 13", screen-printed; four sold together for $2,875 in Jan 2026, Rockhurst), charm bracelets (gold-tone/silver-tone with photo-frame, guitar, hound dog and "Heartbreak" heart charms; on original card is the premium version), jackets (Rich Mart), shoes in original box, lipstick, scarves, wallets/billfolds, pin-backs, photo albums. Original card/box multiplies value.
- **Sun Records (Memphis, 1954–55)**: original 45s $1,000 to $5,000–6,000; 78s $500–3,000. Originals are black vinyl only — coloured or swirled vinyl Sun = reproduction; genuine copies of the first four releases have three "push mark" circles pressed into the label; Sun issued no picture sleeves (Mountain States Collector).
- **RCA**: Sun recordings reissued on RCA (1955) $30–60 per 45, $75–150 per 78 (VG+ to NM, Warman's via MSC). Picture sleeves can be worth more than the record; albums need included posters/postcards intact with no tack holes. Most common later LPs sell for a few dollars.
- **McCormick Distilling decanters**: the "Portrait of Elvis" series — five hand-painted Japanese porcelain figurals on music-box bases, made 1977–1980, filled with Kentucky bourbon; first in series plays "Love Me Tender", inscribed "Sincerely Elvis '77", ~14½" tall. Kovels values $5–35 depending on condition, sealed contents and box. Other McCormick Elvis decanters (e.g. "Elvis '55", "Aloha", minis) exist into the early 1980s (general knowledge, verify).
- **Ephemera**: 1956 show tickets (~$80, Schroeder's 2007; replicas exist), Jailhouse Rock lobby cards ($75; replicas), movie posters several hundred and up (beware reprints), TV Guide Sept 8–14 1956 (~$500 pristine), early pin-backs $100+, later ones $20–30, magazines from ~$10.
- **Post-1977 collectibles**: Bradford Exchange plates, Hallmark ornaments, Franklin Mint, Vandor, Graceland souvenirs — mass produced.
- **Statues**: life-size resin/fiberglass decor figures, local only.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the copyright line: "© 1956" or "1957 Elvis Presley Enterprises" = original line; Boxcar = 1974–81; later EPE Inc./ABG = modern.
- For records: label (Sun vs RCA), black vinyl only for Sun, three push-mark circles on early Sun labels, no Sun picture sleeves.
- Original card, box or tag present (charm bracelets on card, pillows with tag, shoes in box)?
- Printing: 1950s screen printing/lithography with slightly soft registration vs crisp modern digital print.
- Decanters: McCormick name on base, music box works, sealed or empty, box present.
- Autograph shown? Assume unauthenticated; pass unless PSA/JSA/Beckett letter.
- Tickets/posters: paper stock, size and printing method — replicas are common.
- Size: anything statue-size is out (passenger-seat rule).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
1956 items: period printing, EPE copyright line, aged materials. Modern: bright printing, "Elvis Presley Enterprises Inc." with later dates, ABG (Authentic Brands Group) licensing.
Also: 1950s jewellery is light plated base metal on period findings (spring-ring clasps); fabrics are cotton/rayon with screen printing that crazes and fades; paper items show age toning consistent across the sheet. Modern reproductions use bright white paper, glossy stock and digital dots.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paper items: tears, tape, fading. Statues: chipped paint, broken guitar necks/fingers. Decanters: music box works may be dead.
Also: staining and discoloration on fabric items (the Rockhurst pillows sold below estimate partly with staining noted), missing charms on bracelets, tack holes and pen marks on album inserts, seam splits on records' sleeves, chipped porcelain on decanters.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Forged autographs are endemic - requires PSA/JSA/Beckett, outside Dylan's rules. Reproduction concert posters and tickets common.
Also: counterfeit Sun records (coloured vinyl, or fakes made with genuine leftover Sun labels — MSC), replica lobby cards, reprinted movie posters, and modern "1956-style" charm bracelets and pillows. Signed sleeves and photos are the most-faked items.

## Market
Aging collector base; souvenirs glut. 1956 EPE remains strong. Auction houses (Rockhurst's annual "Auction on Elvis Presley Blvd", January) set the top of the market; eBay is where most mid-tier items move. Interest spikes around his birthday (Jan 8) and death anniversary (Aug 16).

## What sellers usually miss
"Old Elvis stuff" boxes with EPE 1956 merchandise mixed with souvenirs. Also: original cards/boxes thrown in loose, picture sleeves worth more than the record, Sun records sitting among RCA reissues.

## Shipping / handling
Small paper/merch cheap. Statues: freight $300+. Decanters: porcelain, double-box ($15–20). Records: proper record mailer.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Copyright line, date, condition; never sell autographs without authentication.

### What to tell the buyer
Quote the copyright/trademark line exactly as printed (e.g. "© 1956 Elvis Presley Enterprises") and photograph it. Give the item type, maker if named, dimensions, and whether original card, box or tag is present. For records, give label, catalogue number, vinyl colour and grade sleeve and disc separately. For McCormick decanters, say whether the music box plays and which song, whether sealed, and whether the box is present. Describe stains, fading, missing charms or chips plainly. Do not use "autographed" or "concert-worn" without third-party authentication. Keywords: Elvis Presley, 1956, EPE, Elvis Presley Enterprises, original, vintage, rock and roll, memorabilia.

## Buy rule
Statues fail the size rule. Autographs require professional authentication - pass. Buy only EPE-1956-marked items at ≤ 1/3 of comps. McCormick decanters (Kovels $5–35) never meet the profit rule.
