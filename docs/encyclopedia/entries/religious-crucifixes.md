## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A cross with corpus (figure of Christ). The **skull and crossbones** at the foot (Adam's skull, Golgotha) and a halo/rays behind the head are typical of French and German devotional crucifixes c. 1880–1930, worn by nuns/priests or on large rosaries. Example 7962665199: ~3" nickel-silver (German silver/white metal) frame with black wood (ebony or ebonized pearwood) inlay, cast corpus with sunburst halo, INRI scroll knob above, skull & crossbones below, suspension ring.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **French "Croix de la Bonne Mort"** / nun's crosses: often marked "FRANCE", sometimes with Stanhope viewer lens in the center (adds $30–80).
- **German/Austrian** nickel-and-ebony pectorals, some marked "Germany" or "Made in Germany" (post-1891).
- **Italian/Vatican** sterling "800" or "925" marks; **Irish penal crosses** (carved wood, 18th c.).
- **Sick-call crucifix boxes** (wall crucifix that opens to hold candles and holy water) $40–120.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Silver hallmarks (French Minerva head, "925", "800", "Sterling") greatly raise value. German silver/nickel is brassy-grey where worn. Real ebony has fine dense grain; ebonized wood shows lighter wood at chips. Quality corpus has detailed face and separate attachment rivets.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing INRI plaque, loose corpus, lost inlay, bent ring, heavy wear on high points.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Italian/Chinese reproductions of skull crucifixes sold as "antique"; tell: bright plating, glued (not riveted) corpus, plastic inlay, uniform casting.

## Market
Steady religious-collectibles buyers on eBay/Etsy; memento mori pieces have extra gothic-decor demand.

## What sellers usually miss
Stanhope lenses (tiny peephole in the crossing showing Lord's Prayer/Lourdes), relic compartments with wax seals, and small silver hallmarks.
