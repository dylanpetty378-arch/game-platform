## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small metal stompboxes with 1–4 knobs and a footswitch. The maker and model name are printed on the face – always legible in the photo; the serial and "Made in Japan"/"Taiwan" are on the bottom/back plate. Value is brand + model + era, and Reverb is the price book. Sellers' words: "distortion pedal", "guitar pedal", "stomp box", usually with the model name in the title (so the seller knows). Craigslist is full of musicians pricing at Reverb; the arbitrage is in "box of pedals" lots, estate/"box of music stuff" lots and mis-titled vintage units where the seller doesn't read the serial.
- **Boss**: rectangular compact, one colour per model, model code printed under the footswitch (DD-3, PS-3, CS-2, DS-1, MT-2, OD-1, CE-2, SD-1). Vintage = "Made in Japan" on the back + **black label** + silver thumbscrew (1977–~1988 per one write-up; "pre-1990 = Japan, 1990+ Taiwan" per another). Taiwan pink/blue label 1990s+ is common.
- **MXR**: smaller plain block box; **script logo** (no LED, 1974–81) = vintage vs **block logo** later.
- **Ibanez Tube Screamer**: green; TS-808 narrow box (1979–81); TS9 original (1982–84, Japan, "R" logo, JRC4558D chip inside).
- **Pro Co RAT**: "whiteface"/big box (LM308 chip) = vintage.
- **Electro-Harmonix Big Muff**: 1970s big-box Ram's Head/Triangle vs Russian Sovtek vs modern nano.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Die-cast aluminium enclosures; vintage era tells are labels, logos, chips and country of origin: Boss black label + silver screw + "Made in Japan"; MXR script logo, no LED; TS9/TS-808 with JRC4558D chip; RAT with LM308 chip; EHX big-box. These add $100–500 and are invisible in a Craigslist title. Modern mainstream pedals lose ~30–40% of new price and sit there (sell at 50–70% of retail).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Value is driven more by model + era than cosmetic condition. Risks: dead pedal, scratchy pots, failing footswitch, battery corrosion, replaced chips/mods on vintage units (lower collector value), missing bottom plate or knobs (general knowledge for mods, verify). Analog Boss/MXR nearly always work, but Dylan's rule: untested electronics = pass unless the seller says it works – ask for a "powered on" photo (signal LED with a battery; no amp needed).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reissues are the main trap: TS9 reissue vs 1982 Japan original; Boss Waza Craft vs MIJ originals (CE-2 vs CE-2W, DM-2 vs DM-2W); MXR script reissues; Klon clones ($50–300) and fake Centaurs (general knowledge on fakes, verify). Check serial, label colour, country and chip.

## Market
Very liquid on Reverb (the price book) and eBay; most sellers are musicians who price at Reverb, so single named pedals rarely have spread. Current-production pedals are retail goods with thin margins. Vintage MIJ and script-era units are steady collector items. No strong seasonality (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Almost nothing on named modern pedals – pedal sellers are musicians and price at Reverb. Arbitrage only in estate/"box of music stuff"/"box of guitar pedals $1 / make offer"/"pedal board, make offer" lots and in vintage units where the seller doesn't read the serial. Era tells: black label / silver screw / "Made in Japan" on Boss; black-label DS-1; script vs block logo on MXR; narrow box on Tube Screamers; TS9 with JRC4558D chip; "Rat" with LM308 chip; DM-2 vs DM-3; CE-2 vs CE-2W reissue. "TS9" could be a $70 reissue or a $300 1982 Japan unit. "Boss SD-1" with a black label = MIJ 1981–88 = $100–150 vs $40 current. "Ibanez overdrive" without TS-808 in the title; Roland/Boss "echo" units. Sellers list "Boss chorus pedal $40" without the label photo – ask for the back. Lots frequently hide one MIJ pedal worth the lot: a $120 lot with a PS-3 inside clears. Pedal pricing on Craigslist is often retail-ish; most named pedals are passes.

## Shipping / handling
< 1–2 lb each, $6–12 (First Class / Ground Advantage). Reverb (~8% + ~3% payment processing) is the best market for anything over $100; eBay 13%. Check "works" in the body; electronics rule.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model, country of manufacture, label/logo era, serial, chip (for TS/RAT), tested working, power supply/battery, box/manual, any mods. Photograph the face, the bottom plate (serial, "Made in Japan", label), the inside (chip) for vintage units, and a powered-on LED. Title: "Boss CE-2 Chorus MIJ black label 1980s, tested" / "MXR Carbon Copy M169 analog delay, works".

## Buy rule
Buy when (any of):
- a $150+ pedal is under 40% of Reverb sold, or any top-tier pedal under 40% of its sold range;
- vintage MIJ Ibanez/Boss or Fulltone/Strymon/Eventide at ≤ 40% of Reverb sold; any Boss black-label MIJ under $60; a vintage MIJ unit listed as a generic "Boss pedal" under $40;
- MIJ Boss black-label, script MXR, big-box RAT, any Ibanez TS-808/TS9 MIJ, Mu-Tron, Space Echo at ≤ 40% of tier, or a pedal lot under $60 containing one of these; pedal lots under $100 that show ≥ 1 collectible pedal; a lot pricing out at ≤ 50% of the sum of medians;
- price ≤ 40% of the Reverb used mid and the pedal is a current/boutique model ≥ $150 used, or any vintage-era tell is visible.
Pass when: priced at 60–70%+ of Reverb used value (the usual Craigslist case); current-production/modern pedals within $50 of Reverb used (no spread); single common-tier pedal; Taiwan Boss, Behringer/Donner; the seller already quotes Reverb values; untested (unless seller says it works). Fair-to-high examples: Crybaby $70, Sparkle Drive $100, Danelectro $100, Arion $120, One Control $50. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees (~8–13%) and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
