## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Saddle-shaped seats from 1870s–1930s horse-drawn mowers, rakes, cultivators and early tractors. Two families:
- Cast iron (1870s–1910s): thick (~1/4"), heavy (6–12 lb), often ornate with raised lettering (maker name and town), patterned holes (stars, hearts, scrolls), a single slot or bolt holes in centre. Edges are rounded, surfaces show casting texture.
- Pressed (stamped) steel (1900s–1950s): thin sheet (~1/8"), rim rolled/flanged, rows of round punched holes, square or round centre hole, light (3–5 lb); usually unmarked or with embossed name only on late John Deere/IH seats. These are the common type and are also reproduced new.

## Makers, brands and variants
Collectors catalogue 1,500+ cast seat patterns. Commonly found: McCormick, Deering, Walter A. Wood (Hoosick Falls), Champion (Springfield, OH), Buckeye (Akron), Osborne, Johnston, Milwaukee, Plano, Adriance Platt, Kemp & Burpee, Hapgood. Rarer small foundries and ornate "scroll" seats drive top prices. John Deere/IH stamped steel seats with logo appeal to tractor collectors.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast: grainy sand-cast texture, raised letters, small casting flaws, gate marks. Old paint remnants (red, green, yellow). Pressed: uniform sheet thickness, clean punched holes, rolled edge, tool marks. Weight and thickness are the fastest tells from photos (look at the edge profile).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks and welds in cast seats (brittle), broken lugs, heavy rust pitting, sandblasted and repainted (lowers collector value). Pressed seats: bends, rust-through.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Cast iron reproductions of popular seats (Champion, Deering, Buckeye, "Plano") have been cast for decades (Taiwan/India decor foundries): sharper but softer detail, uniform thickness, rough unfinished casting gates, fake rust; letters may be shallow. New pressed-steel stool seats are sold for bar stools. Ask for a photo of the underside and edge.

## Market
Pressed seats: steady decor demand (bar stools, garden), ~100+ sales/month. Cast seats: collector niche, smaller but stronger. Spring/summer stronger for farmhouse decor.

## What sellers usually miss
Lettered cast seats lumped into "old farm junk"; underside raised name hidden from photo.

## Shipping / handling
Pressed seat ~4 lb, 16×15" → $15–20; cast seat 8–12 lb → $20–30. Pad cast seats (can crack).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "cast iron" (or "pressed steel") + "implement seat"/"tractor seat" + maker lettering (McCormick, Deering, Champion, Buckeye, Walter A. Wood) + "antique" + "original"; Junk: "farm junk", "bar stool seat", "rustic decor". Example: `Antique Cast Iron Implement Tractor Seat Walter A. Wood Original Paint`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for lettered cast seats (collector niche, median $73–75); unmarked pressed-steel pan seats sell as decor on FB local or eBay at $20–45. No place beats eBay for cast.
- **Price position:** at the sold median for the tier ($50–150 common lettered; $150–400 ornate/uncommon foundry), accept −15%, floor p25 ($50); OBO; cut 10% after a month; spring/summer is the decor peak.
- **Photos:** first photo top-down showing the full lettering; then underside (raised name and casting gates), edge/rim profile, lug/mounting boss, any crack or weld, paint traces, a tape measure for diameter.
- **State:** cast vs pressed, maker lettering exactly as cast, diameter, weight, original paint %, no cracks or welds (or where), repro check (sharp uniform casting, fake rust), flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** cast 8–12 lb, pressed ~4 lb; one box 16×15", pad the cast seat all round (brittle, can crack); $20–30 cast, $15–20 pressed.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Period [McCormick] cast seat with the original foundry lettering and no welds — reproductions are everywhere; this one has the uneven old casting and honest wear a collector checks for."

## Buy rule
Pressed steel at $25–40 sold → negative after shipping. Buy cast lettered seat only if price ≤ $40 and expected sold ≥ $130. Avoid unconfirmed cast seats that might be reproductions.
