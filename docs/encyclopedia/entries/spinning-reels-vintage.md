## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Spinning reel: hangs under the rod, with an open face and a wire bail. Spincast: closed nose cone with a thumb button (Zebco 33/202/404).
- Mitchell 300 (France): black or grey body with the maker on the side. "Garcia Mitchell" = 1960s and later, which is common. The early half-bail versions have a bail arm on one side only. Mitchell Museum data (via forum) puts the half-bail to full-bail changeover in 1951–52, when both were made.
- Mitchell serial numbers: per the Mitchell Museum data quoted on fishingmagic, early reels (to 1949) carry no serial, only foot stamps; 1949–52 reels carry a letter prefix plus five digits (A = 1949/50, B = 1950/51, C = 1951/52), first stamped on the foot and later moved to the housing; plain numeric serials run from 1953 onward. A letter-prefix serial is a strong early-reel tell (medium confidence; forum-quoted).
- Later Mitchell features that date a reel as later/common: auto bail (from about 1955 on the 330, per the same post), 5:1 gearing (400 range from 1963), anti-reverse switch (late 1960s).
- Penn Spinfisher 700 series: made in Philadelphia. The Spinfisher 700 (1961) was Penn's first spinning reel; a one-piece rotor cup was added in the 1960s and the reel renamed 704. Early 704/706 are mint green ("greenies"). In the late 1970s Penn switched to gold and black and renamed them 704Z/706Z. The Z reels are NOT green. Line ran from the small 722 to the large 706; discontinued early 2000s, briefly reissued 2013 (On The Water).
- Zebco and Shakespeare spincasts: brushed-metal or plastic cone, mass-market.
- Seller words: "old fishing reels", "vintage reel", "rods and reels", "Mitchell".
- Look-alikes: Mitchell 300A/300 Pro and later Asian-made Mitchells (post-1980s) share the name; Penn 4400ss, 5500ss and 6500ss are later Penn spinners in grey/gold, not "greenies".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Mitchell 300/301** (301 = left-hand): $20–45 for common 1960s–80s working reels. The half-bail and boxed early reels are $60–150.
- **Penn 704/706 Spinfisher**: $60–150. Green (1960s–70s) reels are the collector versions; gold/black 704Z/706Z are user reels that still sell on reputation (no separate price source this run).
- **Zebco/Shakespeare**: $5–15.
- For ABU Cardinal, Ambassadeur, Penn conventional reels, Alcedo and Hardy, see spinning-and-conventional-reels (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Side plate reads "Mitchell" and the foot or housing says "Made in France"? Then it is a French Mitchell, not a later Asian one.
- Bail arm only on one side (half bail)? Early Mitchell, pre-1952.
- Serial starting with A, B or C followed by five digits? 1949–52 Mitchell.
- Penn reel body mint green with "Spinfisher" and 700/704/706? Collector greenie. Gold and black with "Z"? Later user reel.
- "Philadelphia" or "Made in USA" on a Penn spinner = pre-2000s US production.
- Original box and paperwork in the photo (Mitchell boxes, Penn parts schematics)?
- Spool matches the reel (same colour and maker), handle present, bail not bent.
- Zebco/Shakespeare nose cones in the lot are filler; judge the lot by the few metal open-face reels.

### Golden-egg tells
- Half-bail Mitchell 300 (pre-1952) — ~2–4x a common Mitchell 300 ($60–150 vs $20–45) — bail wire attaches on one side only; look at the rotor in a front photo.
- Letter-prefix serial (A/B/C + 5 digits) Mitchell — in the same $60–150 collectible band — serial on the foot or housing; ask for a close photo.
- Boxed early Mitchell with papers — top of the band and above ($150+ in spinning-and-conventional-reels) — box in the photo with matching model number.
- Penn 700/704/706 in mint green — ~2–5x a Zebco-lot reel ($60–150) — green body, "Spinfisher" on the side; no "Z" in the model number.
- Penn Spinfisher 700 (the 1961 original, before the one-piece rotor) — top of the green band (general knowledge, verify) — model number "700" on the side plate.
- See spinning-and-conventional-reels for the $150–500 top tier (pre-1955 boxed Mitchell, Alcedo Micron, Hardy Altex).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Cast-metal bodies and a stamped country of origin ("Made in France", "Made in USA") point to older, better reels. Plastic housings and "Made in China/Taiwan" point to budget reels (general knowledge, verify).
- An original box and papers add 30–50% (general knowledge, verify).
- Penn Spinfisher parts stayed interchangeable across the whole run (On The Water), so a green reel can carry later gold parts; mismatched colours mean a parts reel and lower collector value.
- Old vs new: old French Mitchells are heavy for size with stamped serials; later Mitchell-branded reels are lighter, with printed graphics and no stamped serial (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Dried grease (stiff crank), bent bail, missing spool, chipped paint (general knowledge, verify).
- Non-working reels are a repair job, which Dylan doesn't do. Ask for a cranking video.
- Penn 70x: bail springs wear out and are a known replacement part (reader comments, On The Water); a bail that won't flip is common and fixable but still a repair.
- Repainted green Penns or re-decaled Mitchells lose collector value; uneven paint edges at the seams are the tell (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Few fakes. Look out for later reissues and mixed spools/handles in lots (general knowledge, verify).
- Penn reissued the 704Z/706Z in 2013; these are new reels, not vintage. Later Mitchell 300 reissues (Asian production) use the name too.

## Market
High volume and oversupplied at the common end; collectors want early Mitchells and Penn greenies. Spring peak (general knowledge, verify). Many green Penns are still fished, so user demand supports prices as well as collectors (On The Water).

## What sellers usually miss
- A half-bail Mitchell or a Penn green 70x mixed into an "old reels" lot.
- Letter-prefix serials on Mitchells; sellers rarely photograph the serial.
- The difference between a green 704 and a gold 704Z; both get listed as "Penn 704".

## Shipping / handling
- About 1 lb each, $8–10 shipped; fees ~13% (per spinning-and-conventional-reels). Rods don't ship economically.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Model, country, left/right hand, whether the crank is smooth, box/papers. Photograph the foot and side plates and include a cranking video.
- Title keywords: "Mitchell 300 Half Bail France", "Penn 706Z Spinfisher".
- Photograph the serial (Mitchell) and the model number and colour (Penn) in good light.

### What to tell the buyer
State the exact model and country stamped on the reel, the serial number (Mitchell) and what period it points to, whether the bail is half or full, and the colour for a Penn (original green Spinfisher vs later gold/black Z model). Say whether it cranks smoothly, whether the bail flips and holds, that the spool and handle are original to the reel, and list any paint loss. Mention box and papers only if present.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a lot with a visible half-bail Mitchell or Penn 70x is ≤ $30.
Pass when: Zebco lots.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
