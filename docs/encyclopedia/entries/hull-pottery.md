## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Semi-porcelain art pottery with molded flowers in relief, matte pastel glazes (pink, blue, cream fading into each other) or glossy colors. Bottom marks: "Hull Art USA" or "Hull USA" impressed, with a shape number and height, e.g. "Hull Art USA 3-8½" (Magnolia matte vase 3, 8½"). Paper labels occasionally.
- **Company timeline:** founded 1905 by Addis Emmet Hull; flood and fire destroyed the factory and most molds in June 1950; reopened 1 Jan 1952 as The Hull Pottery Company; closed March 1986 (Lion and Unicorn). The 1950 fire is the dividing line: pre-fire matte art lines vs post-fire glossy lines.
- **Mark sequence** (Hull Pottery Association): c. 1910 wreath with gallon number (stoneware); "H" in a circle or diamond (kitchen/stoneware/artware, early decades); 1930s–1950 "Hull Art USA" or "Hull USA" with a letter-number code; "Hull Ware" logo on 1940s kitchenware and Little Red Riding Hood; **embossed script "Hull"** first used 1949 on Woodland, then standard post-1952; lowercase "hull" on 1960s House 'n Garden, sometimes with ©; foil labels on Rosella, Granada and Classic; incised line names on Coronet, Pagoda, Tokay, Imperial, Crestone, Gingerbread Man. Sueno artware has no Hull mark, only a mold number. A painted decorator number (e.g. 28) may appear.
- **Reading a code:** letter = line, first number = mold/shape, last = height. "Hull Art W-3 5½" = Wildflower shape 3, 5½" (Lion and Unicorn). Bow-Knot uses "B-", Water Lily "L-", Wildflower "W-", Magnolia gloss "H-"; some pieces carry only mold and size numbers.
- Clay body is cream or buff; bright white or grey bodies suggest not Hull (Lion and Unicorn).

## Makers, brands and variants
Major matte lines (1938–1950, before the 1950 flood/fire rebuilt factory): Magnolia matte (1946–47), Iris (1940–42), Tulip, Wildflower (W- numbers), Orchid, Open Rose/Camellia, Bow-Knot (B- numbers, most valued matte), Water Lily (L-), Dogwood/Wild Rose, Poppy, Granada. Post-1950 glossy: Woodland glossy, Parchment & Pine, Ebb Tide, Blossom Flite, Butterfly, Serenade, Tokay/Tuscany, Continental, then House 'n Garden brown drip (1960s–86). Little Red Riding Hood (1943–57, designed with Regal China decoration), Corky Pig banks, Imperial florist ware.
- Line dates per Lion and Unicorn (verify against Roberts' *Collector's Ultimate Encyclopedia of Hull Pottery*): Calla Lily/Jack-in-the-Pulpit 1938–41; Tulip/Sueno 1938–40; Orchid 1939–41; Iris/Narcissus 1940–41 (entry above says 1940–42); Wildflower 1946–47; **Bow-Knot 1949–50**; Woodland matte 1949–50 (pre-fire), glossy after; Parchment & Pine 1951–52; Ebb Tide 1954–55; Serenade 1957; Tokay/Tuscany 1958; House 'n Garden/Mirror Brown 1960s–1986.
- **Little Red Riding Hood:** marked "Hull Ware" or with the line name and "Pat. Des. No. 135889"; some marked Regal China. Pieces incised "Hull Ware Little Red Riding Hood" are unambiguous (HPA).
- **Cookie jars** other than LRRH: bean pot, baby and apple jars (Kovels); Gingerbread Man jars (incised name) from the House 'n Garden era.

### Quick ID checklist
- Turn it over: "Hull Art USA" + letter-number + height = pre-1950 matte line; script "Hull" = 1949+; lowercase "hull" = 1960s brown/kitchen.
- Matte surface with soft pastel shading = pre-fire; high gloss on a shape claimed pre-1950 = post-fire (Magnolia gloss "H-") or repro.
- Read the height number: 12"+ pieces and floor vases are the money pieces.
- Look for the letter prefix B- (Bow-Knot), W- (Wildflower), L- (Water Lily).
- Check clay colour at the foot: cream/buff = right; bright white or grey = wrong.
- Handles, rims and relief flowers: chips here are the norm; zoom in.
- LRRH: look for "Hull Ware" or "Pat. Des. No. 135889"; gold trim and decals matter.

### Golden-egg tells
- Bow-Knot (B- prefix, 1949–50) — ~3–10× a common Magnolia vase (guide claims $150–400, mint over $500; Terapeak wall pocket median $45) — raised bow-and-ribbon with flowers on matte blue/green shading, "B-" on the base.
- Wildflower W-20 floor vase and other 15"+ matte pieces — guide claims $400–800 at auction vs $13–20 for small Magnolia — "W-20" or height 15+ in the base code; floor-standing size.
- Little Red Riding Hood with gold trim and extra decals (e.g. "poinsettia" apron) — $500–1,000+ claimed vs $50 Terapeak median for ordinary LRRH — gold on apron/shoes, applied floral decals; "Hull Ware" / Pat. Des. mark.
- Woodland **matte** (1949–50, pre-fire) — ~2× Woodland glossy ($75–200 vs $30–100) — satin surface, softer colours; glossy = post-fire.
- Orchid/Calla Lily console bowls, bookends, wall pockets, gold-trimmed pieces — $100–300 vs $25–70 for standard vases — unusual forms; gold trim added at the factory.
- Ebb Tide fish forms and console bowls — top of the post-fire range ($40–150) — fish/shell shapes in glossy two-tone.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Magnolia matte (pink/blue matte with relief magnolia) vs Magnolia gloss (1947–48, 'H-' numbers, white/pink glossy, lower value). Molded numbers on the base identify line/size. Larger sizes (12–15") and ewers/baskets and cornucopias with good color fetch more.
- Reproductions tend to be heavier (denser clay) and slightly undersized (cast from a real piece, so shrinkage); worn-mold pieces have soft relief (Lion and Unicorn).
- "Married" lids on jars and teapots: lid glaze, pattern or mark style does not match the base.
- Some genuine early florist ware and novelties are unmarked or marked "Hull Made" (HPA) — unmarked is caution, not proof of fake.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on handles/rims, crazing (common), glaze skips, restored rims (look for color mismatch), hairlines. Damage cuts value 50–80%.
- Guide rule of thumb (Lion and Unicorn): minor flakes or hairlines ~30–50% of mint; chips, cracks or restorations ~10–25% of mint; rare patterns with damage 60–80% below mint. Matte glazes chip easily at handles, rims and bases.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little Red Riding Hood reproductions are rampant (smaller, crude paint, wrong marks). Matte florals rarely reproduced; Chinese copies occasionally.
- Repro tells: undersized vs catalogue height, heavier, white/grey body, crisp modern decals, missing or blurred "Pat. Des." mark on LRRH.

## Market
Moderate volume, older collector base; mid pieces sit for weeks. Hull Pottery Association is the active collector club.

## What sellers usually miss
Bow-Knot and large matte ewers priced as generic "pink vase". The letter prefix and height on the base (B-, W-, L-) that identify the line.

## Shipping / handling
8–10" vase 2 lb; double-box $15–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Line, shape number, height, mark photo, condition/crazing statement.

### What to tell the buyer
"Hull Pottery, Crooksville, Ohio — [line] pattern, shape [letter-number], [height]", quoting the base mark exactly ("Hull Art USA B-3 6½"). Say whether it is the pre-1950 matte line or the post-1952 glossy production (the factory burned in 1950). Give height in inches, and state chips, flakes, crazing and any restoration plainly; buyers check handles, rims and the relief flowers. For Little Red Riding Hood, quote the "Hull Ware"/patent mark and say whether gold trim is present.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$15 − ~$15 − price ≥ max($50, price) → median ≥ ~$95 at $10; standard 8" matte vases never qualify. Pass unless large/rare matte under $40.
