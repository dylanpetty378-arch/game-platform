## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Curved or straight fighting/dress daggers from Turkey, the Caucasus, Persia, Arabia, North Africa, India and Nepal.
- **Kukri (Nepal)**: forward-angled recurved blade with a notch (cho) near the hilt, wood/horn/brass hilt with flared pommel and bolster, black leather scabbard with two small companion knives (karda and chakmak). pid 7930644440 is this, NOT a khanjar: heavy forward-swept kukri blade, brass/copper-tone turned hilt with ring bolster and flat pommel, engraved zig-zag ricasso, black leather scabbard with brass chape — a modern (1970s–2000s) tourist/decorative kukri, polished blade without forging scale.
- **Khanjar/jambiya**: J-shaped curved double-edged blade with a central rib, hilt of horn/wood/silver with a T or mushroom pommel, scabbard sharply curved (Omani) or upturned (Yemeni).
- **Kindjal/kama (Caucasus)**: straight double-edged blade with offset fullers, silver-niello mounts.
- **Pesh-kabz/kard (Persia/Afghan/Indian)**: T-spine single-edged blade, bone/ivory/walrus grips.

### Golden-egg tells
- Caucasian kindjal with Russian assay-marked silver-niello mounts (84 zolotnik, Tiflis/Vladikavkaz town mark, Cyrillic maker initials) — $500–3,000 vs ~$55 entry median (~10–50×) — straight double-edged blade with offset fullers, scabbard and grip fully sheathed in silver with black niello scrollwork; the tiny "84" plus a kokoshnik/female-head stamp on the silver edge is the photo tell; unmarked white-metal copies are $100–200 (entry comp)
- Persian/Indo-Persian kard or pesh-kabz in wootz (watered) steel with gold koftgari — $300–1,500, far more with gold-inlaid Qajar portrait cartouches, vs $263 Terapeak "Persian wootz dagger" median and ~$55 overall (~5–25×) — T-spine blade showing a wavy/crystalline water pattern in a close-up, gold vine or inscription chiselled into the forte, walrus-ivory ("fish-tooth" mottled) or bone grip slabs riveted through (entry comp)
- Ottoman/Balkan bichaq or khanjar with embossed silver scabbard bearing a tughra (sultan's monogram) or "sah" assay mark — $300–1,500 vs $161 Terapeak "Ottoman silver dagger" median (~2–10×) — repoussé silver or silver-gilt scabbard with floral work, walrus-ivory or horn hilt, blade with gold-damascened Arabic inscription; look for the small struck tughra on the throat of the scabbard (entry comp)
- Omani khanjar with heavy hand-worked silver (Saidi or Royal pattern with seven rings) and a rhino-substitute giraffe/sandalwood hilt — $400–1,500 vs $100–200 for 1970s souvenir khanjars (~5×) — scabbard entirely wrapped in fine silver wire filigree with a dense silver chape, five or seven belt rings, hilt sheathed in silver pins; a rhino-horn hilt (translucent amber with hair-like fibres) is worth more but cannot be sold — skip it (entry comp)
- WWII-issue MK II/MK III kukri with arsenal stamps (Cossipore "Co" or "CSP", "BSI", broad arrow, date 1942–45) — $150–400 vs $20–60 for a tourist kukri (~4–8×) — plain unpolished dark blade with a struck stamp near the spine at the ricasso, flat steel buttcap riveted through the wood grip, simple leather-over-wood scabbard with a steel chape; no brass engraving, no crossed-kukri badge (entry comp)
- Pre-1900 Nepalese "Bhojpure" or long-leaf kukri with a chiraa (hollow fullers) and horn hilt — $200–600 vs $20–60 (~5–10×) — heavy broad blade with multiple fullers running the spine, deep hand-cut cho, hilt of black water-buffalo horn with an iron or brass buttplate, forge scale and pitting on the spine (entry comp)
- Indian katar (push dagger) with chiselled steel and gold koftgari, 17th–18th c. — $500–3,000 vs $50–100 for 20th-c. bazaar katars (~10×) — H-shaped hilt with two parallel side bars and cross-grips, thick reinforced armour-piercing tip, gold vine-work inlaid on the hilt bars; tourist pieces have thin flat bars and acid-etched "patina" (entry comp)
- Moroccan koummya with silver (not brass) mounts, often with Berber enamel and a Jewish silversmith's hallmark — $300–800 vs $80–150 for the common brass-and-copper piece (~3–5×) — sharply upturned scabbard tip, white-metal scabbard testing as silver, engraved geometric panels, horn or rhino-shaped hilt with silver pommel cap (entry comp)
- Yemeni thouma/jambiya with a carved amber-coloured old cow-horn hilt and a heavy silver belt (hizam), the legal stand-in for the unsaleable "saifani" rhino-horn version — $500–750 (Oriental Arms asks $745) vs $40–100 for the 1970s souvenir (~7×) — wide woven belt with silver thread, large silver-wrapped scabbard, old coins riveted to the hilt (http://www.oriental-arms.com/)


## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Age: forged blades with hammer marks, pitting under polish, uneven grinds, dark patina in recesses, worn horn grips with shrinkage cracks, scabbard leather dry and cracked at stress points, silver with old tarnish and assay marks. Modern: mirror-polished blades, cast brass, fresh glue, bright stitching, uniform etched/engraved patterns, plastic "horn". Wootz/watered steel shows crystalline pattern after etching.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust and pitting, broken tips, replaced grips, missing scabbard or companion knives, re-made scabbards, aggressive polishing (destroys value of antique blades).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large export industry in India, Pakistan, Yemen, Oman, Morocco and Nepal making "antique" daggers with artificial aging (acid, sand, shoe polish). Fake tugra and assay marks. Rhino horn hilts are illegal to sell in US interstate commerce (Endangered Species Act) — avoid entirely; ivory restrictions also apply (some states ban sale).

## Market
eBay restricts some weapon sales (knives allowed but shipped with restrictions; check eBay knives policy) — specialist auction houses and arms forums sell antiques. Tourist pieces: slow, low.

## What sellers usually miss
Silver scabbards (weight + age) on Middle Eastern daggers; military arsenal stamps on kukri blades; Russian 84 silver marks on Caucasian mounts. Sellers usually OVER-call modern tourist pieces "antique sword".

## Shipping / handling
14–20 in. long, 1–2 lb: long box, tip protected, $10–15. Check carrier knife rules (USPS allows knives domestically). Fits passenger seat.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** type (Caucasian kindjal / Omani khanjar / Persian kard / Nepal kukri) + century + mounts (silver niello / 84 zolotnik / wootz) + "antique" only with evidence + length. Junk: "sword", "antique" for tourist pieces, "rhino" (illegal to sell). `Antique Caucasian Kindjal Dagger Silver Niello Scabbard 84 Mark 19th C 18in`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers within the knives policy for 20th-c. pieces; arms auctions and collector forums for 19th-c. silver-mounted and wootz (they beat eBay). Tourist pieces local or in lots.
- **Price position:** at the sold median ($100–300 stamped military kukris/early Omani; $300–1,500 19th c. silver-mounted), accept −15%, floor 75%; OBO; cut 15% after 45 days; tourist $15–45 firm.
- **Photos:** dagger and scabbard side by side; blade both sides; hilt close up; silver marks/assay stamps; scabbard repoussé; blade edge and tip; arsenal stamps; weight on a scale; next to a ruler.
- **State:** overall and blade length, weight, materials (silver tested), marks and stamps transcribed, blade condition (pitting, sharpening), honest age ("decorative 20th c. Nepalese kukri") — no "antique" without evidence, by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** carry-on, 14–20" and 1–2 lb; long box, tip capped in cardboard, blade sheathed and tied, $10–15; USPS domestic allows knives — check the carrier.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[19th-century Caucasian kindjal] with [Russian 84 assay-marked] silver-niello mounts and age wear throughout, [length]; tourist replicas sell for [$] and marked period pieces bring [$] at the arms auctions."

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$12 − ~$12 − price ≥ max($50, price). A tourist kukri ($40 median) can't clear. Buy only pieces with silver-mounted scabbard or arsenal stamps and visible age (forge marks, old patina) at ≤ $80 when comps ≥ $250. No rhino/ivory. Avoid high-value antiques needing expert authentication.
