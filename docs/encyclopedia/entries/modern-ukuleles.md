## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A four-string small guitar-family instrument. Sizes: soprano (~21" total, ~13" scale), concert (~23", 15"), tenor (~26", 17"), baritone (~30", 19"). Count frets to the body: soprano ~12, concert ~14, tenor ~15+, and compare to the case.
Brand is usually on the headstock decal or on a label visible through the sound hole. Kala/Makala: "Kala" label in sound hole; Makala headstock has "Makala" in script. Hawaiian makers: Kamaka has a "Kamaka Hawaii" headstock decal and paper label with the "K" logo; KoAloha headstock has a crown-shaped scalloped top; Kanile'a has distinctive K-shaped headstock.
Photo tells of value: koa (figured golden-brown with chatoyant flame/curl), solid wood (grain matches inside and out at the sound hole edge), bone nut/saddle, binding, rosette inlay, friction-free geared tuners, Hawaiian label.
Photo tells of cheap: painted or stained red/brown "mahogany" laminate with a sticker-thin rosette decal, plastic nut and saddle, open-gear basic tuners, "Makala", "Mahalo", "Donner", "Amazon Basics", toy-store colors.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Kala** (California, made in China/Indonesia): Makala line (MK-S soprano, MK-C concert, MK-T tenor) is the entry line, laminate agathis/mahogany. KA-15/KA-S series laminate mahogany; Kala solid-top and all-solid models (KA-SMHS, KA-ASAC, Elite) worth more.
- **Lanikai** (Hohner): LU-21 etc. entry, ACST solid acacia mid.
- **Cordoba**: 15CM/20TM laminate/solid-top; 24T, 25T solid.
- **Ohana**: solid-top and solid mahogany mid tier.
- **Martin** (USA/Mexico): S1, 0XK, T1K Mexico-made $150–350; vintage Martin Style 0/1/2/3 (1920s–60s, mahogany, Nazareth PA) $400–3,000 — technically vintage, but sellers mix them in.
- **Kamaka** (Honolulu, since 1916): "pineapple" uke is iconic; standard soprano koa $700–1,300.
- **KoAloha, Kanile'a, Ko'olau, Pono (Ko'olau sub-brand, Indonesia)**: Hawaiian koa; $700–2,000 used.
- **Fluke/Flea** (Magic Fluke Co., Massachusetts): composite body, wood top; $100–250.
- **Enya, Donner, Kmise, Hricane, Mahalo**: budget, $15–40.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Solid vs laminate: look at sound-hole edge — laminate shows plywood layers or a uniform dark edge. Solid koa/mahogany sounds and sells far better.
- Koa: golden to reddish brown, often curly; mainly Hawaiian or premium imports. Acacia is a cheaper koa look-alike.
- Satin vs gloss finish: gloss is usually higher-end in modern imports.
- Hardware: geared tuners with chrome/gold buttons; Hawaiian makers often use friction or Gotoh UPT pegs.
- Electronics: pickups add little on beginner models.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Top cracks along the grain (from dryness), bridge lifting (check gap under bridge's back edge), neck reset issues on old ukes, fret wear. Laminates crack less.
- Strings off/loose suggests bridge or tuner problem.
- Repairs are out of scope for Dylan — pass anything with lifting bridge or cracks.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Counterfeit Kala/Martin labels exist on Chinese no-name ukes but are uncommon; fake Kamaka is rare.
- "Hawaiian" souvenir ukes (painted plastic, 1950s–70s Mainland makers like Maccaferri Islander plastic) are their own cheap collectible ($30–100).

## Market
Very liquid at all tiers; Reverb is better than eBay for anything $150+. Beginner ukes sell fast but for little. Hawaiian koa ukes sell within days at fair prices. Seasonal bump in November–December gifting.

## What sellers usually miss
- A Kamaka, KoAloha or vintage Martin in a closet sold as "old ukulele" for $50–150 — the realistic home run.
- Hard case: a molded hard case alone is worth $30–60.
- Sellers overrate Makala because new price ~$60 and include the Kala name.

## Shipping / handling
Soprano/concert in gig bag: box ~28×12×6, 3–5 lb, $15–25. Loosen strings slightly; pad the headstock. Hard case adds weight.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give make, model (from label), size, solid or laminate, close-up of label, top, back, sides, bridge, and sound sample. State string condition and any action/intonation notes.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$18) − ship to Dylan (~$18) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Makala/beginner (median ~$40): never profitable shipped. Pass.
- Mid laminate (median ~$100): pay ≤ $15. Mostly pass.
- Martin Mexico (median ~$250): pay ≤ $80.
- Hawaiian koa (Kamaka median ~$800): pay ≤ $200 (cap); Tier A easily. Confirm label photo and no cracks.
- Must play/string-tested if possible; no repairs.
- Fits passenger seat: yes (all sizes).
