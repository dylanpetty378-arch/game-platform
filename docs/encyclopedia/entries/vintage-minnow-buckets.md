## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A two-part metal pail: an outer bucket and a perforated inner insert that lifts out (or floats) to hold live minnows. Galvanized steel with painted or printed logos, wire bail handle.
- **Old Pal** (Woodstream Corp., Lititz PA): red/orange "OLD PAL" lettering on galvanized steel, 1950s–80s; sizes 6, 8, 10 qt; "Floating Minnow Bucket".
- **Frabill** (Milwaukee): green or galvanized buckets with "Frabill" logo, 1940s–80s.
- **Falls City** (Louisville) and **Planet**: early, often painted green with gold stencilling.

Construction types:
- **Floating bucket**: the perforated insert has an air chamber and floats in the water when tethered; marked "Floating" or "Full Floating".
- **Two-piece pail**: outer bucket + lift-out perforated inner well with a hinged lid opening (~5" hole on the Frabill 480).
- **Trolling/"Submarine" bucket** (Falls City Submarine #510, 10 qt): torpedo/perforated body towed behind the boat.
- **Oval buckets**: smaller, early, often Old Pal or unbranded.
- Sizes are marked or listed in quarts (6, 8, 10 qt common); a Frabill 480 is about 9" across and 8¾" high without handle.

## Makers, brands and variants
Old Pal / Woodstream (Lititz PA), Frabill, Falls City, Planet, Bill DeWitt (Ohio), Pflueger, Shakespeare (rare).
- **Old Pal**: brand dates from before WWII (best known for cantilever tackle boxes, 1940s–70s); merged into/acquired by Woodstream in the 1960s, so buckets marked "Old Pal / Woodstream Corp. Lititz PA" are 1960s or later. Model codes appear (e.g. 24G10).
- **Frabill** (Milwaukee): "Frabill's Min-O-Life" floating buckets with red lettering; model numbers 480 and 1480 seen.
- **Falls City** (Louisville KY): "Angler's Choice" #7810 floating bucket (some with wooden bail grip), "Submarine" #510 10-qt trolling bucket.
- **Westpoint** also used the Min-O-Life name (full floating).
- **Green River** and other small makers turn up at estate auctions.
- **Bill DeWitt** (Ohio), **Pflueger**, **Shakespeare**: tackle-brand buckets, scarcer.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the lettering: maker, city ("Lititz PA", "Milwaukee", "Louisville"), model number and quart size.
- "Woodstream" on an Old Pal = 1960s or later.
- Insert present? Open the lid photo; a missing insert halves the use value.
- Wooden bail grip and stencilled paint suggest earlier; plastic grip and printed decal suggest later.
- Paint: original painted body (green, red, orange) with crisp graphics vs plain galvanized.
- Bottom: rust-through holes and pitting kill value; ask for a photo of the base.
- Graphics: pictorial scenes (fisherman, canoe, fish) beat plain lettering.

### Golden-egg tells
- Pre-war painted bucket with stencilled pictorial or gold-leaf graphics (Falls City, Planet, unbranded) — ~3–5× a plain galvanized Old Pal ($50–150 vs $25, entry tiers, verify) — painted body, stencilled lettering, riveted seams, wooden bail grip.
- Mid-century pictorial graphics bucket (fisherman/canoe/fish scenes) — ~2× plain lettering — full printed scene on the body (decorator favourite).
- Falls City "Submarine" trolling bucket — scarcer form than a pail (verify sold) — torpedo-shaped perforated body with tow ring.
- Frabill Min-O-Life in clean original red lettering — a sold-out shop price of $99 (retail, not eBay sold) vs the $25 Old Pal Terapeak median — red "Frabill's Min-O-Life" logo, model number.
- Tackle-brand buckets (Pflueger, Shakespeare) — tackle collectors pay above generic (general knowledge, verify) — brand name stamped or printed.
- Plain Old Pal/Woodstream floating bucket — the common version, ~$18–28 sold (Terapeak).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bright original paint and graphics drive value. Insert present. Rust-free bottom. Pre-war pieces have stencilled paint, riveted seams, and wooden bail grips.
- Galvanized steel shows spangle; later buckets are plastic or aluminum (low interest).
- Repainted bodies have brush marks over rust and no wear at the rim — decor value only.
- Wear pattern on genuine paint: rubbed at the rim and handle ears, intact in protected areas.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust, holes in the bottom, missing insert, faded logo. No repairs worth doing. Missing inner-bucket handles and screws added to hold the insert are common (Frabill 480 example).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Decorative "vintage-style" galvanized buckets are sold as decor. Not common fakes. Converted buckets (hanging lights, planters) are sometimes listed as vintage — drill holes reduce collector value.

Steady among fishing-decor and lodge-decor buyers; Spring/summer bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Early painted buckets in barns are sometimes priced like plain pails. Model numbers and maker city in faded lettering.

## Shipping / handling
Bulky (10–12" round); $18–25 (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, quart size, insert present, rust and paint condition.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker, model number, quart size and city exactly as printed (e.g. "Old Pal, Woodstream Corp., Lititz PA, floating, 8 qt"). Say whether the insert is present and floats, and photograph the bottom inside and out. Describe the paint and graphics honestly (original vs repainted, how legible). Give diameter and height without handle. Date it carefully: "Woodstream" marking means 1960s or later.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$20 to buyer − ~$20 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A $45 sale loses money. Pass on this type except rare pre-war pieces under $20.
