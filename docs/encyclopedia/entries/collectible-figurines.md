## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Josef Originals (1946–85): big-eyed girl figurines, black eyes with lashes, foil label "Josef Originals" + "Japan"/"California"; series Birthday Girls, Days of the Week, Months, Musicale, "Little Internationals". Common ones $10–25; 1950s California-period ("Hedy", "Sunny", "Blue Belle", 8"+ tall) $40–100 each.
- Sid Dickens Memory Blocks: 6×8" plaster tiles, embossed, signed on back; retired tiles $80–200, common $40–80.
- Enesco Cherished Teddies, Precious Moments, Hallmark: $3–15 each; only sealed early Precious Moments with "triangle" marks or the Jonathan & David originals reach $50–150.
- Hummel (Goebel): common 4–5" figurines $20–50; large/early TMK-1/2 $150–500; Hummel clocks $50–100.
- Photo tells per family: Josef = matte-ish pastel dresses, tiny black pupil-less eyes with painted lashes, gold accents, oval foil/paper label; Hummel = rustic children in muted earth colours on a round or square base, incised "M.I. Hummel" signature on the base edge; Sid Dickens = thick rectangular crackled tile with a hanging cord, Victorian/heraldic motifs; Precious Moments = white bisque-look porcelain children with teardrop eyes; Cherished Teddies = resin-look bears with names.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Josef Originals** (Muriel Joseph George): California production (c.1946–early 1960s, earlier, taller, higher value) then Japan production (c.1960s–85); labels and incised marks differ. Series: Birthday Girls, Days of the Week, Months, Musicale, Little Internationals.
  - History (Collectors Weekly): Muriel Joseph of Arcadia CA made Lucite then ceramic jewelry ("Muriel of California"), then figurines with husband Tom George; "Josef Originals" launched 1946 (the spelling was a printing error). Mid-1950s Japanese copies prompted more detailed (costlier) designs; George Good persuaded them to move production to Japan, and in **1959** they trained workers at the Katayama factory. Tom George retired 1981. So Japan production starts c. 1959, not only "1960s".
  - Japan-era series seen in listings include "Joy of Spring" (1960s) alongside Birthdays, Months, Days of the Week.
- **Sid Dickens Memory Blocks** (Vancouver, 1990s–): tiles numbered with a "T-" number (e.g. T23); retired tiles carry the value.
  - Plaster on timber, hand-finished, 6×8 in; a note on the back explains the design's inspiration (Alfresco Emporium). Retailers list retired designs by number, e.g. T104 White Horse, T123 French Crest, T392 Lineage, T410 Hope, T678 Sacred Guardian, plus "AT" numbers (e.g. AT103 Pure Gold). Collectors Club members get limited signed tiles. Low T-numbers are the early, long-retired designs.
- **Enesco Precious Moments** (Sam Butcher): yearly symbols on the base (triangle, hourglass, fish, etc. – early 1980s marks and "no mark/original" pieces worth more; general knowledge, verify). **Cherished Teddies** (Priscilla Hillman) – junk.
- **Goebel Hummel**: trademarks TMK-1 (crown, to c.1950) through TMK-8; TMK-1/2 most valuable; 10"+ large pieces top.
  - Mark dates (Antique Trader): TMK-1 Crown (WG under crown, 1934–1950; often a "double crown", one incised and one stamped); TMK-2 Full Bee (bee inside a V, 1940–1959; large bee to c.1956, incised then black then blue stamp); TMK-3 Stylized Bee (c.1958/60–1972); TMK-4 Three Line (1964–1972); TMK-5 Last Bee (1972–1979); TMK-6 Missing Bee (1979–1991, painter's date added); TMK-7 "Hummel Mark" (1991–1999, "West" dropped); TMK-8 Millennium Bee (2000–2008). Goebel stopped Hummel production on 2008-09-30. "U.S. Zone" wording c. 1946–48 is an early, desirable variant.

### Quick ID checklist
- Josef: label says "California" or "Japan"? Incised "Josef Originals" / "©Josef" on the base? Height (8–9" California girls are the money pieces).
- Josef series name readable on a label or known from the design (Birthday, Months, Days of the Week, Musicale, Little Internationals)?
- Hummel: which trademark on the base (crown, full bee, stylised bee, three-line, no bee)? Incised mould number and "M.I. Hummel" signature present?
- Hummel size: 10"+ or a "/I", "/II" larger size suffix in the mould number?
- Sid Dickens: T- or AT- number and title on the back, hanging cord intact, corners unchipped?
- Precious Moments: base symbol present (none/triangle/hourglass/fish = early) and box?
- Extremity close-ups: fingers, noses, flowers, hats, tile corners.
- Lefton/Napco/unmarked big-eye girls are look-alikes, not Josef.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Josef: glazed ceramic/porcelain, delicate painted lashes, gold trim; California pieces taller and finer. Sid Dickens: cast plaster, crackled and hand-tinted, numbered and signed on back. Hummel: hand-painted porcelain/earthenware; incised mould number and trademark on base. Cheap production: resin, blurred paint, missing marks.
- Old vs new Hummel: underglaze marks and darker, more saturated paint on older pieces; post-1979 pieces carry a painted date; heavier, more muted earthenware body on early pieces (general knowledge, verify).
- Josef: lashes should be fine and individual; blobby lashes and heavy glossy glaze point to copies.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on fingers, noses, flowers, hats; repaired breaks (glue lines, colour mismatch under UV light); missing foil labels (Josef value drops); crazing; Sid Dickens corner chips and broken hanging cords. Ask for base marks and close-ups of extremities.
- Hummel: ground-down bases (to remove chips) and repainted areas are common; a fresh-looking unglazed spot or a different white indicates restoration.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Japanese and other knock-offs of Josef-style big-eyed girls (Lefton, Napco and unmarked) are common – not fakes but lower value. Hummel look-alikes (Napco, "Erich Stauffer"/Arnart). Precious Moments re-issues of popular designs (general knowledge, verify).
- Hummel designs were made for decades, so the same figure exists in every TMK; the trademark, not the design, sets the value.

## Market
Oversupplied: boomer estates are releasing huge numbers of Precious Moments, Cherished Teddies and Hummels, and prices have fallen for a decade (general knowledge, verify). Josef and Sid Dickens have smaller but active collector bases on eBay and FB groups. Mild Q4 gift bump.

## What sellers usually miss
Josef California vs Japan period (California = early, taller, higher value). Everything else is over-named and over-priced by sellers who read "collectible".
- Hummel crown/full-bee marks on otherwise ordinary designs; Sid Dickens low T-numbers.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile, 1–3 lb each; $12–20 each way. Wrap extremities separately; double-box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, series/name, mark or label (California/Japan, TMK, T-number), height, chips/repairs, box/papers. Photograph base marks, labels, close-ups of face and hands. Sell Josef girls by series name.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the maker, design name and series, and the exact mark: for Josef, whether the label or incised mark reads California or Japan (California production ran from 1946 until the move to Japan's Katayama factory in 1959); for Hummel, the trademark name and its date range (e.g. "TMK-2 Full Bee, 1940–1959") plus the incised mould number; for Sid Dickens, the T-number, title and "retired". Give height in inches, note whether original labels, box or certificates are present, and list every chip, repair or crazing with a close-up. Avoid "rare" unless the design is documented as retired or limited.

## Buy rule
