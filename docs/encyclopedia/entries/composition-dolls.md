## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Dolls whose heads (and often limbs or entire bodies) are molded from "composition" — sawdust/wood-pulp and glue, smoothed with a plaster coat and painted. American production c. 1910–1950; replaced by hard plastic after WWII. Photo tells:
- Matte to satin painted skin with **fine crackle lines ("crazing")**, flaking at fingertips, toes, nose and chin, often darkened/yellowed varnish.
- **Mama dolls / babies:** composition shoulder head + arms + lower legs on a stuffed cloth body; legs often stitched straight across at the hip; crier box in the torso.
- **All-composition toddlers/girls (Patsy, Shirley Temple, Anne Shirley):** jointed at neck, shoulders, hips; molded or mohair/human-hair wig; sleep eyes with real lashes; open mouth with tiny teeth (Shirley Temple has 6 teeth and dimples).
- Painted eyes with molded hair = cheaper or earlier babies. Glass/tin sleep eyes, mohair wig, open-closed mouth with teeth = higher grade.
- Distinguish **bisque** (unglazed porcelain: hard, cool, slight sheen, no crazing, chips rather than flakes) and **hard plastic** (1947+; smooth, glossy, no crazing, molded seams).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Effanbee (Fleischaker & Baum, NY)** — Bubbles (1924), Lovums, Grumpy (c. 1912), Patsy family (1928 on: Patsy, Patsy Ann 19", Patsy Joan, Patsy Lou, Patsyette, Patsy Baby, Wee Patsy), Anne Shirley, Skippy, Sweetie Pie, Historical dolls (1939). Marked "EFFANBEE" on head/back; gold heart bracelet/tag.
- **Madame Alexander** — Dionne Quintuplets (1935–39), Little Colonel, Princess Elizabeth, Sonja Henie (1939), Scarlett O'Hara, Jane Withers, Little Genius babies, Portrait ladies (late 1930s–40s, top value). Marked "MME ALEXANDER" or "ALEXANDER" on head/back; cloth clothing tags.
- **Ideal** — Shirley Temple (1934–40, sizes 11–27"; marked "SHIRLEY TEMPLE" on head and back, "Ideal" on some), Judy Garland/Deanna Durbin (1939–40), Snow White, Flossie Flirt, Baby Beautiful. 
- **E.I. Horsman** — Billiken (1909), Campbell Kids, Ella Cinders, HEbee-SHEbee, Buttercup baby (1930s), Dimples; marked "E.I.H. Co." or "HORSMAN" — many unmarked.
- **Arranbee (R&B)** — Nancy, Nancy Lee, Debu'Teen, Dream Baby; marked "R&B" or "Arranbee."
- **American Character** — "Petite" mama dolls, Sally; marked "PETITE" or "AM. CHAR."
- **Vogue** — Toddles (pre-Ginny, 1937–48), marked "VOGUE."
- **Cameo / Joseph Kallus** — Kewpie, Scootles; **Averill** — mama dolls, Bonnie Babe; **Mary Hoyer**; **Buddy Lee (H.D. Lee Co., 1920–48)**; **Amberg**; **Reliable (Canada)**; plus countless unmarked "carnival" and department-store dolls.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Mark location: back of the neck/head, across the shoulders, or on the back torso. Unmarked ≈ budget unless the face mold is identifiable.
- Original clothing carries cloth tags (Alexander) or paper hang tags; original outfits double value.
- Sleep eyes that still open/close, real lashes, original mohair wig (not synthetic) are pluses.
- Composition is never shiny like plastic; heavy repaint looks glossy and too even — buyers discount it.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- **Crazing** (fine cracks) is near-universal; light crazing is acceptable, heavy crazing with lifting/peeling paint cuts value 50–80%.
- **Flaking** at fingers, toes, nose; **cracks** at neck/shoulder joints; worn or re-glued wigs; stuck/cloudy sleep eyes; rotted or torn cloth bodies; missing crier.
- Restoration: professional restoration can preserve value (WorthPoint); amateur repaint or "Craze Control" filler hurts it. Dylan does no repairs.
- Humidity and temperature swings cause further crazing — ship and store stable.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Reproduction Shirley Temple dolls (vinyl 1957/1972/1982, porcelain Danbury Mint) are often sold as "vintage Shirley Temple"; composition is the original 1934–40.
- Unmarked look-alike "Shirley Temple type" dolls from smaller makers (1930s) are period but lower value.
- Effanbee re-issued Patsy in vinyl (1970s–90s) — glossy, lighter, marked with later dates.

## Market
- Large supply, aging buyer base; prices for common composition dolls have fallen since the 2000s. Premium names (Shirley Temple, Alexander, Patsy) still sell steadily; "creepy doll" Halloween demand gives a small October bump for battered ones.
- Platforms: eBay (broad), Ruby Lane/Theriault's (premium), Facebook doll groups.

## What sellers usually miss
- The mark under the wig or across the shoulders — many sellers never look.
- Original tagged clothing and accessories (Shirley Temple pin, Alexander wrist tag) worth as much as the doll.
- Doll trunks with wardrobes (1930s–40s) add $30–150.
- Conversely, most sellers over-price ordinary crazed babies as "antique."

## Shipping / handling
- 15–22" dolls: box 22×12×8, 3–5 lb, $15–25 Ground Advantage. Wrap head in tissue; never let composition sit against plastic in heat (sweats and crazes). Avoid shipping in summer heat when possible.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Photograph the mark, face close-ups in daylight, crazing, hands/feet, body, clothing tags.
- State height, eye type (sleep/painted), wig type, and whether clothes are original or later.
- Use "unmarked" honestly; "attributed to" for mold matches.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 ship to buyer − ~$20 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Required sold median ≈ (price + max($50, price) + $40) / 0.87: a $25 doll needs ~$135 sold, a $65 doll ~$195 — only mid-high tier marked dolls in good original condition qualify.
- Pass on unmarked, heavily crazed, flaking, nude or repainted dolls.
- Ask for: mark photo (back of neck/shoulders), close-up of face in daylight, and whether clothes and trunk/wardrobe are included.
- No repairs (no restringing, rewigging, crazing fill).
