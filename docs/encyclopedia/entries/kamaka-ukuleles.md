## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Honolulu-made solid koa ukes. Headstock decal "Kamaka / Hawaii" with the double-K logo; pre-1970 "gold label" inside the soundhole (1950s–69), "white label" 1970s–2000s, current ones have a dated label. Pineapple-shaped body = Kamaka Pineapple (1920s patent, reissued). HF-1 soprano, HF-2 concert, HF-3 tenor (HF-4 baritone, general knowledge, verify). Sellers usually know the name but not the era. Look-alikes: other Hawaiian makers (Martin-style Kumalae, Kealoha, Koaloha – separate markets), and non-Kamaka ukes with a Kamaka-style decal.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Kamaka** (founded 1916 by Samuel Kamaka; family-run) – the main entry (founding year general knowledge, verify).
- Eras: pre-war (1920s–40s) – Pineapple and early sopranos; **gold label** (1950s–69); **white label** (1970s–2000s); current dated labels.
- Models: HF-1 soprano (most common), HF-2 concert, HF-3 tenor, Pineapple soprano, 8-string tenor (HF-38; general knowledge, verify), Gold Label / Deluxe limited models.
- See vintage-ukuleles for Martin, Harmony and other makers (Harmony stencil soprano tier $60–120 recorded in sweeps).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid Hawaiian koa (curly koa = premium), lacquer finish, simple appointments on standard models, friction tuners on older ones (geared/planetary later). Old vs new: label colour/text, tuner type, decal style, finish checking. Refinished ukes lose most collector value. (general knowledge, verify)

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks (top and back from dry climates), seam separations, lifted bridge, refinish, replaced tuners, neck reset. Any crack/separation = pass per buy rule. Ask for soundhole label photo, sides in raking light, bridge edges, neck angle.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Non-Kamaka ukuleles with a Kamaka decal (junk tier). Kamaka reissued the Pineapple. Labels can be swapped – check construction matches the era. (general knowledge, verify)

## Market
Strong player and collector market (Hawaii, West Coast, Japan); Reverb is the natural venue, eBay second. Sells in days–weeks if priced right. Steady; slight holiday bump. (general knowledge, verify)

## What sellers usually miss
Gold vs white label (era). Anyone who writes "Kamaka" has already priced it as a Kamaka.

## Shipping / handling
3–4 lb in case; $25–35 insured. Reverb 5–8% + payment, eBay 13%. Loosen strings slightly, pad the headstock, humidity-protect in winter.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Kamaka" + model ("HF-1", "Pineapple") + label era ("gold label", "white label") + size (soprano/concert/tenor) + "solid koa" + "Hawaii" + decade; Junk: "ukelele", "Hawaiian uke", "vintage instrument". Example: `Kamaka HF-1 Gold Label Soprano Ukulele 1960s Solid Koa Hawaii Original Case`.
- **Where:** Reverb first (5–8% + payment; Hawaii/West Coast/Japan buyers), eBay second; sells in days–weeks priced right. No local market beats Reverb.
- **Price position:** at the sold median for the era (gold-label soprano $329 median, p75 $400; Pineapple $400, p75 $550; 1990s–2010s HF-1 $500–800 vs ~$1,100 new), accept −10%, floor p25; OBO; cut 10% after 3 weeks.
- **Photos:** first photo front in daylight; then label through the soundhole, headstock decal, back and sides in raking light (cracks, seams), bridge edges, neck angle, tuners, fret wear, case; a short strum video.
- **State:** model, label colour/era, size, solid koa, original finish, crack-free/seam-tight (or where), tuners original, action, strings, case included, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 3–4 lb in case; loosen strings slightly, pad the headstock inside the case, case in a box with fill, humidity-protect in winter; $25–35 insured.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Kamaka [HF-1] with the [gold label] — [1950s–60s] Honolulu-built solid koa, no cracks, original tuners; a new HF-1 is about [$1,100] and the old ones are the ones players want."

## Buy rule
Buy when: any genuine Kamaka ≤ $250 crack-free, or gold-label ≤ $450. Pass when: seller lists "Kamaka" with tiered pricing (they know), or any crack/separation. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Fits a passenger seat.
