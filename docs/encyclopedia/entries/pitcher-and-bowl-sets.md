## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A large pitcher (ewer, 11–13" tall) sitting in a wide basin (14–17" diameter), used on bedroom washstands before indoor plumbing, c.1860–1920. Full "toilet sets" also had a chamber pot, slop jar, soap dish, toothbrush holder and small pitcher.
- English Staffordshire (most common): ironstone or semi-porcelain with transfer-printed patterns in blue, teal, green, brown or multicolour; gilded edges; printed backstamp with pattern name and maker.
- **Flow blue**: deliberately blurred cobalt transfers (1840s–1910s) — the premium category.
- **Blush ware** (1890s–1910s): ivory/peach ground with floral transfers and gilt scrolls (Royal Worcester blush ivory and many imitators — Wood & Son, Carlton, Wiltshaw & Robinson, Doulton Burslem) — like the $40 set in our sweep.
- **American**: Homer Laughlin, Knowles Taylor & Knowles, Carrollton, Sebring — plainer.
- **Reproductions**: 1970s–2000s décor sets (Japan/China/Portugal), lighter, glossy, decal flowers, no crazing, "hand painted" stamps.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Doulton Burslem** (pre-1902 mark) / **Royal Doulton**: flow blue and transfer sets $200–600.
- **Johnson Bros.**, **Alfred Meakin**, **J&G Meakin**, **W.H. Grindley** (flow blue), **Wood & Son** ("Wood & Sons" crown mark), **Ford & Sons** (Burslem), **Colonial Pottery** (Stoke, F. Winkle & Co., c.1890–1931; teal/green transfers — Lens matched "Togo" for our sweep's teal set, verify the backstamp), **Bishop & Stonier**, **Royal Staffordshire (Wilkinson)**.
- **Wedgwood**, **Minton**, **Royal Worcester** — premium.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old: heavy body, fine crazing, glaze wear on the basin's base ring, rough unglazed foot, printed backstamp with pattern name, registry diamonds (1842–83) or Rd No. (1884+).
- Teal/green transfers were popular 1890–1915; gilt sponged edges.
- Repro: light, perfectly white glaze, decal flowers, stamped "Made in Japan/China", no backstamp detail.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Hairline cracks (very common in basins — hold up to light, tap for a dull sound), chips under the pitcher's spout and foot, handle repairs (glued/re-attached), crazing stains (brown), gilt wear, filled chips (UV). Damaged sets lose 50–80%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Décor reproductions (above). Mix-and-match: pitcher and bowl from different patterns or makers sold as a set — check backstamps match.

## Market
Slow, regional and local. Farmhouse/cottage décor buyers; flow blue collectors aging. Shipping cost and breakage push sales to local pickup and antique malls.

## What sellers usually miss
- Flow blue patterns and Doulton backstamps.
- Matching chamber pots and soap dishes that complete toilet sets.
- Reverse: they price every wash set as "1800s antique" at $85–300 without checking for hairlines.

## Shipping / handling
12–18 lb, basin 15–17" wide; double-box with 3" of cushioning each layer; $40–70 shipping; high breakage. Fits a passenger seat (seatbelt it).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, pattern name, backstamp photos of both pieces, dimensions, crack/chip check with light, crazing, gilt condition. Local pickup preferred.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$50 − ~$50 − price. A $150 set nets ~$31 before price: a loss even free. Pass unless (a) flow blue or Doulton at ≤ $40 with sold ≥ $250, or (b) planned local resale (outside the main rule). Passenger-seat size OK, but shipping makes this category a near-automatic pass.
