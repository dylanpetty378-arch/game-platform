## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Modern kits:** black or coloured aluminium pen-style rotary machines, cartridge needle boxes, small ink bottles, transfer paper, practice skins — beginner bundles sold on Amazon for $40–120 new. Pro pens show logos (Cheyenne "C", FK Irons, Bishop wand shape).
- **Vintage coil machines:** iron/brass frame with two coils, springs, binding posts, often maker-stamped on the frame or coil caps; some sold as kits by supply houses.
- **Flash:** sheets of tattoo designs displayed in shops; originals are pen-and-ink with watercolour or acrylic on heavy paper or card, often with pinholes from wall display; some early flash was distributed as blueprints/printed sheets by supply houses (e.g. Percy Waters blueprint flash, 24 × 18 in., "signed in plate"; Potter & Potter).
- Title words: "tattoo gun", "machine", "kit", "rotary", "coil", "flash", "vintage", "estate".
- Look-alikes: permanent-makeup (PMU) pens (thinner, cosmetic branding), engraving pens, printed flash posters and books.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Pro rotary:** Cheyenne (Germany; Hawk Pen, Sol Nova), FK Irons (USA; Spektra Xion/Flux), Bishop Rotary, Inkjecta, Stigma, Peak.
- **Budget:** Dragonhawk, Mast, Solong, Hummingbird, Wormhole.
- **Vintage builders:** Spaulding & Rogers, Percy Waters, Owen Jensen, Paul Rogers, Micky Sharpz (UK).
  - **Percy Waters (1888–1952):** American tattooer and supplier; invented an adjustable two-coil electromagnetic machine in 1929; ran a supply business selling "Tattoo Starter Packs" and printed/blueprint flash (Potter & Potter).
- **Flash artists:** Sailor Jerry (Norman Collins), Lyle Tuttle, Bert Grimm, Percy Waters; Don Ed Hardy (Collins's protégé) — high value.

### Quick ID checklist
- Logo/engraving on the pen body (Cheyenne, FK Irons, Bishop) and serial number; compare font and finish with the maker's site.
- Is there a video of the pen running, with stroke and grip?
- Kit contents: unbranded cartridges, "practice skins", coloring book = beginner tier.
- Coil machine: maker stamp on frame, hand-cut vs cast frame, brass parts, period binding posts; any supply-house box.
- Flash: hand-painted (brush strokes, pencil underdrawing, pinholes, aged heavy paper) vs printed (dot pattern, uniform colour).
- Flash medium: pen/watercolour/acrylic fits 1940s–50s; felt-tip pen work dates after the mid-1960s (EJ's).
- Artist initials, stamp or signature on flash; shop stamp.
- Never buy used needles/cartridges or opened ink.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Original hand-painted 1940s–50s flash, artist-marked** — three designs hammered $2,000 at EJ's Auction (2017) vs $15–50 beginner kits — watercolour on heavy paper, artist initials, pinholes and handling wear.
- **Major collection of vintage flash** — Antiques Roadshow appraisal $8,600–11,000 (auction value) — multiple original sheets, period inks.
- **Percy Waters blueprint flash (c. 1920s)** — sold $1,140 incl. premium, Potter & Potter (Oct 2026) — blueprint (blue) paper, "signed in plate", plate number like "M-17-24".
- **Named-builder vintage coil machine (Percy Waters, Spaulding & Rogers, Owen Jensen, Micky Sharpz)** — $100–1,000+ vs $15–50 kits (general knowledge, verify) — builder stamp on frame, hand-finished iron/brass frame.
- **Sailor Jerry / Bert Grimm original flash** — top of market (verify per sheet) — signature/initials, Honolulu (Collins) or St. Louis/Long Beach (Grimm) shop provenance (general knowledge, verify).
- **Genuine Cheyenne / FK Irons pro pen, working** — $150–500 vs $15–50 budget pens — engraved logo, serial number, original case/box, running video.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Pro pens: precise machining, branded packaging, serial numbers, replaceable stroke modules. Budget kits: generic laser-etched logos, cheap clip cords, off-brand ink. Vintage coil frames: cast or cut iron with stamped builder names.
- Original flash: thick paper or card, pencil underdrawing visible, ink lines with slight variation, watercolour pooling; printed copies show halftone dots or uniform colour.
- Age wear on flash (pinholes, tack holes, smudges, toning) is expected because sheets were handled daily (EJ's).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Used needles/cartridges are biohazards and unsellable; opened ink is unsellable on eBay (and expiry-dated). Motors wear out; batteries in wireless pens degrade.
- Coil machines: rewound coils, replaced springs and non-original parts lower collector value.
- Flash: tears, creases, edge losses, water damage, trimmed margins.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Counterfeit Cheyenne/FK cartridges and pens are widespread on AliExpress. Vintage machine replicas exist.
- Vintage-style flash is widely copied by modern tattooers and reprinted in books; reprints are printed, on modern paper, without pinholes (EJ's).
- Felt-tip pen work presented as 1940s–50s flash is anachronistic (felt-tip from mid-1960s).

## Market
- Pro pens sell fast; beginner kits saturated. eBay restricts sale of used needles and some inks.
- Vintage flash and machines sell to tattooers and folk-art/Americana collectors through specialist auction houses (Potter & Potter, EJ's) as well as eBay.

## What sellers usually miss
- Estate lots with vintage coil machines or old flash sheets.
- Builder stamps on coil frames; artist initials on flash.

## Shipping / handling
Small, light (2–4 lb), $10–15. Flash: ship flat between rigid boards or rolled in a tube.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Brand, model, tested video, what is new/sealed; never list used needles.
- Flash: dimensions, medium, any signature/initials, condition of edges and pinholes.

### What to tell the buyer
For pens: "[Brand model] rotary pen, serial [#], tested running (video in listing), [stroke length], includes [case/grip]; no needles or ink included." For vintage: "Vintage two-coil tattoo machine, frame stamped '[maker]', [iron/brass] frame, [original/replaced] springs and coils; sold as a collectible, not tested for use." For flash: "Original hand-painted tattoo flash, [ink and watercolour] on heavy paper, [size] in., [initials/signature], pinholes and handling wear from shop display as shown."

## Buy rule
Electronics rule: untested pens = pass. Beginner kits never reach profit ≥ $50. Only pro-brand pens with working video, or vintage coil machines/flash at a fraction of value.
