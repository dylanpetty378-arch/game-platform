## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A Russian three-string triangular-bodied lute. Sizes: piccolo, prima (~66–70 cm, the common one), secunda, alto, bass, contrabass (huge). Soviet factory student models have a flat spruce-look top with stenciled orange or black floral decoration, a small round sound hole, a back of 3–6 thin staves, a slotted or solid peghead with 3 tuners, white plastic tuner buttons, and dot inlays.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Lunacharsky Leningrad Musical Instrument Factory (label inside), Shikhovo Factory (Moscow region), Moscow Experimental Factory, Krasnodar, Borisov; 1950s–1991.
- Master-made: Nalimov (pre-revolution, museum level), Paserbsky, Zinkevich, modern Ukrainian makers. Concert models have a 6–7 rib back, ornate rosette and binding, quality tuners.
- Souvenir balalaikas (small, painted, Khokhloma decoration): decor only.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Student: plywood/softwood with a thick lacquer finish and stencil decoration, painted fingerboard. Concert: solid spruce top, maple/sycamore staves with an inlaid fingerboard, bone nut, ornate purfling, and a label with the maker's name.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Top cracks, bellied or sunken tops, lifted bridges (floating bridges often missing), fret wear, warped necks, missing tuners. Strings are cheap; a floating bridge is easily replaced.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None in the usual sense. Modern Chinese/Russian souvenir models are sold as "vintage USSR".

## Market
Small, steady. eBay plus Reverb: roughly 40–80 sold per month. Days to sell: 20–60.

## What sellers usually miss
Concert balalaikas with labelled makers sold as "old Russian guitar". A domra (round body) is sometimes confused with a balalaika, but it is a separate type.

## Shipping / handling
A prima is about 28×16×5 in, so it needs a guitar-style box; $30–45. Slacken the strings and remove the floating bridge, taping it to the body.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the label inside, and show the bridge, frets and top in raking light. Say whether the tuners hold.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$38 out − ~$38 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Student primas never qualify above ~$20. Buy only labelled master or concert instruments, at ≤ ~30% of resale.
