## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Square printed scarves: carré 90 cm (Hermès), 70 cm, 50–55 cm neckerchiefs. Brand usually printed in the design or corner and on a woven or satin label. Coach scarves of the 2000s use the "COACH est. 1941" circles, "C" signature or op-art patterns on silk twill or silk/cotton, with a small black tab label — the example is exactly this (Coach "Op Art"/circle print, c. 2008–2010, ~50 cm).
- Where the signature sits: Hermès prints "Hermès Paris", a circled ©, the design title and the artist's signature inside the design (often small and subtle) (Fellows). Pucci repeats "Emilio" through the print. Vera prints "Vera" (with or without the ladybug) in a corner. Ferragamo and Gucci print the brand name in the border or corner.
- Hermès carrés went into production a century after the house opened (1837 → 1937), created by Robert Dumas (Fellows). Every Hermès design can be traced to a year and an artist; only about 12 new designs a year (Fellows).
- Measure it: 90 cm (35"), 70 cm (27–28"), 45 cm "petit carré"/gavroche, and long narrow twillies; size is part of the identification and the price.
- Look-alikes: "100% silk" generic scarves with designer-style prints, polyester "silky" scarves, and printed-hem fakes.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Hermès: hand-rolled hems rolled toward the front, signature "Hermès Paris" and designer name, © mark; titles. Top value. Requires authentication. Notable designs/artists: "Regina" (1972, reissued 2002), Philippe Ledoux ("Cosmos"; about 90 scarves for the house) (Fellows). Auction example: Fellows "Cosmos" realised £84.50, "Regina" estimated £150–200, so older or worn Hermès can sell well below eBay medians.
- Salvatore Ferragamo, Gucci (horsebit, flora), Emilio Pucci (signed "Emilio" repeat), Chanel, YSL, Dior: $60–250.
- Vera Neumann (ladybug signature), Echo, Liberty of London: $15–60.
  - Vera dating (Adirondack Girl at Heart, from Vintage Fashion Guild usage): business started 1942 in NYC with a homemade silkscreen; late 1940s–early 1950s lowercase "v" signature, no ladybug; late 1950s uppercase "V"; ladybug added from 1959, about the size of the "V"; early 1960s "Vera" + © + ladybug; late 1960s–mid 1970s the "V" larger than a smaller ladybug; mid-to-late 1970s "Vera" + © with no ladybug; post-1993 licensed reissues (Target, Crate & Barrel) also carry a ladybug. Early scarves mostly silk; acetate and polyester from the 1960s on. Collectors chase colourways (yellows, oranges) and florals/geometrics.
- Coach, Anne Klein, Liz Claiborne, Talbots: $10–40.

### Quick ID checklist
- Hem: hand-rolled and plump, rolled toward the printed front, with thread matching the colour at each point (Hermès); flat machine or overlocked hem = lower tier or fake (HEWI).
- Signature: brand + © + artist signature + design title in the print (Hermès); "Emilio" repeat (Pucci); "Vera" + ladybug size relation (Vera dating).
- Care tag: Hermès tag in French and English only (HEWI); fibre content tag present on others ("100% silk", "Made in Italy" for Italian houses).
- Print: crisp registration, no bleeding at fine lines, pattern meets cleanly at the hem and corners.
- Size: measure edge to edge (90/70/45 cm).
- Handfeel/weight (if seller can weigh): a genuine Hermès 90 is ~63 g; fakes are often 40–52 g (HEWI).
- Condition: hold to light for holes, pulls, stains and fading on fold lines.
- "Certificate of authenticity" offered with a Hermès scarf is a warning sign; Hermès doesn't issue them (HEWI).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Silk twill feels heavy and crisp; hand-rolled hems are plump and stitched visibly; machine-hemmed flat edges indicate lower-end or fakes. Color registration sharp on genuine luxury pieces. Hermès silk twill is woven and printed in Lyon; each colour is screen-printed separately (up to ~40 colour passes on one design), giving crisp, non-oversaturated colour with subtle gradients; polyester blends feel lighter, shinier and slippery (HEWI). Stitching on a hand-rolled hem is slightly irregular, not machine-perfect. Old vs new: Vera fabric (silk vs acetate/polyester) and signature form date the piece; vintage silk is softer and may show fold-line fading.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Holes, pulls, stains, fading, perfume smell. Dry-cleaning is not a repair you should plan on. Fold-line wear and pinholes from brooches are common on vintage silk. Ask for: a photo held up to a window (holes and thinning show), corner close-ups and the tag.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Hermès, Chanel, Gucci and Louis Vuitton scarves are heavily counterfeited — needs authentication; Coach fakes exist but are low value either way. Hermès fake tells: machine/overlocked hems, contrasting hem thread, pattern misaligned at corners, flat or oversaturated colour, bleeding at fine detail, smudged signature/title, light weight, care tag in languages other than French/English (HEWI; eluxe). Hermès reissues its own designs (e.g. "Regina" 2002), so a design's first-issue year does not date the scarf. Vera has post-1993 licensed reissues with the ladybug.

## Market
Huge supply; mid-tier sells in weeks, low-tier slow. Poshmark and eBay.

## What sellers usually miss
Vintage Pucci/Vera signatures on scarves in "silk scarf lots"; Ferragamo signature woven into the border. Vera signature form (lowercase "v", no-ladybug or early-ladybug) that dates a scarf to the 1940s–60s; silk vs acetate Vera; Hermès design titles and artist names that buyers search for.

## Shipping / handling
Flat envelope, ~$5.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, size (measure), fiber tag, hem type, flaws.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Brand] [design name if printed] silk [twill] scarf, [measured size] cm/in square. Signed '[exact signature text]' in the [corner/border]; [hand-rolled / machine] hem; tag reads '[fibre and origin]'. [Vera: 'Vera' with ladybug, the signature style used from 1959 into the 1970s.] Condition: [no holes, pulls or stains when held to light / list flaws]; no perfume odour." Never state "authentic Hermès/Chanel" without professional authentication.

## Buy rule
Profit ≥ max($50, price): mall brands fail. Hermès/Chanel need professional authentication — not for Dylan. Only underpriced Ferragamo/Pucci/Gucci lots.
