## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass made in Bohemia (today's Czech Republic) from the 1700s to now. Three very different markets share the name:
- **Post-war export crystal** (1950s–present): cut or pressed lead crystal (24% PbO) bowls, vases, ashtrays, card-suit dishes, sold with a gold or silver foil label "Bohemia Crystal / Hand Cut / Lead Crystal 24% PbO / Czech Republic (or Czechoslovakia)". Diamond-and-fan cutting, sharp and shiny. This is 90% of what turns up.
- **Flash / stain and engraving** (1850s–1980s): clear glass coated with a thin ruby (red), amber, blue or green layer, then wheel-engraved through to clear — deer in forest, castles, spa towns, flowers and vines. Bedside "tumble-ups" (carafe with an upturned tumbler as the lid), decanters, goblets, steins. Egermann (Nový Bor) made the best; souvenir quality is everywhere.
- **Art glass** (1870s–1930s): Moser (Karlovy Vary) heavily enamelled and gilt pieces, faceted amethyst/Alexandrite "Royal" glass; Loetz/Lötz Witwe (Klostermühle) iridescent Art Nouveau; Harrach (Nový Svět) enamelled and gilt; Kralik, Pallme-König, Rindskopf iridescent; 1920s–30s Czech "tango" orange and black cased glass, perfume bottles with intaglio stoppers.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Moser** (Ludwig Moser & Söhne, Karlsbad, 1857–present): acid-stamped "Moser" script or "Moser Karlsbad" on later pieces; 19th-c. enamelled pieces often unsigned. Heavy facet-cut colored crystal (Alexandrit, Heliolit, Royal) 1920s–present. $150–1,500+ for real; much is attributed without proof.
- **Egermann** (Friedrich Egermann invented ruby and yellow stain, 1830s; the factory name continued in Nový Bor through the communist era): ruby flash engraved, foil label "Egermann". $40–200.
- **Harrach** (Nový Svět, 1712–present): 19th-c. enamel and gilt, cobalt with gold; postwar art glass. Rarely marked.
- **Loetz** (Klostermühle, 1836–1947): iridescent Phänomen and Papillon glass, signed "Loetz Austria" on some (most unsigned). $300–5,000. Heavily faked.
- **Postwar state firms** (1948–1990): Borské sklo, Crystalex (Nový Bor), Sklo Union (pressed glass — Rudolfova Huť, Libochovice), Bohemia Glassworks Poděbrady. Exported via Skloexport. Labels read "Bohemia Glass", "Crystalex", "Made in Czechoslovakia".
- **Pressed "Sklo Union"** designs (Frantisek Vizner, Rudolf Jurnikl, Vladislav Urban) 1960s–70s: thick geometric pressed vases and bowls, $30–150, a rising collector niche.
- **Czech Art Deco** (1918–38): "Czechoslovakia" acid stamp in a circle or oval, tango glass, cut perfume bottles, intaglio stoppers. Perfume bottles $40–400.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Flash vs cased: flashed color is a thin surface skin — at the cut edge you see a hairline of red and clear underneath; cased is a thick layer. Both are "cut-to-clear". Cased is better.
- Fine engraving is crisp, shaded, with modelled animals; souvenir work is shallow, repetitive leaf sprays and dot-flowers (like the tumble-up in our sweep).
- Old pieces: ground and polished pontil, wear ring on the base, slight asymmetry, bubbles. New: perfectly even, sharp mould lines on pressed pieces, factory foil label, no wear.
- Lead crystal rings when tapped and is heavy; pressed soda glass is lighter, with rounded "cuts" and mould seams. Card-suit dishes and many ashtrays are pressed, not cut.
- Gilding: 19th-c. gilt is soft, rubbed on high points; 1970s+ gold is bright and uniform.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Rim chips and flea bites (feel the rim), ground-down rims (a giveaway of a repair — rim looks flat-topped, height lower than its mate).
- Worn flash on high points and rims, scratched bases, dishwasher etching ("sick glass" haze).
- Stopper mismatches on decanters; tumble-up tumblers that don't fit the carafe neck.
- Enamel loss on Moser/Harrach. Cracks in iridescent glass under the surface.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Chinese "Loetz-style" iridescent glass (2000s) with fake "Loetz Austria" signatures — scratchy engraving signature, too-even iridescence.
- Fake "Moser" acid stamps added to unsigned glass.
- Ruby flash deer-and-castle glass is still made in Czechia and sold in tourist shops; also German and Chinese copies. Price as decor.
- Reissues of Sklo Union pressed designs are not common, but Czech factories still use old moulds.

## Market
Large supply, modest demand. Foil-label crystal sells slowly (weeks to months) at low prices. Ruby engraved sets move steadily to decor buyers, especially Nov–Dec. Signed Moser and Loetz sell fast at auction and to dealers. Sklo Union pressed glass is a growing MCM niche.

## What sellers usually miss
- Acid-stamped "Moser" or "Czechoslovakia" marks on the base, often very faint; ask for a base photo under a raking light.
- Iridescent art glass listed as "carnival glass" or "oil-slick vase" — a ground pontil and threaded or oil-spot iridescence points to Loetz-era Bohemian.
- 1960s–70s geometric pressed vases (Sklo Union) listed as "heavy glass vase".
- Reverse: sellers call any red cut glass "antique Bohemian" and price it at $60–150.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile. Small dishes $10–14 (double-boxed). Carafes and decanters 2–4 lb, $14–20 double-boxed. Decanter sets 6–10 lb, $25–35. Bubble wrap each piece separately; stoppers wrapped apart from the bottle.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- State flash vs cased if known, engraving subject, dimensions, the base (pontil, marks, wear), and any foil label.
- Show a backlit photo for color and a raking light for engraving and chips.
- Don't call souvenir ruby glass "Egermann" or "Moser" without a mark. Say "Bohemian style, mid-20th century" when unsure.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). With small glass shipping ~$12–18 each way, a piece must sell for ~$110+ at a $20 buy. That rules out foil-label crystal and most ruby souvenir glass. Buy: acid-signed Moser, Loetz-type iridescent vases with ground pontils, 19th-c. enamelled gilt Bohemian beakers, signed Egermann boxed sets, at ≤ 25% of sold. All fit in a passenger seat. No authentication service needed but Loetz attribution is a judgment call — buy only with signature or textbook decor.
