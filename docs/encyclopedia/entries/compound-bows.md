## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Machined aluminium riser (cut-outs, camo or anodised), two cam wheels, cables and a cable slide. Brand is machined or decalled on the riser (Hoyt, Mathews, Bowtech, PSE, Bear, Elite, Prime). Hoyt target/hunting line 2002–2008: UltraTec, ProTec, Vectrix, Trykon, with "XT2000/XT3000" limb lengths printed on the limbs. Sellers typically say "compound bow", "hunting bow", "Hoyt", list draw weight, sometimes "needs string". Mathews single-cam bows have one large cam and a round idler wheel.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hoyt** (Salt Lake City): UltraTec, ProTec, MAGNATec, Vectrix, Trykon; XT2000/XT3000 limbs. Long-ATA target Hoyts still wanted by target archers.
- **Mathews**: Switchback, Drenalin (single-cam classics); current flagships top tier.
- **Bowtech**: Guardian and others.
- **PSE, Bear, Diamond (Bowtech)**: mid-market hunting bows $80–180.
- **Elite, Prime**: high-end, sought when < 5 yrs old.
- Junk: 1980s wheel bows (Bear Whitetail, PSE Nova), Buckmaster, big-box youth bows.
- Accessories: spare limb sets (Hoyt XT3000/XT2000) $60–120 a pair; sights, Doinker stabilisers, releases $15–50 each; Sure-Loc, Spot Hogg sights premium.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Machined (not cast) aluminium risers, quality cam machining, laminated or solid composite limbs; camo dip or anodised finish. Draw weight and draw length are printed on limb/cam labels or modules. Older bows have heavier, longer risers and wheels rather than cams (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked/splintered limbs or cracked cams are safety issues – unsellable as a shooter. Frayed or serving-separated strings/cables are normal ("needs string", $60–90 at a pro shop). Check limb pockets, axle bushings, and for dry-fire history (bent cams, derailed string). Ask for close-ups of limbs (both faces) and cams.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not a fake-prone category; the risks are mis-stated models and bows with replacement limbs of the wrong weight (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Steady; peaks before fall hunting season (Aug–Oct). Buyers: hunters on a budget, target archers, ArcheryTalk and FB archery groups (no-fee venues). Bows depreciate fast as new models release yearly (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Model name is only machined into the riser; sellers list "Hoyt bow" with no model. Extra limb sets and target accessories (Doinker, Sure-Loc, Spot Hogg) are worth more than the seller thinks. "Strings need replacing" is normal and buyers expect to restring ($60–90 at a pro shop) – list it honestly as "needs string".

## Shipping / handling
Bow in soft case ~6–8 lb but 40" long: ships UPS Ground/USPS in a long box $30–50. Do not dry-fire. Resell on eBay (13%) or ArcheryTalk/Facebook archery groups (no fee). Some carriers have rules on shipping bows – check (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, model, year, hand (RH/LH), draw weight range, draw length/module, ATA, limb model, string condition, included accessories. Photograph riser model marking, limb labels, both cams, full bow, accessories.

## Buy rule
Buy when: named Hoyt/Mathews/Bowtech/Elite under $60 complete with sight and rest, or under $100 with spare limbs/case. Pass when: no-name, 1980s wheel bow, cracked limbs or cam, or price over $120 unless < 5 yrs old. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (a 40" bow case does).
