## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Antique (pre-1900)**: hand-cut tin plate with soldered seams (lumpy solder lines), wire-reinforced rolled edges, hand-blown wavy glass with bubbles or horn panes, ring handle, conical punched-tin top with vent holes, crimped candle socket, often a cone "chimney". Wood-framed "barn" lanterns with pegged joints. Patina: dark grey/brown tin oxidation, rust spotting.
- **Folding candle lanterns** (Stonebridge 1908–, UCO, Coleman Candle Lantern, military): brass/aluminium frame with mica windows, fold flat.
- **Modern decor** (1990s+): machine-folded galvanised steel or zinc finish (uniform grey "antiqued" paint/wax), flat float glass with perfectly straight edges, crisp spot welds, glued glass, big pillar-candle platform, often LED or with plastic hooks. The example matches this: uniform crisp joints, clear float glass, galvanised grey, generic socket.

## Makers, brands and variants
Antique tinsmiths largely unmarked; marked makers include Dietz (mostly kerosene), Adams & Westlake (railroad), Perkins Marine (ship), Stonebridge (folding, Chicago), Coleman/UCO (folding), Hitchcock. Modern decor: Pottery Barn, Crate & Barrel, Threshold, Hobby Lobby, Park Designs.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-soldered seams, hand-cut glass, irregular punched patterns, natural tin oxide = old. Uniform paint, staples/spot welds, rivets with Phillips screws, laser-perfect cutouts, "Made in China/India" stickers = new. India-made "antique-style" lanterns are aged artificially (heavy dark wash, deliberate rust).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust-through, broken/replaced panes (modern glass in old frame), resoldered seams, missing doors/handles.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian/Chinese reproductions of punched-tin and ship lanterns are everywhere (aged finishes, "Paul Revere" pierced pattern with machine-regular holes). Treat any "antique ship lantern" in brass with a maritime brand name as a repro unless proven.

## Market
Decor lanterns: huge supply, slow. Genuine early lighting: collector niche (Rushlight Club), auction-driven.

## What sellers usually miss
Stonebridge folding lanterns (look like flat brass/mica junk), genuine early soldered-tin lanterns in barn lots.

## Shipping / handling
Glass panes break; double-box, 3–6 lb, $15–25. Folding lanterns ship easily.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Describe construction (soldered vs welded), glass type, markings, dimensions; label decor pieces as decor.

## Buy rule
Modern decor lanterns resale $15–35 → pass. Antique soldered-tin examples at ≤ $40 with conservative resale ≥ $150 only; passenger-seat sized.
