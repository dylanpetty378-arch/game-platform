## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Synchronous (AC) electric clocks from the 1930s–1970s, with a cord and no key. Photo tells:
- **Telechron / GE**: "Telechron" or "GE" logo on dial, red second hand (Telechron trademark), Bakelite or Catalin cases (Deco), later plastic. Model numbers on bottom/back (e.g. 2H07, 7H241, 2130KA).
- **United Clock Co.** (Brooklyn, 1940s–60s): pot-metal figural cases with copper or gold wash — majorettes, horses, ships, cowboy, ballerina; "United Self Starting" on dial; some are motion clocks (moving figure or water/fire wheel). United also made numbered wall and mantel models (e.g. Model 40 pocket-watch wall clock, Model 45 gold-leaf wall clock, Model 99 wall clock), with "United Clock Corp. Brooklyn" on the back (Chairish, plusmodels listings).
- **Mastercrafters** (Chicago): motion clocks — Swinging Girl, Blacksmith, Fireplace; plastic with metal. (The Made in Chicago Museum list covers Swing Girl, Fireplace, Magic Lite, Waterfall, Bird on a Swing, Swinging Playmates, Yankee Clipper, Horse Over Clock and Model 308 "Dorell"; "Blacksmith" is not on it — verify the maker on any Blacksmith clock.)
- **Lanshire, Sessions, Westclox, Spartus**: kitchen and figural.
- **Howard Miller / George Nelson designs (1949–1960s)**: Ball clock, Sunburst, Asterisk, Spike, Eye, Kite. Paper/foil Howard Miller label and Kienzle or GE movements. Vitra reissues since 1999.
- **MCM starburst** (Welby, Elgin, Verichron, Syroco): wood/brass starburst wall clocks, many battery-converted.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Telechron** (Ashland, MA, 1912–1992; GE from 1943): Deco models (Electrolarm, Cathedral, 4F) $50–300; Catalin/Bakelite up to $500. clockhistory.com has a searchable Telechron/GE model list: match the model number stamped on the case to confirm the exact model and year.
- **General Electric**: 1950s–60s lighted kitchen and desk clocks like 2130KA (lighted, plastic with fabric-look insert) $15–45.
- **United Clock Corp.**: majorette, Indian chief, horses, Lucky Seven, Rodeo motion clocks: static figural $30–80, motion $80–200.
- **Mastercrafters**: motion clocks $60–250 working. Company history (Made in Chicago Museum): founded 1939 at 216 N. Clinton St., Chicago (Ben and Kate Lerman); Mastercrafters Clock & Radio Co. from 1947; Mastercrafters Clock Corp. from 1964; moved to 1750 W. Fulton St. in 1959 under Doris and Bernard Ellman; sold 1980 and 1985; production ended by 1988; 5–6 million clocks made. Swing Girl: patent applied 1948, Tenite plastic case in walnut or onyx finish, ~10¾" × 7¾" × 3", ~3½ lb, lit garden scene, $15.95 retail in 1950; the same swing action is in Bird on a Swing and Swinging Playmates. Fireplace: light-motion fire, made about 20 years with Sessions, Sunbeam and other movements. Magic Lite and Waterfall: light-motion in the Swing Girl case, sold as late as 1970. Model 308 "Dorell" (1955) was ruled confusingly similar to the Atmos clock. Movements and model numbers are inconsistent, so the same-looking clock can have different works; some dials read "Clock Movement by Sessions".
- **Howard Miller (George Nelson)**: originals $300–1,500; Vitra reissues $150–400. Timeline (Vitra): Nelson commissioned in 1947; the first collection of 14 wall and table clocks launched 1949; George Nelson Associates (with Irving Harper doing much of the design work) created 100+ clocks for Howard Miller over about 35 years; Vitra has reissued them since 1999 and has 24 models in production.
- **Westclox, Sessions, Seth Thomas, Hamilton, Lux, Kit-Cat** (Kit-Cat old ones $40–100).

### Quick ID checklist
- Cord and no key-winding arbor = electric; check the dial/back for maker and the model number.
- Telechron: red second hand, "Telechron" dial, model number on the base. Look up the model on clockhistory.com and read the rotor date code if the back is open.
- Motion clock: look for a window, an illuminated scene, or a moving figure. Swing Girl and Waterfall are Tenite cases about 10¾" tall.
- United: copper or gold wash on pot metal, "United" on the dial, "Brooklyn" on the back, and a model number.
- Nelson/Howard Miller: a Howard Miller label on the back. On a Ball clock, check that the ball tips and spokes are all original and that it has an AC cord. An impressed or printed Vitra mark means it is a reissue.
- Battery conversion: a modern quartz box behind the dial and no cord. This halves the value of collector models.
- Catalin: translucent marbled or butterscotch/red cast resin, heavier than plastic. Bakelite is usually brown or black and opaque.
- Ask for a video of it running and of the second hand turning.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Bakelite/Catalin and early plastics (heavy, warm feel) = Deco premium. Lightweight styrene = 1960s+.
- Figural pot metal: original copper or gold wash with patina vs. later spray paint.
- Originals: original cord with cloth or early rubber insulation, matching mounting labels, original movement (not battery conversion; battery conversion cuts value 50% on collector models).
- **Dating Telechron rotors** (telechronclock.com): the rotor carries a date code. Late 1920s–1933 = month then 2-digit year ("6 30" = June 1930). c. 1933–1940 and 1941–49 = 1-digit year then month ("510" = October 1935; "35" = May 1943 — the decade is told by rotor style). 1950–1990 = year digit, month, decade digit ("8115" = November 1958). Late codes starting with 9 (e.g. 9007) are probably lot numbers. Rotor types: brass-bodied B-2 is common in early and middle eras; F rotors are early; H-3 with brass faces from the 1930s; aluminium-bodied rotors are late.
- Tenite (Mastercrafters) cases are thin and crack; wiring is fragile, which is why working Swing Girls are uncommon (Made in Chicago Museum).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Motors seize (gummy oil) — hums but doesn't run; replacement rotors are available but count as repair. Frayed cords (fire risk), cracked crystals, faded dials, missing second hands, corroded pot metal, cracked Bakelite.
- Motion clocks: check the motion separately from timekeeping; a clock can keep time while the swing or light effect is dead. Ask for a video of both.
- A replaced rotor with a date code later than the case's era is a repair, not a defect, but say so.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
George Nelson designs are reissued (Vitra) and knocked off (cheap Chinese copies on Amazon). Originals: Howard Miller label, wooden ball tips painted, brass/steel spokes, AC motor. Kit-Cat reissued continuously since 1960s.
- Vitra reissues (since 1999) are legitimate licensed products but sell at the lower tier ($150–400); quartz battery movement = reissue or copy.

## Market
Steady but slow; MCM decorators drive Nelson/starburst demand. Common clocks sit for weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
Howard Miller originals at garage-sale prices; motion clocks listed as "old clock" (check for moving parts or a window); Catalin colors (butterscotch, red) under plastic dust. Also: a readable Telechron rotor date code that proves age, and United model numbers on the back.

## Shipping / handling
Desk clocks 2–5 lb, ship $12–20; pad crystals; figural metal is robust but the figure can bend at the ankles. Tenite motion-clock cases crack: double box. Nelson clocks with spokes ship in oversize boxes; remove the hands if possible.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model, whether it runs and keeps time (tested how many hours), cord condition, and original or converted movement.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the maker and model number as printed on the clock, and the movement maker if it is different (for example Sessions in a Mastercrafters). State how many hours it ran on test and whether it kept time. For motion clocks, say whether the motion or light effect works, separately from timekeeping. Describe the cord: original cloth or rubber, or replaced. Say whether the movement is original AC or a battery conversion. For Telechron, quote the rotor date code and what it decodes to. For a Nelson design, say whether it is a Howard Miller original (label on the back) or a Vitra reissue (Vitra has made them since 1999). Measure height, width and depth.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$15 ship out − ~$15 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). Only Nelson-design originals and working motion clocks or Catalin Deco qualify; electric clocks are electronics — must be shown running. Fits a passenger seat. No authentication.
