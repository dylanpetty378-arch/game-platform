## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A double-edge (DE) or single-edge (SE) safety razor: a head (cap + guard) that clamps a disposable blade, on a short metal handle. Valuable versions are identified by head type and mechanism:
- **3-piece Old Type (1904–1921)**: separate cap, guard and handle; open-comb guard (teeth like a comb); often in a small hinged metal or leatherette case with velvet lining. Handle with fine knurling and ring/ball ends. Gold-plated or nickel.
- **New Improved (1921–1930)**: 3-piece with a solid bar, hinged-end details, often with a diamond-pattern handle.
- **TTO (twist-to-open, butterfly) 1934+**: one-piece; knob at the handle bottom opens "doors". Super Speed (1947–80s), Aristocrat (1940s–50s, often gold), Toggle (1960s), Fat Boy (adjustable, 1958–62), Slim adjustable (1962–70s), Black Beauty, Red Tip/Blue Tip Super Speeds.
- **Adjustable dial** (numbers 1–9 on a ring under the head) = Fat Boy or Slim — the most demanded users' razors.
- **Single-edge**: Gem, Ever-Ready (banjo-like heads), Schick injectors (narrow injector blade).
- Cases matter: Gillette sets in fitted brass/leatherette cases with original blade packets, gold "travel" kits, Aristocrat in red/blue plush cases.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Gillette (Boston, 1903–)**: Old Type (1904–21; earliest "single ring" 1904–05 are rare), Pocket Edition gold-plated 1908–1920s in metal cases, New Improved 1921–30, Tech (1938–70s, cheap), Ball End Tech, Aristocrat (1940s–50s; "Aristocrat Jr."), Super Speed (date codes: letter + number on handle base from 1955: e.g. A1 = Q1 1955… E4 etc.), Toggle, Fat Boy (date codes 1958–62), Slim (1962–70s), Lady Gillette (gold, 1960s), President, Senator, Rocket. Date codes stamped in the head plate from 1955 to 1970s let you date a razor to a quarter.
- **Gem / Ever-Ready (American Safety Razor Co.)**: single-edge; Gem Micromatic (1930s–50s) $15–40; Gem Junior/1912 $10–30.
- **Schick**: injectors (Type E/G, Eversharp hydromagic) $15–50.
- **Rolls Razor (UK)**: hone-and-strop in a box; $40–120 complete.
- **Valet AutoStrop**: strop-sharpened; $20–60.
- **Wilkinson Sword "Sticky"**, **Merkur (Germany)** older models, **Rex Ambassador** (rare adjustable, $300+), **Mergress**, **Hoffritz**.
- Store brands/clones: Rexall, Kroger, Durham Duplex, Gillette-patent licensees; usually low value.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Gold plate (often worn to brass on high spots) on premium Gillettes; nickel plating on common ones; brass base metal. Plastic handles (Tech) = cheap.
- Gold-plated 1910s Pocket Edition sets in engraved metal cases with intact velvet and blades sell better than loose razors.
- Strong tells for value: adjustable dial, gold TTO with date code, original box/case and paper inserts, early patent dates (1904) on the guard.
- Pitting under plating, plating loss ("brassing") and bent doors lower value sharply for shaving users.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Bent TTO doors or misaligned head (shaves badly) — major defect; check photos of the head from the side.
- Brassing (plating worn through), pitting, corrosion around the blade bar.
- Replated razors exist (gold or rhodium replating by hobbyists) — disclose; sells to users, less to collectors.
- Cases: velvet fading/moth damage, broken hinges/clasps, missing blade boxes.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Few fakes. Modern reissues (Merkur 34C, Parker, Rockwell, Edwin Jagger, Chinese "Old Type" clones such as Yaqi/Weishi) can be mistaken for vintage. Chinese 3-piece clones have no Gillette patent stamping and a crisp modern finish. Some sellers marry cases and razors.

## Market
Steady: hundreds of vintage razors sell per month on eBay; the wet-shaving community buys users (Fat Boy, Slim, Super Speed Red Tip). Collectors pay for early/rare cased sets. Good adjustables sell within days; Old Types sit.

## What sellers usually miss
- The dial numbers 1–9 under the head (Fat Boy/Slim) — sellers list as "old Gillette razor".
- The date code on the head (e.g. "E1") confirming 1950s–60s production.
- Gold Aristocrat vs common gold-tone Tech.
- Valuable blade packets (old Gillette Blue Blade, Rexall, Pal) in the case; full sealed blade packs sell to collectors.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket/shoebox size, <1 lb. Ship in a small padded box; wrap the head so doors are not bent. ~$6–10 USPS Ground Advantage.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State model, date code or patent dates, plating condition with close photos, whether doors align and the mechanism turns smoothly, and whether the case/blades are original. Say "not tested for shaving; cleaned/not cleaned". Do not call clones Gillette.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$8 ship to buyer − ~$8 ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). That needs a sold median of about $105+ at $25 and $160+ at $75 — i.e. only adjustables at $20–40, gold Aristocrat/cased early sets at ≤ $40, or rare models. Pass on Old Type, Tech, Super Speed above ~$15. No authentication issue. Fits a car seat easily.
