## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Plates removed from 18th–19th c. natural history books. Production methods by value:
- **Hand-colored engravings/lithographs** (pre-1870): watercolor washes on black-line prints; colors slightly overlap lines; plate mark (embossed rectangle) for engravings.
- **Chromolithographs** (1870s–1910s): printed color from multiple stones; dot/grain pattern under magnification; uniform colors; German books (Brehms Tierleben, Naumann "Naturgeschichte der Vögel Mitteleuropas" 1896–1905, Meyers Konversations-Lexikon) produced vast numbers; typical book-page size ~7×10".
- **Modern offset reproductions**: rosette dot pattern, bright white paper, often "Audubon" — low value.

## Makers, brands and variants
Audubon (Havell folio 1827–38; octavo 1840–44 Bowen lithographs; Bien 1860), John Gould (large folio lithographs, hand-colored, by Richter/Hart), J.G. Keulemans, Naumann (German birds, 1896–1905, chromolith), Brehm (animals), Buffon (French hand-colored engravings), Jardine/Lizars, Alexander Wilson, Catesby, Thorburn, Lear.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Plate captions in German ("Taf.", "Geier", species names) point to Brehm/Naumann; plate numbers and artist names in corners; laid/wove paper aging; verso text show-through (book plates often have text on back). Original period frames (Victorian Eastlake/ebonized/gilt liner, carved oak) can carry more value than the print.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Foxing, toning, mat burn, trimmed margins, water stains, glued-down to acidic board, broken frame corners, cracked glass. Re-framing costs exceed value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Countless modern reproductions; 1940s–80s Audubon prints (Macmillan, Amsterdam Audubon). Use a loupe for dot patterns; check verso text.

## Market
Steady decorator demand for loose plates (cheap, often in lots); framed glass pieces ship badly, sell slowly locally.

## What sellers usually miss
Gould/Audubon octavo plates mixed in with decorator prints; valuable Victorian frames (Eastlake, ebonized with painted/engraved liners).

## Shipping / handling
Framed with glass 16–20" — risky; remove glass or use Plexi; $20–35. Loose plates in flat mailers $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Identify book/source, plate number, method (chromolithograph vs hand-colored), size, frame age, flaws.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Framed German chromolith (~$70 median, $25+$25 shipping) never qualifies. Hand-colored Audubon octavo/Gould folio only at ≤ 1/3 of sold. Fits a car seat if < ~30".
