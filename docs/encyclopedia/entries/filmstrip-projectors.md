## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Projectors for 35mm filmstrips (rolls of still frames) used in schools 1930s-1990s. Boxy wrinkle-finish (brown, gray, tan) housings with a lamp chimney, a lens barrel, a filmstrip channel and supply reel arm on top or side, sometimes a slide carrier. DuKane sound versions include a record turntable.

Generations:
- **1920s–1930s "Picturol" era** — small cylindrical or upright black-enamel or nickel projectors, often in a fitted wooden case; SVE (Society for Visual Education, Chicago) coined "Picturol" for its filmstrip rolls and projectors. Models in the Eastman Museum include Picturol E.A.Q., E.A.Q.-2, E.A.F., E.A.G. and K (all c.1938), plus Q and CC. The Henry Ford's E.A.Q. (1935–1950) is inscribed "MFD BY SOCIETY for VISUAL EDUCATION, INC." and also names Electro Acoustic Products Co., Fort Wayne, Indiana, with a serial number.
- **1930s–1950s lantern/filmstrip combos** — Spencer Lens Co. (Buffalo NY) Delineascope (models D, M, MK3, SA) and Filmascope; black sheet-metal or crackle-finish bodies with chrome trim; some take glass lantern slides too.
- **1950s–1970s classroom units** — wrinkle-finish cast aluminium in tan, grey or brown; Viewlex, Standard (500 series), SVE, Bell & Howell, Graflex/GAF, DuKane.
- **1950s–1980s sound filmstrip units** — DuKane combined a filmstrip projector with a record player (e.g. Radiomuseum lists the DuKane 14A230C "Film Projector with Record Player", c.1952; the Eastman Museum holds a DuKane 14A240E); later units used cassettes with inaudible advance tones.

Where the mark sits: a nameplate or cast lettering on the side or lamp housing, a model/serial plate underneath. Look-alikes: 2×2 slide projectors (no filmstrip channel), 8mm/16mm movie projectors (reels and sprocketed gate), opaque projectors, and magic lanterns.

## Makers, brands and variants
DuKane (St. Charles IL), Standard Projector & Equipment Co. (Standard 500 series), SVE (Society for Visual Education, Picturol), Viewlex, Bell & Howell, Graflex/GAF, Spencer Lens Co. (Deco).

- **SVE / Society for Visual Education (Chicago)** — the originator of the filmstrip format in US schools; Picturol projectors were built for SVE by Electro Acoustic Products Co. of Fort Wayne (Henry Ford inscription). Picturol models: E.A.Q., E.A.Q.-2, E.A.F., E.A.G., K (c.1938), Q, CC.
- **Spencer Lens Co. (Buffalo NY; later American Optical)** — Delineascope D, M, MK3, SA; Filmascope M. The Ingenium Delineascope D is light sheet metal, black finish, 34 × 14 × 28 cm.
- **DuKane Corp. (St. Charles IL)** — sound filmstrip projectors and school PA/intercom gear; models carry codes like 14A230C, 14A240E.
- **Standard Projector & Equipment Co.** — Standard 500 series classroom projectors.
- **Viewlex**, **Bell & Howell**, **Graflex/GAF** — 1950s–80s classroom units; the commonest finds.

### Quick ID checklist
- Filmstrip channel/gate and a supply-reel arm present (not a slide-only projector)?
- Maker plate: SVE "Picturol", Spencer "Delineascope"/"Filmascope", DuKane model code, Viewlex, Bell & Howell, GAF.
- Body: black enamel/nickel and a fitted wooden case = 1930s–40s; wrinkle-finish tan/grey aluminium = 1950s–70s; plastic = 1970s+.
- Record turntable or cassette deck built in = DuKane-type sound unit.
- Lamp: is the bulb present, and is it shown lit? Lamp type printed on the housing.
- Lens: clear, or hazed/fungus?
- Cord: original cloth cord (needs replacing) or later rubber/plastic.
- Case, manual, spare lamp and filmstrip canisters in the photo?

### Golden-egg tells
- 1930s SVE Picturol in its original fitted case, complete and working — caution: Terapeak shows Picturols mostly sell $14.99–28 (p25–p75, n=9), so the $60–150 early tier is unproven for Picturols; only a complete cased working example is a candidate — black or nickel cylindrical body, "Picturol" and SVE name, wooden case with lid.
- Spencer Delineascope / Filmascope with chrome Deco styling — $60–150 Deco tier vs $20–60 classroom units (general knowledge, verify) — "Spencer Lens Co. Buffalo" plate; chrome trim; streamlined housing.
- Working DuKane record-player sound filmstrip unit with records/filmstrips — $40–120 vs $20–60 untested classroom units (general knowledge, verify) — built-in turntable; DuKane model plate.
- Lots of filmstrips with licensed content (Disney, Peanuts) — can out-earn the projector (general knowledge, verify) — canister labels showing Disney or Peanuts titles; record or cassette included.
- Any unit shown with lamp lit and image on a wall — moves untested ($20–60) to working ($40–90) — a photo or video of the projected frame.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast aluminum bodies with wrinkle finish; original cases add value; working lamps (DEK/CZX bulbs) are increasingly expensive.

- 1930s: pressed sheet steel with black enamel or nickel plating, glass condenser lenses, wooden carrying cases.
- 1950s–60s: cast aluminium with wrinkle (crinkle) paint, coated lenses, fan-cooled lamps.
- Quality tells: heavy cast bodies, coated glass lenses, smooth focus helical, intact heat-absorbing glass. Cheap later units use plastic housings and simpler lenses.
- Old vs new: cloth cords and two-prong plugs = pre-1960s; three-prong plugs and plastic bodies = later.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dead lamps, frayed cords, seized fans, hazed lenses. Electrical testing required.

- Missing or cracked heat-absorbing glass lets the lamp burn filmstrips.
- Seized fan motors cause overheating; never run a unit with a stalled fan.
- Missing filmstrip gate/channel or reel arm makes it a display piece.
- DuKane sound units: dried idler wheels, dead amplifiers, worn styli.
- Ask for a photo of the lamp lit and the image on a wall, plus the lamp type.

## Market
Niche (nostalgia teachers, prop houses); slow. Projectionists and early-media collectors want 1930s Picturol and Spencer units; classroom nostalgia buyers want working 1960s–70s units with filmstrips.

## What sellers usually miss
Old filmstrip collections (sets of canisters) - Disney/Peanuts filmstrips have collectors. Also the maker of early Picturols (SVE/Electro Acoustic) and the fitted case.

## Shipping / handling
10-15 lb, $25-35; lens protected. Remove the lamp and pack separately; brace the lens barrel.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model, lamp works, cord condition, accessories.

### What to tell the buyer
Give the maker and model exactly as on the plate (e.g. "SVE Picturol Model E.A.Q., Society for Visual Education, Chicago") and its approximate date. State plainly whether it has been tested: lamp lights, fan runs, image focuses (include a photo of the projected frame if possible). Name the lamp type, describe the cord (original cloth cords should be replaced before regular use), and list accessories: case, manual, spare lamp, filmstrips (with titles). Note lens haze, paint wear and any missing parts. Measure it, and photograph the nameplate, the filmstrip gate and the lens.

## Buy rule
Untested electronics rule applies. At $100 it can never clear the rule. Pass. Only a cased 1930s Picturol/Delineascope or a working DuKane with licensed filmstrips at ≤ $20.
