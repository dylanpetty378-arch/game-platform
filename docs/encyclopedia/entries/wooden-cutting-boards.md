## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Hardwood (maple, walnut, teak, oak, sycamore) boards. Collectible types:
  - **Animal-shaped boards** (pigs, fish, chickens, mice — 1950s–70s); Digsmed made a "mouse" charcuterie board with button eyes.
  - **Mid-century Danish teak boards** with stamps (Dansk Designs by Jens Quistgaard, Digsmed, Kalmar). Often **staved** (strips of teak glued edge to edge, visible as parallel stripes), sometimes with a slanted/canted edge, juice groove, or a carved well.
  - **Antique European carved bread boards**, round, with a carved rim of wheat ears and mottoes ("Give us this day our daily bread", "Bread"), usually English 19th c. (sycamore is the classic wood: pale, fine-grained; general knowledge, verify).
  - **French planche à pain** boards, some with an attached iron knife on a pivot.
  - **Primitive American breadboards** with hand-plane marks and knife scoring; breadboard-end (cleated) construction.
- **Where the mark sits:** Dansk boards are branded/stamped on the underside "DANSK DESIGNS DENMARK IHQ" (most common), sometimes "DANSK International Designs Ltd. IHQ Denmark"; later production carries "Thailand" (1stDibs). Digsmed: "DIGSMED DENMARK" / "Danmark" stamp on the underside (1stDibs, Chairish).
- Title words: "teak", "staved", "Danish modern", "MCM", "Quistgaard", "IHQ", "primitive", "farmhouse", "pig", "breadboard".
- Look-alikes: modern acacia "teak-look" boards (Target/HomeGoods), new distressed farmhouse boards.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Dansk / Jens H. Quistgaard ("IHQ")**: 1950s–70s teak serving trays and cutting boards; stamp "Dansk Designs Denmark IHQ". "Rare Woods" line pieces name the species (e.g., "Mutenye") on the verso. Later pieces made in Thailand. Dealer asking prices for the broad Dansk IHQ category on 1stDibs run $220–2,975, average ~$395 (asking, broad category, not boards specifically).
- **Digsmed (Flemming Digsmed, Digsmed Designs of Denmark)**: 1950s–70s teak tableware: cutting/charcuterie boards, salad bowls, trays, lazy Susans, spice racks; one dealer says the factory closed in 1975 (1stDibs). Novelty shapes (mouse board, fish platter). Marked "Digsmed Denmark". 1stDibs asking range $250–695, avg ~$317 (dealer asking).
- **Goodwood** (Thailand teak 1960s–70s), **Catskill Craftsmen** (NY), **Snow River** (Vermont), **John Boos** (butcher block, Effingham IL), Bennington-region makers. Kalmar (listed in the old entry; verify). Primitive/folk boards unbranded.
- **English carved bread boards (19th c.)** and **French planche à pain** (an 18th-c. example with an attached wrought-iron knife on 1stDibs). 1stDibs antique bread boards: asking $220–3,675, avg ~$417.

### Quick ID checklist
- Flip it: is there a branded stamp? "Dansk Designs Denmark IHQ" or "Digsmed Denmark" = the money tier; "Thailand" = later/lower.
- Wood: teak (oily, golden-brown, straight grain) vs acacia (blotchy, wild grain) vs maple/oak.
- Staved strips with tight glue lines and a crisp profile = Danish production.
- Carved rim with wheat ears or a motto = English bread board; check for even wear on the carving.
- Primitive: uneven thickness, plane tracks, knife scoring concentrated in the middle, breadboard ends with cut or square nails / wooden pegs.
- Shape: animals (pig, fish, mouse, chicken) — look for age (dark patina, scoring) vs new crisp cuts.
- Look for warping, splits along glue lines, and sanding that erased patina.
- Size and weight: large dough boards and bowls may be too bulky; confirm dimensions.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Dansk Quistgaard IHQ staved teak board/tray, Denmark-marked** — ~3–6× a generic board ($25–80+ sold vs $8–25; dealers ask $220+) — branded "DANSK DESIGNS DENMARK IHQ" underside, striped staved teak.
- **Dansk "Rare Woods" pieces** — above standard IHQ teak (dealer asking) — species name (e.g., "Mutenye") stamped beside the Dansk mark.
- **Digsmed figural board (mouse, fish)** — premium over a plain Digsmed board (dealer asking $250+) — "Digsmed Denmark" stamp, animal outline, inset eyes.
- **19th-c. English carved bread board with motto** — ~$60–200 sold vs $8–25 — round, carved wheat-ear rim, "Give us this day…", pale sycamore with worn carving high points.
- **French planche à pain with original attached knife** — upper tier (dealer asking well above $220) — iron knife hinged to the board end, hand-forged blade.
- **Large early primitive dough board/bowl with old surface** — $150–400 — hand-hewn, deep old scoring, dry/dark surface; check size rule.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Old: dry silvery or dark oiled patina, deep knife scoring, uneven thickness, hand-cut profiles, square nails/cut nails on breadboard ends. New: crisp CNC edges, uniform finish, glued strip construction, plywood.
- Danish teak: oil finish (not varnish), tight factory glue lines, clean router profiles, branded rather than printed marks; teak dries pale grey when neglected and darkens when re-oiled.
- Over-oiled or lacquered antique primitives lose the dry surface collectors pay for.
- Wear placement: genuine scoring is heaviest in the centre on the working side and the underside is clean; fakes have even, random distressing on both faces.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Cracks/splits, warping, mould, deep stains, glue line separation. Over-sanding destroys antique value.
- Teak boards: separated staves, water rings, dishwasher bleaching and raised grain.
- Repairs show as fresh glue squeeze-out, mismatched colour along a stave, or new screws/dowels.
- Ask: any cracks or loose staves, has it been sanded or refinished, is the stamp legible.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Distressed "farmhouse" boards made new (Hobby Lobby/Etsy) mimic primitives; check tool marks and wear location.
- Dansk reissued some Quistgaard designs in the 2000s–2010s (general knowledge, verify); reissues carry modern Dansk marks without "IHQ Denmark".
- New carved bread boards with mottos are made for décor; sharp carving and even stain give them away.

## Shipping / handling
Light to moderate; $10–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Wood, dimensions, maker stamp, food-safe finish (mineral oil), cracks.
- Photograph the stamp, both faces, edges/glue lines and any scoring.

### What to tell the buyer
"Mid-century [Dansk / Digsmed] solid teak [cutting board / serving tray], [size] in., stamped '[exact mark]' on the underside. Staved teak construction, [oil finish refreshed with food-safe mineral oil / original finish]. [No cracks or loose staves / small separation at one end, shown]. Jens Quistgaard designed for Dansk from the 1950s; 'Denmark IHQ' marks are earlier than the later Thailand production." For antiques: "19th-century [English carved / primitive] bread board, [wood], hand-carved [motto], honest wear and knife marks consistent with age; not sanded or refinished."

Generic boards fail; pass. Buy Dansk/Digsmed teak at ≤ $20 when median ≥ $90, or antique primitives similarly.
