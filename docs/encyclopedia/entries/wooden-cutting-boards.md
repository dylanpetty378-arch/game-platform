## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hardwood (maple, walnut, teak, oak) boards. Collectible types: animal-shaped boards (pigs, fish, chickens — 1950s–70s), mid-century Danish teak boards with stamps (Dansk Designs by Jens Quistgaard, Digsmed, Kalmar), antique European carved bread boards ("Give us this day our daily bread"), primitive American breadboards with hand-plane marks and knife scoring.

## Makers, brands and variants
Dansk (Quistgaard, stamped "Dansk Designs Denmark"/"IHQ"), Digsmed (Denmark), Goodwood (Thailand teak 1960s–70s), Catskill Craftsmen, Snow River (Vermont), John Boos (butcher block, IL), Bennington-region makers. Primitive/folk boards unbranded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: dry silvery or dark oiled patina, deep knife scoring, uneven thickness, hand-cut profiles, square nails/cut nails on breadboard ends. New: crisp CNC edges, uniform finish, glued strip construction, plywood.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracks/splits, warping, mould, deep stains, glue line separation. Over-sanding destroys antique value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Distressed "farmhouse" boards made new (Hobby Lobby/Etsy) mimic primitives; check tool marks and wear location.
