## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tin-glazed (or, in the 20th c., often white-body earthenware) square tiles, usually 5 in / 13 cm square, painted in cobalt blue ("Delfts blauw"), manganese purple, or polychrome. Subjects: windmills, canals, sailing ships, children's games, Bible scenes, flowers, animals in a circle with corner motifs ("ox-head" or "spider" corners). A tableau is several tiles painted as one picture, usually 2×2, 2×3, 3×4, mounted in a wood frame. In photos look for: brush strokes that cross tile joins slightly misaligned (hand painted then fired separately), small glaze pooling, varied line weight. Transfer prints show perfectly even dots/lines and identical repeats.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **De Porceleyne Fles (Royal Delft)**, Delft, 1653–present. Back mark: blue jug/vase over "Delft" with a painted date-letter code and painter initials. Tableaux by named painters bring the most. Date letters from 1879 onward let you date pieces.
- **Royal Tichelaar Makkum** (Friesland), active since 1500s/1600s. Mark: "T" with "M" or "Makkum" and a crowned cartouche. Known for polychrome and manganese tiles, also faithful reproductions of 17th c. tiles.
- **Westraven** (Utrecht, c. 1930s–1980s?). Polychrome and blue landscape tiles and tableaux, often marked "Westraven Holland" with a painted signature/number on the back. Common on Craigslist; mid-market (general knowledge, verify dates).
- **Ravesteijn** (Utrecht, 1900s–1970s) — similar landscape tiles, framed sets.
- **De Delftse Pauw**, **De Blauwe Fles**, "Delft Blue Holland" souvenir shops (1950s–now): mostly hand-decorated or transfer souvenir tiles, low value.
- **Antique 17th–18th c. tiles** (Rotterdam, Harlingen, Utrecht, Delft): thick (≈ 7–11 mm), soft buff/pink body, nail holes in the corners (from the template used for pouncing), worn crazed glaze, mortar residue on back. Sold singly or in lots; whole antique tableaux are rare and expensive.
- Look-alikes: German "Delfter", English Minton/Wedgwood transfer tiles, Japanese/Taiwanese 1970s "Delft" ceramics, and Portuguese azulejos (different style).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Hand-painted 20th c. tableau: visible brushwork, painter signature or monogram lower corner, painted number on the back, glossy glaze over colour.
- Back of tile: maker stamp, body colour (white = modern earthenware, buff/pink = older), ribbed or plain back. Antique tiles are hand-cut and slightly irregular.
- Frame: original Dutch oak or dark stained frames are typical; tiles set in grout or plaster. Pine/MDF frames suggest US re-framing.
- Quality: fine detail in clouds and reflections, shaded figures, perspective; cheap ones have flat colour fills and blobby figures.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips at tile corners and edges (very common, can hide under the frame lip), cracks running across one tile, grout loss, glaze crazing (acceptable on antique), repainting/overpainting of chips, frames re-glued. Tiles may be loose in the frame — a transit risk. Water damage from use as a trivet or wall set near stove.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Makkum and Royal Delft both make reproductions of antique designs — legitimate but modern. Taiwanese and Chinese "Delft" souvenir tiles 1970s–now. Antique "17th c." tiles are faked by artificially aging modern tiles (fake nail holes, tea-stained bodies); real wear is uneven and the glaze is pitted. Fake Royal Delft marks appear on Asian pottery: check the date-letter code and painter initials.

## Market
Steady but thin. Framed tableaux sell in days–weeks if well photographed; buyers are Dutch-heritage collectors and decorators. Antique tile lots have a dedicated collector base (Netherlands, US). Sell-through moderate (est. 30–45%).

## What sellers usually miss
- Royal Delft date-letter codes and painter initials on the back (they often list "Delft tile" with no back photo).
- Antique tiles with nail holes in the corners listed as "old tile".
- Manganese (purple) antique tiles mistaken for faded/odd colour.
- Lots of loose antique tiles from old fireplace surrounds.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy (a 6-tile framed panel ≈ 6–10 lb). Double box, foam board front and back, check tiles are tight in the frame (shim if loose). UPS Ground ≈ $20–30. Never ship with glass on (remove or tape).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the back (stamps, signatures, numbers), count tiles and give overall and per-tile size, state each chip. Say "hand painted" only when brush strokes are visible. Give maker and approximate decade, note "20th century" for Westraven/Ravesteijn types.
