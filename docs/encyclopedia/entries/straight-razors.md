## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A folding razor: blade pivoting on a pin between two "scales" (handles). Photo tells:
- **Blade**: maker etching or stamp on tang/blade face, width (5/8" common, 6/8"+ more valuable), grind (full hollow = 20th c. Solingen; wedge/near-wedge = older Sheffield), spine profile, toe (round/square/spike/French).
- **Tang stamps**: "Germany" (post-1891), "Solingen", "Sheffield", "Made in USA" (post-1914 country-of-origin era).
- **Scales**: celluloid (often marbled, amber "faux ivory" yellowed), hard rubber/vulcanite (black, often "horn" pattern), bone, real horn (translucent), ivory (needs CITES caution), plastic (1950s+).
- **Boxes**: original pasteboard boxes with dealer/retailer names (e.g. "P. Wichert, 337 Girard Ave, Philadelphia" — a Philadelphia cutler/retailer that sold German-made razors under its own name and model names like "Star"). Retailer-branded boxes are common in 1890s–1930s US.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Solingen**: J.A. Henckels (Twin Works), Böker (Tree brand, "King Cutter"), Dovo, Puma, Wacker, Friodur (Henckels stainless), Herder, Bismarck, Kropp (English-branded but Solingen-made after 1920s), Hessenmeister, C. Friedr. Ern; also hundreds of US retailer brands (Wichert, Torrey's imported lines, Wester Bros, Clauss).
- **Sheffield**: Wade & Butcher (Celebrated Hollow Ground, "For the Barbers' Use"), Joseph Rodgers, George Wostenholm I*XL, Joseph Elliot, Frederick Reynolds, Thomas Turner. Big near-wedges $80–300.
- **US**: Case Bros., Robeson Shuredge, Geneva Cutlery, Shumate, Torrey, Dubl Duck (Goldedge, Wonderedge — with Japanese "Sumo" etching high), Genco, Clauss, "Spike" (a cheap brand from the 1920s–40s, celluloid scales with nail logo, low value).
- **Modern**: Dovo, Thiers-Issard, Boker, Ralf Aust (new $150–400; modern resale $80–250).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Etched blade lettering intact (gold-washed etching on NOS), bright polish, original box with paper "ready for shaving" inspection label = NOS / near-NOS.
- Edge: look for chips, "frown" (edge worn concave), heavy pitting at the edge = restoration-only.
- Hone wear on spine shows use; heavy hone wear lowers value.
- Scales: cracked celluloid, warped, and "celluloid rot" (outgassing that rusts the blade) are common.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust and pitting (the main value killer), chipped/cracked blades (fatal), cracked or missing scales, loose pins, honing wear. Restoration (rescaling, polishing, honing) is a repair — outside Dylan's rule; buyers ("restorers") do buy rusty named razors cheaply.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake Wade & Butcher etchings exist on cheap Pakistani razors; modern Pakistani "Damascus" razors flood eBay. Check for tang country stamp and real age.

## Market
Very liquid on eBay: active shaving and restoration communities buy daily. Lots of 5–10 razors sell within days; restorers pay $8–20 each for rusty named razors.

## What sellers usually miss
NOS boxed razors, big Sheffield wedges, 7-day sets and Dubl Duck/Wade & Butcher in a "grandpa's razors" lot. Sellers price per lot, not per razor.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket-size; a lot of 9 ships under 2 lb in a padded box, $8–12. Blades must be closed and taped/sleeved.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, country, width, grind, scale material, edge condition (no chips, honing wear), rust/pitting, box present, NOS or used, shave-ready or not.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$10 ship out − ~$10 ship in − price ≥ max($50, price). For lots: ≤ $10 per razor when blades look clean and named. Sell individually (best) or as lots. Fits a passenger seat. No authentication. Avoid ivory scales (legal issues).
