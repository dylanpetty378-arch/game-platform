## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A cup with a ledge ("mustache guard") across part of the inside rim, with a small opening to sip through, made c. 1860–1920 to protect waxed moustaches. Invented by English potter Harvey Adams (1860s). In photos, you need a top-down view to see the guard; from the side it looks like an ordinary cup. Common decoration: gilt "Think of Me", "Remember Me", "Present" lettering; hand-painted flowers; raised gold beading; lustre.

## Makers, brands and variants
- German and Austrian porcelain (Carl Tielsch, RS Prussia/RS Germany, Victoria Austria, unmarked Bavaria).
- French Limoges.
- English Staffordshire ironstone and bone china.
- American ironstone, majolica (Etruscan/Griffen Smith & Hill), silver-plate quadruple plate (Meriden, Reed & Barton, Rogers) — silver-plated cups sell well.
- **Left-handed cups** (guard placed for left hand) — scarcer, premium.
- Modern reproductions 1970s–present (Japan, China), often with "souvenir" sayings.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Victorian: hand-applied gilt, enamel jewelling, slightly thick translucent porcelain, worn gilt from use, unmarked or impressed numbers. Modern: decal-perfect transfers, bright gold, often stamped "Japan" or "China".

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken mustache guard edge (common), rim chips, hairline cracks, gilt wear, stains. Repairs at the guard are common.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
1960s–80s Japanese and Chinese reproductions are the majority on the market; often thick, sharp edges, and modern backstamps.

## Market
Small, steady collector market; sells slowly at moderate prices; gift buyers in barbering/beard community.

## What sellers usually miss
Left-handed guards; silver-plate cups listed as "old metal cup"; RS Prussia moulds without marks.

## Shipping / handling
0.5–1 lb; protect the guard, double box, $9–12.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "mustache cup" + maker (RS Prussia, Limoges, majolica, quadruple plate) + "left handed" if so + "with saucer" + "Victorian" / "antique". Junk: "moustache mug", "shaving mug", "old cup". Example: `Antique RS Prussia Mustache Cup & Saucer Hand Painted Gilt Victorian Left Handed`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (small collector market, sells slowly at moderate prices — auctions end at $15); FB local only for lots. Nothing sells it for more than eBay.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median for the tier (plain Victorian $15–40, hand-painted with saucer $35–90, majolica/plate/RS Prussia $75–250, left-handed $150+); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) top-down into the cup showing the guard; then mark on the base, side with gilt lettering, saucer front/back, guard edge and rim close-up for chips, hand holding it for scale.
- **State:** maker/mark (or "unmarked, impressed numbers"), left- or right-handed guard, saucer included or not, height and rim diameter, hand-painted vs transfer, gilt wear, chips/hairlines/guard repairs by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 0.5–1 lb; wrap the guard separately, double-box in a 6×6×6 inside an 8×8×8, under-2-lb class, $9–12.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "This is a [maker], c. [1880–1910] Victorian moustache cup with the original [saucer]; [left-handed] guards are the scarce version and clean [majolica/silver-plate] examples sell for [$X] — this one has no chips or repairs."
