## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Real glass-tube neon on a metal or acrylic backer, with a transformer brick on the cord – distinct from LED "neon-look" rope signs (flat, uniform glow, no visible glass bends). Brewery promo signs (Coors, Bud, Miller, Pabst, Shiner, Lone Star) from the 1980s–2000s are the bulk of what shows up. Neon wall clocks (Cheerwine, Coke, Route 66) are mostly 1990s–2010s licensed reproductions. See also `advertising-signs`.

Where to look for dating information: a maker's label or metal plate on the back or frame (many signs carry a sticker or plate with the manufacture date), the transformer's metal tag, and any brewery "official" marking (Collectors Weekly thread). Unlit photos show the glass: look for round tubes, hand-made bends, black "blockout" paint on tube sections that should not glow, and electrode housings (thicker capped ends) where tubes end.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Brewery promo neons** – Coors, Budweiser, Miller, Pabst, Shiner, Stroh's: 1980s–2000s common tier.
- **Regional/defunct brands** – Lone Star, Hamm's, Olympia (and similar): collectible, especially 1950s–70s.
- **Neon clocks** – Cheerwine, Coca-Cola, Route 66: mostly licensed 1990s–2010s, $60–120.
- **Pre-war / porcelain-backed neon** – top tier, local only. Example of the type: 1950s porcelain-enamel "Drink Grain Belt Beer – On Tap" sign with neon on a porcelain face (1stDibs dealer listing — asking, not sold).
- Sign makers' labels (e.g. Everbrite, Lakeshore) on the back help date them (general knowledge, verify).
- **Everbrite (Milwaukee area)** – the dominant beer-sign maker. Founded March 1927 in Milwaukee as Sheet Metal Products, Inc.; made beer signs from the early 1930s as Prohibition ended; renamed Everbrite Electric Signs, Inc. in 1935; headquarters in South Milwaukee from 1963; moved HQ to **Greenfield, WI in the late 1980s**; renamed Everbrite, Inc. in the 1990s; Everbrite, LLC from 2003; added a Shenzhen, China plant in 2005 (Everbrite history). Practical dating: an Everbrite label with a South Milwaukee address points to roughly 1963–late 1980s; a Greenfield address points to late 1980s or later (Collectors Weekly comment + Everbrite history).
- **Transformers**: many older US signs used "Franceformer" transformers (France brand); newer Anheuser-Busch signs are mass-produced in China (Collectors Weekly comment, collector opinion).

### Quick ID checklist
- Unlit photo shows round glass tubes with bends and electrode ends = real neon; flat flexible strips = LED "neon".
- Separate heavy transformer box (often on the cord or behind the sign) = neon; small wall-wart adapter = LED.
- Back label: maker (Everbrite etc.), city (South Milwaukee vs Greenfield), date sticker or plate.
- Transformer tag: "SER" + 3–4 digits may encode year/month (e.g. 383 = March 1983) — only if the transformer is original (collector comment, verify).
- Backer: porcelain or painted steel "can" = older; black-painted wire grid frame or acrylic = 1980s+ (general knowledge, verify).
- Brand: regional/defunct (Hamm's, Lone Star, Olympia, Grain Belt) beats national (Coors, Bud, Miller).
- Ask for a lit video showing every section glowing evenly without flicker.
- Measure: anything bigger than a passenger seat is excluded.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real neon: hand-bent glass tubes, electrodes at the ends, a transformer box, visible tube supports. LED: flat flexible strips, uniform glow, low-voltage adapter. Older signs: metal "can" or backer, heavier transformers, cloth/rubber cords; newer: lightweight black-painted wire frame (general knowledge, verify).

How colours are made (Kings of Neon): pure neon gas glows red-orange; argon with a trace of mercury glows blue; other colours come from phosphor coatings inside the tube (e.g. argon + blue phosphor → purple; yellow-coated tube → green). Glass neon runs at high voltage (roughly 3,000–15,000 V), which is why it needs a transformer; LED "neon" runs at 12–24 V. A section that glows pinkish/dim where it should be blue or white usually means lost mercury/gas — it needs re-pumping (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or cracked tubes, dead sections (gas leak), failed transformer, flickering. Re-tubing costs real money; a dead section on a common sign makes it junk. Ask for a video of it lit (general knowledge, verify).

Also: missing tube supports/standoffs, chipped blockout paint (bright spots where it should be dark), replaced transformers (fine for use but removes the original transformer date tag), frayed cords, cracked acrylic backers, and porcelain chips on porcelain-faced signs. Glass neon typically lasts 8–15 years with maintenance (Kings of Neon), so a 1990s sign that "works" may be near the end of its gas life.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Licensed reproduction neon clocks (Cheerwine, Coke) are the common case. LED "neon" passed off as neon. Modern reproductions of vintage brewery neons exist (general knowledge, verify).

Also: Terapeak results for vintage brand names include new LED "neon" signs — e.g. a "16x15 Lone Star Beer Shield LED Neon" and a "New Texas Lone Star Neon Light Sign" both appear in the Lone Star query below — so title words "vintage" and "neon" prove nothing. Signs without an "official" brewery marking may have been consumer retail items rather than distributor-supplied bar signs (collector inference, Collectors Weekly).

## Market
Bar/man-cave decorators; very common supply. Best sold locally (Facebook Marketplace/Craigslist) because shipping is risky (general knowledge, verify). Gift-season demand may lift prices in Nov–Dec (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The brand and the fact it's real neon. Rarely an arbitrage item – sellers see "Coors neon" value. Only worth it when the sign is a scarce regional brand under $40. Also the back label/transformer date, which can move a sign from "90s" to "70s" in the listing.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile glass – shipping neon is the deal-killer ($40–70 with proper boxing, high breakage). Treat as local-only unless profit is $150+.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State brand, size, real neon vs LED, working (lit video), number of colours/sections, maker label and date, transformer condition. Photograph it lit and unlit, the back label, and the transformer. Keywords: brand + "neon sign" + "vintage" + size + "working" + "bar sign".

### What to tell the buyer
"Real glass neon (not LED), original transformer" — only if verified from the unlit glass and transformer. Give the maker and address from the back label and what it implies (e.g. "Everbrite, Greenfield WI label — late 1980s or later"), any date sticker/plate, overall dimensions, number of colours and whether every section lights evenly (link a lit video). Disclose any dim/flickering section, chipped blockout paint or replaced transformer. Say "local pickup" for signs you will not ship.

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1970s-or-earlier or scarce-regional neon under $40 and the sign is local. Pass when: common 1990s brewery neon over $40, any neon that must ship. Large signs bigger than a passenger seat are excluded by the size rule. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
