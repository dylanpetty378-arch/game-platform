## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Real glass-tube neon on a metal or acrylic backer, with a transformer brick on the cord – distinct from LED "neon-look" rope signs (flat, uniform glow, no visible glass bends). Brewery promo signs (Coors, Bud, Miller, Pabst, Shiner, Lone Star) from the 1980s–2000s are the bulk of what shows up. Neon wall clocks (Cheerwine, Coke, Route 66) are mostly 1990s–2010s licensed reproductions. See also `advertising-signs`.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Brewery promo neons** – Coors, Budweiser, Miller, Pabst, Shiner, Stroh's: 1980s–2000s common tier.
- **Regional/defunct brands** – Lone Star, Hamm's, Olympia (and similar): collectible, especially 1950s–70s.
- **Neon clocks** – Cheerwine, Coca-Cola, Route 66: mostly licensed 1990s–2010s, $60–120.
- **Pre-war / porcelain-backed neon** – top tier, local only.
- Sign makers' labels (e.g. Everbrite, Lakeshore) on the back help date them (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real neon: hand-bent glass tubes, electrodes at the ends, a transformer box, visible tube supports. LED: flat flexible strips, uniform glow, low-voltage adapter. Older signs: metal "can" or backer, heavier transformers, cloth/rubber cords; newer: lightweight black-painted wire frame (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or cracked tubes, dead sections (gas leak), failed transformer, flickering. Re-tubing costs real money; a dead section on a common sign makes it junk. Ask for a video of it lit (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Licensed reproduction neon clocks (Cheerwine, Coke) are the common case. LED "neon" passed off as neon. Modern reproductions of vintage brewery neons exist (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Bar/man-cave decorators; very common supply. Best sold locally (Facebook Marketplace/Craigslist) because shipping is risky (general knowledge, verify). Gift-season demand may lift prices in Nov–Dec (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The brand and the fact it's real neon. Rarely an arbitrage item – sellers see "Coors neon" value. Only worth it when the sign is a scarce regional brand under $40.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile glass – shipping neon is the deal-killer ($40–70 with proper boxing, high breakage). Treat as local-only unless profit is $150+.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State brand, size, real neon vs LED, working (lit video), number of colours/sections, maker label and date, transformer condition. Photograph it lit and unlit, the back label, and the transformer. Keywords: brand + "neon sign" + "vintage" + size + "working" + "bar sign".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1970s-or-earlier or scarce-regional neon under $40 and the sign is local. Pass when: common 1990s brewery neon over $40, any neon that must ship. Large signs bigger than a passenger seat are excluded by the size rule. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways.
