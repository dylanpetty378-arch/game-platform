## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Plates sold as collectibles, usually transfer-printed decals of paintings, with backstamps stating series, issue number, artist, "limited edition" and firing days; often with box and certificate of authenticity (COA). The boom ran 1970s–1990s; values collapsed in the 2000s.
- **Bradford Exchange / Hamilton / Knowles / Franklin Mint / Danbury**: 8–9" plates, gold rims, series numbers, mail-order; box and COA.
- **Royal Copenhagen and Bing & Grøndahl Christmas plates**: blue-and-white underglaze, year in the design; issued every year since 1895 (B&G) and 1908 (RC).
- **Royal Worcester "Fabulous Birds" (1970s)**, Wedgwood jasper Christmas plates, Lenox Boehm birds, Goebel Hummel annual plates (since 1971), Gorham Rockwell plates.
- **Audubon plates**: decal or transfer prints of Audubon birds on generic porcelain (Japan, Taiwan, Bavaria), dinner-plate size; decorative only unless part of a good service.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Bradford Exchange** (Niles IL, 1973–): artists Lena Liu, Thomas Kinkade, Sandra Kuck, Rob Sauber; backstamp numbered; $3–15 each.
- **Hamilton Collection**, **Edwin M. Knowles** (made for Bradford; Wizard of Oz, Gone With the Wind series), **Franklin Mint** (also sterling silver plates in the 1970s: melt value).
- **Bing & Grøndahl** (1895–) and **Royal Copenhagen** (1908–): pre-1915 issues $50–400; 1895 B&G "Behind the Frozen Window" $3,000+; post-1950 $5–25.
- **Royal Worcester**: "Fabulous Birds" series (1976–), Kingsbury and Doughty bird plates; $10–30.
- **Lalique**: 1965 "Deux Oiseaux" first annual crystal plate $50–150.
- **Rosenthal Bjørn Wiinblad Christmas plates (1971–)**: $40–150 early issues.
- **Gorham Rockwell**, **Goebel Hummel**, **Anri**, **Lladró**: $5–30.
- **Sterling silver collector plates** (Franklin Mint, Lunt, Reed & Barton, Towle 1970s): valued by silver weight, often $100–300 each. These are the real hunting target.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Read the backstamp: "Sterling" or ".925" = silver plate collectible worth melt. "Hand painted" on old service plates vs "decal".
- RC/B&G: year visible in the design; pre-1915 thinner and slightly greyer porcelain.
- Transfers: dot or line screen visible under zoom; true hand-painting shows brushstrokes and slight relief of enamels.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on rims, gold rim wear, scratches from plate hangers (spring-wire hangers mark rims), missing boxes/COAs (reduce already-low value by ~30%).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not commonly faked because values are low. "Limited edition" by firing days means huge runs (tens of thousands). Some sellers present decal "Audubon" plates as Audubon originals.

## Market
Collapsed and oversupplied; thrift stores and estates are full of them. Lots move slowly at $1–5 per plate. Sterling and early Danish plates are the exceptions.

## What sellers usually miss
- Sterling silver plates from Franklin Mint/Reed & Barton mixed in with porcelain ones.
- Pre-1915 B&G/RC Christmas plates in "blue plate" lots.
- They overestimate everything else based on original issue price and "limited edition".

## Shipping / handling
Light but fragile; $10–15 each with box. Multiple plates ship in a dish-pack.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State series, issue number, artist, year; include box/COA; disclose hanger marks. Price in lots.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 to buyer − ~$12 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Porcelain limited-edition plates: pass at any price.
- Sterling collector plates: buy only if price ≤ melt × 0.6 (ask weight and "Sterling" stamp photo).
- Pre-1915 RC/B&G: buy at ≤ $20 when year confirmed and resale ≥ $100.
