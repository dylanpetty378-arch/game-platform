## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
St Louis-made industrial task lamps, 1940s–70s. Two families: (1) "Floating Fixture" – long twin-tube fluorescent head on a spring-balanced double arm, clamp or weighted base, tan/brown/grey hammertone; label "Dazor Mfg. Corp. St. Louis" on the head. (2) "Flying saucer" / UFO incandescent desk lamps (models 2004, 2008, 1102) – domed round shade on a slim arm, 1950s, the ones designers pay for. Also M-209/M-210 magnifier lamps.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Dazor Manufacturing Corp., St. Louis** (founded 1930s; still makes task lighting – general knowledge, verify).
- Floating Fixture fluorescents (clamp base vs weighted desk base vs rolling floor base M-210).
- Saucer/UFO incandescent models 2004, 2008 (sold $479), 1102 (sold $257 – recorded as "swivel desk lamp" in the comp and as a saucer model in the ladder; verify the form).
- M-209 magnifier lamp ($120), M-210 rolling floor lamp ($100–195).
- Look-alikes: Luxo L-1 (Norway/US, spring arm, round shade), Ledu, O.C. White, Faries – related industrial lamps (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Steel arms and springs, cast iron weighted bases, hammertone enamel (tan, brown, grey, green). Original finish with honest wear sells better to designers than repaint; professional powder-coat restorations sell at decorator prices on 1stDibs. Label plate on the head identifies model. Check original cloth or rubber cord (often brittle – rewire needed) (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Bent arms, weak/broken springs (arm won't hold position), missing clamp, cracked diffuser, failed ballast on fluorescents, brittle wiring (safety – rewire), rust. Ask for a photo of the label, a working shot, and the arm holding an extended position.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No significant fakes; Dazor's later production and generic articulating lamps get mis-titled as vintage Dazor (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Industrial/MCM decor buyers and designers; workshop users buy cheap working units. eBay and Etsy for mid tier, 1stDibs/Chairish for restored saucers. Moderate volume; saucers are the liquid, high-value piece (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The word "Dazor" – sellers write "vintage articulating fluorescent lamp" or "drafting lamp". Check the head label. Saucer shape = top tier.

## Shipping / handling
Floating fixture: ~10 lb but 3 ft long and springs – box 40"×10"×8", $30–45 each way. Saucer desk lamps ~6 lb, $20. Sell on eBay/Etsy; fees 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model number from the label, base type, finish colour and originality, working status (tested), wiring condition, reach/arm length and height. Photograph the label, arm extended, base, shade/head inside, and the lamp lit.

## Buy rule
Buy when: saucer/UFO model under $100, or floating fixture with weighted base under $40. Pass when: clamp-base fluorescent over $40 (6786 math: $120 sale − 13% − $45 in − $45 out − $45 price ≈ −$30, i.e. a loss; the earlier note said "≈ $0"). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (floor/rolling models are excluded).
