## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Periodicals; value is set by cover subject, title, date and condition. Tells in photos: cover subject (celebrity, event), issue number/date, mailing label (reduces value), cover price (older = lower price), spine wear. Children's and hobby magazines (Highlights, Barbie, Boys' Life) are mostly low value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Life** (weekly 1936–1972): Marilyn Monroe, JFK assassination, Beatles, Apollo covers sell $15–60; ordinary covers $3–8.
- **Saturday Evening Post**: Norman Rockwell covers $10–40.
- **Playboy** (1953–): Dec 1953 #1 $1,000+; 1950s issues $20–100; 1970s+ $3–15.
- **Famous Monsters of Filmland**, **Mad** (1950s), **Rolling Stone** (1967–early 70s tabloid), **TV Guide** (1950s–60s, celebrity covers).
- **Fashion**: Vogue, Harper's Bazaar (1930s–60s with Avedon, Penn, Dalí art) $30–200.
- **Pulps** (1920s–50s: Weird Tales, Amazing Stories, Black Mask): $30–2,000+.
- **Highlights for Children** (1946–): "fun with a purpose", sold by subscription in huge numbers; 1950s–70s issues $3–12.
- **Barbie: The Magazine for Girls** (Mattel/Marvel, 1980s–90s): $5–20 each; Barbie Bazaar (collector magazine) also cheap.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original vs reprint: reprints of famous issues (Life moon landing, Playboy #1 facsimile) say "reprint" or have modern paper. Newsstand copies (no label) beat subscription copies.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Mailing labels, clipped coupons, torn covers, water damage, mould, smoking smells, detached staples, missing pages (common in children's magazines with activities completed).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Facsimile reprints of Playboy #1, Life moon issues, Action/Superman-related magazines; "commemorative" reissues.

## Market
Huge supply; most issues never sell. Key covers move steadily. Lots are slow and heavy.

## What sellers usually miss
- Ads and articles inside (e.g., first appearance of a product or a famous author's story) can make an ordinary-looking issue valuable.
- Big lots sometimes contain a key (Famous Monsters, pulps).

## Shipping / handling
Media Mail ($4–6 for one issue, ~$1/lb more for lots) only if no advertising-focused content—magazines with ads technically don't qualify for Media Mail; use Ground Advantage $6–10. Bag and board.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Issue date/number, cover subject, mailing label yes/no, completeness, condition grade, notable content.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$7 to buyer − ~$7 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Single common issues never qualify. Buy keys with confirmed ≥ $80 comps at ≤ $15, or lots with several visible keys at ≤ $1 per issue. Pass on children's magazines.
