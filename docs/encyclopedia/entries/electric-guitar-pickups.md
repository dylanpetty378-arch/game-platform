## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Loose humbuckers/single coils with leads. Tells: **Gibson** modern (490R/498T, Burstbucker, '57 Classic) have "Gibson" stamped on the baseplate and a sticker with the model; a used 490R/498T pair sells $120–180, Burstbucker Pro pair $150–220, '57 Classic pair $180–250. **Seymour Duncan** (JB/59/Pearly Gates, logo on the bobbin/baseplate) $50–90 each; **DiMarzio** $50–90; **EMG** active (black plastic covers) $50–80 each; **Fender** vintage-style singles $30–60 each, **Fender Custom Shop** sets $150–250. **Vintage money**: Gibson PAF (1957–62, "PATENT APPLIED FOR" sticker on the base, $3,000–8,000 each), Patent-number stickers 1962–75 ($500–1,500 each), T-Top ($400–800), 1950s Fender Strat/Tele pickups ($300–1,000). Unmarked ceramic pickups from import guitars $5–15.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Gibson modern**: 490R/498T (pair $120–180), Burstbucker/Burstbucker Pro (pair $150–220), '57 Classic (pair $180–250).
- **Gibson vintage**: PAF 1957–62 ($3,000–8,000 each), Patent-number 1962–75 ($500–1,500), T-Top ($400–800; T-Top period is the mid-60s–70s, overlapping the patent-number era – general knowledge, verify).
- **Seymour Duncan**: JB, '59, Pearly Gates, $50–90 each.
- **DiMarzio**: Super Distortion, PAF Pro etc., $50–90.
- **EMG** active: $50–80 each.
- **Fender**: vintage-style singles $30–60; Custom Shop sets $150–250; 1950s pre-CBS $300–1,000.
- **Boutique**: Lollar, Fralin, Bare Knuckle – collectible tier ($150–350 sets).
- **Import ceramics / Squier**: $5–20.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Alnico magnets (PAF-style, vintage) vs ceramic bar magnets (cheap imports and some hot designs); nickel or chrome covers; braided vintage lead vs 4-conductor modern leads; black butyrate bobbins on old Gibsons; date stamps or sticker types on baseplates. Long leads are worth more (fit any guitar). A multimeter reading (7–9 kΩ for PAF-style) shows it works (general knowledge on magnets/leads, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Open coil (reads infinite resistance), cut leads, rewound coils (cut vintage value hard), removed or re-soldered covers on vintage Gibsons, corroded covers, missing screws/springs. Ask for resistance reading and baseplate photo.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake PAF stickers on later pickups, re-stickered Patent-number pickups, and boutique "PAF replicas" sold as originals exist; vintage claims need baseplate, solder and bobbin inspection by someone experienced (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Fast, liquid market on Reverb and eBay; guitarists buy for swaps, so modern pickups trade near a stable used price. Vintage Gibson/Fender pickups go to collectors and builders (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
A "humbucker from an old Gibson" without the base photographed – ask for the baseplate (sticker/patent stamp). Modern Gibson sets are priced at market by their sellers.

## Shipping / handling
< 1 lb, $6–9. Reverb 8%. Electrically simple – a multimeter reading in the listing (7–9 kΩ for PAF-style) is a plus.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State brand, model, position (neck/bridge), DC resistance, lead type/length, cover type, magnet type if known, and which guitar it came out of. Photograph the baseplate (stickers, stamps), leads and covers. Title: "Gibson 490R 498T humbucker set, long leads, from 2002 SG".

## Buy rule
Buy when: any "old Gibson pickup" with a patent-number or PAF sticker under $300; boutique sets under $80. Pass when: modern Gibson/Duncan at market. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees (Reverb ~8%) and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
