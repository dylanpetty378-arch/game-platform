## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Depression glass (c.1928–1940):** machine-pressed tableware sold cheaply or given as premiums. Colours: green (often uranium – glows bright green under UV), pink, amber, yellow ("topaz"), cobalt, ruby, jadeite/opaque. Thin walls, mould seams, faint bubbles and "straw marks" are normal. Patterns are moulded all over (Cameo = a dancing girl in a garland medallion with festoons; Mayfair = open rose; Cherry Blossom; Royal Lace; Princess; Block Optic = plain vertical blocks).
- **Elegant glass (c.1925–1960):** hand-finished glass by Heisey, Cambridge, Fostoria, Tiffin, Duncan & Miller, Imperial. Fire-polished edges, ground/polished bases, etched (acid) patterns rather than moulded ones, heavier and clearer. Heisey often carries an H-in-diamond mark.
- **Mid-century pressed/novelty glass (1950s–1980s):** Westmoreland, Fenton, L.E. Smith, Indiana, Imperial, Viking. Ruby-flashed ("ruby stain") pieces have a red coating on clear glass – look for clear glass showing where the coating stops or is scratched. Amberina shades red at the rim to yellow at the base.
- **How to read a listing photo:** shape + pattern + colour identifies the piece. Then look for: a foot (stemmed/footed shapes are often the scarcer ones), a lid (lidded candy jars sell for 2–4× bases alone), roping or beading at the rim, ground bases, and a pontil.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Hocking Glass Co. (Lancaster, OH; Anchor Hocking from 1937):** Cameo/"Ballerina" (1930–34; green most collected; pink very scarce; yellow limited to place-setting pieces; crystal uncommon but little demand), Mayfair, Block Optic, Princess, Miss America, Fire-King (1940s–70s jadeite and ovenware). Cameo has 70+ green shapes; the tall candy jar base was modified into the **5¾" footed vase** (thin flared lip; the candy base has roping at the rim instead), and the **8" vase** (ovoid, no foot) was designed for the line (Just Glass).
- **Federal, Hazel-Atlas, Jeannette, MacBeth-Evans, U.S. Glass, Indiana:** the other big depression makers. Jeannette made jadeite, Cherry Blossom and Adam; Hazel-Atlas made Royal Lace and cobalt; Federal and Hazel-Atlas made most clear ribbed reamers.
- **Westmoreland (Grapeville, PA, 1889–1984):** milk glass, English Hobnail, Paneled Grape, "Swirl and Ball" ruby-flashed candy dishes, and the original "Bathing Beauty/Ballerina" soap/trinket dish. Mark "WG" monogram from c.1949; many pieces unmarked. Moulds went to other makers after 1984.
- **Mosser Glass (Cambridge, OH, 1970s–today):** reissues the Westmoreland Bathing Beauty dish in cobalt, jadeite and others (sometimes an "M" under the foot) and made Cameo miniatures in the 1980s (Collectors Weekly; Just Glass).
- **King's Crown / "Thumbprint":** first Adams & Co./U.S. Glass (1890s, EAPG), then **Tiffin-Franciscan** (1940s–60s), then **Indiana Glass** (1960s–2000s, huge production). Indiana's thumbprints tend to be rounder; Tiffin's more elongated (Glass Messages). Value is low either way.
- **L.E. Smith (Mt. Pleasant, PA):** Moon and Stars (1960s–2000s reissues of an 1880s pattern) in amberina, ruby, amber, blue, green. Very common.
- **Fenton (Williamstown, WV, 1905–2011):** hobnail, crests, carnival, hand-painted; marks "Fenton" in an oval from 1970.
- **Hollywood Regency glass on gilt-metal stands with prisms (1960s–70s):** imported, decorative; low value (a comparable sold for $15.50, Laurel Leaf Farm).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Uranium glass:** glows vivid green under a UV flashlight; this confirms green depression or Vaseline glass of the era but many modern reproductions glow too, so UV only proves uranium content.
- **Old vs new:** old depression glass has crisp but slightly soft pattern detail, light wear rings on the base, occasional bubbles and mould seams. New glass is heavier, with too-thick bases, blurry patterns and over-saturated colour.
- **Hand-finished elegant glass:** polished bases, fire-polished rims, ring when tapped (lead content), etched patterns you can feel as matte.
- **Ruby flash vs solid ruby:** flash is a stain on clear glass (look at the clear foot/stem and scratches); solid ruby glass is red all through. Flash is worth less and wears off in dishwashers.
- **Satin (frosted) finish:** factory acid finish on some Cameo and Fenton pieces; sometimes added later by owners trying to hide scratches.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Chips and flea-bites on rims, feet and lid edges; cracks radiating from the base; "sick glass" (cloudy etching inside vases and decanters that will not wash out).
- Ground rims or feet hide chips – a rim that feels flat or sharp, or a piece shorter than listed, has been ground. Ground pieces lose 50%+.
- Ruby flash and gold trim wear: worn flash is close to worthless.
- Lids: a mismatched or missing lid halves a candy jar's value. Check the lid's pattern and fit.
- No acceptable repairs. Glass with glued repairs is unsaleable.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Cameo:** only the salt and pepper shakers were reproduced (faint pattern, thick bottom; salt only fills halfway). Mosser Cameo miniatures are scale models, not fakes (Just Glass).
- **Reproduced depression patterns:** Cherry Blossom, Mayfair (shot glasses, cookie jars), Madrid ("Recollection" by Indiana, dated 1976), Sharon butter dishes, Adam butter dishes, Royal Lace cobalt, Miss America butter dishes. Check a repro guide before paying more than $40 for butter dishes, shakers and pitchers.
- **Reamers:** cobalt, green and jadeite "depression style" measuring-cup reamers are new imports – they show up as recent eBay sales at $25–33 (Terapeak, Sep–Oct 2026). Old ones have wear on the cone ridges and base.
- **Bathing Beauty dish:** Mosser still makes it new (sold new $30–37, Terapeak 2026). Old Westmoreland examples are rare and need a provenance/mark check.
- **Moon and Stars, King's Crown, Swirl and Ball:** decades of reissues; assume the newer issue.

## Market
- Depression glass prices fell 50–80% from their 1990s peak as collectors aged; ordinary pieces now sell slowly at $10–40. Scarce shapes in green Cameo, pink Mayfair, Royal Lace cobalt and jadeite still sell, mostly to completist collectors.
- Speed: common pieces weeks to months; scarce Cameo shapes days to a few weeks at the right price. Uranium/"glows" keywords bring a newer, younger audience.
- The Cameo 8" vase sells roughly monthly; the 5¾" footed vase a few times a year.

## What sellers usually miss
- **Shape matters more than pattern.** Owners price all Cameo alike; the 5¾"–6" footed vase (3½" wide, flared lip) sells for 3–5× the 8" ovoid vase.
- Lids: a lidded candy jar is worth far more than the base alone; sellers sometimes list base and lid separately.
- Old reamers in rare colours (jadeite McKee, custard, pink/green Hazel-Atlas with "Sunkist" embossing) are $40–150; clear ones are $10–20.
- Elegant glass (Heisey, Cambridge, Fostoria etched) is often lumped in with "old glass" at $5–10 a piece; certain colours (Heisey Alexandrite, Cambridge Carmen, Tiffin twilight) are worth $50–200+.
- Conversely, sellers over-price ruby flash, King's Crown, Moon and Stars, and anything "uranium" by Lens matches to asking prices.

## Shipping / handling
- Wrap each piece in 2" of bubble, double-box with 2" of peanuts between boxes. A single vase ships ~$10–14 (Ground Advantage, 2–3 lb packed). Stemware sets of 12+ pieces ship $25–40 and break often.
- Buyers of uranium glass like a UV photo in the listing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Name the maker, pattern, colour, exact shape and size ("Hocking Cameo Ballerina green 5¾" footed vase, 1930–34"), and show the pattern close-up, the base, the rim under raking light and a UV glow photo.
- State "no chips, cracks, grinding or sick glass" only after checking with a fingertip around rims and foot.
- Tell buyers it is original 1930s Hocking (not reproduced in this shape) and that pattern and colour are period-correct.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200, fits a passenger seat (all of this type does). Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 shipping to buyer − ~$12 shipping to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price).
- In practice only scarce shapes clear it: a Cameo 5¾" footed vase (median ~$100) clears it only at ≤ ~$25; at the $220 high it clears it at ≤ ~$75. Ordinary pieces (median < $60) never clear it.
- Never buy ruby flash, King's Crown, Moon and Stars, Swirl and Ball, Mosser dishes, sets of stemware, or anything reproduced in its shape without a repro check.
- Ask for height and a photo of the rim and base before buying any vase or candy jar.
