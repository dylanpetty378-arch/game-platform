## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1890–1925 Neapolitan-style mandolins with a ribbed rosewood/maple bowl back, canted spruce top, inlaid pickguard, bent-top. Thousands were made by Chicago and New York factories. Makers that matter, read off the label inside the soundhole or the headstock: **Lyon & Healy** ("Washburn", "American Conservatory", "Lakeside" labels), **Martin** (bowl-backs 1895–1920, stamped on the back of the headstock – rare and valuable), **Vega**, **Bacon**, **Vinaccia/Calace/Embergher** (Italian, signed label – collector tier), **Bruno**, **Regal**, **Stella/Oscar Schmidt**. No-label German/Italian imports are the junk tier. Tells of a better one: more ribs (17–40 narrow ribs vs 9 wide), ebony fingerboard, engraved tuner plates, pearl inlay in pickguard and fretboard, tortoise edge binding. Condition killers: separated ribs, lifted/cracked top, sunken top at the bridge, neck pulled forward (strings far off the fingerboard at the 12th fret).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Lyon & Healy (Chicago)** – American Conservatory and Lakeside (decent, $120–300), Washburn with inlay (collectible).
- **C.F. Martin** – bowl-backs c.1895–1920, stamped on headstock back; plain models collectible, fancy styles top.
- **Vega, Bacon** – collectible.
- **Vinaccia, Calace, Embergher** (Italian, signed labels) – top.
- **Bruno, Regal, Stella/Oscar Schmidt** – decent/common.
- **Unlabeled German/Italian imports** (7–9 wide ribs) – junk.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Rosewood (Brazilian on better ones) or maple ribs with fine marquetry/purfling between ribs, spruce top, ebony fingerboard on better models, engraved tuner plates, pearl and tortoise trim. Rib count is the quick tell: many narrow ribs = higher grade; 7–9 wide ribs = import. Shellac/French-polish finishes on originals (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Separated ribs, lifted/cracked top, sunken top at the bridge, neck pulled forward, missing tuner buttons, cracked pickguards (celluloid decay), worn frets. "Without strings" is neutral – stringing is $10. Ask for a side photo of the neck angle and a soundhole label photo. Neck resets and top repairs are expensive relative to value (general knowledge, verify).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No significant fakes. Labels can be missing or replaced; Martin is confirmed by the headstock stamp, not a label (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Abundant supply; buyers are decorators (wall-hangers), classical/Neapolitan mandolin players for the Italian makers, and collectors for Martin/Washburn. Slow for unlabeled pieces (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
The label (they say "antique tater bug"); the rib count; whether the top is sunken. "Without strings" is neutral – stringing is $10 – but ask for a side photo of the neck angle.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile bowl; 4–5 lb; needs a box with 3" of padding all round: $35–50 ground insured. Reverb 8% / eBay 13%. Loosen strings.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker/label, rib count, wood, playability (neck angle, action), cracks/separations, top condition, tuners, case. Photograph label, headstock front/back (Martin stamp), bowl, top around the bridge, and a side view of the neck. Keywords: maker + model + "bowl back mandolin" + "tater bug" + rib count + "Brazilian rosewood" if true.

## Buy rule
Buy when: labeled L&H/Washburn/Vega/Bacon under $60 with straight neck; any Martin stamp under $200. Pass when: unlabeled or $100+ (shipping + fragility erase the margin); any separated rib. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat (it does).
