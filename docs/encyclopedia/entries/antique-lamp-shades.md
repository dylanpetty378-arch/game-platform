## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass shades for oil, electric parlour and banquet lamps. Key types:
- 10" fitter "hurricane"/student shade: a ruffled or plain top opening on a melon/round body, sitting on a 10" (sometimes 7" or 4") fitter ring.
- GWTW ball globe: round, often hand painted with roses, sits on a matching base.
- Student shade: 7" or 10" open dome, often cased (white inside, green/cranberry/blue outside).
- Art glass tulip shades for 2 1/4" fitters (Steuben Aurene, Quezal, Lustre Art, Tiffany Favrile): iridescent, signed on the fitter rim.
- Reverse-painted shades (Handel, Pairpoint, Jefferson, Moe Bridges): painted on the inside, signed near the rim.
A modern repro tell: a perfectly even pink/peach frosting with fired-on floral decals, a molded seam, and a sharp ground rim.
- "Fitter" = the outside diameter of the bottom collar that sits in the lamp's shade holder; measure it, not the shade's widest point. Common sizes: 2¼" (electric tulip/art glass), 4", 7", 10" (oil and student lamps).
- Cased glass shows two layers at the rim edge (white inner, coloured outer); hold it to light and the colour looks even and opaque-ish. Flashed or painted colour shows as a thin skin that can be scratched off.
- Art glass tulip shades are small (Quezal examples at Corning are about 11–13 cm high, 11–13 cm wide) and mould-blown, with a polished fitter rim (CMOG).
- Reverse-painted shades look dull from outside when unlit; the scene is painted on the inner surface and the outside is often "chipped ice" textured.
- Look-alikes: modern pressed "hobnail" milk-glass shades, Asian-made GWTW globes, new production cased student shades sold as replacements.

## Makers, brands and variants
Fenton (1970s–90s pink/rose shades, often marked), L.G. Wright (repro GWTW), Aladdin/Mantle Lamp Co (1900s–1950s), Consolidated Lamp & Glass (Victorian), Fostoria (Victorian shades), Steuben, Quezal, Tiffany, Handel, Pairpoint, Jefferson, Moe Bridges, Bradley & Hubbard, Miller.
- **Aladdin / Mantle Lamp Co. of America**: founded 1908 by Victor Johnson (Aladdin trademark 1908). It bought a glass factory in 1926 and from then made its own glass lamps, shades and chimneys. Nu-Type burners from 1932; Model B burners 1933–1955 (burner knob marked "Mantle Lamp Co., Nu-Type, Model B, Aladdin … Chicago"). Merged with Aladdin Industries 1949; lamp division sold 1999 and still operates as Aladdin Mantle Lamp Co. (Clarksville, TN), so new Aladdin shades exist (Kovels). Collector club: Aladdin Knights of the Mystic Light.
- **Handel Co.** (Meriden, CT): began 1885 as Eydam & Handel, incorporated 1903; P.J. Handel died 1914 and Fannie Handel ran it; thrived into the 1930s. Shades are hand-signed, often in gilt, "Handel Co." with a pattern number (e.g. "2186") and sometimes an artist's name; birds and florals are typical, dragons and unusual scenes rarer; a shade with its matching base is worth much more than the shade alone (Journal of Antiques).
- **Quezal Art Glass and Decorating Co.** (Queens, NY, 1902–1924): iridescent mould-blown shades, wheel-engraved "Quezal" on the polished fitter rim (CMOG).
- Value nuance: signed + matching pair/set > single; pulled-feather and hooked decoration on art glass > plain iridescent; reverse-painted scenics with artist signature > florals.

### Quick ID checklist
- Measure the fitter (2¼", 4", 7", 10").
- Look at the rim edge for two layers (cased) vs one (flashed/painted).
- Decoration: brushstrokes (hand painted) vs dot-screen printed (decal).
- Mould seam lines up the side = pressed/modern.
- Check the inside of the fitter rim for an engraved or acid signature (Quezal, Steuben, Lustre Art, Tiffany).
- Reverse-painted? Look for a gilt or painted "Handel"/"Pairpoint" signature and a pattern number near the bottom rim inside.
- Run a finger-test equivalent in photos: chips, flakes or regrinding on the fitter.
- Look for "Made in Taiwan"/foil labels or a crisp, uniformly ground rim (modern).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old: hand-blown with a soft, slightly uneven ground rim, hand-painted enamel with brushstrokes, cased layers visible at the rim, acid etching, and wear on the fitter rim. New: molded with seam lines, decal transfers (crisp dotted printing under magnification), spray-frosted colour, uniform thickness.
- Iridescent art glass: genuine Quezal/Steuben iridescence is layered, with gold/blue/pink play that shifts with angle; modern iridescent shades are often a single uniform sheen.
- Signatures: Quezal is wheel-engraved on the polished fitter rim (CMOG); Handel is hand-signed (gilt common) with a number (Journal of Antiques). A crisp diamond-point or acid-stamp signature on an otherwise modern-looking shade is a red flag.
- Weight: old cased shades are heavy for size; modern thin pressed shades are light.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rim flakes on the fitter, cracks (heat-cracks from oil lamps), worn paint, and ground-down rims to remove chips (reduces value). Check for heat-stress cracks radiating from the top opening.
- Reverse painting flakes and loses pigment; in-painting shows as different gloss under raking light. Original, unrestored paint matters to Handel buyers (Journal of Antiques).
- Ask for a photo lit from inside (shows cracks and repaint) and a close-up of the fitter rim all the way round.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Huge volume of 1960s–90s GWTW-style reproductions (L.G. Wright, Fenton, Asian imports). Fake "Tiffany"/"Steuben" signatures on modern iridescent shades. "Antique" in a title rarely means anything for pink frosted decal shades.
- New cased and opal student shades are made today as replacements (Antique Lamp Supply sells a new 10" fitter green cased student shade at $140.98 retail), so a cased student shade is not automatically antique; look for wear on the fitter, period irregularity and age patina.
- Aladdin is still in business, so later Aladdin-branded shades exist alongside pre-1950s ones.

## Market
Slow except for art glass. The decal-shade tier sells a few per week at low prices; most buyers are local lamp restorers. Days to sell: 30–90.

## What sellers usually miss
Signed art glass tulip shades sold in boxes of "old light fixtures"; cased cranberry student shades listed as "pink shade"; signatures hidden on the fitter rim.
- Handel pattern numbers and artist names inside reverse-painted shades.
- Pairs/sets of matching tulip shades (worth more than singles).

## Shipping / handling
A 10" fitter shade is about 12×12×9 in, so it needs a 16–18 in double box; $18–30. Breakage risk is significant.
- Tulip shades (about 5 in) ship in a 10–12 in double box; wrap the fitter rim separately.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give the fitter diameter, height, the top-opening size, and light-through photos. Say whether it is hand painted or a decal, and whether there are chips on the fitter rim.

### What to tell the buyer
State the fitter size, height and top opening; whether the glass is cased, flashed, milk glass or clear frosted; and whether decoration is hand painted or decal. Quote any signature exactly and where it is ("wheel-engraved 'Quezal' inside the polished fitter rim"; "gilt 'Handel Co. 2186' inside the lower rim"). Describe the fitter rim honestly (chips, flakes, regrinding) and say whether the shade was checked lit from inside for cracks. For Aladdin, note the Mantle Lamp Co. made its own glass from 1926; for Quezal, the firm worked in Queens, NY, 1902–1924.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$22 out − ~$22 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Decal/modern shades never qualify. Buy cased student shades and signed art glass at ≤ ~30% of the sold median, with no fitter chips. Avoid buying "Tiffany"-signed shades without authentication; that is a professional-authentication item.
