## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glasses and mugs printed (applied colour label, ACL) or embossed with a brand. A&W mugs are heavy, clear, "dimpled" or thumbprint-panel mugs.
- **A&W dating (general knowledge, verify):** 1950s–60s "bullseye and arrow" logo (A&W in orange circle with arrow); 1970s "US map" logo with A&W in brown oval on orange map (a 1976-ish bicentennial-era design); 1980s–90s plain brown/orange oval logo. Embossed-only mugs are earlier. Baby/kids mugs (3–4") sell individually.
- **Fast-food character glasses:** Pepsi/Warner Bros Looney Tunes (1973–76), Star Wars Burger King (1977), McDonald's Garfield, etc.
- **Coca-Cola:** pre-1930 flare "syrup line" glasses are valuable; modern bell glasses are common.

## Makers, brands and variants
Libbey, Federal Glass, Anchor Hocking, Brockway (made the glasses for brands). Brands: A&W, Hires, Dad's, Frostie, Mug, IBC, Coca-Cola, Pepsi, 7Up, Dr Pepper, McDonald's, Burger King, Arby's, Pizza Hut.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Bright, unfaded ACL; no dishwasher haze; no chips at the rim or handle base. Embossing = earlier.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Faded logos from dishwashers, chips, cloudiness. Not repairable.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
A&W and Coca-Cola reissued retro designs repeatedly; Coca-Cola syrup-line glasses are reproduced. Fast-food glasses are rarely faked.

## Market
Huge supply, buyers are casual nostalgia shoppers. Slow.

## What sellers usually miss
Rare variants (misprints, regional franchise mugs) exist but are hard to spot. Generally sellers overprice.

## Shipping / handling
Heavy glass; $10–15 for three mugs, double-boxed (general knowledge, verify).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Logo version, height, ounces, maker mark on base, any fading.
