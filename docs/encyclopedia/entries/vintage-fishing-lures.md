## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Painted wooden plugs (1900s–1950s) with glass eyes, cup-and-screw hook hardware, and maker boxes (two-piece cardboard, sometimes with the lure's catalogue number). Later lures are plastic (1950s+) with painted or tack eyes (general knowledge, verify).
- Tackle-box lots: most contents are modern plastic (Rapala, Rebel, Beetle Spin) at $1–3 each.
- Seller words: "old fishing lures", "tackle box", "wooden lures", "vintage bait".
- Era bands collectors use: late 1800s to c.1920s "true antique" (scarcest before c.1915); 1930s–1950s classic/vintage; post-1950s modern vintage, mostly plastic and low value unless exceptional (Appraisily).
- Photo tells for wood: paint crazing/age lines that follow the wood grain, chips showing bare wood (not white plastic), slotted screws, a "lip" plate held by screws, glass eyes set in the head.
- Photo tells for plastic: translucent or very glossy bodies, moulded seam along the back/belly, moulded-in eyes, screw-eye hangers, no paint loss to wood.
- Look-alikes: modern "folk art" carved decor lures and resin decorative lures sold in home-decor shops; modern "Classic" reissues by Heddon/Creek Chub.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Creek Chub Bait Co.** (Garrett IN): Pikie Minnow, Wiggle Fish, Darter; numbered boxes (general knowledge, verify).
  - Company formed 1916 (idea from 1906) by Henry Dills, Carl Heinzerling and George Schulthess; first lure the "Wiggler/Wriggler" with a movable metal lip (patent filed 1915, granted 1920). Dills patented a scale finish sprayed through mesh (filed 1918, granted December 1919) — the "scale" paint on Creek Chubs. Pikie introduced 1920. Kept making wood lures after many competitors went plastic in the 1950s; experimented with plastic in the 1950s–60s. Sold to Lazy Ike Corp. (Des Moines) in 1978; the Garrett factory closed by 1979 (Wikipedia, US Lures).
  - Model numbers: Pikie = 700 series (e.g. 933 Baby Pikie seen in listings); Injured Minnow, Darter, Plunker, Baby Crawdad, Jointed Pikie are common names (US Lures, Appraisily).
- **Heddon** (Dowagiac MI): Dowagiac minnows, Vamp, Crab Wiggler, River Runt. Tiny Torpedo and Crazy Crawler are common loose ($5–15).
  - James Heddon carved his first lures in the late 1890s; firm formally established 1902. Wood dominant 1902–1930s; "Spook" plastic bodies from the late 1930s (Appraisily).
  - Most collected early pieces: pre-WWI wooden Dowagiac Minnows (100/150 series five-hook minnows). Other models collectors name: Lucky 13, Vamp/Vamp Spook, Crazy Crawler, Punkinseed (wood, 1940s–50s), River Runt Spook, Zara Spook, Torpedo/Baby Torpedo.
  - Heddon colour codes on box end labels: RH (red head/white, common), Frog/Frog Spot, PS (perch scale), X-prefix "Shore" finishes (e.g. XRY yellow shore), Strawberry Spot, Rainbow, Shiner, Goldfish, Coach Dog, luminous "Night". The same code can be common on one model and scarce on another (Appraisily).
  - River Runt Spook: pre-1950 examples often have a down-eye line tie; later moved up (Appraisily).
- **Pflueger** (Akron): Neverfail, Globe, Metalized Minnow. Neverfail has its own distinctive hook hanger.
- **South Bend, Shakespeare, Paw Paw, Jamison, Arbogast** (Jitterbug, Hula Popper), **Bomber, Fred Arbogast, Al Foss** (general knowledge, verify).
  - South Bend minnows use "toilet seat" surface hardware; Shakespeare (Kalamazoo) Revolution and Rhodes minnows; Buel and Chapman pre-1900 metal spoons/minnows (Appraisily).
- Rare: early glass-eye wood, unusual colours, "underwater minnows" with three or five hooks, and lures with the original box and paperwork (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Body material: wood (chips show wood grain) vs plastic (seam, translucent, glossy).
- Eyes: glass (domed, set in), tack (flat metal tack), painted, or moulded-in.
- Hook hanger: through-wire, cup-and-screw, L-rig/U-rig surface plate, two-piece "flap" or one-piece surface plate, "toilet seat" (South Bend), or plain screw-eye (later).
- Screws: slotted (period) vs Phillips or bright new (later/replaced).
- Box: maker name, end-label model number and colour code that match the lure.
- Prop style (on Torpedo/minnows) and whether props are maker-stamped.
- Paint: factory scale/gill marks vs repaint (paint in screw slots, over dings).
- Count of treble hooks on minnows (3 and 5-hook underwater minnows are the early ones).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Glass eyes, hand-painted gill marks, cup-and-screw or "L-rig" hook hangers, and a wooden body = older (general knowledge, verify).
- Plastic bodies, tack or painted eyes and screw-eye hangers = later.
- A box with a matching lure (catalogue number and colour code) multiplies value.
- Glass eyes are common on pre-1940s wood lures (Appraisily); Creek Chub used glass eyes on Pikies and Injured Minnows of the wood era (US Lures listings).
- Heddon hardware sequence (River Runt line, Lure Lore): two-piece surface hanger ("flap rig") on early lures → one-piece surface rig (late-1940s transition pieces mix a one-piece front with two-piece rear) → surface-mounted cup. Earlier Heddon hardware: L-rig c.1906–08, cup-rig and two-piece hardware late 1900s–early 1910s, standard screw eyes later (Appraisily).
- Belly weights with dull patina are period; bright shiny weights may be replacements.
- Wood-era scale finishes (Creek Chub's patented mesh-spray) show a fine net pattern under magnification.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Paint chips and age lines (normal), hook rash, cracked bodies, missing eyes or props, rusted hooks, repainting (kills value), and boxes with torn ends.
- Grading language: "excellent", "very good" and "good" refer to the paint.
- Collector grading also uses a 10–1 scale (10 mint, 9 near mint, 8 excellent) and MIB (mint in box) (Appraisily).
- Defects to look for: varnish crazing, hook pointers, corrosion on hardware, replaced hooks or props, re-varnish (shows as a glossy uniform coat that fluoresces under blacklight), replacement glass eyes that sit poorly.
- Over-cleaning or polishing strips patina and lowers value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Heddon and Creek Chub reissued classic lures (including "Classic" lines). Modern repaints and "folk art" reproductions with aged finishes exist. Reproduction boxes too (general knowledge, verify).
- Repaint/fake tells (Appraisily): paint in screw slots or pooling in crevices, brush strokes over dings, UV fluorescence inconsistent with age, solvent smell, bright modern screws or screw eyes that don't fit the old holes, mixed-era hardware, unstamped props where stamped ones are expected, "fantasy" colours never issued for that model.
- Box tells: end label code not matching the lure, fresh glue, uniformly "aged" cardboard, reproduction labels, wrong picture style for the era.
- Lazy Ike-era (post-1978) Creek Chub production is not the Garrett product; check box and materials.

## Market
A strong collector community (NFLCC) and decorators. eBay is the main venue; Lang's for high-end. Spring and winter collector shows (general knowledge, verify).
- Box and papers are cited as roughly 2–5× multipliers only when era, codes and condition align (Appraisily, unverified).

## What sellers usually miss
- Boxes and glass-eye wooden lures mixed into tackle-box lots.
- Box end-label codes (model + colour) that identify a scarce colour.
- Early hardware types (L-rig, two-piece flap, toilet seat) that date a lure earlier than the seller says.
- Down-eye pre-1950 River Runt Spooks.

## Shipping / handling
- Light: single lures under 0.5 lb, ship $5–8; a tackle-box lot is 5–10 lb, $15–25 (general knowledge, verify).
- Wrap hooks (cork or tubing) so they do not scratch paint or tear boxes; ship boxes flat-protected.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker, model, colour, eye type, hardware type, length, paint condition, box (end label, catalogue number).
- Photograph both sides, the belly hardware, the eyes and the box ends.
- Title keywords: "Vintage Creek Chub Pikie Glass Eye Wood Lure in Box", "Heddon Dowagiac".

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker and model with the catalogue/colour code from the box ("Creek Chub 700-series Pikie, wood, glass eyes, in matching box"). State body material (wood or plastic), eye type, hook-hanger type and screw type, length, and paint grade in collector terms, naming every chip, hook rash and crazing. Say whether hooks, props or eyes are original or replaced and whether the box matches the lure. For context, Creek Chub (Garrett, Indiana, 1916–1978) patented its scale finish in 1919; Heddon (Dowagiac, Michigan, est. 1902) moved to plastic "Spook" bodies from the late 1930s.
