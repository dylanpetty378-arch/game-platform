## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lithographed tin containers for smoking tobacco, c.1890–1980s. Tells:
- **Vertical pocket tins** (hinged lid, curved to fit a pocket) – most collected; "Push up" or "Open other side" embossed on the lid edge; net weight (1½ oz, 1⅝ oz) printed on the base front.
- **Flat/box tins** (Edgeworth rectangular with hinged lid, example 7955504538; "Larus & Bro. Co. Richmond VA").
- **Round canisters** (Sir Walter Raleigh 14 oz, Prince Albert 1 lb) – 1960s–80s "Collector's" reissue canisters (example 7935473224) are $5–15.
- **Lunch-box and pail tins** (Tiger, Fast Mail, Dixie Queen, Union Leader pail) $50–400.
- **Age tells**: pre-1930 tins have heavier, richer litho and often a "U.S. Tax Paid" stamp/strip; 1950s–70s tins add zip codes (post-1963), UPC (post-1974), and Surgeon General warnings (post-1966 on cigarette packs; tobacco tins later).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Common (high production)**: Prince Albert (R.J. Reynolds, 1907–; red; example 7951570662 five PA tins – one sun-faded to orange/yellow), Velvet (Liggett & Myers), Sir Walter Raleigh (Brown & Williamson; yellow and later orange-red variants), Union Leader (eagle and Uncle Sam), Edgeworth, Half and Half, Granger, Revelation, Briggs, Kentucky Club (jockey on horse), Tuxedo (American Tobacco; green with portrait), Model.
- **Scarce**: Forest & Stream (fisherman/canoe), Dixie Queen, Hiawatha, Bagdad (short version), Big Ben (horse), Hi-Plane (single- and four-engine planes), Buckingham, Lucky Strike roll cut (early flat), Totem, Stag, Cavalier, Picobac, Taxi Cab, Pedro – $50–2,000+.
- **Racist imagery** brands are prohibited on eBay – do not buy.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Grade (1–10) controls value: 8.5+ = bright, minimal scratches, no rust, working hinge. Lids that won't close, rust spots, dents, fading and scratches drop value 50–90%.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust, dents, scratched litho, sun-fading (one red Prince Albert in example 7951570662 has faded to orange-yellow), broken hinges, missing lids, paint touch-up.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese reproduction pocket tins of scarce brands (Forest & Stream, Hiawatha, Dixie Queen) since the 2000s – too-bright colours, no maker imprint, crude seams. 1980s "Collector's" reissue canisters (Sir Walter Raleigh, Prince Albert) and nostalgia tins.

## Market
Big collector base, heavy supply of commons. Common tins sell slowly in singles and better in lots. No strong seasonality.

## What sellers usually miss
Scarce pocket tins mixed into "tobacco tin collections" – scan for Forest & Stream, Dixie Queen, Hi-Plane, Big Ben, Buckingham. Sellers usually over-price commons.

## Shipping / handling
Single tins 4–8 oz $6–8; lot of 10 pocket tins 3–4 lb $12–16.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Brand, weight, variant, grade, hinge condition, photos of all sides; mention tax-stamp remnants.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$10) − ship to Dylan (~$12) − price ≥ max($50, price). A tin or lot needs ~$85+. Buy scarce pocket tins at ≤ $30 each or lots priced ≤ $5 per tin that contain at least one scarce brand. Pass common lots at more than ~$4 per tin and anything rusted.
