## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1960s–70s West German ceramic vases, jugs and lamp bases with thick, bubbly, cratered "lava" glazes in hot colours (orange, red, purple, black) or drippy glossy glazes. Bases are moulded with "W. Germany" or "West Germany" and a shape number/size (e.g., "517-30" for Scheurich, "313" Ruscha). Lamp bases often had brass/wood fittings.

## Makers, brands and variants
Scheurich (most common; "W. Germany" + shape-height number), Roth Keramik (cratered lava), Ruscha ("Vulcano" glaze, shape 313 designed by Kurt Tschörner), Bay Keramik (Bodo Mans designs), Carstens Tönnieshof, Jopeko, Otto Keramik (unmarked or "Otto" scratched, top-tier lava), ES Keramik (Silberdistel, hand-thrown), Dümler & Breiden, Spara, Jasba, Marei, Steuler, Ilkra.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real fat lava: thick raised glaze with bubbles and craters you can feel; earthenware body; moulded shape numbers on base. Lookalikes: US/1990s+ "crackle" paint finishes, resin, Chinese mass-made ceramics with smooth glazes.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on lava peaks, rim chips, base wear, drilled holes (lamp conversion lowers value for vases), rewiring.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese "lava-style" vases; some contemporary studio work. Marks and shape numbers help.

## Market
Strong mid-century modern demand; Europe and US buyers; moderate volume.

## What sellers usually miss
Unmarked Otto Keramik; lamp bases where the ceramic is West German.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile; double-box. Tall vases 16–20" ship $20–35.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give maker, shape number, height, glaze description, condition.

## Buy rule
Under Dylan's rules, buy marked West German lava with mid/upper tier comps at ≤ $40; pass on decor-store lamps.
