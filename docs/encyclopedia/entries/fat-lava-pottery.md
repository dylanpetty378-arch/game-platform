## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1960s–70s West German ceramic vases, jugs and lamp bases with thick, bubbly, cratered "lava" glazes in hot colours (orange, red, purple, black) or drippy glossy glazes. Bases are moulded with "W. Germany" or "West Germany" and a shape number/size (e.g., "517-30" for Scheurich, "313" Ruscha). Lamp bases often had brass/wood fittings.
- Period: West German art pottery was made 1949–1990, mostly in the Westerwald; over 100 potteries; the heyday ran 1950s–70s (Wikipedia; Peacock). "W. Germany"/"West Germany" means made 1949–1990; unmarked or "Germany" alone may be pre-1949 or post-1990 (Peacock).
- Base mark format: shape (model) number, usually three digits, then height in cm, e.g. Scheurich 242-22 (Wikipedia; Peacock). Ruscha sometimes adds a single size digit after the form number, e.g. 311/5 (Modern Redux).
- Maker names were usually on paper stickers, now mostly lost, so the moulded number is often the only maker clue (Wikipedia).
- "Fat lava" strictly means the thick, raised, volcanic-textured glaze; sellers apply it to any West German piece. Separate it from drip, flambé, matte and incised décors when you price (Peacock). Smooth drip glazes are Scheurich-type, not fat lava (see field notes).
- Seller words: "WGP", "West German", "fat lava", "MCM vase", "Keramik", "W. Germany pottery", "lava lamp" (often meaning a lamp base, not a liquid lava lamp).

## Makers, brands and variants
Scheurich (most common; "W. Germany" + shape-height number), Roth Keramik (cratered lava), Ruscha ("Vulcano" glaze; shape 313 designed by Kurt Tschörner, 1954), Bay Keramik (Bodo Mans designs), Carstens Tönnieshof (Heinz Siery designs), Jopeko, Otto Keramik (unmarked or "Otto" scratched, top-tier lava), ES Keramik (Emons & Söhne), Dümler & Breiden, Spara, Jasba, Marei, Steuler, Ilkra.
- **Ruscha** (Rheinbach): started 1948 when Rudolf Schardt took over Klein & Schardt; closed 1996, after which Scheurich acquired many Ruscha designs and moulds. Post-1996 pieces sometimes carry silver Ruscha labels. Form numbers under 100 are sometimes preceded by a letter. Red-brown clay bodies are thought to come from supply-shortage periods (Modern Redux).
- **"Vulcano" décor**: created 1951 by Otto Gerharz at Ruscha, an early forerunner of fat lava glazes (Modern Redux).
- **Otto Keramik**: founded 1964 by Otto Gerharz after he left Ruscha (Modern Redux). Pieces are often unmarked; top-tier lava.
- **Scheurich, Carstens, Bay, ES, Dümler & Breiden**: the most prolific producers (Wikipedia). Scheurich is known for coloured drip glazes on white clay; Bay for quieter architectural forms; Carstens for bold geometric decoration; Dümler & Breiden for true fat lava in deep earth tones (Peacock).
- Mark Hill's *Fat Lava* book (4th ed. 2016, reprinted March 2026) has one chapter per major maker (Scheurich, Ruscha, Roth, Carstens, Bay and others) with marks and labels; it re-attributes some pieces, so older attributions online can be wrong (Mark Hill; Wikipedia).

### Quick ID checklist
- Base photo shows "W. Germany"/"West Germany" and a number like 517-30 or 313? (Number first, height second.)
- Glaze is raised and cratered (shadowed bubbles in side light), not a flat crackle paint or a smooth drip?
- Body is ceramic (unglazed foot shows clay colour: white, cream or red-brown), not resin?
- Any remaining paper label (Ruscha silver label, Bay, Scheurich, Carstens)?
- Height: 40 cm+ floor vases are a different price band from 15–20 cm table pieces.
- Lamp: is the ceramic body a recognisable shape with a W. Germany base, or a decor-store body with a gilt collar?
- Rim and lava peaks free of chips; no drilled hole in a vase base.

### Golden-egg tells
- Otto Keramik lava (often unmarked) — ~2–3x common Scheurich (Terapeak median $114, p75 $146 vs common $25–60) and $300–1,500 for the best — very thick bubbled lava over a contrasting drip, often brown or black ground; unmarked or scratched base.
- Ruscha "Vulcano" décor (Otto Gerharz, from 1951) — upper/top tier ($150–400, rare forms higher; general knowledge, verify) — red, dark green and goldenrod volcanic mottling; Ruscha form numbers such as 313 or 311/5 on the base.
- Carstens Tönnieshof pieces by Heinz Siery — upper tier ($150–400; general knowledge, verify) — heavy, brutalist textured forms; Carstens numbering; sold on 1stdibs under Siery's name.
- Roth Keramik deep-cratered lava — upper tier ($150–400) — crater-pocked surface that looks like pumice in side light.
- Dümler & Breiden true fat lava in deep earth tones — mid–upper (general knowledge, verify) — thick raised glaze in browns and oranges.
- Tall floor vases (40–60 cm) in any good lava glaze — ~2–3x the same maker's table vase (mid tier $60–180 vs low $25–60) — height in cm is the second number on the base.
- Not a golden egg: ES Keramik (Emons & Söhne) — mostly low (Terapeak median $20, p75 $61).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real fat lava: thick raised glaze with bubbles and craters you can feel; earthenware body; moulded shape numbers on base. Lookalikes: US/1990s+ "crackle" paint finishes, resin, Chinese mass-made ceramics with smooth glazes.
- Most pieces are moulded (slip-cast); some Ruscha floor vases are hand-thrown and incised (1stdibs listing of a hand-thrown Ruscha "G.-863-40").
- Quality tells: crisp moulded numbers, even unglazed foot ring, glaze that pools and varies piece to piece; cheap copies have uniform printed-looking surfaces and no numbers.
- Old vs new: period pieces show base wear on the foot ring; a post-1996 Ruscha-design piece may carry a silver Ruscha label (Modern Redux).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on lava peaks, rim chips, base wear, drilled holes (lamp conversion lowers value for vases), rewiring.
- Repaired rims show as a matte or slightly different-coloured patch under raking light; ask for a rim photo straight down.
- Lamps: European plugs/Type C cords on original lamps (the Modern Redux Ruscha lamp was 250V with a US adapter); a rewire is acceptable but should be disclosed.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese "lava-style" vases; some contemporary studio work. Marks and shape numbers help.
- Ruscha forms continued under Scheurich after 1996 (Modern Redux), so a Ruscha shape number alone does not prove 1950s–70s production.
- Attributions are still being corrected (Wikipedia; Mark Hill 4th ed.); check a number against a published guide before quoting a maker.

## Market
Strong mid-century modern demand; Europe and US buyers; moderate volume. Interest began in the mid-1990s and grew after Graham Cooley's 2006 "Fat Lava" exhibition at King's Lynn and Mark Hill's book (Wikipedia).

## What sellers usually miss
Unmarked Otto Keramik; lamp bases where the ceramic is West German; the height number on the base, which tells buyers the size without a tape measure.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile; double-box. Tall vases 16–20" ship $20–35.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give maker, shape number, height, glaze description, condition.
- Photograph the base mark straight on, the glaze in raking light, and the rim from above.

### What to tell the buyer
Quote the base mark exactly (e.g. "W. Germany 517-30"), and name a maker only when the mark, label or a published form number supports it. Give the height in cm and inches and describe the glaze (raised lava, drip, or matte) and colours. State any chips on lava peaks or rim, any drilled hole, and whether a lamp has been rewired. Note that West German art pottery dates 1949–1990, and that the piece will be double-boxed.

## Buy rule
Under Dylan's rules, buy marked West German lava with mid/upper tier comps at ≤ $40; pass on decor-store lamps.
