# Surveying transits, levels and drafting instruments (K&E, Gurley, Dietzgen, Berger, Buff & Buff, Brunton)

Summary: modern dumpy levels $30–80; K&E/Dietzgen mid-century transits with box $120–250; brass pre-1920 Gurley/K&E Paragon/Brunton $300–700; 19th-c. solar compasses and mining/railroad transits $800–3,000.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- A brass or black-crinkle instrument on a 3- or 4-screw levelling base, usually stored in a wooden dovetailed box.
- The maker is on the horizontal circle or a plate: **Keuffel & Esser (K&E)**, **W. & L.E. Gurley (Troy NY)**, **Dietzgen**, **Berger**, **Buff & Buff**, **Brunton** (pocket transit).
- The serial number dates it. K&E serials under ~50,000 = pre-1920, brass, higher value (verify against a K&E serial table).
- A transit has a telescope that flips over plus vertical and horizontal circles. A level has only a telescope on a horizontal bar.
- Seller words: "antique surveying tool", "transit", "level", "theodolite".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **W. & L.E. Gurley** (Troy NY, 1852–): the premium US maker. Brass transits, solar compasses, mining transits (general knowledge, verify).
- **Keuffel & Esser** (NJ): "Paragon" is the top line. Brass early, black finish mid-century.
- **Dietzgen, Berger, Buff & Buff, Young & Sons** (Philadelphia; early compasses and transits).
- **Brunton**: a pocket transit (a compass and clinometer in a palm-size case), used by geologists. Leather case adds value.
- **David White, CST, Sokkia, Topcon**: 1970s–90s builder's levels, low value.
- Top variants: solar attachments, side telescopes (mining), railroad transits, and 19th-c. vernier compasses.

### Golden-egg tells
- Gurley or Young & Sons solar compass / transit with Burt's solar attachment, 19th c. — $800–3,000 (entry Top) vs $120–250 black mid-century K&E (~5–12×) — extra tilted sun assembly on top: latitude arc, hour circle and a small lens-and-plate arm above the compass box (entry comp)
- Mining transit with side (auxiliary) telescope — $800–2,000 (~5–8×) — a second, smaller telescope mounted beside the standard on an extended horizontal axis, brass, often with a reflector on the circle (entry comp)
- Railroad/engineer's brass transit pre-1920 — K&E "Paragon" (serial under ~50,000) or Gurley with box and tripod — $300–700 (~2–3×) — lacquered (not polished) brass, silvered circles with vernier windows, "Paragon" or "W. & L. E. Gurley, Troy, N.Y." engraved on the plate (entry comp)
- Early American vernier surveyor's compass by a listed maker (Harttmann & Saxe, Knox & Shain, Joseph Farr, T. R. Graves, John Oakes, Edward Meister) — pre-1860 compasses in the $1,000–3,000 band (verify) — large brass compass with folding sight vanes and the maker's name engraved on the dial face (https://www.surveyhistory.org/)
- Berger tunnel transit (e.g. #4029) — specialised, few made; above the $300–700 standard-transit band (verify) — eccentric/offset telescope on a short standard for sighting past the axis (https://www.antiquesurveying.com/)
- Early Brunton pocket transit in leather, "Wm. Ainsworth & Sons, Denver" plate — $150–400 vs $50–100 for later/modern Brunton (~3×) — brass or black case with Ainsworth maker name, hinged mirror lid, leather case (entry comp)
- Watch-out: Indian "nautical brass" repro theodolites with fake "Stanley London"/"K&E" engraving — $30–60; light weight, crude engraving, no serial number (entry comp)


## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Early: lacquered brass, silver-inlaid graduated circles and fine engraving. Mid-century: black crinkle paint over aluminium or brass.
- Complete with box, tripod, plumb bob, sunshade, and the tools and adjusting pins in the box lid.
- Original lacquer is worth more than polished brass; collectors dislike over-polishing (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Hazy or fungus-filled optics, broken crosshairs, seized axes, missing vials, a missing lens cap or sunshade, and dented brass.
- A missing box or tripod lowers value about 30%.
- Ask for photos through the eyepiece and of the circles, the serial number and the box interior.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Decorative "nautical brass" reproduction transits and theodolites from India are common. They are light, crudely engraved, and carry fake names like "Stanley London" or "Keuffel & Esser" (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Collectors of scientific instruments and the surveyor and engineer community; decorators for brass pieces. Sells steadily on eBay; specialist auctions for 19th-c. pieces. Mid-century black transits are plentiful (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Maker and serial number; "antique surveying tool" with brass showing.
- Tripod, box and plumb bob add 30%.

## Shipping / handling
- 15–25 lb with box: $40–60 double-boxed with the instrument removed from its base. Sell on eBay. Ship wooden tripods separately or sell them locally.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + "surveying transit"/"level"/"pocket transit" + "brass" or "black" + era + "with box"/"tripod" + model (Paragon) + serial. Junk: "antique surveying tool", "steampunk", "scope". Example: `Antique Gurley Brass Surveying Transit with Wooden Box Serial Dated 1912 Troy NY`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers (instrument collectors, surveyors, decorators; sells steadily); 19th-century solar compasses to a specialist auction; tripods sold FB local.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (mid-century black K&E/Dietzgen with box $120–250, pre-1920 Gurley brass/Paragon/Brunton $300–700, solar/mining $800+); box, tripod and plumb bob add ~30%; accept −10%, floor −20%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) instrument on its base, three-quarter, brass/finish showing; then maker engraving, serial number, the circles and verniers, the view through the scope, box interior with accessories, plumb bob and tripod; video of the axes moving and the level bubble.
- **State:** maker, model, serial and its date, finish, optics clear or hazy, crosshairs present, axes move freely, verniers readable, box and accessories listed, tripod included or local only, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** 15–25 lb with box; remove the instrument from its base, wrap separately, double-box, 20–25 lb class, $40–60; wooden tripods ship separately or sell locally.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A [Gurley / K&E Paragon] transit, serial [X] dating it to [year], in its box with [plumb bob/tripod] — the plentiful black 1950s transits are the $150 ones; pre-1920 brass [maker] examples sell for [$X], optics clear."

## Buy rule
Buy when: a brass Gurley/K&E/Young with box is under $120, or a Brunton is under $50.
Pass when: a black 1950s–70s K&E at $150+ (market price); levels.
Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (instrument and box do).
