## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- A brass or black-crinkle instrument on a 3- or 4-screw levelling base, usually stored in a wooden dovetailed box.
- The maker is on the horizontal circle or a plate: **Keuffel & Esser (K&E)**, **W. & L.E. Gurley (Troy NY)**, **Dietzgen**, **Berger**, **Buff & Buff**, **Brunton** (pocket transit).
- The serial number dates it. K&E serials under ~50,000 = pre-1920, brass, higher value (verify against a K&E serial table).
- A transit has a telescope that flips over plus vertical and horizontal circles. A level has only a telescope on a horizontal bar.
- Seller words: "antique surveying tool", "transit", "level", "theodolite".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **W. & L.E. Gurley** (Troy NY, 1852–): the premium US maker. Brass transits, solar compasses, mining transits (general knowledge, verify).
- **Keuffel & Esser** (NJ): "Paragon" is the top line. Brass early, black finish mid-century.
- **Dietzgen, Berger, Buff & Buff, Young & Sons** (Philadelphia; early compasses and transits).
- **Brunton**: a pocket transit (a compass and clinometer in a palm-size case), used by geologists. Leather case adds value.
- **David White, CST, Sokkia, Topcon**: 1970s–90s builder's levels, low value.
- Top variants: solar attachments, side telescopes (mining), railroad transits, and 19th-c. vernier compasses.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Early: lacquered brass, silver-inlaid graduated circles and fine engraving. Mid-century: black crinkle paint over aluminium or brass.
- Complete with box, tripod, plumb bob, sunshade, and the tools and adjusting pins in the box lid.
- Original lacquer is worth more than polished brass; collectors dislike over-polishing (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Hazy or fungus-filled optics, broken crosshairs, seized axes, missing vials, a missing lens cap or sunshade, and dented brass.
- A missing box or tripod lowers value about 30%.
- Ask for photos through the eyepiece and of the circles, the serial number and the box interior.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Decorative "nautical brass" reproduction transits and theodolites from India are common. They are light, crudely engraved, and carry fake names like "Stanley London" or "Keuffel & Esser" (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Collectors of scientific instruments and the surveyor and engineer community; decorators for brass pieces. Sells steadily on eBay; specialist auctions for 19th-c. pieces. Mid-century black transits are plentiful (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- Maker and serial number; "antique surveying tool" with brass showing.
- Tripod, box and plumb bob add 30%.

## Shipping / handling
- 15–25 lb with box: $40–60 double-boxed with the instrument removed from its base. Sell on eBay. Ship wooden tripods separately or sell them locally.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Maker, model, serial number and its date, finish, optics (clear/hazy), crosshairs present, axes move freely, box and accessories.
- Photograph the maker's engraving, the serial number, the circles, the box interior and the view through the scope.
- Title keywords: "Antique Gurley Brass Surveying Transit with Box", "K&E Paragon Transit", "Brunton Pocket Transit".

## Buy rule
