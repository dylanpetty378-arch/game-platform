## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A cast-iron hand pump with a pitcher-shaped open top and spout, a hinged handle on top, and a round flanged base that bolts onto a well/cistern pipe. It is a single-acting suction (lift) pump: a leather cup on a plunger draws water up to about 25 ft. It was used at kitchen sinks on cisterns and at shallow yard wells, from the later 1800s through the 1930s and beyond (they never went out of production). Parts: the **body** (barrel with spout), the **cap** (lid with the handle pivot ears), the **handle** (cast or wrought iron, sometimes with a wood grip), the **plunger** with leather cup and check valve, and a **base valve** (leather flapper) above the flange. A complete pump is typically 14–18 in tall and weighs ~15–25 lb (Red Jacket c.1930: 17.5 in, 20 lb).
Words sellers use: "antique well pump", "pitcher pump", "cistern pump", "farmhouse", "garden decor", "No. 2". Popular with garden and farmhouse decorators and with off-grid users (still functional).
Look-alikes: **force pumps and pump stands** (tall closed-top pumps with a separate spout; a different, larger item); **lift pump heads** on pipe stands; cast-aluminium or resin garden-fountain "pumps" (light, no plunger).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **F.E. Myers & Bro., Ashland, Ohio**: the most common good name. Bodies cast "F.E. MYERS & BRO ASHLAND O" with an eagle-and-anchor emblem on some models; patent dates from the 1880s (the force pump at Garth's carried "The Myer's Force Pump" and patents 1882–1888).
- **Fairbanks, Morse & Co.**: name cast on body or cap; scarcer on pitcher pumps than on engines and scales.
- **Goulds Mfg. Co., Seneca Falls NY**: well-made, crisp castings.
- **Red Jacket Mfg. Co., Davenport, Iowa (founded 1875)**: often red paint; cast part codes such as "W2-2; 1-3" (c.1930 example).
- **Deming Co., Salem, Ohio**: cast "DEMING CO SALEM O" and a size number (#2 example on eBay).
- **Baker Mfg. (Monitor), Evansville WI; Dempster Mill (Beatrice NE); Aermotor; Woodford**: more often windmill and pump-stand makers; pitcher pumps exist but are less common.
- **Modern US-made**: Heller-Aller / Hitzer (Berne, IN) and others still cast pitcher pumps. They are good, heavy pumps with crisp lettering but no age; priced as new tools, not antiques.
- **Modern Chinese/Indian imports** (Tractor Supply, Amazon, farm stores) are the majority of what's for sale, often painted red, green or black, cast "No. 1/2/3" or nothing.
- Sizes: makers cast a size number (No. 1, 2, 3) that tracks cylinder bore and pipe thread; bigger numbers are bigger pumps. The size number alone does not mean old.
- Nickel-plated pitcher pumps were the indoor (kitchen) version and are scarcer than painted outdoor ones (appraisily).

### Quick ID checklist
- Raised maker name and town on the body, cap, base flange or handle (appraisily lists all four places to look).
- Raised patent dates (an 1880s–1900s date brackets the era; the pump was made on or after it).
- Crisp, deep lettering and sharp edges vs soft, blurry, thin castings.
- Complete: body + cap + handle + plunger with leather; handle pivot pins present.
- Old square nuts, slotted screws and worn paint layers vs Phillips or metric hardware and powder coat.
- No cracks at the base flange, spout throat, pivot ears or cap threads (the usual deal-breakers).
- Original paint remnants or old decals vs fresh glossy red/green/black.
- "Decor only" stickers or new wood handles on an old body = married or decor piece.

### Golden-egg tells
- Nickel-plated indoor kitchen pitcher pump by a named maker — est. 2–3× a painted outdoor one (verify) — silver plating flaking to iron, sink-flange base, maker name visible.
- Early patent-dated maker pump (1870s–1890s raised patent dates, F.E. Myers eagle-and-anchor, ornate cast decoration) — the existing entry's $100–250 top tier vs $40–120 for a plain marked pump — read the cast dates and look for relief ornament on the body.
- Complete marked US pump in original paint (Myers, Goulds, Deming, Red Jacket, Fairbanks Morse) — $40–120 vs $25–45 for a complete import — maker name cast in, worn period paint, square nuts.
- Working pump with new leathers, demonstrated — functional (off-grid) buyers pay more than decorators (est. +$20–40, verify) — a video or a photo of water coming out.
- Coastal and cottage décor markets — appraisily notes "1.5–2x premiums" regionally — matters for where to sell, not for what to buy.
- Bodies without cap, handle or plunger — the parts tier ($10–30) whatever the maker.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old US pumps: raised maker names and size numbers cast into the body or cap, heavier iron, leather plunger cup. Imports: no maker name or "No. 1/2/3" only, rough casting, glossy paint, bolts with metric threads. More: old castings have deep, crisp letters and a smooth skin with sand texture; fasteners are square-head bolts and slotted screws; the handle is often wrought or cast with a turned end; layers of paint chip to show older colours beneath. Imports have grainy, pitted skins, soft or no lettering, thin walls (lighter for the size), bright powder coat or glossy enamel in one even coat, Phillips-head or metric hardware. Modern US-made (Hitzer) pumps are well cast but carry current marks and fresh paint.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing handle/cap/plunger (like the example), cracked bodies (freeze damage), seized pivots. Decor buyers accept rust; functional buyers want parts complete. Cracks show as a fine dark line, often at the base flange or spout throat, and are fatal for a working pump. Repairs: brazed cracks (brass-coloured seams), replacement handles (new wood or a different maker's handle that doesn't sit right), repainted bodies. Leathers are consumables; a dried-out leather is normal and cheap (leather cups are sold at Home Depot). Ask: is it complete inside, is it cracked, will the handle move, can you photograph the inside of the spout and the underside of the cap?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern imports are routinely sold as "antique". The tell is the absence of maker castings and fresh, even paint. Also watch for: new US-made pumps (Hitzer/Heller-Aller) offered as old; "decor only" import pumps that can't pump at all; married pumps (cap and handle from another maker; mismatched colour or fit). Generic codes in place of a maker name point to new production (appraisily).

## Market
Steady garden-decor demand, spring peak; heavy (15-25 lb) so shipping eats margin. Two buyer groups: decorators (want a marked, pretty, complete pump; accept rust) and off-grid or cabin users (want it to work; pay for completeness and sound castings).

## What sellers usually miss
Maker marks on complete old pumps; most sellers price at $30-60 regardless. Also missed: nickel plating under grime (indoor pumps), and patent dates on the cap.

## Shipping / handling
15-25 lb, $20-30 by ground; well packed with base protected. Pad the handle ears and spout lip (cast iron snaps if dropped on an edge).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, completeness, whether it pumps (with a new leather), dimensions, base bolt pattern. Photograph the cast lettering and any patent dates close up, the inside of the cap, the plunger and leather, and the flange underside; give height, spout length and pipe thread size.

### What to tell the buyer
"Cast-iron pitcher pump by [maker], [town] — the maker name [and patent date ____] is cast into the [body/cap]. Complete with body, cap, handle and plunger; [original leather / new leather fitted]. No cracks at the flange, spout or pivot ears. [Tested: draws water / Not tested.] [H] in tall, base flange [diameter] with [n] bolt holes, [size] pipe thread. This is an American-made pump, not a modern import (imports have no maker name and fresh paint)."

## Buy rule
Need resale ≥ ~$120 to clear $50 at a $20 price with $25 each-way shipping - only complete marked early pumps. Pass on parts.
