## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A desk lamp holding one or two straight fluorescent tubes (usually 15W T8 18" or 20W 24") under a long horizontal metal hood, carried on a single pedestal or a pair of uprights on a heavy cast or stamped steel base. Made in huge numbers for offices, schools and drafting rooms from the late 1930s to the 1960s. Visual families:
- **Flying wing / streamline**: hood with swept, tapering wing-like ends, often a curved "tail" pedestal; Art Deco or Streamline Moderne styling. This is the desirable look (Art Specialty Co "Lite Master", some Eagle and Faries models).
- **Pedestal "tombstone"**: rectangular slab hood on a stepped, flared square base with ribbed neck (the pid 7929762122 type). Common, plain.
- **Articulated drafting/task**: Dazor floating-arm, Luxo L-1 with circline tube and magnifier — a different collector market (task-lighting users, industrial décor).
- **Bankers / double-arm**: twin uprights with a trough hood, sometimes with a glass/plastic diffuser.
Look for: rocker or pull switch on the base, starter canister visible under the hood (pre-1960 preheat type), a cloth or brown rubber cord, a foil/paper maker label under the base.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Art Specialty Co., Chicago** — "Lite Master" line, 1940s–50s; flying-wing and Deco pedestal designs, often two-tone (brown/cream, grey/chrome). Paper or foil label under base. The best-known name; Lens over-attributes everything to it.
- **Faries Manufacturing (Decatur, IL)** — Deco/streamline desk lamps, both incandescent and fluorescent, 1930s–50s; quality castings.
- **Eagle Electric** — budget office pedestal fluorescents, 1950s–60s; plain.
- **Dazor Manufacturing (St. Louis)** — floating-arm "Floating Fixture" task lamps, 1940s–70s; spring-balanced arm, a steady industrial-décor seller.
- **Luxo (Norway)/ Jac Jacobsen** — L-1 and magnifier lamps; usually clamp-mounted, different market.
- **Industrial Lamp Corp, Emeco-era office lamp makers, Underwriters-only unbranded** — the bulk of what turns up.
Variants that matter: chrome or polished-aluminium trim vs painted; two-tone paint; glass diffuser (scarce, breaks) vs open tube; integrated pen tray or clock (adds interest).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Cast iron or heavy stamped-steel base = older/better; lightweight sheet-steel base with plastic foot pads = 1960s+ budget.
- Factory finishes: crinkle/wrinkle (black, brown, grey), hammertone (grey-blue, bronze), baked enamel two-tone. Crinkle brown/grey = common office stock.
- Old: preheat starter socket, heavy core-and-coil ballast in the base or hood, cloth or brown rubber cord, no polarized plug. New/reproduction-era: electronic ballast, polarized plug, plastic end caps.
- Chrome accents, sculpted Deco steps, swept "wing" ends and a shapely cast neck are the value tells. A plain slab hood on a flared square base is the common type.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Ballasts fail or hum; starters go bad; tube sockets crack (brittle bakelite). Most need rewiring — that is a repair (Dylan rule: no repairs, untested electronics).
- Paint loss at hood edges and base corners; surface rust under hood; dents in hood ends.
- Old PCB-containing capacitors in pre-1979 ballasts — buyers sometimes ask; a sealed working ballast is fine but leaking tar is a red flag.
- Common seller "restoration": rattle-can repaint (kills the vintage premium for collectors; decorators don't care).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Little faking. Modern "industrial" reproductions (Restoration Hardware-style, Pottery Barn) use LED or incandescent bulbs, not fluorescent tubes, and have new hardware — easy to tell. Repainted lamps are the main "misrepresentation".

## Market
Steady but modest; decorator/industrial and Art Deco buyers. Plain office pedestals sit for weeks; flying-wing styles sell in days to a couple of weeks if priced right. Estimated eBay US sold ~40–100/month across all fluorescent desk lamps (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
- That the streamline flying-wing versions are worth 2–4× the plain pedestals — they title both "vintage desk lamp".
- Paper labels under the base (Art Specialty, Faries) that prove the maker.
- A glass diffuser or integrated clock/pen tray that lifts value.
Conversely, Lens and sellers often over-attribute plain lamps to "Lite Master" and quote flying-wing prices.

## Shipping / handling
18–26" long, 6–12 lb. Box ~28×10×14; remove tube and ship separately or not at all (tubes break and contain mercury — best to sell "tube not included"). Wrap hood edges; double-box heavy cast bases. Expect $20–35 to buyer.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker if labelled (Lite Master Art Specialty / Faries / Dazor) + "Art Deco" + "flying wing"/"streamline" + "fluorescent desk lamp" + finish (two-tone / chrome) + era + "works". Junk: "vintage desk lamp" alone, "industrial" for plain crinkle, "Lite Master" without a label. `Art Specialty Lite Master Flying Wing Fluorescent Desk Lamp Two-Tone Deco 1940s`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for flying-wing/Deco styles (sell in days); FB Marketplace local for plain crinkle pedestals (shipping $20–35 eats them).
- **Price position:** at the sold median ($60–120 clean Deco; $120–250 flying wing/Faries), accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 30 days; plain pedestals $25–60 local, firm.
- **Photos:** lamp on plain background from the front three-quarter; base label; switch and ballast; hood interior and diffuser; paint wear close up; lit with a new tube (video) or stated untested.
- **State:** maker only if the label proves it (else "Art Deco style"), finish, dimensions, tube length/wattage, "lights with new tube" or "untested, for restoration", ballast hum/rewiring noted, diffuser/clock/pen tray present, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** passenger-seat, 18–26" long, 6–12 lb; box 28×10×14, tube removed and not shipped (mercury), hood edges wrapped, cast base double-boxed, $20–35.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Art Specialty Lite Master / Faries] flying-wing Deco fluorescent lamp, [1940s], label under the base, original [two-tone] paint, lights with a new tube; plain office pedestals go for [$] and this streamline form brings [$]."

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $40 and the photo shows a streamline/flying-wing hood or chrome/two-tone Deco styling, reportedly working, no broken sockets. Profit check: $130 × 0.87 − $25 − $20 − $40 ≈ $28 → still under $50, so in practice only buy flying-wing types at ≤ $25 or confirmed Lite Master/Faries labelled examples with clean paint. Pass on plain crinkle pedestals at any price above ~$5 (cannot clear $50 profit). Untested = treat as non-working (Dylan rule: no untested electronics).
