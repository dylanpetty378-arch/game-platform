## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Short, fat chrome-and-black cylinder (60–90 mm long) with a glass front, no aperture ring, a single focus ring, and a rear thread (43 mm on Kowa). Originally bolted to the front of 16 mm/35 mm cinema projectors to un-squeeze CinemaScope film; indie filmmakers now mount them on camera lenses for 2x "anamorphic" flares and oval bokeh. Titles usually say "projection lens", "anamorphic", "cinemascope", "Prominar". The model is engraved on the barrel: **Kowa Prominar 8-Z / 16-H** (same optics, the grail), **16-D** (smaller, 2x, cheaper), **16-S**, **Vidoscope**, **Bell & Howell Anamorphic** (rebadged Kowa 8-Z or 16-D), **Sankor 16C/16D/16F**, **Isco Ultra Star / Iscorama** (Iscorama 36/54 are $2–5k), **Schneider Cinelux**, **Elmoscope**, **Singer/Kollmorgen**. Look-alikes: ordinary (spherical) projection lenses – long thin tubes with no squeeze, worth $5–30 (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Kowa (Japan)** – Prominar 8-Z = 16-H (grail, $700–1,200); 16-D (common, $180–500); 16-S; Vidoscope ($280).
- **Bell & Howell** – rebadged Kowa 8-Z or 16-D; identify by size.
- **Sankor** 16C/16D/16F – decent tier.
- **Isco** – Ultra Star / Ultra Star HD ($400–700), Iscorama 36/54 ($2,000–5,000), Isco Red/Blue (top).
- **Schneider Cinelux**, **Elmoscope II**, **Singer/Kollmorgen** – Cinelux big-barrel theatre units are junk tier for camera use.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Chrome/black anodised metal barrels, multi-element glass. Engraving with model and serial on the barrel. Clear glass with no haze, fungus (web-like filaments) or balsam separation (rainbow/edge cloudiness) is what matters. Smooth focus ring with no stiffness (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Haze/fungus halves the price; element separation is serious. Scratches on front element, dents in the barrel, stuck focus. Cleaning by a lens tech is possible for haze/fungus, but separation is costly to fix (general knowledge, verify). Ask for a photo shot through the lens with a flashlight behind it.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No fakes known. Modern cheap camera anamorphics (Sirui, Great Joy) compete with and have softened prices of the vintage units; they are not passed off as vintage.

## Market
Buyers are indie filmmakers and lens hobbyists; Reverb buyers are the filmmakers. Demand peaked ~2015–2022 and has softened with cheap new anamorphics. Low volume (Terapeak: 5 16-D sales in the lookback). Sellers who know have priced up (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Estate/garage sellers list these as "projector lens" or "movie projector part" for $10–40 because they don't know filmmakers want them. The model number on the barrel is everything: 8-Z/16-H = 3–4x the price of a 16-D. Check photos for the engraving and for clear glass (haze/fungus halves the price).

## Shipping / handling
Under 1 lb, small box, $10–15 either way. Sell on eBay (13%) or Reverb (~8%) – Reverb buyers are the filmmakers.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State exact model (engraving), squeeze factor (2x), rear thread size, glass condition (haze/fungus/separation/scratches), focus action, and any included mounts/clamps. Photograph the engraving, both elements with a light through them, and the rear thread. Keywords: brand + model + "anamorphic" + "2x" + "projection lens" + "Prominar"/"Iscorama".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 8-Z / 16-H under $300; 16-D under $120; Iscorama at any price under $800 (then verify). Pass when: large theatre projector anamorphics (>1 kg), any visible haze, or a 16-D over $150 (profit can't reach max($50, price)). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Must fit a passenger seat (all do).
