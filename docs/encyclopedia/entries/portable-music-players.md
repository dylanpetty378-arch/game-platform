## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-held audio players. Value tells: cassette Walkmans with "WM-D6C Professional", "WM-DD", "WM-701", "WM-F" in gold/chrome; MiniDisc (MZ-R/N/RH); iPod Classic (click wheel, metal back). Low value: plastic flash MP3 sticks/pucks, generic brands.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Sony**: Walkman cassette (1979–2010), Discman/CD Walkman, MiniDisc Walkman (1992–2013), Network Walkman NW-series flash/HDD (1999–2006; ATRAC/SonicStage, needs old software), NW-A/E later.
- **Aiwa**, **Panasonic**, **Toshiba**, **Sanyo**: cassette rivals; Aiwa HS-PX "Cassette Boy" collectible.
- **Apple iPod**: 1st/2nd gen, Classic, mini, nano — collector market for sealed/rare colours.
- **Creative**, **iRiver**, **Zune**: niche.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal-body Walkmans (WM-2, WM-DD, WM-701) beat plastic. Dead belts/capstans in cassette units; leaking AA cells; ATRAC players need SonicStage on old Windows to load music.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Belt failure (nearly universal in untested cassette players), battery corrosion, lost Li-ion capacity, scratched screens, missing dock/cables.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake iPod Classics (Chinese clones) and refurbished/modded iPods; new retro cassette players (Jensen, We Are Rewind) are not Sony.

## Market
Huge, fast for cassette Walkman and iPod; slow for orphan-format flash players.

## What sellers usually miss
Model numbers on the back; metal-cased WM-DD types sold as "old Walkman".

## Shipping / handling
Small, light (<1 lb), $5–8. Pocket size.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model number, tested functions (play/FF/REW/record), battery health, accessories.

## Buy rule
Untested electronics = pass (Dylan's rule). Flash players median ~$35 → no profit. Buy only seller-demonstrated working high-tier cassette/MiniDisc models priced ≤ 1/3 of comps.
