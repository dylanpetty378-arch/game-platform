## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-held audio players. Value tells: cassette Walkmans with "WM-D6C Professional", "WM-DD", "WM-701", "WM-F" in
gold/chrome; MiniDisc (MZ-R/N/RH); iPod Classic (click wheel, metal back). Low value: plastic flash MP3 sticks/pucks,
generic brands.

Where the model number sits: Sony cassette and MD units — on the lid, the side, or a label/engraving on the back or in
the battery compartment ("WM-xxx", "MZ-xxx", "NW-xxx", "D-xxx" for Discman); iPod — laser-etched on the back with model
"A1xxx" and capacity. Get this photo before anything else; "Walkman" alone tells you nothing.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Sony**: Walkman cassette (1979–2010), Discman/CD Walkman, MiniDisc Walkman (1992–2013), Network Walkman NW-series
  flash/HDD (1999–2006; ATRAC/SonicStage, needs old software), NW-A/E later.
  Cassette milestones (Gizmodo): TPS-L2 (1979, the original, two headphone jacks and a talk-over mic); WM-2 (1981, first
  very small Walkman); WM-F2 (1982, first with playback, recording and FM); WM-10 (1983, ultra-compact, one AA with a
  step-up converter); Sports Walkman (1984, water-resistant, gasketed, yellow; regional special editions); WM-W800 (1985,
  dual-deck); WM-F107 (1987, solar); WM-DD series (first personal model with a direct "disc drive" motor instead of a belt,
  lower wow and flutter, metal case); WM-3000 "My First Sony" (1990, kids'); WM-GX202 (still sold in Japan in 2009).
  The WM-D6C "Professional" (recording deck with VU meter) and WM-701 are top-tier per this entry's ladder (general
  knowledge, verify).
- **Sony MiniDisc**: MZ-R (recorders), MZ-N (NetMD, USB transfer), MZ-RH (Hi-MD). MZ-RH1 is described as Sony's final MD
  recorder, with Hi-MD and playback of all legacy formats; a working, scuffed example with adapter, USB cable and pouch
  was asked at US$835 on Reverb (asking, not sold; StereoNET RADAR).
- **Aiwa**, **Panasonic**, **Toshiba**, **Sanyo**: cassette rivals; Aiwa HS-PX "Cassette Boy" collectible.
- **Apple iPod**: 1st/2nd gen, Classic, mini, nano — collector market for sealed/rare colours. All iPod Classics carry
  model number A1238: 80 GB "thin" and 160 GB "thick" (Sept 2007), then 120 GB thin, then 160 GB thin (iFixit) — so
  capacity matters more than the model number for identifying the generation.
- **Creative**, **iRiver**, **Zune**: niche.

### Quick ID checklist
- Exact model number from lid/back/battery door (WM-, MZ-, NW-, D-, A1xxx). No model number photo = ask.
- Body: metal case (WM-DD, WM-2, WM-701, WM-D6C) vs plastic.
- Drive: belt-drive units almost always need new belts if untested; WM-DD types use a direct motor (Gizmodo).
- Functions: play only vs record (WM-F2, WM-D6C, MZ-R/N/RH) vs FM tuner ("F" in many Sony model numbers, e.g. WM-F2).
- Battery compartment: corrosion from leaked AA cells; original battery-case/gumstick for MD units.
- Display/screen and click wheel (iPod): scratched back is normal; check the capacity engraving and "Designed by Apple in California".
- Accessories: original remote, earbuds, box, AC adapter, dock/USB cable (NetMD/Hi-MD need USB and software).
- Demo: a video of it playing a tape/disc, FF/REW and (for recorders) recording.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal-body Walkmans (WM-2, WM-DD, WM-701) beat plastic. Dead belts/capstans in cassette units; leaking AA cells; ATRAC
players need SonicStage on old Windows to load music. iPod Classics are often modded (flash storage, larger battery)
which buyers may value but which changes the "original" description (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Belt failure (nearly universal in untested cassette players), battery corrosion, lost Li-ion capacity, scratched screens,
missing dock/cables. Also: pinch-roller hardening and speed drift (cassette), sticky lid dampers, dead MD laser or
"Disc error", swollen or dead built-in batteries (iPod, Network Walkman), hard-drive clicks (HDD iPods). Belts are sold as
replacement kits for many Walkman models (eBay belt stores), so a belt-only fault is repairable, but it is time.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Fake iPod Classics (Chinese clones) and refurbished/modded iPods; new retro cassette players (Jensen, We Are Rewind) are
not Sony. Apple publicly warned of counterfeit iPods as early as 2006 (AppleInsider). Clone tells: wrong font or logo on
the back, no or wrong model/capacity engraving, plastic back where metal is expected, non-Apple menus on boot (general
knowledge, verify). Refurbished iPods may have aftermarket backs and batteries.

## Market
Huge, fast for cassette Walkman and iPod; slow for orphan-format flash players.

## What sellers usually miss
Model numbers on the back; metal-cased WM-DD types sold as "old Walkman". Also: recording models (WM-F2, WM-D6C) and
Sports Walkmans; MZ-RH1 vs ordinary MZ-R; iPod Classic capacity (160 GB thick vs thin); original remotes and boxes.

## Shipping / handling
Small, light (<1 lb), $5–8. Pocket size. Remove AA batteries before shipping; eject tapes/discs.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model number, tested functions (play/FF/REW/record), battery health, accessories.

### What to tell the buyer
"Sony [model] [cassette / MiniDisc / CD] Walkman, model number [x] (photo of label). Tested: [plays at correct speed,
FF/REW, auto-reverse, record, radio] on [date] with [fresh AA / AC adapter]; [belt replaced/original]. Cosmetic: [scratches,
lid, battery door]. Battery compartment: [clean / corrosion]. Includes [remote, earbuds, adapter, box, cable]. For iPods:
model A1238, [capacity] GB, [original / modded with flash storage and new battery], battery holds [x] hours." If untested,
say "untested, sold as-is for parts or repair" — never "should work".

## Buy rule
Untested electronics = pass (Dylan's rule). Flash players median ~$35 → no profit. Buy only seller-demonstrated working high-tier cassette/MiniDisc models priced ≤ 1/3 of comps.
