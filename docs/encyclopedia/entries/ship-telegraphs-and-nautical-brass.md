## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A pedestal or wall "Full Ahead / Slow / Stop" dial with brass handles.
- **Reproductions** (India, 1980s–now) are often sold as "Brelco"-style decor: lightweight brass, modern screws, printed dial, ~12" tall. $80–200.
- **Genuine** Chadburn (Liverpool), Robinson / A. Robinson, Bendix, Henschel and Siebe Gorman units are heavy (40–150 lb), with cast maker plates, a mechanical chain drive and glass dials. $800–3,000.

The same split applies to:
- binnacles
- portholes (real = 10–20 lb cast bronze/brass with dogs)
- sextants (real: Plath, Hughes, Tamaya $300–800; Indian repro $30–60)

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Telegraphs**: Chadburn (Liverpool); Robinson / A. Robinson; Bendix (US); Henschel (US); Siebe Gorman. Telegraphs with a named-vessel provenance are top tier ($2,000–6,000).
- **Binnacles**: Kelvin & Hughes, complete, are top tier.
- **Sextants**: C. Plath, Hughes (Husun), Tamaya, $300–800 boxed. Indian repro $30–60.
- **Clocks**: Chelsea ship's bell clocks (collectible) and WWII US Navy clocks by Chelsea/Seth Thomas ($150–500).
- **Lamps**: Perko lamps ($150–500).
- **Decor repros**: "Brelco" (as used by sellers), unbranded Indian brass, $40–200.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Genuine units are heavy cast bronze or brass, with cast (not stamped or etched) maker plates and slotted period screws. They have glass dials with painted or enamel faces, chain or rod mechanisms inside, and real wear and patina.
- Repros are thin, light brass with modern Phillips screws and printed paper or plastic dials. They have a bright lacquered or "antiqued" finish and no internal mechanism.
- Weight is the fastest tell: "heavy" in the ad usually means real.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- On a genuine unit, cracked glass is minor. Missing handles, pointers or mechanism parts matter more. Polishing is accepted.
- Repros have little value in any condition.
- Ask for the maker plate photo, the weight, photos inside the housing, and the dial close up.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Indian reproductions dominate the market, and many carry fake maker plates ("Chadburn", "Kelvin Hughes", "Ross London") on the repro bodies. A plate alone is not proof, so check weight and construction (general knowledge, verify). Repro sextants with fake "Kelvin Hughes" or "Hezzanith" plates are very common.

## Market
Nautical decor buyers absorb repros cheaply. Genuine maritime antiques sell to collectors, coastal decorators and through maritime auctions (Eldred's and similar). They are slow sellers, with freight a major cost (general knowledge, verify). Coastal and pickup markets are best for genuine pedestal units. Mild spring and summer lift.

## What sellers usually miss
A photo of the maker plate. The weight ("heavy" in the ad means real). Cracked glass is minor on a genuine unit.

## Shipping / handling
Repro: 10 lb, ~$25. A real pedestal unit needs freight ($150+), so genuine ones are pickup-only. Genuine pedestal telegraphs and binnacles exceed passenger-seat size and are excluded. Wall telegraphs, portholes, clocks, sextants and lamps fit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Title: "[Maker] Ship's Engine Order Telegraph [type] [period] Bronze/Brass". State the weight, height, maker plate wording, dial condition, and whether the mechanism works. Photograph the plate, the dial and the interior mechanism. For repro or decor pieces, state "decorative reproduction" plainly.

## Buy rule
Buy when: a maker plate is visible (Chadburn, Robinson, Henschel, Bendix), the weight and construction agree, and the price is ≤ $200 (rare). Pass when: a "vintage ships telegraph" 12" with a printed dial, or any Indian repro. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat: pedestal telegraphs and binnacles are bigger and excluded; wall units and small brass only.
