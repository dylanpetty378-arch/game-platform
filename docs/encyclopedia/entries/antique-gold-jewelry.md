## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pre-1920 gold jewelry: Victorian (1837–1901) earrings, brooches, lockets, mourning pieces; Edwardian (1901–1915) lacy platinum-topped; Art Nouveau (1890–1915). Tells: hand-made findings, a C-clasp on brooches (pre-1900), a soft rosy-yellow colour, wear on high points, and hand engraving. Earrings for pierced ears had screw-backs (1900–1930s) or wires; modern butterfly-back posts and a 1990s-style jewelry box point to modern pieces.

## Makers, brands and variants
Mostly unsigned. Notable: Castellani (Etruscan revival), Giuliano, Tiffany, Krementz (rolled-gold 1900s on), Carter Gough & Co, Bippart Griscom & Osborn, Riker Bros (Art Nouveau). Gold-filled marks: "1/20 12K GF", "GF", "RGP".

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid gold marks: 10K, 14K, 18K, 417/585/750 (often missing on pre-1906 US pieces). Gold-filled shows brass at worn edges. Glass "rubies" have rounded facet edges and bubbles; real garnets/rubies have sharp facets.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Solder repairs, replaced findings (modern posts on old earrings), missing stones, dents, worn-through gold-fill.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Lots of reproduction "Victorian" jewelry; fake karat stamps on plated pieces. Testing (acid or XRF) is needed, which is a professional-verification issue for high-value pieces.

## Market
Very liquid at melt for solid gold; antique premium pieces sell steadily. Days to sell: 7–30.

## What sellers usually miss
Unmarked pre-1906 US solid gold (tests 14k); Victorian hair-work mourning pieces; Etruscan granulation.

## Shipping / handling
Pocket-size. Ships in a padded mailer with insurance, $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give the karat test result, weight in grams, dimensions and stone ID (glass vs. genuine), and close-up photos of the marks.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$6 out − ~$6 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Only buy when the karat mark and weight are confirmed and the price is at or below melt plus about 20%, or for a clearly antique premium piece at ≤ ~30% of resale. Signed high-end pieces need professional authentication, which is outside Dylan's model.
