## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Flat-seated riding saddle (no horn) with knee flaps, stirrup bars, and girth billets under the flap (general knowledge, verify). Types: close contact/jumping, all-purpose, dressage (long straight flaps). Maker name and model are usually stamped on the cantle or the flap near the stirrup bar; seat size (e.g. 17") and tree width (narrow/medium/wide, or cm) are often stamped under the flap or on the cantle (general knowledge, verify).

More detail:
- **Type by flap**: close contact = forward-cut flap, minimal knee roll, shallow seat; all-purpose/general purpose = moderately forward flap and deeper seat; dressage = long, straight flap, deep seat, usually long billets (short girth); monoflap = single flap combining saddle flap and billet guard (general knowledge, verify).
- **Where size is marked**: Crosby put "M" or "W" on the near-side stirrup bar; Stübben marks tree width on the far-side billet guard in cm (28–32 cm, 31 = medium) and older Stübbens used letters A–D, which can wear off (EquiSearch). Stübben, Passier and Hermès saddles carry serial numbers; a Stübben serial can be traced to the year, maker, flap size and tree size (EquiSearch).
- **Seat size**: measured from the saddle nail (head nail beside the pommel) to the centre of the cantle, in inches (general knowledge, verify).
- **Tree width is not standard across brands**: some use cm between the tree points, others narrow/medium/wide; a Stübben 32 doesn't fit like a Prestige 32; roughly 27 = narrow, 30 = medium, 32 = wide (Equine Ink). Estimating from photos: fist test at the gullet, D-ring spacing, or point angle (narrow ~86°, medium ~90°, wide ~94°, Equine Ink citing Lorien Stables). Marked width may be wrong if a previous owner adjusted the tree; old trees spread slightly.

Look-alikes: Western saddles (horn, big skirts), Australian stock saddles (poleys/knee pads and horn on some), synthetic English saddles (Wintec — fabric-like synthetic, light), and cheap Indian-made leather saddles with a generic "English leather" label.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Stübben** (Germany): Siegfried used $300–500. Founded 1894 in Krefeld by Johannes Stübben (Handmade in Germany). Known for a wood-and-steel spring tree; seat depth set by the seat webbing (Equine Ink). Serial numbers traceable to year and specs (EquiSearch). Tree width in cm on the far-side billet guard.
- **Pessoa** (sold by Dover; made in various countries) and **Prestige** (Italy): $400–900. Prestige Italia founded 1974, headquartered in Trissino, Italy, US office Wellington, FL (Prestige). Prestige tree widths are numbers (e.g. 32) that do not match Stübben sizes (Equine Ink).
- **No-name / Indian-made leather**: $50–150.
- Premium French makers (CWD, Devoucoux, Antares) and Passier (Germany) resell far higher; synthetic Wintec lower (general knowledge, verify – not in the earlier summary). CWD (France) was founded in 1998 (CWD Sellier). Passier and Hermès carry serial numbers (EquiSearch).
- Vintage US makers (Crosby, Hermès older pieces) vary widely (general knowledge, verify). Crosby stamped tree width on the stirrup bar (EquiSearch).
- **Kieffer** (Germany): fibreglass-reinforced synthetic tree that certified dealers can adjust (Equine Ink).
- **County** (US): trees can be adjusted, so a "medium" may now fit as a wide (Equine Ink).

What moves value: brand and model; current-generation vs old model; seat size (17"–17.5" sells most readily, very small or very large seats are slower — general knowledge, verify); standard medium/medium-wide trees; adjustable or changeable-gullet trees; wool-flocked panels (easier to reflock, EquiSearch); condition of tree, billets and seat; original serial/paperwork.

### Quick ID checklist
- Brand and model stamp visible (cantle, flap near the stirrup bar, or under the flap)?
- Serial number photographed (Stübben, Passier, Hermès and most premium makers)?
- Tree width stamp: stirrup bar (Crosby M/W), far-side billet guard (Stübben cm or A–D), or under the flap.
- Seat size stamp, or nail-to-cantle measurement.
- Flex test video: pommel against thigh, pull the cantle — slight give is normal; buckling = tree problem (EquiSearch).
- Billets: cracks, stretched holes, delamination (one of the first things to go — EquiSearch).
- Seat piping: splits along the seat edge (high-friction area).
- Panels: lumpy, flat, hard or uneven flocking; cracked panel leather.
- Leather: deep, even, supple vs dry, cracked, painted-edge cheap leather; "Made in India" stamp.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Quality: thick supple calfskin or buffalo leather, neat even stitching, wool- or foam-flocked panels, a solid (spring) tree, maker stamp. Cheap: thin stiff leather with painted edges, crude stitching, stamped "Made in India" (general knowledge, verify).
More: trees are wood or fibreglass/plastic (EquiSearch); spring trees have steel strips that give a little; panels are wool flock or layered wool/felt/foam — neither is clearly better but wool is easier to restuff (EquiSearch). Care matters more than age: a well-kept older saddle can beat a neglected newer one, and a dry but sound saddle can be revived with conditioner (EquiSearch).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Broken or twisted tree (kills value – check by flexing pommel to cantle), worn billets, cracked dry leather, lumpy panels needing reflocking, torn knee rolls. Ask for photos of the gullet/tree area, billets and panels (general knowledge, verify).
Repair costs (EquiSearch, varies by repairer): replacing all billets from ~$100; restuffing panels from ~$50; seat replacement expensive; tree repair is specialist and costly. Tree cracks often can't be seen without taking the saddle apart, so a flex test is the minimum.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Counterfeit/badged no-name saddles with fake maker stamps exist in the low end (general knowledge, verify). Check that the serial number format and stamp style match the brand; for Stübben the serial can be traced through the maker (EquiSearch). No web source on specific counterfeit English saddle brands was found this run.

## Market
Riders buy used saddles constantly via eBay, tack-shop consignment, Facebook groups and Saddlefitter-type sites; fit (seat size, tree width) decides sales (general knowledge, verify). Most sellers negotiate, and many shops allow a trial on the horse (EquiSearch) — buyers expect full measurements and often a trial or return option.

## What sellers usually miss
unknown – likely the maker/model and tree size (general knowledge, verify). Likely candidates: the serial number (which dates and specifies a Stübben), the tree-width stamp on the billet guard or stirrup bar, and the model name.

## Shipping / handling
~$40 to ship (earlier summary); saddle box ~15–20 lb (general knowledge, verify). Pad the pommel and cantle, cover the seat, ship with no stirrups or leathers attached unless sold together.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker, model, seat size, tree width, flap length/type, panel type, condition of tree and billets, serial. Photograph the stamps, gullet, panels and billets (general knowledge, verify).

### What to tell the buyer
State the brand, model and serial number; seat size (and how measured); tree width as stamped and in the brand's own system (e.g. "Stübben 31 cm = medium"), noting that sizes vary between brands; flap type and length; panel type (wool or foam). Say whether the tree passes a flex test and has not been adjusted (or that it has). Describe billets (original/replaced, cracks), seat and piping, panels (even/lumpy, last reflocked), and leather condition. Photograph both sides, the gullet from the front, the panels from underneath, the billets and every stamp. Keywords: brand, model, English saddle, close contact / dressage / all purpose, seat size, tree width, used.

## Buy rule
Named German/French makers under $120 (shipping ~$40). Pass broken trees and no-name. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways. Fits a passenger seat.
