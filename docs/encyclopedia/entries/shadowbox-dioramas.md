# Shadowbox dioramas (1980s–2000s resin occupational shadow boxes; Pomeroy/Enesco/Home Interiors type; folk-art and Victorian shadowboxes; hair wreaths; sailor's valentines; cased taxidermy; Joseph Cornell-style assemblages)

Buy rule (short): Pass on resin occupational shadowboxes; buy only handmade 19th–early 20th-c. folk-art or Victorian shadowboxes at ≤ $40.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Framed box under glass with miniatures arranged as a scene.
- **1980s–2000s commercial (Taiwan/China):** resin miniatures (doctor's coat, bag, stethoscope, microscope), printed paper ephemera, distressed wood frame with fake "wormholes"; themes: doctor, dentist, pharmacist, lawyer, sewing, golf, fishing, kitchen, nursing.
- **Handmade/folk:** irregular construction, real tiny objects, carved figures, dated labels.
- **Victorian (1837–1901):**
  - **Hair wreaths:** human hair (sometimes horse hair filler) woven around fine wire into flowers and leaves, with beads/buttons/seeds, mounted on silk or velvet in a deep shadowbox frame; often a horseshoe shape, open end up "to catch luck"; many 18 in.+ across; memorial versions place the newest deceased's hair at the centre and may include a note naming the people (Sauk County Historical Society).
  - **Wax flowers** and **cased taxidermy birds** in glazed boxes or under domes.
  - **Memorial shadowboxes** with photos, coffin plates, hair.
- **Sailor's valentines:** octagonal wooden box frames, ~8–18 in. wide, glass front, sometimes two hinged together, filled with small shells glued on cotton in compass-rose, heart and anchor patterns, sometimes spelling a message; mostly c. 1830–1900, made in Barbados and sold at Belgrave's / the New Curiosity Shop in Bridgetown; some carry its label inside (Beachcombing Magazine).

## Makers, brands and variants
- Mostly unbranded imports; occasional "Sterling & Noble", "Burwood", "Home Interiors" labels.
- Collectible: Victorian hairwork/wax flower boxes, sailor's valentines, folk-art carved dioramas, miniature room artists.
- **Taxidermy case makers:** labelled cases from named Victorian/Edwardian taxidermists command the premium; a large Victorian Birds of Paradise diorama attributed to Rowland Ward (London) hammered £18,000 at Tennants (April 2022); a cased fur seal pup by James Hutchings (Aberystwyth) £2,000 (Tennants). Makers often put a paper trade label inside the case back or on the base (general knowledge, verify).
- **Sailor's valentines:** New Curiosity Shop (Bridgetown, Barbados) labels; modern makers still produce them by the same method.

### Quick ID checklist
- Material of the miniatures: resin with uniform paint (commercial) vs real metal, wood, shell, hair, wax, feathers (period/handmade).
- Frame: faux-wormhole distressed pine = 1980s–2000s import; octagonal hinged frame with shells = sailor's valentine; deep glazed case with painted background = taxidermy/wax.
- Back of the frame: paper trade label, period newspaper backing, square nails, hand-written inscription, or modern staples/MDF/sawtooth hanger.
- Hair: fine glossy fibres looped on wire in flower forms, often brown/blond/black mix.
- Note or card inside naming people/dates (memorial).
- Glass: old wavy glass vs flat modern glass.
- Condition: loose pieces lying at the bottom, insect damage (taxidermy), faded or flaking backgrounds.
- Size: big cases (more than ~24 in.) can fail the passenger-seat/shipping test.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Victorian human-hair memorial wreath in original deep frame** — ~5–20× a resin occupational box ($150–1,000+ vs $15–45, verify) — horseshoe or wreath of tiny wire-and-hair flowers on velvet/silk, deep gilt or walnut frame.
- **Antique Barbados sailor's valentine (c. 1830–1900)** — top of the $150–1,000+ band and above (dealer asking on 1stDibs much higher) — octagonal frame, tiny West Indian shells in compass/heart patterns, sometimes "New Curiosity Shop" label.
- **Labelled Victorian cased taxidermy (named maker)** — hundreds to thousands; attributed Rowland Ward diorama £18,000 (Tennants 2022) — trade label inside case, painted habitat background, glass on three sides.
- **Victorian wax-flower arrangement in shadowbox** — $150+ vs $15–45 (verify) — translucent wax petals, slight dust/bloom, original case.
- **Handmade folk-art miniature room or carved diorama with maker/date label** — $40–150 vs $15–45 — real tiny furniture, carved figures, hand-lettered label.
- **Joseph Cornell-style assemblage by a known artist** — artist-dependent; needs signature and provenance — signed box construction with found objects.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Resin items with uniform paint = mass production. Real metal/wood miniatures and handwritten labels = handmade.
- Old backboards: thin hand-sawn or planed boards, square or early wire nails, period newspaper; new: MDF, staples, foam board.
- Victorian frames: walnut, rosewood veneer, gilt gesso with chips showing white/red underlayer.
- Taxidermy: glass eyes, painted backgrounds, period cotton wool; feather loss and fading show age.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Detached pieces, cracked glass, faded prints, dust inside.
- Hair wreaths: crushed or unravelled flowers, moth/insect damage, water staining on the silk/velvet.
- Sailor's valentines: missing shells (replacements are usually brighter/different species), lifted glue.
- Taxidermy: moth/beetle damage, feather loss, fading in sunlight.
- Ask: any loose pieces inside, glass original, any trade label on the back, any insect activity.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Not relevant at the low end; "antique style" new pieces common.
- Modern sailor's valentines (Barbados and elsewhere) are made by the same method with newer materials; check for modern glue, bright shells, new frames and modern labels.
- Hair wreaths are rarely faked, but frames are often replaced.
- Taxidermy: protected species carry legal sale restrictions (ask before listing; CITES/Migratory Bird Treaty Act — general knowledge, verify before any purchase).

## Market
- Occupational shadowboxes sell slowly as gifts to professionals; glass and size make shipping costly.
- Victorian hairwork, sailor's valentines and taxidermy sell to curiosity/oddities collectors and specialist auctions.

## What sellers usually miss
- Commercial boxes are often called "vintage"; rarely undervalued.
- Estate sellers call hair wreaths "dried flower arrangements" and sailor's valentines "shell art".

## Shipping / handling
- 14×12×4" box under glass: 5–7 lb, $18–25; glass breakage risk.
- Remove or tape glass; pack contents to stop loose pieces moving; double-box.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Dimensions, theme, contents secure, glass condition.
- Photograph the back and any label; close-ups of materials.

### What to tell the buyer
For commercial: "Decorative [doctor's office] shadowbox, resin miniatures and printed paper, distressed wood frame, [size] in.; all pieces secure; glass intact." For Victorian: "Victorian [human-hair mourning wreath / wax-flower arrangement] in its [original deep frame], [size] in. Hair woven over fine wire into flowers and leaves, mounted on [velvet/silk]. Condition: [some loose flowers / small water mark], shown. Hairwork was made as fancy work and as memorials during the Victorian era (1837–1901)." For sailor's valentines: state octagonal size, single or double, any label, and any missing/replaced shells.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$20) − ship to Dylan (~$20) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Commercial boxes never work. Victorian/folk only.
