Buy rule (short): Pass on resin occupational shadowboxes; buy only handmade 19th–early 20th-c. folk-art or Victorian shadowboxes at ≤ $40.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Framed box under glass with miniatures arranged as a scene.
- **1980s–2000s commercial (Taiwan/China):** resin miniatures (doctor's coat, bag, stethoscope, microscope), printed paper ephemera, distressed wood frame with fake "wormholes"; themes: doctor, dentist, pharmacist, lawyer, sewing, golf, fishing, kitchen, nursing.
- **Handmade/folk:** irregular construction, real tiny objects, carved figures, dated labels.
- **Victorian:** wax flowers, hairwork wreaths, taxidermy birds, memorial shadowboxes.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Mostly unbranded imports; occasional "Sterling & Noble", "Burwood", "Home Interiors" labels.
- Collectible: Victorian hairwork/wax flower boxes, sailor's valentines, folk-art carved dioramas, miniature room artists.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Resin items with uniform paint = mass production. Real metal/wood miniatures and handwritten labels = handmade.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Detached pieces, cracked glass, faded prints, dust inside.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Not relevant at the low end; "antique style" new pieces common.

## Market
- Occupational shadowboxes sell slowly as gifts to professionals; glass and size make shipping costly.

## What sellers usually miss
- Commercial boxes are often called "vintage"; rarely undervalued.

## Shipping / handling
- 14×12×4" box under glass: 5–7 lb, $18–25; glass breakage risk.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Dimensions, theme, contents secure, glass condition.

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$20) − ship to Dylan (~$20) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Commercial boxes never work. Victorian/folk only.
