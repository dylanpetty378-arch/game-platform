## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Heavy solid-brass (or brass-plated) electric table lamps, 1930s–1990s. Stiffel (Chicago, 1932–2000s) is the name sellers attach to almost every brass lamp. Real Stiffel tells in a photo: thick, polished heavy-gauge brass with a deep lacquered gold colour; brass "Stiffel" stamped on the socket shell or a foil/paper label under the base; their patented 3-way socket with a switch knob that pulls/turns at the top of the column; finial often a brass ball; many models have the characteristic Stiffel "push-up" height-adjust (vertical rod with a tension spring) on floor lamps. Classic forms: candlestick column with stepped round base, urn/baluster, column with black or white enamel insert, Hollywood Regency tole and faux bamboo, and drum-shaded pairs. "Oil-lamp-style" lamps have a clear or cut glass font, crowned gallery (imitating a kerosene burner) and a column — these were made by Stiffel but much more often by unbranded US makers (Leviton-socket, stamped thin brass).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Stiffel** (Theophile Stiffel, Chicago, 1932; brand later sold, production moved; new "Stiffel" by Lighting Enterprises after 2000s). Marks: "STIFFEL" stamped on socket shell, cloth/foil label under felt. Value is in pairs and designer forms (tole, bamboo, Hollywood Regency, Billy Haines-style), not in single candlestick lamps.
- **Frederick Cooper** (Chicago, 1923–2010s): heavier decorator lamps, often with porcelain/tole bodies; paper label.
- **Chapman** (Chapman Manufacturing, 1970s–90s): brass/glass, faux bamboo, animal forms; foil label, dated stamps.
- **Westwood, Rembrandt, Paul Hanson, Laurel**: mid-century US makers; Laurel and Paul Hanson draw MCM buyers.
- Generic/import: stamped thin brass (dented easily), plastic-insert columns, glass fonts; often 1960s–90s, "made in Taiwan/Korea" or unmarked.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Solid brass: heavy, magnet does not stick, threads and joints crisp. Plated steel: magnet sticks, rust at seams, light weight.
- Original Stiffel lacquer: even gold with no brush strokes; worn lacquer shows mottled brown spotting, which buyers accept on mid-century pieces.
- Hand-cut/pressed glass fonts vs moulded with seam lines: moulded is cheap production.
- Old vs new: cloth twisted cord, bakelite plug, 2-prong unpolarised plug, brass socket with "pat. pend" = old; clear PVC cord and polarized plug = new or rewired.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Lacquer loss/spotting (cosmetic; polishing removes value-holding patina for some buyers), dents in spun brass bases, cracked glass fonts, broken harp, old cord (sell as "needs rewiring" or rewire — rewiring is a repair, Dylan avoids it). Missing original shade is normal; buyers expect no shade.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Post-2000 "Stiffel" under licence is lighter and cheaper. Many lamps are listed as Stiffel with no mark at all — the attribution is the seller's guess. Insist on the socket stamp or label.

## Market
Steady decorator demand; designer forms sell in days, generic singles sit for months. Local sale (FB Marketplace) beats eBay for anything that is not a designer form because of shipping.

## What sellers usually miss
Unmarked tole and faux-bamboo pieces (Chapman, Frederick Cooper) and Laurel/Paul Hanson MCM designs get listed as "old brass lamp" — the shape is the tell, not the name.

## Shipping / handling
28–34" tall, 6–10 lb; double box, harp removed and wrapped, glass font packed separately. $25–40 to ship nationally. Fragile if glass.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** "Stiffel" (only if stamped) / Frederick Cooper / Chapman, "table lamp pair", form (faux bamboo/tole/urn/candlestick/Hollywood Regency), "solid brass", height, "mid century". Junk: "Stiffel style", "vintage lamp" alone, "works great" without a shade note. `Stiffel Solid Brass Faux Bamboo Table Lamps Pair 30" Hollywood Regency Signed`
- **Where:** FB Marketplace local for singles and anything generic (shipping $25–40 kills eBay); eBay BIN with offers for signed designer pairs and forms (median $100 single Stiffel).
- **Price position:** signed single at the sold median ($100), OBO, accept −20% local; pairs at $150–350, designer forms at $250–800, firm 3 weeks; generic $25–70 local. Cut 15% at 30 days.
- **Photos:** first: lamp lit (no shade) against a plain wall; then socket shell stamp, label under felt, base underside, glass font, magnet on the brass, lacquer spotting and dents close-up, cord and plug, harp.
- **State:** maker mark wording (never call it Stiffel unmarked), height, solid brass (magnet test), socket type, cord condition, works or not, lacquer wear and dents by photo number, no shade included.
- **Pack / haul:** 28–34" tall, 6–10 lb; harp removed, glass font packed separately, double box, $25–40; fits a passenger seat.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "Signed [Stiffel] [faux bamboo] pair, solid brass with the original lacquer — single generic brass lamps are $40; the matched designer-form pairs are what sells at $250+."
