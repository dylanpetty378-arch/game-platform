## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pairs of weighted ends that hold books upright. Sold as pairs; a single is worth 20–35% of a pair.
- **Cast iron (1900s–1930s)**: painted or plated (Judd, Bradley & Hubbard, Hubley, Connecticut Foundry). Ships, Scottie dogs, Lincoln, Indian chiefs, galleons, owls, parrots. Rough sand-cast back, often hollow back with an open cavity.
- **Bronze-clad / "bronzed" (1910s–30s)**: Armor Bronze, Pompeian Bronze, Marion Bronze, Galvano: thin bronze electroplated over a plaster or cast-iron core, usually felted base with a paper/foil label or stamp. Rubbed edges show white plaster or dark iron.
- **Solid bronze**: heavy, uniform patina, crisp chasing, maker stamp (Tiffany Studios NEW YORK + number, Gorham Co Founders, Roman Bronze Works). Rare on Craigslist.
- **Frankart (NY 1921–early 1950s)**: Art Deco nude female or stylised animal figures, spelter (white-metal) with "jade green", "cream" or "bronzoid" paint finishes, stamped "Frankart Inc." + patent/date on the back or base edge. Often originally with glass or wood.
- **Roycroft (East Aurora NY, 1900s–30s)**: hand-hammered copper, orb-and-double-cross "R" mark, flared or tabbed designs with brown/copper patina.
- **Stone/onyx/agate/petrified wood**: polished cut slabs; Mexican/Pakistani onyx and Brazilian agate (1960s–today) are common decor. Real petrified wood and polished geode pairs are mineral-shop items.
- **Borghese (1940s–60s, Los Angeles)**: gilt-gesso/chalkware with applied decoupage prints (e.g. Carle Vernet horse prints), red "Borghese" foil label; light, chips at the base.
- **Decor assemblages**: old shoe lasts, plane parts, gears, spools mounted on a new wood base. These are new-made decor; value is the old part.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Frankart Inc.** (New York, 1921–c.1955): stamped "Frankart Inc." with "Pat. appl'd for" or year; nudes $150–500/pair; animals $60–150. Reproductions exist (see Fakes).
- **Roycroft**: orb-and-cross mark; hammered copper $100–300/pair; "Roycroft" in later script indicates later production.
- **Bradley & Hubbard** (Meriden CT, to 1940): "B&H" triangle-lamp mark; cast iron and brass $50–150.
- **Judd Co.** (CT): numbered cast iron, often polychrome; $40–120.
- **Armor Bronze Co.**, **Pompeian Bronze Co.**, **Marion Bronze**, **Galvano (Gorham Galvano process)**: bronze-clad; $40–150; Galvano and Gorham higher.
- **K&O (Kronheim & Oldenbusch)**, **Jennings Brothers (JB)**: spelter and gilt-metal, 1920s–40s; $30–100.
- **Hubley**: cast iron dogs/animals; $50–150.
- **Tiffany Studios**: bronze, Zodiac/Pine Needle/Grapevine/Bookmark patterns; $400–2,000+.
- **Dodge Inc.**, **Syroco** (wood composition) and **Borghese**: decor-tier $15–50.
- **Alva Studios / Austin Productions / Alva Museum Replicas**: resin or plaster copies of museum pieces, signed in the mould; $15–40.
- Mid-century: **Hagenauer** (Vienna, stamped "WHW"/"Hagenauer Wien") $300–1,500; **Ben Seibel / Jenfredware**; **Danish teak (Kay Bojesen-style)**; **Jere** brass.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Weight: solid bronze and cast iron are heavy for size; spelter is lighter and grey under rubs; bronze-clad shows plaster or iron at chips.
- Backs: Frankart and quality spelter have a stamped name on the back or base; Tiffany Studios stamp under base.
- Paint: original Frankart finish is thin, soft and slightly worn on high points; repainted pieces are thick and glossy.
- Felt bases: old felt with residue of paper labels is good; modern felt over a clean base suggests a new item.
- Stone: real stone is cold, heavy (a pair can be 5–15 lb) and shows natural banding mirrored across a book-matched pair (cut from one block). Price is low regardless.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint loss and rub-through on spelter and bronze-clad; broken-off arms, tails, ears and arrows (spelter snaps); glued repairs at ankles; missing felt; rust on cast iron; chips at the edges of stone/onyx (very common) and cracks along natural fault lines; chalkware chips and flaking gilt on Borghese. Over-cleaned bronze loses value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Frankart reproductions (1980s on) are cast resin or lighter metal with crude detail; real Frankart mark is stamped into the metal, not cast in raised letters on resin.
- Cast-iron repros (Scotties, ships, Indian heads) made in Asia since the 1980s: rough grainy surface, bright paint, light weight, no wear on the bottom.
- "Bronze" resin: room-temperature, light, rings dull.
- Tiffany Studios fake stamps exist; authentication needed at that level (outside Dylan's lane).

## Market
Steady, moderately liquid on eBay; decorators buy stone and chalkware cheaply; collectors pay for Frankart, Roycroft and figural cast iron. Heavy pairs carry high shipping, which compresses margins on anything under $80.

## What sellers usually miss
- Frankart stamped on the back edge under paint and grime; sellers list as "Art Deco lady bookends".
- Roycroft orb mark on the underside of hammered copper.
- Bronze-clad vs. solid bronze: sellers overprice bronze-clad as "bronze"; the reverse miss (solid bronze priced as metal decor) is rare but the money case.
- Most sellers overvalue stone and onyx (Lens median prices for stone pairs come from retail decor listings, not sold data).

## Shipping / handling
Heavy: cast iron/stone pairs 4–15 lb → $15–30 each way. Wrap each separately, protect projecting parts; onyx chips in transit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph backs and bases for marks, give weight per piece and height, state material plainly ("bronze-clad over plaster", "spelter with original paint"), and note any glued repair. Sell as a pair.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$18 to buyer − ~$18 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Stone/onyx/agate, resin, Borghese, decor assemblages, singles: pass at any price over ~$10 (resale under $40).
- Marked cast iron or bronze-clad pairs: buy at ≤ $20 only if resale ≥ $100.
- Frankart / Roycroft / Gorham / Tiffany Studios–marked: buy at ≤ $60 after a mark photo; Tiffany Studios level needs authentication: pass.
- All fit a passenger seat.
