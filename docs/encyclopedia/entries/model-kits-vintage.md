## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Boxed unbuilt kits. Value tells: sealed/unstarted, box art era (1960s–70s Monogram/Aurora/Revell with painted box art), scale (1/48 and 1/32 aircraft, 1/24 cars, 1/35 armor > 1/72), and brand (Aurora figures/monsters, early Tamiya, Hasegawa "Minicraft" era, Airfix Series 1 bagged kits). 1980s–2000s 1/72 Hasegawa/Airfix are plentiful. Box end-flaps carry the kit number, copyright year and scale (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Aurora** (US, 1950s–1977): monster/figure kits (Frankenstein, Wolf Man, etc.) — top tier sealed $150–400 (2025-10 comp), up to $1,000 for rare.
- **Monogram** (US): 1960s painted box-art kits collectible; 1/48 aircraft decent sealed.
- **Revell** (US/Germany): 1950s ships sealed top tier; 1960s box-art kits collectible.
- **Tamiya** (Japan): 1/35 armor decent sealed; 1/12 motorcycles collectible; early (1960s–70s) Tamiya more collectible.
- **Hasegawa** (Japan): 1/72 1980s sealed $12–25; "Minicraft"-era imports.
- **Airfix** (UK): Series 1 bagged kits (early, collectible); 1/72 Ju 52 1970s box $20–35.
- **Italeri**: 1/72 1980s+ common.
- Rare Japanese sci-fi kits (anime/tokusatsu): top tier.
- Value multipliers: factory-sealed, first-issue box art, figure/monster subjects, glow-in-the-dark Aurora reissues are lower than originals (general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Injection-moulded styrene sprues in a printed card box. Tells for value: shrink-wrap original (not re-wrapped), parts still on sprues, decals present and not yellowed, instructions present. Old-style box art (painted illustration rather than photo) indicates 1950s–70s issues (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Started kits (parts cut off), missing parts or decals, yellowed/cracked decals, crushed or water-stained boxes, and re-shrink-wrapped "sealed" boxes. Open-box kits drop to the junk tier unless rare; buyers ask for a parts count. Ask: sealed or opened? parts bagged? decals present?

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reissues are the main issue: Aurora moulds were reissued by Monogram, Polar Lights and Moebius; Revell and Monogram repeatedly reissued 1960s kits with new box art (general knowledge, verify). Reissues are worth a fraction of originals — check copyright year and maker on the box. Re-shrink-wrapped boxes are passed off as factory sealed.

## Market
High-volume, low-ticket eBay category; buyers are modellers (who want to build) and collectors (who want sealed originals) (general knowledge, verify). Common kits sell slowly at $10–25; Aurora and 1960s box-art kits sell readily. Bulk estates of kit stashes are common supply. Winter is the traditional modelling season (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Aurora and 1960s box-art kits in a bin lot; everything else is a $15 kit that costs $10 to ship.

## Shipping / handling
Light but bulky; $10–15 each. Lots of listing work per dollar. eBay 13%. Ship boxed kits in a box, not a bag, to avoid crushed corners.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, kit number, scale, subject, issue year (from box), sealed vs opened, parts complete/decals present/instructions present. Photograph box top, all sides (especially end flap with year), sprues and decals if opened. Title keywords: "Vintage 1964 Aurora Frankenstein Model Kit Sealed", "Monogram 1/48 ... unbuilt complete".

## Buy rule
Buy when: an Aurora/1960s Monogram/early Tamiya is visible in a lot at ≤ $5/kit average. Pass when: 1/72 1980s+ bins at ≥ $8/kit. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (lots of kits can exceed it; size check the lot).
