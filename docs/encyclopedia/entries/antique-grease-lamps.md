Buy rule (short): Pay ≤ $20 only for genuine hand-forged iron betty/crusie lamps (hinged lid, hanging hook, wick channel); pass on tin cans with wicks.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Open-wick lamps burning grease, tallow, lard or fish/whale oil, used from the 1700s to late 1800s; later tin wick lamps (kerosene) through the 1940s.
- **Betty lamp**: shallow iron/tin pan with an inner wick channel and hinged lid, hanger + pick on a chain; hand-forged rivets.
- **Crusie/Phoebe**: two stacked open pans with pinched spout, a hanger; Scottish/Scandinavian.
- **Miners' "teapot" cap lamps**: small tin/brass oil-wick lamp with a spout and hook to clip onto a cap (1850–1910s), brands like Dewar, Wolf; mostly 3–4" tall.
- **Tin spout wick can / smudge pot / kerosene torch**: cylindrical can with a long tubular spout holding a cotton wick — railroad/road flares, barn torches, and homemade 20th-century lamps. The example is this.

## Makers, brands and variants
Mostly unsigned blacksmith work; some betties signed/dated (German "Pennsylvania Dutch" makers, e.g., Peter Derr, 1830s–50s brass/iron betties, very valuable). Miners' lamps: Hibernia, Dewar, Wolf, Shanklin. Smudge pots: Toro, Dietz flare pots.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Hand-forged iron with rivets, hammer marks, uneven edges = old. Machine-seamed rolled tin, crimped seams, modern solder = 20th century. Brass inlays/decorative finials = high.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust, missing lids, missing picks/hangers; replaced hangers lower value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Betty lamps were widely reproduced in the 20th century (colonial-revival tin and iron, Williamsburg reproductions) — these have stamped parts and uniform rust. Fake "aged" miners' lamps exist.

## Market
Small collector niche (Rushlight Club, primitives buyers). Plain items sell slowly.

## What sellers usually miss
Real 18th–19th c. iron betties sold as "old rusty lamp" in barn lots; Derr signatures on brass betties.

## Shipping / handling
Small, 1–2 lb, $10–14.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State construction (forged/riveted vs rolled tin), dimensions, completeness (lid, hook, pick), and honest period.
