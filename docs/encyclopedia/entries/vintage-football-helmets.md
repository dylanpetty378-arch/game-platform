## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Leather era (c.1895–1949)**. Flat-top / "beehive" (1910s–early 20s: soft leather segments meeting in a flat or pinched crown, no ear holes); **dog-ear** (1920s: leather ear flaps that stick out sideways, often with a separate chin strap); **wing / winged-front** (1930s: raised leather "wings" on the forehead and top, multiple panels, rounder); late leather (1940s: hard-moulded leather/fibre shell with a stitched rolled edge and web suspension). Maker stamp is pressed into the leather at the back of the neck or the forehead, with a model number (Spalding ZH1, Wilson D271T/F2050, Hutch H-18/H-22, Rawlings "18").
- **Early plastic (1939–early 1960s)**. Riddell patented the first plastic suspension helmet in 1939; wartime shortages kept leather alive until c.1949. 1940s–early 50s plastic shells often have a **leather or vinyl edge binding stitched around the rim**, a rolled leather brow, snap-on ear holes and a **web suspension laced in the crown** (six-point "cradle"). Riddell RT / RK (Kra-Lite, ABS) and MacGregor H-series follow in the 1950s–60s. The suspension pad is often printed "Riddell Suspension Helmet" with patent numbers; size is pencilled or stamped on the straps.
- **Facemask era (1955+)**. Single or double bar masks bolted through the shell. Holes at the front sides or brow = mask was fitted at some point.
- Look-alikes: modern "vintage style" leather decor helmets (no maker stamp, uniform machine stitching, bright cowhide), 1970s–90s youth plastic helmets, and motorcycle/pith/fire helmets (separate entries).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **A.G. Spalding & Bros.** (1890s–1960s) — top-quality leather; stamp "SPALDING" in an oval or script plus model (ZH1, ZH2, etc.); also early head harnesses.
- **Rawlings** (St Louis) — 1920s–50s leather and later plastic; stamp "Rawlings" + model number at the back.
- **Wilson / Wilson-Western** (Chicago) — "Wilson-Western" 1920s, "Wilson" later; model codes (D271T, F2050, 82T).
- **Goldsmith (P. Goldsmith Sons, Cincinnati) → MacGregor Goldsmith (1936) → MacGregor** — leather and 1950s plastic; MacGregor shells were also issued as **Korean War-era tank crew helmets with ear receivers**.
- **Hutch (Hutchinson, Cincinnati)** — H-18, H-22 (top model of the 1940s), often black leather.
- **Riddell (Chicago, founded 1927 by John T. Riddell)** — first plastic suspension helmet (1939); RT and RK (Kra-Lite, 1950s–60s) shells; NFL standard by the 1960s.
- **Reach, Draper-Maynard (D&M), Thoroughbred, Nokona, Spot-Bilt** — smaller makers; D&M and Thoroughbred dog-ears collected.
- **Store brands** — Sears J.C. Higgins, Ward's Western Field/Hawthorne, youth-sized "boys" helmets: the low end.

### Quick ID checklist
1. Adult or youth? Ask for the size stamp/strap marking (adult 7–7⅝; youth "S", "M" boys or under 6¾).
2. Maker stamp and model number — back of neck, forehead, or the suspension pad.
3. Era by shape: flat-top/beehive → dog-ear → winged leather → plastic with stitched edge binding → plastic with mask holes.
4. Shell integrity: cracks, splits at ear holes, dried-out leather, missing suspension.
5. Paint: original team colours vs later spray repaint (overspray on the leather binding, paint in the vent holes).
6. Provenance: team decals, numbers, school stencils, letters.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Pre-1920 flat-top / beehive or head-harness helmets** — ~3–10× an ordinary 1930s leather helmet ($500–2,000+, estimate) — soft segmented leather, no hard shell, flat or pinched crown, no ear holes, often no maker visible.
- **1920s dog-ear leather with a maker stamp (Wilson-Western, Spalding, D&M, Thoroughbred)** — ~2–3× ($250–600, estimate; Grey Flannel unattributed dog-ear $238) — ear flaps that flare out like a cap, single-colour leather, stamp on the back.
- **Premium-model leather (Spalding ZH1, Hutch H-22, two-tone winged)** — ~2× a no-name leather ($250–450; Spalding ZH1 $385 SCP 2015) — clear model stamp, two-tone panels, wings on the crown, full chin strap.
- **Pro-team Riddell RT/RK suspension shells with period decals** — ~10–30× a plain shell ($1,000–3,000+; Eagles Riddell $3,175 SCP 2014) — team logo decals, numbers on the back, "Riddell Suspension Helmet" pad, period single-bar mask holes.
- **1950s MacGregor/Rawlings "tanker" football helmets with ear receivers** — military collectors pay ~2× a plain plastic shell (dealer asks $200–400, estimate) — football shell with olive-drab paint and headphone cups or wiring.
- **Matched four-helmet era sets (dog-ear, winged, Hutch, Nokona) in excellent condition** — ~$500+ each in a set (Collectrea four-helmet lot $2,370, Fall 2013).
- No premium: youth sizes, store brands (J.C. Higgins, Western Field), 1970s+ plastic, modern repro leather — $25–80.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Quality leather: thick, heavy, hand-stitched seams, felt or wool lining, padded ear cups and a riveted chin strap with a buckle. Cheap: thin split leather, cardboard fibre core, glued lining.
- Early plastic (Tenite / cellulose acetate) is brittle, yellows and crazes; later ABS (Kra-Lite) is tougher. Leather or vinyl edge binding stitched through the rim = late 1940s–mid 1950s.
- Web suspension with lacing and a leather sweatband = 1940s–60s; foam pads glued in = 1970s+.
- Paint: factory finishes are smooth with clean edges; a later spray repaint shows overspray on the binding, paint in the vent holes, and older colour underneath at chips.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Leather: dry rot, flaking, stiff or split panels, missing chin strap, torn lining, mould smell. Leather that powders when flexed is decor-only.
- Plastic: cracks radiating from ear holes and mask holes, shrunk or warped Tenite, missing suspension webbing.
- Repaints (common on school helmets passed down) cut value to decor level unless the repaint is a period team repaint with a documented history.
- Ask for: inside view of the suspension, the maker stamp, size, close-ups of the ear holes and rim.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Modern Chinese and Pakistani "vintage leather football helmets" sold as decor: perfectly even stitching, bright tan or brown leather, polyester lining, no or crude stamp, no wear inside.
- Riddell and others make throwback/replica suspension helmets for display; modern hardware and labels give them away.
- Fake team decals put on plain shells to make "game-used" helmets; real game-used needs letters/photo-matching (professional authentication — not Dylan's lane).

## Market
Steady decor and sports-memorabilia demand; peaks August–January (football season, Christmas gifts). Buyers: man-cave decorators, bars/restaurants, collectors of team equipment. eBay is the main venue; auction houses only for team/game-used. Order of magnitude: tens of sales a month on eBay (estimate). Plain leather sells in 1–4 weeks at a fair price; plain plastic shells are slower.

## What sellers usually miss
- The maker stamp and model number (inside back or forehead) — sellers title "old football helmet" and never photograph it.
- That a stitched edge binding plus web suspension dates a plastic shell to the late 1940s–50s, not the 1970s.
- Military "tanker" conversions with ear receivers, which cross into a second collector market.
- That youth sizes and repaints are worth far less — sellers often overprice those.

## Shipping / handling
Shipped weight 3–5 lb in a 14×12×12 in box; $15–22 to ship. Stuff the shell with paper so it can't flex; wrap dry leather in tissue, not bubble wrap directly on fragile finish. Not fragile in the glass sense, but Tenite shells crack if crushed.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State: maker and model stamp (photo), era, adult size, leather or plastic, suspension and chin strap present, any repaint, cracks or dry rot, inside photos. Title keywords: "Vintage 1940s 1950s [maker] [leather/suspension] football helmet adult size [model]". Say clearly if it is a decor piece, and never claim "game used" without documents.

## Buy rule
Buy when: adult-size, maker-stamped leather helmet ≤ $40, a dog-ear or flat-top ≤ $75, or a pre-1960 plastic suspension shell with intact suspension ≤ $30, and profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$18 to buyer − ~$18 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Pass when: youth size, store brand, repro, cracked, dry-rotted, or repainted with no maker visible unless ≤ $15. Team/game-used claims need professional authentication — pass unless priced as a plain helmet. Fits a passenger seat.
