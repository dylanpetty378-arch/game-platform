## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Mid-century US candlesticks are mostly "weighted" or "reinforced": a thin sterling shell over a pitch/plaster fill with a felt or metal base cover. Marked "Sterling Weighted", "Sterling Reinforced with Cement", "Weighted", "Reinforced" or "Cement Filled" (Appraisily; APMEX). Silver content per short stick is ~0.5–1 troy oz (general knowledge, verify).
- The filler can be cement, pitch (pine resin and plaster), wax, plaster, lead, sand or a steel rod, and it can run up the whole column, not only the base (Silver Collector forum; Appraisily). One cited shell measured about 0.003" thick, and one 4-lb weighted candelabra held only ~5.83 troy oz of sterling, roughly 10% of its weight (Silver Collector forum). Weight in the hand is therefore misleading: never multiply scale weight by spot (APMEX; Appraisily).
- "Sterling" in the US means at least 925/1000 silver; it describes the alloy, not whether the object is silver all through (Appraisily). Weighted sterling is not silverplate: plate is a coating on base metal (EP, EPNS, "silver on copper", "silver plate") (APMEX).
- Short console form (3–4"): gadrooned or beaded oval base, urn-shaped cup, often with removable bobeche. The example is exactly this: low pair with gadroon base edges, tarnished.
- Tall forms (8–12"): columnar, Corinthian-capital, baluster or tapered; candelabra (2–5 lights) with removable arms.
- Valuable versions: solid unweighted sticks (heavy for size without felt base), hallmarked English (lion passant, date letter, maker), Georg Jensen (Denmark, "925S" and Jensen mark), Tiffany ("Tiffany & Co. Makers Sterling"), Arts & Crafts hand-hammered (Kalo, Shreve).
- Where marks sit: US pieces on the base edge or the underside rim next to the "weighted" wording; English pieces on the base flange and often again on the nozzle/bobeche; Jensen on the underside of the foot with the design number.
- Title words: "sterling weighted", "reinforced", "Duchin", "Gorham", "Towle", "Shabbat", "console candlesticks", "hallmarked", "Georgian", "Jensen".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **US weighted (the common tier)**: Gorham, Towle, Duchin Creation, Web Silver, Fisher, Empire, International, Frank M. Whiting, Preisner, Alvin, Watrous. Also J.E. Caldwell (Appraisily). Mostly 1930s–1970s. Their weighted short pairs make up most of the supply.
- **S. Kirk & Son (Baltimore)**: repoussé (raised hand-chased floral) candlesticks, including weighted ones; the repoussé decoration lifts them well above plain weighted pairs (Terapeak example $227.5 for a Kirk repoussé pair vs median $49.95).
- **Tiffany & Co.**: "Tiffany & Co. Makers Sterling" with pattern and order numbers; Tiffany also made weighted (cement-filled) sticks, which still sell above generic ones because of the name (Silver Collector forum discusses a cement-filled Tiffany set; value step general knowledge, verify).
- **English**: Georgian/Victorian cast or loaded sticks (loaded = filled; very common from Sheffield and Birmingham), Elkington. Cast English sticks are solid and heavy. Hallmarks: lion passant (sterling), town mark (leopard's head London, anchor Birmingham, crown Sheffield), date letter, maker's initials, and from 1784 to 1890 the sovereign's-head duty mark (general knowledge, verify).
- **Danish: Georg Jensen**: solid, hand-finished, with design numbers. Examples: Sigvard Bernadotte design 855B pair, 29.3 troy oz for the pair; "Pomegranate" two-light candelabra no. 324, c. 1915–1930, 74 troy oz for the pair (1stDibs listings).
- **Arts & Crafts**: Kalo Shop (Chicago, "Hand Wrought at the Kalo Shop"), Shreve & Co. (San Francisco): visible hammer facets, solid.
- **Mexican**: "Sterling Mexico 925" and Taxco makers; Mexican solid silver candlestands appear in auction lots (Appraisily cites Serna). Spratling and Aguilar marks are the collector names (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Photograph or ask for the full line of marks: is there a "Weighted", "Reinforced" or "Cement Filled" qualifier next to "Sterling"?
- Underside: felt, cardboard or a pressed metal plate = weighted. An open hollow underside showing the inside of the silver, or a solid cast foot, = unweighted.
- Hallmark punches (lion, town mark, date letter) in a row = English; check for a duty head (pre-1890).
- Dented base where it meets the column, crinkled metal, or a leaning stem = thin weighted shell (Silver Collector forum).
- "GEORG JENSEN" in an oval/dotted mark with "925S" or "Sterling Denmark" and a design number under the foot.
- "Tiffany & Co. Makers" or "S. Kirk & Son" wording; full raised floral chasing = Kirk repoussé.
- Hammer facets all over plus "Hand Wrought" = Arts & Crafts (Kalo/Shreve).
- EP, EPNS, "silver on copper", "Sheffield" with no lion passant = silverplate, not sterling.
- Height and the pair's gross weight, so solid vs weighted can be judged.

### Golden-egg tells
- Georg Jensen with design number (e.g. 855B Bernadotte, 324 Pomegranate) — ~20x+ the weighted pair; $1,000–20,000+ tier — "GEORG JENSEN" oval/dotted mark, "925S", design number under the foot; solid, heavy (29–74 ozt per pair in the cited listings).
- Unweighted solid US or Continental sticks — ~6–40x; $300–2,000 tier — no felt or base plate, hollow underside showing seams, no "weighted" wording; value floor is the silver weight.
- English Georgian hallmarked (date letter before ~1830, duty head) — $300–2,000+ even when loaded — row of punches on the base flange, often repeated on the nozzle.
- Tiffany & Co. — $1,000+ for solid pairs (general knowledge, verify); weighted Tiffany above generic weighted — "Tiffany & Co. Makers Sterling" stamp with pattern/order number.
- S. Kirk & Son repoussé — ~4–5x the generic weighted pair (Terapeak example $227.5 vs median $49.95) — dense raised floral chasing over the whole stick, "S. Kirk & Son" or "Kirk" mark.
- Arts & Crafts hand-hammered (Kalo, Shreve) — $300–2,000 tier (general knowledge, verify) — hammer facets, "Hand Wrought" mark.
- Large weighted candelabra by a named maker (Gorham, Preisner) — ~2–4x the short console pair — removable 3–5-light arms, 12"+ tall.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Base: felt-covered or metal plate with "weighted" = low value. Unweighted shows sterling interior with seams. Monograms reduce value slightly; they narrow the buyer pool but do not remove collector interest (Appraisily).
- Weighted shells are very thin (~0.003"), so they dent and split easily, and restorers often decline to repair them (Silver Collector forum).
- Solid cast sticks: crisp detail, thick edges, no rattle; weight matches silver content.
- Rattling when polished = loose filler inside (Silver Collector forum).
- Old vs new: English date letters date the piece exactly; US makers' marks and pattern numbers date to era; mid-century weighted console pairs are 1940s–70s.
- Matched pairs outperform singles; check both pieces of a pair are actually identical (APMEX; Appraisily).

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Dents, splits in thin shells, filler leaking, bent bases, leaning stems, worn felt, missing bobeches, wax residue. Weighted sticks dent easily.
- Do not peel back felt, pry bases or drill to check construction: it destroys evidence and value (Appraisily).
- Heavy polishing softens detail; clean only lightly around a hidden mark (APMEX).
- A damaged weighted piece sells on decorative appeal, close to nothing above its small melt (Appraisily).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Silverplate passed off as sterling; check mark. Fake English hallmarks on Asian silver.
- "Sheffield" alone is a place name and usually means plate (old Sheffield plate or modern EPNS) unless lion passant and date letter are present.
- Jensen and Tiffany forms have been copied in plate; check the mark and design number.

## Market
Plenty of supply; weighted pairs sell slowly; Judaica buyers support some demand. Refiners pay little for weighted pieces and may charge fees that exceed the silver (Silver Collector forum), so the floor is decorative demand. Solid and named-maker pieces sell to collectors and through auction houses.

## What sellers usually miss
- Unweighted solid pairs mislabelled as "silver candlesticks" (maybe plate). Jensen marks.
- Kirk repoussé listed as just "weighted sterling".
- English hallmarks on the nozzle/bobeche as well as the base (a matching set of marks confirms originality).

## Shipping / handling
Pair ~1.5–2 lb, $12–16. Wrap each stick separately; thin weighted bases dent in transit.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Mark photos, weighted/unweighted, height, weight, condition, any dents. Photograph the whole piece, the profile, the underside, every mark and any seam or base cover (Appraisily).

### What to tell the buyer
"Pair of sterling silver candlesticks by [maker], marked '[exact mark line, including 'Weighted' or 'Reinforced' if present]'. [Weighted: sterling shell over a filled base, as made, so gross weight is not silver weight.] Height [x] in; gross weight [y] oz for the pair. Condition: [dents/none], bobeches [present/missing], felt [original/replaced]. Marks photographed; no polishing beyond light cleaning." For English pieces, state the town mark, date letter and resulting year; for Jensen, state the design number and the silver weight.
