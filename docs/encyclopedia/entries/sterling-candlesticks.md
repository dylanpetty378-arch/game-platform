## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Mid-century US candlesticks are mostly "weighted" or "reinforced": a thin sterling shell over a pitch/plaster fill with a felt or metal base cover. Marked "Sterling Weighted" or "Sterling Reinforced with Cement". Silver content per short stick is ~0.5–1 troy oz.
Short console form (3–4"): gadrooned or beaded oval base, urn-shaped cup, often with removable bobeche. The example is exactly this: low pair with gadroon base edges, tarnished.
Valuable versions: solid unweighted sticks (heavy for size without felt base), hallmarked English (lion passant, date letter, maker), Georg Jensen (Denmark, "925S" and Jensen mark), Tiffany ("Tiffany & Co. Makers Sterling"), Arts & Crafts hand-hammered (Kalo, Shreve).

## Makers, brands and variants
US weighted: Gorham, Towle, Duchin Creation, Web Silver, Fisher, Empire, International, Frank M. Whiting, Preisner, Alvin, Watrous. English: Georgian/Victorian cast or loaded sticks (also often loaded), Elkington. Danish: Georg Jensen.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Base: felt-covered or metal plate with "weighted" = low value. Unweighted shows sterling interior with seams. Monograms reduce value slightly.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents, splits in thin shells, filler leaking, bent bases, worn felt. Weighted sticks easily dent.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Silverplate passed off as sterling; check mark. Fake English hallmarks on Asian silver.
