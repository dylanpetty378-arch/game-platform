Buy rule (short): Pass on mass-produced quartz/novelty pocket watches; they never clear the $50 profit bar. Exceptions: sterling-cased commemoratives below melt, and pre-war Ingersoll character watches.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1980s–2010s pocket watches with base-metal (silver-tone or gold-tone) cases with stamped relief scenes (eagles, trains, wolves, motorcycles, deer), cheap curb chains with a belt clip, ornate scrolled bows, and quartz movements marked "Japan Movt" or "Quartz". Brand names on dials ("Geneva", "Infinity", "Royal") are generic. A cast relief that looks soft and has casting pits is the giveaway.

The category also catches older character and novelty watches that are worth real money: 1930s Ingersoll (US and British) Mickey Mouse and other character pocket watches (pin-lever, no jewels, wind-up, chrome-plated 18-size case about 46–47 mm), and Soviet Molnija mechanical pocket watches (cal. 3602, 50 mm, themed dials with trains, Kremlin, WWII Victory, fire brigade).

## Makers, brands and variants
Geneva (a generic import brand), Infinity, Gotham, Charles-Hubert Paris (quartz and mechanical; the 17-jewel hand-wound skeleton models retail new around $134), Franklin Mint, Bradford Exchange (licensed themes), Westclox (quartz era), plus licensed Coca-Cola/Disney items.
- **Ingersoll Mickey Mouse (1933–39)**: launched at the 1933 Chicago World's Fair. First version has three small Mickeys around the seconds subdial at 6; 1934–35 simplified to one; British Ingersoll versions 1935–36 marked "British Ingersoll"/"Made in England"; pre-1938 Mickeys have pie-cut eyes, 1938+ oval eyes; hands are Mickey's gloved arms (moulded on pre-war). After 1944 Ingersoll became part of US Time and pocket versions gave way to wristwatches. Later licensed editions: Bradley, Elgin, Helbros, Fossil, Seiko.
- **Molnija (Chelyabinsk Watch Factory, USSR)**: cal. 3602, 18 jewels, 50 mm, many commemorative dials; NOS serviced examples retail ~$170.
- **Franklin Mint sterling**: marked sterling/925 in the case, numbered, with certificates.

### Quick ID checklist
- Back: battery hatch or "Quartz"/"Japan Movt" → novelty quartz, low value.
- Dial brand "Geneva", "Infinity", "Royal", "Gotham" → generic import.
- Case marked "Sterling" / ".925" / "Franklin Mint" → check weight; sterling commemorative tier.
- Mickey on the dial with "Ingersoll": count Mickeys at the seconds dial (three = 1933), check eye shape (pie-cut = pre-1938), back plain with "Made in U.S.A." or "British Made".
- Cyrillic "МОЛНИЯ" on the dial or movement → Soviet Molnija mechanical; photograph the movement (3602).
- Skeleton dial showing gears and "17 Jewels" → Chinese-movement mechanical (Charles-Hubert class), $20–50.
- Ask if it runs (quartz with a new battery; mechanical after winding) and whether it keeps time.

### Golden-egg tells
- **1933 Ingersoll Mickey Mouse, three Mickeys on the seconds dial** — $300–600 unboxed, $800–1,500+ boxed vs $5–25 novelty (guide figures) — three small Mickeys at 6, pie-cut eyes, moulded arm hands, plain back.
- **1934–39 US Ingersoll Mickey** — $150–350 loose, $400–800 boxed — single Mickey seconds, "Ingersoll" near 6, white/cream dial.
- **British Ingersoll Mickey (1930s–50s)** — $100–250 running — "British Ingersoll" or "Made in England" on dial/back.
- **Franklin Mint sterling pocket watch** — Terapeak median $196 (n=7) vs $15 novelty median — "Sterling" hallmark inside the case back, Franklin Mint name, numbered.
- **Molnija 3602 with commemorative dial, running** — dealer retail ~$170 NOS serviced; used examples typically far less (general knowledge, verify) — Cyrillic dial, 3602 movement with Molnija lightning logo.
- **Charles-Hubert 17-jewel mechanical** — Terapeak median $50 (p75 $95) vs $8–25 quartz — "Charles-Hubert Paris" on dial, "17 Jewels" mechanical, no battery hatch.
- **Original box/papers on any of the above** — box roughly doubles an Ingersoll (guide).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Zinc alloy or brass with silver plating; mineral glass; a battery hatch on the back. Sterling commemoratives are marked 925/sterling and come with a certificate. Mechanical novelty watches have a winding crown that actually winds and a ticking balance; quartz ones step once a second. Ingersoll pre-war: chrome-plated brass case, pin-lever movement, set by a separate pin rather than pulling the crown.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dead batteries (leakage corrodes the movement), worn plating, broken hinges. Ingersoll: refinished or reprinted dials, replaced hands, non-running pin-lever movements ($80–200 for a non-running 1933).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Generic novelty watches: not applicable; these are mass-market goods. Ingersoll Mickey: reprinted dials in original cases (the common deception), dials too crisp or colours too vivid, oval eyes on a "1933" dial, flat printed hands, Swiss or quartz movements, "Made in China/Taiwan" backs, reproduction boxes. Fossil and other licensed 1990s Mickeys are legitimate reissues worth $80–200 new in box.

## Market
Saturated. Thousands listed, low prices. Days to sell: 30+. Character and Soviet watches have their own collector bases; Disneyana buyers pay for boxed Ingersolls.

## What sellers usually miss
Little upside here. Occasionally a real antique Swiss/American watch gets lumped in with novelty ones; see swiss-silver-pocket-watches. Also sterling cases unmarked in the title, and 1930s Ingersoll character watches in drawer lots.

## Shipping / handling
Small padded box, $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Say whether it runs with a new battery, describe the movement (quartz), and give the dimensions.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Brand] pocket watch, [quartz with Japan movement / 17-jewel mechanical / Ingersoll pin-lever]. Case [base metal, silver-tone / sterling .925, hallmarked], [diameter] mm. [Runs and keeps time with a new battery / after winding]. Dial [original, no refinish]. Includes [chain/box/certificate]." Photograph the dial, back, inside of case back and movement.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$6 − ~$6 − price ≥ max($50, price). Never qualifies for generic quartz. Pass. Buy only a sterling Franklin Mint ≤ ~$60, or a pre-war Ingersoll character watch ≤ ~$50 with original dial.
