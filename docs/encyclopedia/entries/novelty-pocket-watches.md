Buy rule (short): Pass. Mass-produced quartz/novelty pocket watches never clear the $50 profit bar; the only exceptions are sterling-cased commemoratives below melt.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1980s–2010s pocket watches with base-metal (silver-tone or gold-tone) cases with stamped relief scenes (eagles, trains, wolves, motorcycles, deer), cheap curb chains with a belt clip, ornate scrolled bows, and quartz movements marked "Japan Movt" or "Quartz". Brand names on dials ("Geneva", "Infinity", "Royal") are generic. A cast relief that looks soft and has casting pits is the giveaway.

## Makers, brands and variants
Geneva (a generic import brand), Infinity, Gotham, Charles-Hubert Paris (quartz and mechanical), Franklin Mint, Bradford Exchange (licensed themes), Westclox (quartz era), plus licensed Coca-Cola/Disney items.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Zinc alloy or brass with silver plating; mineral glass; a battery hatch on the back. Sterling commemoratives are marked 925/sterling and come with a certificate.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dead batteries (leakage corrodes the movement), worn plating, broken hinges.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not applicable; these are mass-market goods.
