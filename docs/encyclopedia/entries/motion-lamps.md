## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lamps where heat from the bulb spins an inner printed cylinder (sleeve, "spinner") inside an outer printed shade, creating moving scenes (fire, waterfalls, ships, fish). Heat rises through vanes on the spinner's top, which sits on a pin bearing.
- **1920s–30s Scene in Action (Chicago)**: cast-metal (zinc) bodies, slag-glass panels, scene shown through a window in a figural or architectural housing; stamped copyright date on the back (e.g. "Copyright 1931 by Scene in Action Corp.").
- **1950s Econolite (Los Angeles)**: cylinder or oval lithographed shade on a metal base (brass-finish, often on three ball feet), metal cap on top. The shade is printed with "ECONOLITE CORP. LITHO U.S.A. © [year] [number]". Typical shade 6 in diameter × 9.5 in tall, ~11–12 in overall.
- **1950s L.A. Goodman (Chicago)**: plastic and metal, often fireplaces, waterfalls and campfires.
- **1970s–90s**: plastic bodies, oil-wheel and fibre-optic types, tropical-fish and fireplace scenes (Visual Effects, Rabbit Tanaka).
- **Modern**: plastic tubes with printed fish, often motor-driven, barcode stickers.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Scene in Action Corp (Chicago, 1920s–30s)** — earliest, metal construction; e.g. "Serenader" (gondola scene, green slag glass, 1931).
- **Econolite Corp (Los Angeles, late 1940s–1960s)** — the most collected. Titles seen: Niagara Falls (1953, 1955), Forest Fire, ship and train scenes (John Bull, Pioneer Express), Antique Autos/Stutz Bearcat (1957), Snow Scene/church-mill-sleigh (No. 766-7, 1957), oval fireplace (1958, oval shade model 761), pot-belly stove, Merry-go-round, The Fountain, Hopalong Cassidy, 1962 Seattle World's Fair, 1963 Venice. **Roto-Vue** and **Roto-Vue Jr.** are Econolite lines (the old entry listed "Rotovue Jr." as a separate maker — corrected).
- **L.A. Goodman Mfg (Chicago, 1950s)** — fireplaces, waterfalls, campfire "prospector" scenes (1956).
- **Gritz-Ritter** (1950s, listed in the earlier entry; verify).
- **Visual Effects Inc. (1970s)** — fireplace and scene lamps; a 1970 example asked $350.
- **Rabbit Tanaka (Kyoji Tanaka / Rabbit brand)** — 1980s–90s, not 1970s as the old entry said: the Rabbit lamp line is 1980s and the Lumaseries kinetic wall pieces are 1996. Tropical-fish, oil and kinetic lamps.
- **Modern** (1990s+): Kohl's/Spencer's/Brookstone rotating aquarium lamps, often made in China/Hong Kong.

### Quick ID checklist
- Read the bottom edge of the shade: "ECONOLITE CORP. LITHO U.S.A. © 19xx" plus a number = genuine 1950s Econolite.
- Cast-metal body with slag glass and a stamped 1920s–30s copyright = Scene in Action.
- Metal base and cap (brass or bronze finish), paper/litho shade = 1950s; moulded plastic base with barcode or store sticker = 1990s+.
- Shape: round versus oval shade (oval ones such as the 761 fireplace are scarcer).
- Is the shade round and flush with the base? A dented or flared lower edge stops the spinner (Napoleon listing).
- Is the spinner present, and is it original paper/acetate or a new replacement?
- Ask for a video of it lit and turning.
- Check the cord: cloth or brittle rubber = needs rewiring; note it.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal caps + lithographed scenes = 1950s. Moulded plastic body, barcode/Kohl's sticker = 1990s+. Litho colour on 1950s shades is layered and slightly offset at registration; 1990 reproductions are brighter and printed on thinner, glossier plastic with a dated UL label. Brass-finish bases on 1950s Econolites tarnish and oxidise; plastic "brass" does not.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Warped/cracked sleeves (heat), faded print, bearing pin wear, rewiring needed. Also: dented or distorted lower shade edge, which makes the shade rub the spinner and stop it (Napoleon listing); cracks near the top of the shade (Roto-Vue 766-7 listing); missing spinner. **New replacement spinners are sold for $80–150** (used.forsale listings), so a lamp with a replaced spinner is "working" but not all-original — ask.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Econolite designs reissued in the 1990s–2000s (lighter plastic, UL labels with dates); a "1990 reproduction" was listed at $65. Replacement spinners (new) are common; married spinners from other titles also occur (check the scene matches the shade).

## Market
Nostalgia market, moderate volume. Buyers: mid-century and kitsch collectors, Disney/Western character collectors for licensed titles. Working, round-shaded lamps with video sell fastest.

## What sellers usually miss
Econolite sleeves in garage-sale lots; Lens over-attributes modern lamps to Rabbit Tanaka. Sellers rarely quote the litho line and © date on the shade, which dates the lamp exactly.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile plastic/glass, 12–16", $15–20. Ship the spinner wrapped separately; pack the shade so it cannot go oval.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Specify maker if labelled, decade, whether it spins and lights.

### What to tell the buyer
"[Maker] motion lamp, '[title]', shade printed '[litho line, © year, number]'. [Height] in tall, [diameter] in shade. Tested: lights [yes/no], spinner turns [continuously/intermittently]; video available. Spinner is [original/replacement]. Shade [round, no cracks / note dents]. Cord [original/rewired]. Bulb not included." Photograph the litho line, the base, the spinner and the lit lamp.

## Buy rule
Untested electronics count as non-working. Only vintage Econolite-class lamps clear the rule.
