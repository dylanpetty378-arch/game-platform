## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lamps where heat from the bulb spins an inner printed cylinder (sleeve) inside an outer printed shade, creating moving scenes (fire, waterfalls, ships, fish). 1950s versions have paper/acetate sleeves with metal tops and bases; 1970s ones use plastic and oil wheels; modern ones are plastic tubes with printed fish and electric spinning.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Scene in Action Corp (Chicago, 1920s–30s)** — earliest, metal construction.
- **Econolite (LA, 1950s)** — Niagara Falls, forest fire, ship, train; lithographed plastic sleeves, metal caps; most collected.
- **Gritz-Ritter, LA Goodman, Rotovue Jr.**
- **Visual Effects / Rabbit Tanaka (1970s–80s)** — tropical fish, fireplace, oil-wheel lamps.
- **Modern** (1990s+): Kohl's/Spencer's/Brookstone rotating aquarium lamps, often made in China/Hong Kong.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Metal caps + lithographed scenes = 1950s. Moulded plastic body, barcode/Kohl's sticker = 1990s+.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Warped/cracked sleeves (heat), faded print, bearing pin wear, rewiring needed.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Econolite designs reissued in the 1990s–2000s (lighter plastic, UL labels with dates).
