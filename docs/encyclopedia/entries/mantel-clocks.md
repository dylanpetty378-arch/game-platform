## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
American 8-day spring-driven (and earlier weight-driven) clocks, 1850s–1930s, in wooden or "adamantine" (celluloid faux-marble) cases with brass movements. Label inside the back door names the maker; the movement is often stamped (e.g. "Seth Thomas 89").
- Ogee = tall rectangular weight-driven box with a mirror/picture tablet (Gilbert, Seth Thomas, c.1850–90).
- Adamantine columned cases = Seth Thomas 1890–1917 (label inside back): black with gold-painted columns, the most common type. Also made in this style by Ingraham, Gilbert, Sessions, New Haven, Ansonia.
- Black marble/slate = French or Ansonia "enamelled iron".
- Tambour ("Napoleon hat") 1920s.
- Revere/Telechron Westminster electric = 1930s–50s.
- Oak "kitchen"/parlor clocks (Ingraham, Sessions, Gilbert, New Haven) c.1890–1915, pressed-oak gingerbread cases.
- Tells for a good one: adamantine columns intact, original dial and hands, maker's label, stamped movement. "Needs cleaning/doesn't run" = repair = pass.
- E. Howard banjo/regulator clocks and their weights are a different, higher market.
Sellers write "antique mantle clock", "grandfather style clock", "chime clock".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Seth Thomas** (Thomaston CT): adamantine mantels ($90–180 running), No. 89 movement, Sonora chime (4-bell $250–400; 5-bell top tier), Corsaire ship's-bell with barometer ($150–250), No. 2 regulator (top tier).
- **Gilbert**: ogees ($90–150 running w/ key), black mantels (common).
- **Waterbury**: 8-day mantels (decent); Westminster No. 501 listed in top tier (verify).
- **Ansonia**: crystal regulators (collectible, $250–800), enamelled-iron "black marble" cases, early figurals (top).
- **Ingraham**: ornate oak kitchen clocks with alarm and original labels (collectible).
- **Sessions, New Haven**: mostly common black mantels and oak parlor clocks; New Haven Victorian clocks Lens-median ~$95–110 asking.
- **Chelsea** (Boston): ship's bell, 8.5" brass ship's bell is top tier.
- **E. Howard** (Boston): banjos and regulators $800–5,000; even an original Howard #1 weight was asked $195.
- **Herschede**: tubular chime clocks (see Contradictions note below — most 9-tube Herschedes are floor clocks).
- **Revere/Telechron**: 1930s–50s electric Westminsters, low.
- Non-American seen: Linden (figural porcelain, modern), Seikosha (Japanese).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Adamantine: celluloid veneer over wood imitating marble/onyx; look for peeling or bubbled veneer on the top and columns.
- Ogee cases: mahogany/rosewood veneer over pine; reverse-painted or mirror tablet in the lower door — original tablet adds value.
- Movements: brass plates with maker stamp; time-and-strike (two winding holes) or chime (three holes). Three winding arbors = chime model (Sonora/Westminster), worth checking.
- Quartz conversions (battery movement in an antique case) collapse value to junk tier.
- Refinished cases and replaced dials/hands lower collector value (general knowledge, verify).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Not running is the big one: a clean-and-overhaul costs well over the clock's value for common models (general knowledge, verify), so "needs cleaning/doesn't run" = pass. Also: veneer loss/adamantine peeling, cracked or replaced glass tablets, missing pendulum or key, broken suspension spring, replaced dials, missing bells on Sonora chimes. Ask for a video of it running and striking, and a photo of the label and movement.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Low fake risk for ordinary American mantel clocks. Watch for: quartz conversions sold as antique, married cases/movements, reproduction labels, modern "antique-style" clocks (Linden, Howard Miller) titled "vintage" (general knowledge, verify). E. Howard/Chelsea have higher-end repro/married-part risk (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Huge supply, softening demand: mantel clocks are a mature, well-known category and most are correctly priced (general knowledge, verify). Buyers are NAWCC collectors and repairers (for chime models, regulators, Chelsea/Howard) and decorators (for nice-looking running cases). Heavy and fragile, so local sales often beat eBay. Rarely arbitrage except for named chime/regulator/ship's-bell models.

## What sellers usually miss
"Sonora chime" or "Chelsea" names; chime variants and crystal regulators; original glass tablet; whether it runs/strikes; three winding holes = chime. Plain adamantines are correctly priced ~$100. Most mantel clocks are heavy, fragile and well-known — rarely arbitrage.

## Shipping / handling
10–25 lb, fragile, $30–50 each way double-boxed; movement should be removed or secured and pendulum removed (one write-up: 15–25 lb, $35–50 boxed). Breakage risk high; prefer local.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker (label photo), model, movement stamp, 8-day vs 30-hour, time-only/strike/chime, running and striking status (video link), case material and condition, original vs replaced dial/hands/tablet, included key and pendulum, dimensions (H × W × D). Photograph label, movement front and back, dial, tablet, case top. Title keywords: "Antique Seth Thomas Sonora Chime Mantel Clock Running", "Ansonia Crystal Regulator", "Chelsea Ship's Bell Clock".

## Buy rule
Buy when: Chelsea/Sonora chime/Ansonia crystal regulator running at ≤ $100, ideally local. Pass when: ordinary ogee/adamantine/tambour over $40 — shipping kills it — or anything "not running". Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (mantel clocks do; floor/hall clocks do not).
