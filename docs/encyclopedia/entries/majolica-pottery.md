## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Earthenware moulded in relief and coated with bright coloured lead glazes.
- **Victorian majolica (1850–1900)**: Minton (introduced 1851), George Jones, Wedgwood, Holdcroft, Brownhills, Etruscan (Griffen, Smith & Hill, Phoenixville PA, 1879–90), Choisy-le-Roi, Sarreguemines. Forms: oyster plates, sardine boxes, cheese bells, begonia-leaf trays, pitchers, jardinieres, game pie dishes. Back usually glazed in mottled brown/green/blue ("tortoiseshell") or pink/turquoise linings. Marks impressed.
- **Italian 20th-c. (1950s–70s)**: Bassano and Nove decorative fruit topiaries/centerpieces (piled fruit, often on a gilt or green pedestal), Capodimonte-style; marked "Italy" with numbers; heavy, hollow-cast.
- **Portuguese**: Bordallo Pinheiro (cabbage ware, 1884–present), Secla; marked.
- **Decorator (1970s–today)**: Fitz and Floyd, Mottahedeh (reproduction Victorian designs), Department 56, Pier 1, and many Chinese "majolica-style" cachepots with rabbits, birds and branch handles.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Minton: impressed MINTON + date cypher, shape number; $300–10,000+.
- George Jones: GJ monogram pad mark; $300–5,000.
- Wedgwood: impressed WEDGWOOD + 3-letter date code; $150–2,000.
- Holdcroft (Joseph Holdcroft), Brownhills, Adams & Bromley, Samuel Lear.
- Etruscan (GSH monogram): shell and seaweed, cauliflower, begonia leaf; $80–400.
- Sarreguemines, Choisy-le-Roi, Onnaing (French): $60–400.
- Bordallo Pinheiro: $30–200 (new production cheaper).
- Italian: "Made in Italy" topiaries $40–150; large pairs higher.
- Fitz and Floyd (1960s–), Mottahedeh, "Majolica by Haldon": $20–100.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Victorian: soft earthenware, glossy translucent glazes pooling in recesses, mottled underside glaze, impressed marks, wear on the foot rim showing buff body.
- Modern Asian: hard white or grey body, opaque paint-like glaze, unglazed white foot with sticker, sharp mould detail.
- Italian topiaries: hollow, heavy with thick glaze; gilt pedestal often painted metallic; check every fruit/leaf.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips and glaze flakes at rims and leaf tips (very common), hairlines, crazing, restorations (Victorian pieces often restored with painted overglaze: check under UV/blacklight). Restoration halves Victorian values. Italian topiaries lose leaves and grape tips.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large volume of Chinese reproductions of Victorian majolica (George Jones, Minton, Etruscan) since the 1990s with fake impressed marks; tells: heavy, white/grey body, unworn feet, too-bright glaze. Mottahedeh reproductions are marked.

## Market
Victorian majolica prices fell from 2000s peaks but the collector base (Majolica International Society, Strawser auctions) remains. Italian topiaries ride Grandmillennial decor trends and sell steadily.

## What sellers usually miss
- Impressed Minton/Wedgwood/George Jones marks on "old green leaf plates".
- Etruscan GSH monograms.
- They overprice modern majolica-style decor and Italian topiaries using Lens asks ($1,850 median here is a high-end retail match).

## Shipping / handling
Fragile with protrusions; topiaries 3–8 lb, need double boxing and every fruit padded; $20–30 each way.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph marks and undersides, describe glazes, list any restoration (check with blacklight), give dimensions.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$22 to buyer − ~$22 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Modern majolica-style decor: pass.
- Italian topiary: buy only at ≤ $10 with resale ≥ $100 (rare).
- Victorian marked piece (Minton/George Jones/Wedgwood/Etruscan) with no restoration: buy at ≤ 1/4 resale (usually ≤ $60) after mark photo.
