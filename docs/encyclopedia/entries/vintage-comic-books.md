## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Saddle-stapled periodical comics, ~7 × 10¼" (Golden Age slightly taller/wider, ~7¾ × 10½"), newsprint interiors. Date by **cover price** in the top corner: 10¢ (to 1961), 12¢ (1961–69), 15¢ (1969–71), 20¢ (1972–74), 25¢ (1974–76), 30¢ (1976–77), 35¢ (1977–79), 40¢ (1979–80), 50¢ (1980–81), 60¢ (1981–85). Golden Age books are often 52–68 pages, thicker, with "10¢" and painted-look covers; 80-Page Giants (DC 1964–71) are thicker squarebound.
Spot from photos: publisher logos (DC bullet 1977–2005; "DC Comics Inc." / "National" earlier; Marvel corner box with character head 1963+; "Marvel Comics Group"), cover price, issue number in the corner box, and the **barcode box** (newsstand vs direct-edition from 1979 on).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **DC (National Periodical)** — Superman, Action, Batman, Detective, Adventure, World's Finest, Justice League, Flash, Green Lantern, Showcase; 80-Page Giants (Batman G-17 etc.).
- **Marvel (Atlas pre-1961)** — Amazing Spider-Man, Fantastic Four, X-Men, Avengers, Tales of Suspense, Journey into Mystery, Hulk, Daredevil; Bronze-age horror (Tomb of Dracula, Werewolf by Night).
- **Dell (to 1962) / Gold Key (1962–84)** — licensed TV/cartoon (Tarzan, Star Trek, Disney, Hanna-Barbera, Top Cat).
- **Archie, Harvey (Richie Rich, Casper), Charlton, Classics Illustrated (Gilberton; printing history inside the back cover distinguishes originals from reprints)**.
- **Underground comix** — separate entry (underground-comix).
- **Variants**: Marvel 30¢ (1976) and **35¢ (June–Oct 1977) price-test variants** (Recalled Comics); **Whitman** prepack copies (diamond/blank-UPC/"Whitman" logo, 1977–84); **Canadian price variants** (1982–86 newsstand, higher cover price); **Mark Jewelers** centrefold insert (1972–1990s, US military-base distribution) (GoCollect); **newsstand vs direct** (1979+); UK pence variants (cover price in pence, lower value in the US).

### Golden-egg tells
- **Marvel 35¢ price variants (cover-dated Jun–Oct 1977)** — ~5–20× the 30¢ regular copy (Star Wars #1 35¢ far more) — cover price "35¢" in a 30¢-era box, e.g. Star Wars #1–4, Amazing Spider-Man #169–173, Iron Fist #14, X-Men #105–107 (Recalled Comics lists ~60 titles).
- **Marvel 30¢ price variants (1976)** — ~3–10× regular (verify) — "30¢" on a 25¢-era cover, Apr–Aug 1976 dates.
- **Silver Age first appearances and keys** — $100–10,000+ vs $15 for a neighbour issue — e.g. Amazing Spider-Man #1–50, Fantastic Four #1–52, X-Men #1–10, Showcase #4/#22, Tales of Suspense #39, Journey into Mystery #83, Detective #359 (Batgirl), Brave & the Bold #28 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Bronze Age keys** — $40–500+ raw mid-grade — Incredible Hulk #180–181 (Wolverine), Amazing Spider-Man #129 (Punisher), Giant-Size X-Men #1, X-Men #94, House of Secrets #92 (Swamp Thing), Tomb of Dracula #10 (Blade), Iron Fist #14, Werewolf by Night #32 (Moon Knight), Jungle Action #5–6 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Golden Age (10¢, pre-1955) any superhero/horror/crime** — $50–1,000+ even in low grade — thick books, painted/"good girl"/horror covers, "10¢" and pre-Comics Code (no CCA stamp before 1955).
- **Mark Jewelers insert copies** — premium of ~10–50% raw, more on graded keys (GoCollect) — a jewellery ad insert in the centrefold, "MJ" noted on CGC labels.
- **Whitman / newsstand-only copies of 1980s keys** — premium on high grades — Whitman logo or blank barcode; newsstand barcode on 1990s keys.
- **Kill: 1985+ commons, foil/multiple-cover 1990s, Classics Illustrated later printings, UK pence copies sold as US** — $1–5.

### Quick ID checklist
- Cover price visible on each book (10¢/12¢ = good sign).
- Publisher and title; issue number from the corner box.
- Look for #1s, first appearances (check after purchase list), 35¢/30¢ prices in the 1976–77 window.
- Condition from photos: spine stress/roll, tears, missing pieces, water damage, brittle tan pages, coupons cut, "subscription crease" (vertical fold).
- Are all books pictured? Lots often show the best on top.
- Seller type: comic shops with per-book or placeholder ($1/$15) prices are at market — kill (yiayiascomics.com).
- Graded slabs (CGC/CBCS) are priced at market by the slab; only the raw, unknown books carry the edge.
- Reprints: "Marvel Tales", "Marvel Milestone Edition", "Facsimile Edition", Golden Record/Famous First Edition tabloids.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Newsprint interiors (yellow/tan with age; brittle = big deduction), glossy or matte covers, two staples. Grading (CGC 0.5–10) drives value: a 9.0 can be 5–10× a 4.0. Restoration (colour touch, trimming, glued spines) collapses value for keys and must be disclosed.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Spine splits, detached covers, missing centrefolds, water damage, mould, coupons cut, writing on covers (date stamps from newsstands are normal and minor), brittle pages, trimmed edges, tape. Pressing and cleaning are allowed if not restoration; colour touch and added paper are restoration.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Facsimile editions (2019+ Marvel/DC facsimiles carry "Facsimile Edition" and modern barcodes); Marvel Milestone reprints; 1966 Golden Record ASM #1 reprint; Famous First Edition tabloids; Toys "R" Us/Kellogg's giveaway reprints; fake 35¢ variants made by pasting prices (rare; CGC checks). Counterfeit Golden Age keys exist but are rare at Craigslist price levels.

## Market
Huge and liquid: tens of thousands of eBay comic sales per month; raw Silver Age sells fast; keys sell within days; modern commons are nearly unsaleable singly (lots only). Prices peaked in 2021–22 and have cooled for modern speculation keys (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
That 1960s Gold Key/Archie are worth little while a single 12¢ Marvel or DC Giant is worth $20–40; 35¢ variants in 1977 Marvel runs; Mark Jewelers inserts; first appearances in otherwise ordinary issues. Inheritors often post whole boxes at $25–50.

## Shipping / handling
Bag and board each book; lots ship in a comic box or flat-rate box. Single raw books in rigid mailers $5–6; lots of 15–30 $10–20. Ship to Dylan $10–20.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Sell keys singly with front/back/spine photos and an honest raw grade estimate; sell commons in themed runs. Grade keys with CGC only when the grade gap pays for the fee (~$30–90 + shipping).

### What to tell the buyer
"[Title] #[issue], [publisher], cover date [month/year], [cover price] cover. Raw, ungraded — my estimate [grade range], see photos of front, back and spine. Pages [off-white/tan], [no] tears, [no] writing, coupon [intact]. No restoration known."

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200. Profit = sold × 0.87 − ~$12 to buyer − ~$12 to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). A 16-book Silver Age lot at ~$150 resale clears at ≤ ~$50. Buy keys raw only with clear photos and no signs of restoration; a key worth > $500 raw needs a CGC grade to sell well — that is grading, not authentication, but budget the fee and the 4–8-week turnaround. Kill comic-shop listings with placeholder prices. Comics fit a passenger seat.
