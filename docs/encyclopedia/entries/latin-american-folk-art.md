## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Tonalá burnished (barro bruñido/ "bruñido")**: low-fired matte clay, hand-burnished to a soft satin, painted in grey, blue-grey, rust, cream and black with birds, flowers, rabbits, deer; jugs (pitchers), vases, plates, animals. 1930s–60s pieces are finer, thinner-lined and softer-coloured; post-1970 tourist pieces are thicker and coarser.
- **Petatillo (Tonalá)**: very fine cross-hatched background ("little mat") behind animals; the finest Tonalá work.
- **Ken Edwards / El Palomar (Tonalá, 1960s–2000s)**: high-fired stoneware, blue-grey birds and rabbits on cream; marked "KE" or "Ken Edwards Tonalá Mexico". Collectible.
- **Jorge Wilmot (Tonalá)**: high-fired stoneware with refined birds/flowers, signed; $150–800.
- **Mata Ortiz / Casas Grandes (Chihuahua, 1970s–)**: thin-walled, perfectly round, burnished pots with fine geometric black-and-red paint, signed on base by potter name and village.
- **Oaxaca**: San Bartolo Coyotepec barro negro (shiny black, Doña Rosa); Arrazola/San Martín Tilcajete alebrijes (painted copal-wood fantasy animals; Manuel Jiménez, Jacobo & María Ángeles signed).
- **Michoacán bateas and Olinalá lacquer**: carved wooden trays and chests with black or red lacquer ground painted (Uruapan, Pátzcuaro) or lacquer-scraped (rayado, Olinalá) with large roses and flowers.
- **Clay banks and animals (Mexico and Guatemala, 1960s–90s)**: painted clay or papier-mâché owls, pigs, roosters; often sold in pairs; tourist pieces.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Tonalá families: Jimón (Jimon) Galván, Nicasio Pajarito, Candelario Medrano (sculptural, rare), Salvador Vasquez (petatillo masters).
- Ken Edwards (El Palomar), Jorge Wilmot, Erasto Lucano.
- Mata Ortiz: Juan Quezada (founder, $1,000+), Olivia Domínguez, Lydia Quezada, Héctor Gallegos, many lesser signers ($80–400).
- Metepec trees of life: Soteno family (Tiburcio, Oscar), Castañeda.
- Ocumicho devils (Michoacán): painted clay devil scenes, $50–250.
- Peruvian retablos (Ayacucho, Jesús Urbano Rojas), Ecuadorian bread-dough figures, Guatemalan painted clay.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Older burnished Tonalá: thin clay, fine brushwork, soft fugitive colours, wear on the base and lip; Tonalá is porous and absorbs oils, darkening over time.
- Signatures: Mata Ortiz signatures incised/painted on the base; Ken Edwards stamp; alebrije artisans sign underneath with town.
- Bateas: hand-carved one-piece wood (shows tool marks underneath), natural lacquer (aje/chia oil) ages with fine crazing; later ones use enamel paint on plywood or turned wood.
- Mata Ortiz quality: wall thinness, perfect symmetry, fine-line density and burnish depth.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Tonalá burnished ware chips and flakes easily at lip and base; paint rubs; water damage (it is not watertight). Cracks and splits along grain in bateas (often a full-width split, as in the example). Alebrije ears/tails broken and glued. Mata Ortiz: hairlines and rim chips cut value 60–80%.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
"Mata Ortiz style" pots from Juárez and import shops with poor paint and painted (not burnished) surfaces; fake famous-potter signatures. Imported "alebrije" resin copies. New Tonalá made in large volumes for tourist trade (not fake, just low value). Ken Edwards style made by successors after his death.

## Market
Steady buyer base among Southwest/Mexican-modern decorators; Etsy is strong for this category. Most supply is tourist ware; only signed and finest pieces move at good prices.

## What sellers usually miss
- Mata Ortiz signatures under pots bought on a trip and listed as "Indian pottery".
- Ken Edwards "KE" stoneware in "Mexican pottery" lots.
- Older petatillo work priced like tourist pieces.
- Sellers overprice large painted clay pieces and bateas based on Etsy asks.

## Shipping / handling
Tonalá and Mata Ortiz are fragile and thin; double box, 3" padding. Bateas ship flat (box 20" square) ~$20–30. Pottery $15–25 each way.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Name the town and technique (burnished Tonalá, Olinalá lacquer, Mata Ortiz), show the base and signature, disclose cracks/repairs honestly, give dimensions. Etsy and eBay both work.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$20 to buyer − ~$20 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Unsigned tourist pottery, clay banks, bateas: pass above ~$10.
- Signed Mata Ortiz or Ken Edwards: buy at ≤ $40 if resale ≥ $150 after a base photo.
- Wilmot/Quezada-level signatures: buy at ≤ $100 only with clear signature photos.
- Size: most fit a passenger seat.
