Buy rule (short): Buy only toys with crisp castings, nickel or original rubber/ cast spoked wheels with peened (not screwed) axles and original worn paint, at ≤ $40; assume shiny red rough castings are repros.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast iron vehicles and figures, 1900–1940. Example 7946186737: ~5–6" red four-door sedan, rough grainy casting with filled seams, open windows, black rubber tires on cast white-metal spoked hubs, thick glossy red paint with orange-brown "wear" — characteristic of reproductions (Taiwan/China 1970s–2000s) of A.C. Williams/Arcade-style sedans. Genuine A.C. Williams small sedans have one-piece casting with cast wheels painted or white-rubber tires on nickel hubs, and finer detail.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Arcade (Freeport IL, 1885–1946)**: "ARCADE" cast or decal; Fords, Chevys, buses, Yellow Cab; separate nickel driver figures.
- **Hubley (Lancaster PA)**: motorcycles, fire engines, often marked.
- **Kenton (Kenton OH)**: horse-drawn rigs, fire pumpers.
- **A.C. Williams (Ravenna OH)**: small unmarked cars, often painted bright colours, one-piece.
- **Dent, Grey Iron, Kilgore, Champion, Freidag**.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real: smooth fine-grained surface, tight-fitting halves, peened/rivet-headed axles, wear on high points through to grey iron (not to orange rust), dirt in recesses. Repro: sandy pitted surface, gaps at seams, Phillips/hex screws, uniform fake wear, bright paint, heavier rust coloring, rubber tires on cheap hubs.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Repainted (largest value killer), broken/welded parts, replaced wheels/axles/drivers.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Saturated: probably the majority of "antique cast iron cars" on Craigslist are reproductions. Look for maker names cast inside; repros copy them too, so rely on casting quality.
