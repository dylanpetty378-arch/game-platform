Buy rule (short): Buy only toys with crisp castings, nickel or original rubber/ cast spoked wheels with peened (not screwed) axles and original worn paint, at ≤ $40; assume shiny red rough castings are repros.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast iron vehicles and figures, 1900–1940. Example 7946186737: ~5–6" red four-door sedan, rough grainy casting with filled seams, open windows, black rubber tires on cast white-metal spoked hubs, thick glossy red paint with orange-brown "wear" — characteristic of reproductions (Taiwan/China 1970s–2000s) of A.C. Williams/Arcade-style sedans. Genuine A.C. Williams small sedans have one-piece casting with cast wheels painted or white-rubber tires on nickel hubs, and finer detail.
- Construction: most vehicles are two cast halves joined by a rivet or peened pin (or one-piece on small A.C. Williams cars); wheels on steel axles with the ends peened (mushroomed) over; separate nickel-plated drivers, grilles or wheels on better pieces.
- Other families in the same market: mechanical and still banks (A.C. Williams, J. & E. Stevens, Grey Iron), cap guns (Kilgore, Hubley), horse-drawn rigs and fire pumpers (Kenton, Hubley, Dent), airships and planes (Dent ZEP, Hubley "Air Ford"), farm toys (Arcade).
- Seller words: "antique cast iron", "Arcade", "Hubley", "Kenton", "Model T", "fire pumper", "Yellow Cab", "Greyhound bus".

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Arcade (Freeport IL, 1885–1946)**: "ARCADE" cast or decal; Fords, Chevys, buses, Yellow Cab; separate nickel driver figures.
  - Known lines: realistic commercial vehicles, farm equipment, Yellow Cab taxis with driver (1920s), and the 1933–34 Century of Progress (Chicago World's Fair) Greyhound buses/trams in several sizes (Chairish, NYHS). Often an "ARCADE" decal or cast name inside or under the body.
- **Hubley (Lancaster PA)**: motorcycles, fire engines, often marked.
  - Detailed cars, trucks, motorcycles and emergency vehicles; 1920s–30s pieces with moving parts are highly prized; Pumper Wagon Fire Engine No. 544 dated 1906–1919 (Antique Trader). Later (post-war) Hubley switched to die-cast zinc, which is a different, lower market.
- **Kenton (Kenton OH)**: horse-drawn rigs, fire pumpers.
- **A.C. Williams (Ravenna OH)**: small unmarked cars, often painted bright colours, one-piece.
  - Founded in Chagrin Falls, Ohio (Antique Trader lists Chagrin Falls), later in Ravenna; also known for still banks and animal figures (e.g. circus elephant bank).
- **Dent, Grey Iron, Kilgore, Champion, Freidag**.
  - Dent Hardware (Fullerton PA): airships (ZEP Zeppelin), fire and horse-drawn toys. Gong Bell (East Hampton CT): bell and ringing toys; near-mint rare pieces reach five figures (Antique Trader).
- What multiplies value: maker identity, size (large Arcade buses and trucks), original paint and decals, original drivers/figures, nickel trim and wheels intact, horses and riders unbroken, original box.

### Quick ID checklist
- Underside/inside photo: cast maker name or decal (ARCADE, HUBLEY, KENTON, DENT)? Remember repros copy names.
- Axles: ends peened over (original) vs cotter pins, screws, nuts or Phillips heads (later/repro).
- Join between halves: tight fit with an old rivet or pin vs gaps, shiny screws.
- Casting: fine detail on grille, lettering, spokes; edges ground off cleanly vs blurred detail, pitted "orange-peel" surface and slightly undersized proportions typical of copies cast from an original.
- Paint: thin, worn through on high points to grey/dark iron with dirt in recesses vs thick glossy paint and uniform orange "rust" spotting.
- Wheels: cast iron, nickel-plated, or white/black rubber on nickel hubs matching the maker's era; cheap white-metal spoke hubs = repro.
- Size measured nose to tail; weight (originals are typically heavier).
- Drivers, figures, horses: original separate castings present and unbroken?

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Real: smooth fine-grained surface, tight-fitting halves, peened/rivet-headed axles, wear on high points through to grey iron (not to orange rust), dirt in recesses. Repro: sandy pitted surface, gaps at seams, Phillips/hex screws, uniform fake wear, bright paint, heavier rust coloring, rubber tires on cheap hubs.
- Source conflict to note: a general Antique Trader article says originals were sand-cast with rougher surfaces and that reproductions are smoother with sharper detail. Both kinds exist: the common Craigslist repros (cast from an original as a pattern) are grainy and soft, while some better modern repros are crisp. Judge by the whole set of tells (hardware, wear, paint, wheels, marks), not surface alone.
- Genuine rust is darker and ingrained; artificial rust is lighter orange and sits on top; flawless glossy paint and shiny new screws mean replica (Antique Trader; 1stDibs). A magnet test only confirms iron, not age.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Repainted (largest value killer), broken/welded parts, replaced wheels/axles/drivers.
- Cast iron is brittle: broken horse legs, drivers' heads, ladders, tow hooks and bumpers; brazed or epoxied repairs show as lumps or colour changes. Replacement cast parts (drivers, figures, wheels) are sold openly and get fitted to originals (Terapeak "Kenton" results include replacement figures).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Saturated: probably the majority of "antique cast iron cars" on Craigslist are reproductions. Look for maker names cast inside; repros copy them too, so rely on casting quality.
- Banks: Book of Knowledge and other reproduction mechanical banks (e.g. Uncle Sam) carry fresh glossy paint; many are marked on the base but marks get ground off.

## Market
Specialist; genuine sells well, repros slow.
- Buyers are toy collectors (auction houses, eBay, toy shows); condition and original paint drive price far more than age alone.

## What sellers usually miss
Small genuine A.C. Williams/Arcade pieces in grandpa's box priced as repros ($10–20).
- Original drivers and nickel wheels, and the Arcade decal/name hidden under the body.

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb; small box $10–12.
- Wrap protruding parts (ladders, drivers, horse legs) — brittle iron snaps if dropped.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Underside photo, measurement, casting, axle type; call repros "reproduction / decorative".

### What to tell the buyer
Give maker (and how identified: cast name, decal, or attribution by known casting), model/type, length in inches, and the construction facts a collector checks: peened axles, rivet/pin joins, wheel type, original or replaced drivers. Describe paint honestly as original with wear (and percentage remaining), touched up, or repainted, and list any breaks, repairs or replaced parts. Call reproductions "reproduction / decorative" in the title.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $11 − $11 − price ≥ max($50, price). Need crisp casting + peened axles + honest wear. Rough red repros: pass.
