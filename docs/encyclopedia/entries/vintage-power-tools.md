## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Corded electric tools from the 1930s–1990s. Value lives in two groups: (1) **tools users still want** – heavy, rebuildable, US-made professional tools (Porter-Cable 690/7518 routers, 503/505 sanders, 360/362 belt sanders; Milwaukee 0234 Hole-Shooter, Sawzall 6511, Magnum; Skil 77/HD77 worm-drive saws; Delta/Rockwell 315 and Speedmatic saws; Bosch older 1581 jigsaws), and (2) **tools collectors want** – Machine Age designs (streamline cast-aluminium/chrome housings 1935–60), early Porter-Cable (B-1 belt sanders, Take-About sander), Stanley Electric Tools (Stanley Rule & Level routers, 1930s–60s), Black & Decker 1920s–40s pistol-grip "Holgun" drills, Walker-Turner, Thor. Photo tells:
- Nameplates: Sears "Craftsman" red/black or silver/black badge with a model number "xxx.yyyy" – the prefix identifies the contract maker (e.g. 103 = King-Seeley stationary 1930s–60s; 113 = Emerson Electric stationary; 315 = Singer then Ryobi portables; others vary – "207" prefix appears on 1950s–60s portable sanders/polishers, maker not confirmed).
- Era: cast-iron or cast-aluminium housings, cloth/rubber cords, two-prong plugs, toggle switches = 1940s–60s; grey/black plastic clamshells = 1970s+; double-insulated symbols = 1970s+.
- Examples: 7952618137 Craftsman 3×21 belt sander (1970s–80s, plastic case, two-prong plug); 7981213827 Craftsman 4 in belt / 6 in disc motorized benchtop sander (113-series, Emerson, 1980s, yellow lock key, 4 A); 7951383449 Craftsman 207.2561 sander/polisher (1950s–60s, polished aluminium streamline housing, grey plastic handle and knob, rubber cord, chuck key); 7947382232 Craftsman cast-iron 8 in bench table saw with tan/gold base and gridded cast-iron wings (King-Seeley 103-series, 1950s–60s).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cast housings, ball bearings, brushes accessible by caps, metal gear cases, heavy rubber cords, original steel cases and manuals = good. Plastic housings, sleeve bearings, two-prong lamp-cord = consumer. Machine-age design (polished aluminium, flowing lines) is a collector plus even for mid-grade brands. Original boxes/cases add 20–50%.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Frayed or cracked cords (replacement is a repair), brittle/warped plastic (207.2561 seller notes broken top cover and deformed handle), worn brushes, noisy bearings, seized motors, missing guards, rust on cast iron tables, broken switches, missing chuck keys. Stationary tools: missing fences, motors, belts. All "untested" tools must be treated as broken.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Not faked. Reissue confusion: Porter-Cable/Delta/Craftsman names were sold to Stanley Black & Decker (Craftsman 2017) and later tools are China-made with the same names – value is in USA-made vintage only.

## Market
Steady, user-driven, regional. Small portable tools sell fine on eBay (hundreds per month) but at low prices; stationary machines sell only locally (Facebook/Craigslist) to hobbyists and restorers. Spring and fall (project seasons) and Father's Day are slightly better. Collectors (OWWM, vintage tool groups) pay for early, unusual and complete tools in original cases.

## What sellers usually miss
- Porter-Cable, Milwaukee and Stanley professional tools mixed into "old tools" lots priced like Craftsman.
- Original metal cases, manuals and accessories (they matter to collectors).
- Conversely, sellers over-value "vintage Craftsman" consumer tools – the brand name carries nostalgia, not money.

## Shipping / handling
Portable sanders/drills 4–10 lb, $15–25; heavier cast tools $25–40; benchtop belt/disc sanders 30–50 lb ($50+, freight-ish); table saws, bandsaws, jointers – local only. Drain no fluids; pack cords. eBay 13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model and serial from the nameplate, maker behind the Sears number, tested (video of it running is best), condition of cord/plastic/bearings, everything included (case, keys, guards, manual). Say "sold as-is, vintage tool, check wiring before use" for old cords.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer (~$20) − ship to Dylan (~$20) − price ≥ max($50, price). A portable tool must sell for ~$110+ at $0 cost. Buy only: Porter-Cable 503/505/330/126/518, Skil 77 (early), Stanley Electric routers/planes, pre-war B&D/Porter-Cable, shown running on video or photographed on, at ≤ $30–40. Pass: all Craftsman consumer portables, Craftsman benchtop sanders and saws (weight/local), anything untested ("no untested electronics"), anything with frayed cords or broken housings (repair), any stationary machine (won't fit the passenger seat or ship).
