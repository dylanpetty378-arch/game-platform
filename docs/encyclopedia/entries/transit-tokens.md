# Transit tokens (US bus, streetcar and subway fare tokens; makers Scovill, Osborne, Meyer & Wenthe, Ingle System, Patrick & Co.)

Catalog reference: Atwood-Coffee Catalogue of United States and Canadian Transportation Tokens (American Vecturist Association, https://www.vecturist.com — URL not verified this run).
Slug: transit-tokens · Aliases: bus token, trolley token, subway token, fare token, good for one fare, vecturist token, car token
Summary: Common mid-century city tokens $1–8 each; lots of 50–100 mixed $25–80; scarce small-town, early (pre-1920) or error tokens $20–150; rare Atwood rarities $200–1,000+.
Buy rule (short): Buy only large mixed lots with many different small-town/early tokens under ~$0.50 each; single common tokens are always pass.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Small (16–25 mm) brass, white metal, zinc or aluminium discs, often with cut-out centres (letters like "T", stars, diamonds) or the "solid" type, stamped "Good for one fare", city/company name. Collected as "vecturism"; catalogued by Atwood-Coffee numbers (state abbreviation + city number + variety letter, e.g. AZ 870 ...).

## Makers, brands and variants
Manufacturers: Scovill Mfg (Waterbury), Osborne Register, Meyer & Wenthe (Chicago), Patrick & Co (San Francisco), Ingle System (Rochester), Johnson Fare Box. Big-city tokens (NYC subway "Y" cut, Chicago, Philadelphia, DC, Baltimore) were minted in millions — common. Small-city and short-lived systems, ferry/toll bridge tokens, early streetcar tokens and named-company tokens are scarcer.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Brass (most), white metal, nickel-silver, aluminium (wartime), bi-metal. Diamond/criss-cross engine-turned field common on Scovill/Osborne. Condition matters little except for rare types.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Wear, verdigris, holes, bends. Cleaning lowers value for serious collectors.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Rarely faked; some fantasy pieces and modern restrikes exist (NYC). Not a concern at this price level.

## Market
Large supply, small collector market; many sell, most for a few dollars. Lots move better than singles.

## What sellers usually miss
Scarce small-town tokens in "misc tokens" jars; non-transit trade tokens (good-for tokens from saloons/stores, which can be $20–200) mixed in.

## Shipping / handling
Envelope/plain mailer $1–5. Tiny, durable.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Give city, company, Atwood number if known, metal, diameter, cut-out shape; group common ones in lots.

## Buy rule
Single tokens fail the $50 minimum always. Only lots where you can ID ≥ several scarce Atwood types or early trade tokens, ≤ $30, expected lot sold ≥ $120.
