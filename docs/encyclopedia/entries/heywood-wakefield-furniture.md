## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Three distinct eras from the Gardner, MA company: (1) **wicker/rattan** 1880s–1930s; (2) **Old Colony / Colonial** 1930s–40s maple with turned legs, "Heywood-Wakefield" burned stamp in a shield/eagle cartouche (the stamp in 8797's photo); (3) **Streamline / Modern** 1936–66 solid birch in Wheat or Champagne blonde lacquer with rounded "bullet"/dogbone forms – this is the money line. Modern-line stamp is a burned-in "dogbone" (bowtie) mark with model number like M151G. Seller words: "Heywood Wakefield", "blonde MCM", "Hey-Wake". Look-alikes: Conant Ball, Cushman, Kroehler blonde pieces (no H-W stamp; general knowledge, verify).

More detail:
- **Company history** (Wikipedia):
  - Heywood Brothers (1826) and the Wakefield Rattan Co. (1855) merged in 1897 as Heywood Brothers & Wakefield, and became the Heywood-Wakefield Company in 1921.
  - Designers in the 1920s–40s included Paul Frankl, Donald Deskey, Gilbert Rohde, Russel Wright, Alexis de Sakhnoffsky and W. Joseph Carr.
  - It also made theatre, school and rail seating.
  - Operations shut down in 1979.
  - The name was bought out of bankruptcy in 1991–92 (South Beach Furniture Co., Leonard Riforgiato and Andrew Capitman), and new pieces are made today in Winchendon, MA (renamed Heywood-Wakefield Co. in 2022).
- **Marks, by source**:
  - ModCityMad: a burned-in bowtie ("dogbone") mark on the back or underside, reading "HEYWOOD-WAKEFIELD CO." and "GARDNER MASS.". It is the most reliable authentication mark.
  - Atomic Ranch: a black eagle logo stamp was used from 1949, usually in the left-hand drawer or on the underside. Earlier Streamline pieces had paper tags that are usually lost, so unmarked does not rule out pre-1949 H-W.
  - Model numbers with an "M" prefix (M151G, M530, M538, M776) are stamped or stencilled on many pieces.
  - Mark style evidently varies by period. Photograph whatever is present and quote it exactly.
- **Form vocabulary**: "bullet" or "boomerang" dresser pulls, "dogbone" dining chairs (the back splat shape), surfboard coffee table, step end table, nightstand, vanity bench, record cabinet. Curves were steam-bent: wood was steamed in retorts, bent by hand or hydraulic press and oven-dried for 4–24 hours (Atomic Ranch). The soft rounded edges and bent curves are an H-W signature.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Wicker/rattan era** (1880s–1930s): Heywood Brothers & Wakefield Rattan merged 1897; paper-label or stamped wicker. Mostly low value unless fine Victorian.
- **Old Colony / Colonial** (1930s–40s): maple, turned legs, tiered tables – low value.
- **Streamline Modern** (1936–41): the earliest blonde designs (Gilbert Rohde-era influence), highest value case pieces (general knowledge, verify).
- **Modern line** (1946–66): named suites – Kohinoor, Encore, Rio, Sculptura, Catalina, Niagara (general knowledge, verify); dogbone dining chairs, surfboard coffee table, step tables, nightstands.
- Finish names: **Wheat** (warmer, yellowish) and **Champagne** (paler, pinkish/grey); also rare Amber and Bermuda colours (general knowledge, verify). Original Wheat finish is critical to value.

Line and model detail from collector sources:
- **Streamline Modern (1936–41)**: credited to Alexis de Sakhnoffsky; the earliest and rarest (ModCityMad). Atomic Ranch says the 1930s Streamline line was in maple, while later Modern pieces are birch.
- **Encore** (ModCityMad: 1947–1966): the most commonly found line, with bullet pulls and horizontal lines. The M 538 Encore nightstand dates c.1950–55 (Atomic Ranch).
- **Kohinoor**: recessed hardware and subtle curves, more formal than Encore. ModCityMad gives 1941–1966. Other references place it in the 1950s, so verify against a catalog reprint.
- **Contessa** (1955–66): sculpted, carved drawer fronts (ModCityMad).
- **Sculptura**: the M 776 deluxe vanity dates c.1952–58 (Atomic Ranch).
- **StyleMaster**: the M 530 bed dates c.1950–53 and is reproduced by the new company (Atomic Ranch; Retro Renovation).
- **Finish correction (2026-10-13)**: **Amber** is the standard finish of the *new* (post-1992) company, lighter than the vintage colours (Atomic Ranch; Retro Renovation). It is not a rare vintage colour, as the line above implies. Vintage finishes are **Wheat** (slightly darker golden, the common one) and **Champagne** (paler, with a rose blush) (Atomic Ranch).
- **Small passenger-seat pieces** (the only ones we can buy): nightstands (M 538 Encore), step and lamp tables, single dogbone chairs, vanity benches, magazine racks and small bookcases.

### Quick ID checklist
- Find the mark: a burned bowtie "HEYWOOD-WAKEFIELD CO. GARDNER MASS.", a black eagle logo stamp (1949 on, left drawer or underside), a shield or eagle cartouche (Old Colony), or a paper label.
- Look for an "M" model number stamped or stencilled on the back, underside or inside a drawer, and record it.
- Is it a blonde, rounded, steam-bent form (Modern line, the valuable one), maple with turned legs (Old Colony, low), or wicker?
- Is the colour warm golden (Wheat) or pale with a pink blush (Champagne)? An even, very light "Amber" with crisp edges and CNC-perfect curves suggests new production (1992 on).
- Check the construction: solid wood throughout, including drawer sides and backs; no veneer or particle board (ModCityMad).
- Look for paint, dark stain or orange polyurethane. Painted or dark-stained Modern pieces lose 30–70%.
- Check for steel plates and bolts at table legs, which indicate new production (Retro Renovation).
- Judge the size: nightstands and step tables fit the car; dressers, beds and dining sets do not.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Solid northern yellow birch, nitrocellulose "toner" lacquer finish. Original finish shows even warm patina, slight amber at edges, the burned dogbone stamp and stencilled model numbers in drawers. Refinished pieces often look too yellow/orange or too white, lack stamps (sanded off), or have sprayed polyurethane. Dovetailed drawers and dust panels are standard. (general knowledge, verify)
- **Vintage vs new production** (Retro Renovation):
  - The new company acquired no original tooling. It reverse-engineered patterns and later cut them with a CNC router, so new curves are very uniform.
  - New dining table legs attach with steel plates and bolts, not wood-on-wood joinery.
  - New pieces carry the standard Amber finish. Refinishing a vintage piece to "natural" also gives a colour close to Amber, so a pale piece with no marks needs a construction check.
  - Both old and new are solid northern yellow birch.
- **Original finish signs** (ModCityMad): wear in the places hands touch, slight yellowing of the lacquer, and hardware patina that matches. A professional re-lacquer in Wheat costs $300–800 for a typical case piece.
- **Solvent test caution**: ModCityMad suggests a nail-polish-remover test on a hidden spot and says original lacquer "shows minimal reaction". That is backwards for nitrocellulose lacquer, which acetone softens, while cured polyurethane resists it. Do not use or quote this test; rely on visual tells and the marks.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Common: lacquer checking and crazing, water rings, sun-bleaching, chipped edges on rounded corners, loose chair joints, replaced seat fabric (acceptable). Painted/refinished Modern pieces lose 30–70%. Ask for close photos of the top surface under raking light, the stamp, and drawer interiors.
- A refinish in the correct Wheat colour by a known restorer is accepted by many buyers (Atomic Ranch shows a professionally refinished buffet). A home refinish with orange stain or poly is not.
- Dogbone chairs: check for loose back splats, cracked bent rails and replaced seat boards.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Heywood-Wakefield name was revived (1990s–2000s, Heywood-Wakefield Co. in Gardner reproduced classic designs) – reissues look new and carry newer labels (general knowledge, verify).
- **Correction (2026-10-13)**: the revival company bought the name and rights in 1991–92. It built in Massachusetts (now in **Winchendon**, MA, a few miles from Gardner), not in the original Gardner factory, and still makes pieces in 2022–2026 (Wikipedia; Retro Renovation). It makes beds (M 530 StyleMaster, Kohinoor), the M 926 desk/vanity, an M 326 cabinet bookcase, a reconfigured M 395 record cabinet, larger cocktail and dining tables and sofas. New pieces in Amber, or with steel leg plates, are reissues and should not be priced as vintage.
- Look-alike blonde furniture by other makers is often titled "Heywood-Wakefield style".

## Market
Strong, dedicated mid-century buyer base, especially in the Northeast and Midwest; sells locally and via Chairish/1stdibs/FB Marketplace. Case pieces sell in days–weeks at fair prices; maple/wicker sits. Demand stable; blonde MCM remains popular. (general knowledge, verify)
New production in Winchendon supplies buyers who want new pieces, but vintage original-finish pieces remain the collector target.

## What sellers usually miss
"Vintage Heywood Wakefield" covers all three eras; buyers pay for the blonde Modern line only. Painted/refinished Modern pieces lose 30–70%.
Also: M model numbers (which identify the line and years), the Champagne vs Wheat difference, and that an unmarked early piece may still be Streamline (paper tags lost).

## Shipping / handling
Furniture – local pickup or freight. Chairs/nightstands can ship Greyhound/uShip $80–150. Resell locally (FB Marketplace, no fee) or eBay local pickup.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State line/suite name and model number (from the stamp), finish (Wheat/Champagne), original vs refinished, dimensions, condition of the top. Photograph the dogbone stamp, model number, drawer dovetails, finish closeups. Title keywords: "Heywood Wakefield Wheat original finish", "M151G", "Kohinoor", "dogbone".

### What to tell the buyer
Give the maker mark exactly as found (burned bowtie "Heywood-Wakefield Co. Gardner Mass." or eagle stamp) with a photo, and the M model number. Name the line only when the model number or a catalog reference confirms it (e.g. "M 538 Encore nightstand, c.1950–55"). State that it is solid northern yellow birch with steam-bent curves. Name the finish (Wheat or Champagne) and say whether it is original lacquer or refinished, by whom and when if known. Give height, width and depth, and list checking, water marks, edge chips and loose joints. If the piece has no mark, say so plainly. Never present an Amber-finish piece as vintage without checking construction, because the company has made new pieces since 1992.

## Buy rule
Size: most Heywood-Wakefield pieces (dressers, dining tables, chair sets, coffee tables) are bigger than a passenger seat and are **excluded** by the size rule. Only small pieces that fit the passenger seat qualify. Buy when: Modern-line (blonde birch, dogbone/bullet) piece ≤ $100 and fits the car. Pass when: Old Colony maple or wicker unless free – resale ceiling ~$150 and it's heavy. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule).
