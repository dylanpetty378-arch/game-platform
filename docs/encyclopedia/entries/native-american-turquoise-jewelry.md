## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Silver (sterling or coin) jewelry set with turquoise and other stones, made by Southwest Native artists (Navajo silversmithing from the 1860s, Zuni lapidary from the 1920s). Types: cuffs (stamped, cluster, row), link bracelets, squash blossom necklaces, concho belts, rings, bolo ties, petit point/needlepoint (Zuni small stones), channel inlay.
Handmade tells: hand-cut bezels with serrated/sawtooth edge, stamp work (hand dies), twisted wire and silver drops ("raindrops"), backs with hallmarks (maker initials, "Sterling"), stones of irregular natural shape with matrix (spiderweb), heavy gauge. Import tells: cast uniform settings, thin metal, "925" plus a country stamp (Mexico, Thailand, China), perfectly calibrated oval stones, dyed bright blue "turquoise" (howlite or magnesite), plastic/"block" stabilized material.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Natural turquoise has matrix, varying hue, small surface pits; stabilized is uniform, slightly plasticky; dyed howlite has white showing in cracks and too-even matrix veins; block turquoise (reconstituted) shows grainy uniform composition. Silver weight: a heavy men's cuff 60–120 g; light link bracelet 15–30 g. Oxidation in recesses and soft wear on high points indicate age.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Cracked or missing stones (replacements reduce value), loose bezels, broken links, resized/repaired cuffs (solder marks), worn-through stamped areas, over-polishing.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Huge problem: Indian Arts and Crafts Act makes selling non-Native work as "Native American" illegal. Fake hallmarks and fake "old pawn" tags. Dyed howlite/magnesite. Artist-signed high-end pieces need authentication (outside Dylan's rules). Describe unmarked pieces as "Southwestern style" unless provenance.

## Market
Large, liquid market, strong buyer base on eBay, Etsy and Instagram. Sell-through 40–50% for honest listings; 1–4 weeks. Heavy vintage sells fast.

## What sellers usually miss
Hallmarks on backs (they don't recognise maker initials); weight (heavy silver = melt floor); natural high-grade stones (Lander, Lone Mountain spiderweb) on plain-looking pieces; estate "old Indian jewelry" lots priced by count. Sellers usually OVER-price light link bracelets.
