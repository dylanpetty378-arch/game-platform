## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand-carved wooden saints, Virgins and Christ-child (Niño Jesús / Santo Niño / Menino Jesus) figures from Spanish, Mexican, Spanish-colonial (New Mexico, Philippines, Guatemala) and Portuguese/Brazilian churches and home altars, 17th–early 20th c. Tells of a real one: gesso ground with chipped/flaking paint showing white gesso and bare wood underneath; gilt on robes (bole-red showing through worn gilt); glass or painted eyes; hands carved separately and sometimes missing fingers; square-nailed or doweled bases; worm holes; dull matte surface. Titles sellers use: "antique wooden statue", "old Catholic statue", "church statue", "Jesus figure", "saint carving", "Santos". Reproductions: glossy even paint, uniform machine-sanded surface, "Made in Mexico/Philippines" stamp, resin (check for mould seams), stylised "Santa Fe" folk carvings from the 1980s–2000s.

## Makers, brands and variants
Almost always anonymous workshop or folk carvers; value comes from region, age, size and subject rather than maker.
- **Spanish / Spanish-colonial (Mexico, Guatemala)**: estofado gilding (gold leaf under paint, scratched to show pattern), glass eyes, realistic faces (general knowledge, verify).
- **Philippine santos**: hardwood (often molave/narra), ivory-faced examples exist and are legally restricted (general knowledge, verify).
- **New Mexican santos/bultos**: cottonwood, gesso and natural pigments; 19th-c. santeros are a distinct, well-documented collector field; 20th-c. revival carvers are mid-value (general knowledge, verify).
- **Portuguese/Brazilian**: Menino Jesus figures (Lens matched a Menino Jesus de Praga auction lot).
- Subjects: Santo Niño / Niño Jesús (articulated or with crown), Virgin Mary, St Vincent Ferrer (winged), Christ busts, convent figures.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Carved wood (cedar, pine, cottonwood, hardwoods) under gesso and polychrome; gilt over red bole. Age tells: shrinkage cracks, worm holes, worn high points, dull oxidised surface, hand-tool marks, square nails or wooden dowels, separately carved hands. Cheap production: uniform sanding, glossy acrylic paint, resin with mould seams, rotary-tool marks, staples or Phillips screws, fresh wood showing at "distressed" spots.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint and gesso loss, missing fingers/hands, worm damage, cracks — buyers pay for honest age loss. What hurts: modern overpaint, glued-on replacement parts, active worm (frass powder), and heavy "restoration". Repairs show as fresh, brighter paint patches or putty fills. Ask for base/underside, back-of-head and hand close-ups to confirm hand carving and old nails.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Large market of resin and newly carved "antique" santos from Mexico, the Philippines and Indonesia, artificially distressed. Tells: even crackle, paint loss in "logical" places only, no gesso layer, modern fasteners, stamps/stickers. 1980s–2000s Santa Fe-style folk carvings are honestly new but often relisted as antique.

## Market
Steady: roughly a dozen genuine-looking santos sold on eBay Jul–Oct 2026 in the $190–400 band, with occasional four-figure pieces. Buyers: Latin American / Spanish-colonial art collectors, religious-antique dealers, Southwest (Santa Fe, Tucson) decorators. Top pieces belong at Invaluable/LiveAuctioneers houses. Mild Christmas/Easter interest for Niño figures (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Size and gilt. They describe condition ("badly chipped") as a negative; buyers pay for exactly that age evidence. They never say "polychrome", "gesso", "Santo Niño", "Spanish colonial" – search by those to sell. Ask for base/underside and back-of-head photos to confirm hand carving and old nails.

## Shipping / handling
Fragile (fingers, flaking gesso). Double-box with the figure wrapped loosely in tissue then bubble; 25" piece ≈ 8–12 lb boxed, $45–60 UPS Ground. Sell on eBay (religious antiques category) or to a Santa Fe / Tucson dealer; auction houses (Invaluable/LiveAuctioneers) for the top tier.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Height, subject, likely region and century (with reasons: gesso, gilt over bole, glass eyes, square nails), materials, every loss and repair, and provenance if any. Photograph front, back, base, head close-up, hands and the gilt. Title keywords: "antique carved polychrome santo", "Spanish colonial", "gesso", "gilt", "Santo Niño", height.

## Buy rule
Buy when: an 18"+ carved, gessoed figure with gilt and honest paint loss is under $200 – expect $400–800 sold. Pass when: under 12", glossy/even paint, resin, "Made in" stamp, or the seller already lists it as "antique santos polychrome" at $300+. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (life-size church figures are "bigger" and excluded).
