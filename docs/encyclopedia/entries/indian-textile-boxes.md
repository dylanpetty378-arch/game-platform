## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cardboard or wood boxes covered in fabric (satin, brocade) with zari (metallic thread), sequins, beads and couched gold cord: produced in huge volume for gifts and weddings since the 1970s. Older pieces use real gilt-silver wire and wood frames.

Three distinct families get titled "Indian box":
1. **Fabric-covered embroidered boxes (zari, zardozi, sequin, brocade)** — the common find. Square, round or heart-shaped, 3–8" across, cardboard or thin plywood carcass, satin or velvet covering, couched metallic cord, sequins, beads, sometimes a mirror or a padded lid. Velvet or satin lining inside. Made in Agra, Delhi, Lucknow and for export.
2. **Kashmiri papier-mâché (Srinagar)** — moulded paper pulp, gessoed and hand-painted (naqashi) with floral, bird, hunting or court scenes, highlighted in gold, then lacquered. Early-20th-c. examples have figurative scenes (a woman smoking a hookah with attendants, hunting scenes, birds in a landscape) and run 2¾–6" across (1stDibs set of four).
3. **Sadeli (Bombay mosaic) boxes, late 18th–19th c.** — sandalwood or other wood boxes covered in geometric micro-mosaic made from bundled rods of tin, copper, horn, ivory, sappan wood and ebony sliced into strips; often combined with ivory panels and fittings (Lyon & Turnbull). Bombay, sometimes Surat. Workboxes, writing boxes, card cases and small trinket boxes.

Look-alikes: Palestinian tatreez embroidered boxes, Framecraft needlework trinket boxes (UK kits), Chinese silk brocade boxes, Persian khatam boxes (Iranian micro-mosaic, very similar to sadeli — khatam is usually on a modern box with plastic or bone), and Vizagapatam ivory-veneered boxes.

## Makers, brands and variants
Unmarked workshops (Agra, Delhi, Lucknow); Kashmiri papier-mâché (Srinagar); Sadeli (Surat/Bombay, 19th c.).

- **Zardozi/zari workshops (Agra, Delhi, Lucknow, Bareilly)** — unmarked. Real zari is silk thread wrapped with silver and then gilded (gold-electroplated); imitation ("tested") zari is copper-core thread with a thin silver coating; plastic zari is lurex, a metallic plastic film (Aza Fashions).
- **Kashmiri papier-mâché (Srinagar)** — mostly unsigned; some pieces carry an artist's or shop's name inked or painted underneath ("Made in Kashmir" or a Srinagar dealer's name on 20th-c. tourist pieces).
- **Sadeli (Bombay; Surat)** — unmarked workshops; Anglo-Indian export to Britain. Small examples are cataloged by museums (Horniman 988.8: Bombay box with mosaic lid in blue, green, red and white metal).

### Quick ID checklist
- Carcass: cardboard showing at worn corners = modern gift box; wood = better.
- Thread: dull warm gold with dark tarnish on silver = real metal; bright, even, mirror-shiny gold that never tarnished = synthetic/lurex.
- Sequins: plastic (flat, iridescent, curled) = modern; metal spangles (tarnished, stamped) = older.
- Papier-mâché: hand-painted figurative or fine floral work with gold, light weight, lacquer crazing = Kashmiri; printed decals or flat stencilled flowers = recent tourist.
- Mosaic: tiny geometric stars and chevrons of metal, horn and pale (ivory/bone) rods = sadeli (or Persian khatam — check plastic).
- Ivory components? Note for legal/platform rules.
- Labels: "Made in India" sticker, Hindi/English shop stamp = 20th-c. export.
- Size and interior: velvet/satin lining, compartments, mirror.

- Late-18th/early-19th-c. Bombay sadeli workbox, sandalwood with ivory fittings — ~£504 at Lyon & Turnbull (2025) vs $8–25 modern fabric boxes — geometric micro-mosaic bands over the whole surface, sandalwood interior, ivory pots and spools, paw feet.

- Early-20th-c. Kashmiri papier-mâché with figurative scenes (court, hookah, hunting) — dealer market (1stDibs) vs Terapeak median $14 for typical Kashmiri boxes — hand-painted faces and animals, gold highlights, crazed lacquer.
- Antique zardozi box with real gilt-silver wire on velvet, wood frame — $25–60+ (Terapeak "zardozi embroidered box" median $48, n=7) vs $8–25 — tarnished metal wire, coiled "purl" wire, metal spangles, hand-stitched.
- Silver-mounted Indian box (hallmarked or tested silver mounts) — "silver-mounted" upside in the buy rule — engraved silver corners, hasp or lid plaque; check for marks.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Plastic sequins, synthetic brocade, glued edges = modern. Tarnished real metal wire, hand-stitched, wood carcass = older.

- Real zari adds noticeable weight and has a warm, subtle glow rather than a high shine; faux zari rubs off to reveal a red, green or plastic-like base; a burned thread of real zari leaves ash and smells of metal, synthetic melts (Aza Fashions — do not burn-test a buyer's item; use rubbing and magnification).
- Zardozi technique: raised work over padding, coiled purl wire (dabka), metal sequins (sitara) — hand stitches visible; machine embroidery shows uniform satin stitch and a paper backing.
- Papier-mâché: genuine pieces are pulp built up in layers over a mould; brush strokes and gold under lacquer; age shows as fine crazing and soft wear on edges.
- Sadeli: the mosaic is built from tiny triangular/hexagonal sections set into a wood core; metal strips (tin/pewter) oxidise grey; ivory yellows and shrinks.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Fraying, lost sequins, crushed corners, stains.

- Sadeli: lifting or lost mosaic strips, split sandalwood, missing ivory fittings; repairs with modern resin show as smooth glossy fills.
- Papier-mâché: chipped edges, flaking lacquer, overpainting.
- Textile: moth damage to velvet, faded satin, glue staining at seams, tarnish on real metal (not a defect; do not polish).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Mostly modern production sold as "vintage". Persian khatam and modern Indian "mosaic" boxes (often with plastic and bone) are sold as antique sadeli. New Kashmiri tourist papier-mâché is artificially "aged".

## Market
Slow, low-value decor. Antique sadeli and good Kashmiri pieces sell through UK and US auction houses and 1stDibs/Chairish, not eBay — partly because of ivory restrictions.

## What sellers usually miss
Little; occasional Sadeli or silver mounts. Also: early-20th-c. figurative Kashmiri papier-mâché lumped in with tourist pieces.

## Shipping / handling
Light; $6–12. Sadeli and papier-mâché are fragile — wrap in tissue, box with padding.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Dimensions, interior, thread type.

### What to tell the buyer
Name the type plainly (fabric-covered zari/sequin box, Kashmiri hand-painted papier-mâché, or Bombay sadeli mosaic) and give an honest date range; modern gift-shop boxes should be called "vintage 1990s–2010s" or similar, not antique. Give dimensions, the carcass (cardboard or wood), the lining and compartments, and describe the thread (real metal thread with tarnish, or bright synthetic) and sequins (metal or plastic). For papier-mâché, say "hand-painted" only if brush work is visible. If any part may be ivory, do not list it on eBay (eBay prohibits ivory) and check US federal and state ivory laws before selling anywhere. Photograph corners, base, lining and any label.

## Buy rule
Profit = resale × 0.87 − ~$8 out − ~$8 in − price ≥ max($50, price). Modern: always pass. Antique sadeli (no ivory, or sold through a compliant venue) or figurative Kashmiri papier-mâché at ≤ 1/3 resale.
