## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Oklahoma art and dinnerware pottery founded by John Frank in 1933 (Norman), moved to Sapulpa 1938. Signature glazes: Prairie Green, Desert Gold, Woodland Moss, Onyx Black, Brown Satin, White Sand, Peach Glow, Flame (bright red-orange, 1970s), Red Bud, Coffee. Moulded mark "FRANKOMA" plus mould number (e.g. 235) on the base. Clay body tells age: **Ada clay (cream/tan, used to ~1954)** vs **Sapulpa red brick clay (1954+)** — look at the unglazed foot ring.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Frank Potteries (Norman, 1933–38)**: "Frank Potteries" mark; scarce.
- **Pot & Puma mark (1934–38)**: highly collectible.
- **Frankoma Ada clay era (1938–1954)**: figurines (Fan Dancer, Indian Chief, Bucking Bronco, Pacing Puma), vases.
- **Sapulpa red clay era (1954–2004)**: dinnerware (Plainsman, Wagon Wheel, Mayan Aztec, Lazybones), annual Christmas plates (1965–), political mugs (1968–), bicentennial pieces.
- **Later owners (1991 Joniece Frank; 2008 bankruptcy; Frankoma Inc. until 2010)**: late production low.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Ada clay = lighter, cream-tan body, often heavier glaze runs; red clay = brick-red foot. Glaze names affect desirability; Flame and Prairie Green are common.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips on foot ring and rim, crazing, glaze skips (factory), utensil marks on dinnerware.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Frankoma reissued earlier moulds in red clay (check clay colour). Few outright fakes.

## Market
Large supply, regional (Texas/Oklahoma) collector base. Common pieces slow.

## What sellers usually miss
Ada clay vs red clay: same mould, very different value. Lens matches of $900+ for common moulds are false positives.

## Shipping / handling
1–4 lb, thick stoneware, double box $10–15.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Mould number, glaze name, clay colour photo of foot, dimensions.
