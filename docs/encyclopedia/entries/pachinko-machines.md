# Pachinko machines (vintage Japanese, 1960s–80s: Nishijin, Sankyo, Sanyo, Daiichi, Heiwa, Ginza)

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Upright pinball-like board ~32"x20", chrome frame, hundreds of brass pins, flipper-less; balls fed from a top tray. Makers printed on the face or back: Nishijin, Sankyo, Sanyo, Daiichi, Heiwa, Ginza. 1970s "Super DX" style = ball-feed tray on top; 1960s single-shot = thumb lever, no tray; electro-mechanical (Nishijin Powerflash/Circuit, 1974–75) have lights/relays. Seller words: "pachinko", "Japanese pinball", "slot machine".

Look-alikes: pachislo (pachinko slot) machines from the 1990s+ are a different, electronic product (general knowledge, verify); modern pachinko machines (1980s+ electronic, LCD) are heavier and low value in the US (general knowledge, verify).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Nishijin**: most common in the US; Powerflash/Circuit electro-mechanical models (1974–75) are top tier.
- **Sankyo**: common 1970s machines (decent tier).
- **Sanyo, Daiichi, Heiwa, Ginza**: other common makers.
- **Variants**: 1960s single-shot (thumb lever, no tray) — collectible; themed faces (dolphins, poker, sports) — collectible; pre-1960 — top.
- About 95% of US machines are common 1970s models (brought back by servicemen and imported in bulk, general knowledge, verify).

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Wood cabinet, chrome-plated frame, glass front, brass pins in a printed/painted playfield, steel balls. Quality tells: bright chrome, intact playfield artwork, straight pins, complete payout mechanism and ball tray, original key. EM models: relays and lights should function.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rusted chrome, bent or missing pins, faded/flaking playfield, broken glass, jammed payout, missing ball tray or key, no balls. Restoration takes 70–80 hours, so non-working = parts only ($10–40, ≤ $10 value per pachinkoplanet). No repair rule applies.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
No meaningful fakes. Risk is mixed/married parts and repainted playfields sold as original (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
Small, slow market; buyers are game-room/man-cave decorators and a few collectors (general knowledge, verify). Mostly local because of weight and glass. Plentiful supply of common 1970s machines keeps prices low.

## What sellers usually miss
Era. A single-shot 1960s or electro-mechanical machine listed as "old pachinko $80" is the only real upside. Themed faces are worth more than plain.

## Shipping / handling
~35 lb, glass face, pins bend – $60–90 to ship, high breakage risk. Local pickup only unless rare. Profit rarely clears Dylan's rule once shipping both ways is counted.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State maker, model/era, single-shot vs tray-fed vs EM, working status (video of play and payout), included balls and key, chrome and playfield condition, dimensions. Photograph front playfield, back with maker label, chrome close-ups. Title keywords: "Vintage 1960s Nishijin Single Shot Pachinko Machine Working", "Electro-Mechanical Powerflash".

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1960s single-shot or EM model ≤ $100 and working, local. Pass when: "for parts or repair" (no repair rule), common 1970s at any price over $50 if it must ship. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat (about 32"x20" — it does).
