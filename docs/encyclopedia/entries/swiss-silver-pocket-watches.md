Buy rule (short): Buy only running, maker-named (Longines, Omega, Zenith, IWC) silver Swiss watches or enamelled decorative cases at ≤ 1/3 of resale; pass on generic cylinder fob watches above ~$40. High-grade/repeaters need professional authentication, so out of scope.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
1870s–1920s Swiss pocket watches in silver cases, often marked 0.800, 0.935, "Argent", "Fine Silver", or with Swiss hallmarks (a bear or grouse for silver, after 1880). Ladies' (fob) sizes are 30–36 mm; gents' 45–52 mm. "Bijou", "Remontoir", "Cylindre 10 Rubis" and "Ancre" are generic dial or movement words, not makers. A white enamel dial with Roman numerals, blue-steel or Breguet hands, a sub-seconds dial, and an engine-turned bezel are typical of 1880–1900 exports. Large 45 mm+ with a sub-seconds dial are gents' size.

## Makers, brands and variants
Generic Swiss (Fontainemelon, A. Schild ébauches), Longines, Zenith, Omega, Waltham Swiss imports, Elgin (US). "Bijou" dials appear on Swiss export watches sold in the US c.1880–1900.

- Silver chronograph (monopusher, centre-seconds, often "Doctor's" or "Timing" watches, 1880s–1910s) — $365–599 asked for running examples, "Timing and Repeating Watch Co Geneva" $695 asked vs $40–150 (~4–10×, asking) — a long centre-seconds sweep hand with a 1/5-second track around the dial edge, a pusher in the crown or at 2 o'clock, 45–52 mm gents' size (entry comp: eBay asks)
- Omega 24-hour dial in silver, c.1910 — $1,200 asked vs $40–150 (~10×, asking) — "OMEGA" on the dial and movement, numerals 13–24 in an inner or outer ring (often red), gents' size (entry comp: eBay asks)
- Longines silver open-face (1890s–1920s), especially with "7 Grands Prix" engraved on the dust cover — $225–599 asked, Lens median $380 (n=5) vs $40–150 (~3–5×, asking) — "Longines" on the dial and the winged-hourglass logo with a serial on the movement; the engraved award list ("7 Grands Prix", "Paris 1900") on the inner cuvette (entry comp: eBay asks, Lens)
- High-grade Geneva signatures (Vacheron & Constantin, Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet) inside a "Swiss silver watch" — "Vacheron Genève" cylinder watch $850 asked; real ones far more (authentication tier) — maker's name on the movement bar (not just the dial), Geneva-stripe decoration, a case number matching the movement; the vast majority of "Vacheron" dial cylinder watches are period fantasy names, so require the movement photo (entry comp: eBay ask)
- Hallmark tell for case metal: three bears (two large, one small) = 0.935 (from 1888, for Britain); a single rampant bear = 0.875; a grouse/capercaillie = 0.800 (from 1882). These confirm silver but add little value on their own (https://vintagewatchstraps.com/swisshallmarks.php)
- ~1× (do not flag): "Bijou", "Remontoir", "Cylindre 10 Rubis" generic 0.800 ladies' fob or open-face watches, even with guilloché enamel backs — $40–150 sold (Terapeak: red guilloché enamel $39.99, working A. Mathey $149.99) (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Silver cases tarnish; look for 0.800/0.935 marks inside the back and dust covers. A cylinder escapement is cheap, a lever escapement better. Jewel count is on the movement. An enamel dial with hairline cracks is common.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Not running (needs a service at $150–300), cracked dials, broken crystals, worn hinges, broken cylinder escapements (often uneconomic to repair), and dents.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese "antique" mechanical watches with fake old dials; marriages (mismatched dial, case or movement).

## Market
Steady; NAWCC collectors, steampunk and jewellery buyers. eBay: 500+ per month. Days to sell: 14–45.

## What sellers usually miss
Longines/Omega signatures on the movement (not the dial); gold cases mistaken for silver; enamelled backs.

## Shipping / handling
Small padded box, insured, $6–10.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the movement, case marks, dial, and give the diameter. Say whether it runs and keeps time.

## Buy rule
Price ≤ $200; profit = median × 0.87 − ~$8 − ~$8 − price ≥ max($50, price). Buy only running, maker-named watches at ≤ ~35% of resale. Non-running generic watches never qualify, and needing a service breaks the no-repairs rule.
