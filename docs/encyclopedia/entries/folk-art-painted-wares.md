## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Wood or tin household objects decorated with folk motifs: Pennsylvania German distelfinks (birds), hearts, tulips, swags; Scandinavian rosemaling scrolls; Peter Hunt's peasant-style flowers and figures on repurposed furniture; tole (painted tin) trays and coffee pots with fruit and gold stencils. Mid-century revival pieces (1940s–70s) are the bulk; 19th c. originals are rare.
Tells in a photo: old = paint worn through at handles/rims, crackled and darkened varnish, hand-cut staves, wooden or iron bands with hand-cut fingers (laps), square nails; revival = bright clean colours, machine-made staves with steel bands, black ground, symmetrical brushwork, often signed and dated on the base.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Pennsylvania German originals** (1780s–1880s): dower chests, boxes, tinware (Filley, Pennsylvania toleware).
- **Revival decorators** (1930s–70s): Peter Hunt (Provincetown, 1896–1967; signed "Peter Hunt"), numerous regional PA Dutch painters (e.g. Johnny Ott — hex signs, Lester Breininger — redware), many amateur tole-class painters. Lens matched this listing to "Helen Hume" — name unverified this run.
- **Scandinavian rosemaling** — Norwegian-American painters, Vesterheim Gold Medal artists.
- **Tole tinware**: 1950s Nashco, Lackawanna, Hand-painted Italian tole.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original 19th c. paint: soft wear, oxidised varnish, paint in cracks; revival: oil/acrylic on modern-dark ground. A signature does not make it valuable unless the painter has a market. Quality = confident brush strokes, balanced composition.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Paint flaking, stave shrinkage/gaps (this bucket shows a crack/gap on a stave), rot, repainting over old surfaces (kills value of originals), lost bails/lids.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Old buckets/boxes repainted in "PA Dutch" style to pass as original; reproduction distelfink pieces from Lancaster County gift shops; fake aged paint.

## Market
Decor-driven, seasonal-ish (fall/farmhouse); sells slowly unless early or signed. Strong in PA/New England.

## What sellers usually miss
Original 19th c. surfaces under grime; Peter Hunt signatures; old tin under later paint.

## Shipping / handling
2–5 lb, bulky; box with padding, $15–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Photograph the base (signature, date), give dimensions, describe paint loss and cracks, say "mid-century hand-painted" not "antique" unless proven.
