## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Date stamp on the bottom of the fount: month and 2-digit year ("3 56" = March 1956). Model stamped near it or on the fount side decal. Shapes: single-mantle 200/200A (red, small, most common 1951–83); two-mantle 220/228 (green, bigger, 228 has a wide "big hat" ventilator); 242/243 single-mantle 1930s–40s (nickel founts); 236/237 kerosene; 202 "Professional" (chrome fount, 1954–64). Colour tells: 200A is red (most), green 200A pre-1952 and the red/green "Christmas" 200 (1951) are prized; black-band 200A (1951–52) prized (but terry-marsh shows the black base rest through the 1950s, so the date stamp, not the band, proves 1951–52); chrome/nickel founts earlier. Globes: Pyrex with Coleman logo; "Sunshine of the Night" early. Sellers' words: "old Coleman lantern", "camping lantern", sometimes with the date.
- **Date-stamp formats** (Old Town Coleman): from 1951 on, month left / year right ("4 82" = April 1982); c.1947–1953 an alphanumeric code where the number is the year and the letter (commonly read as A = Jan–Jun, B = Jul–Dec) the half-year ("A 51"); c.1924–1947 year left / month right, with single-digit years common in the 1920s–30s and two digits from about 1941. Canadian-built lanterns may carry "C" or "D". Coleman reused parts, so treat dating rules as "usually".
- **Company-name stamp** when no date: Coleman Lamp Company 1913–1925; Coleman Lamp & Stove Company 1925–1934; Coleman Lamp and Stove Company 1934–1945; The Coleman Company Inc. 1945–present (Old Town Coleman).
- **200 vs 200A vs 242**: the 200 (Wichita, 1950–51) has an inverted-U brass tube burner (not the 242C's cast burner) and a single-leg burner frame; 242s have a two-leg frame; the 200 fount filler is about 1/2" ID vs about 3/8" on a 242 (terry-marsh; TNTTT). 200/200A/202 share the red-handled reflector 200-790; collars, frames and vents swap between 200 and 242, so "Frankenlanterns" are common (TNTTT).
- **Model 200A** (from 1951, made about 32 years): the earliest were green-painted steel with a Coleman decal by November 1951; black base rest by 1953; red with an aluminium base rest in 1960; maroon instead of red in 1961 (terry-marsh). A December 1953 factory white fount and engraved plated presentation lanterns are noted.
- **Model 202 Professional**: ten years from 1954; early ceramic burner cap, later metal; standard ventilator green; presentation examples with black enamel ventilator and engraved names (terry-marsh).
- **220D/228D** (late 1940s–1951): nickel-plated brass founts for most of the run (1948 the last nickel 228D seen); green painted by "A 51"; large valve wheel, two-piece stamped burner, pump held by two small screws. **220E/228E**: steel founts, earliest seen July 1951 (terry-marsh).
- Look-alikes: other pressure-lantern brands (AGM, Thermos, Sears/Hiawatha, Montgomery Ward, Petromax/Aladdin), modern Coleman battery and propane lanterns, and decorative "railroad" kerosene lanterns.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Coleman (Wichita, KS)**: models above; letter suffix on 220/228 (B through K) tracks the sub-model sequence (general knowledge, verify).
- **Quick-Lite / Arc** lanterns, 1920s–30s – collectible.
- **Coleman Canada** (Toronto) and **Sears-branded** ("Sears 72325") variants – often overlooked.
- Military / IR 200A and presentation 202 – top tier.
- Other brands (AGM, Thermos, Sears/Hiawatha, Montgomery Ward) are related but separate (general knowledge, verify).
- 1930s–40s models (terry-marsh 1936–45): **242B** (dated 1937–41) nickel-plated fount, Nov 1941 example brass-sided with green paint; **243** (1936) economy, painted fount, one-piece ventilator, steel burner; **243A** from 1937; **228B** 1937–42 (WPA-tagged and "U.S."-stamped examples); **220B** 1937–42 (Bureau of Reclamation / US Forest Service marked); **236** 500-cp gasoline, early 1940s, made in small numbers; **237A/237B** kerosene (237B military 1943–44); **252/252A** military (1944–45); **242C** 1942–Dec 1950, cast brass burner, early green paint then nickel-plated brass; US Forest Service "F.S."-stamped 242Cs from 1947 noted as unusual.
- **Model 200 OCD**: November 1951 Office of Civil Defense lanterns built in Wichita from Toronto 200 parts; engraved OCD examples exist (terry-marsh).
- **Military IR 200A**: a 1959 infrared lantern with Fresnel lens and IR filter for directing aircraft (terry-marsh).
- **Government-marked** lanterns (WPA, CDF, USFS, Bureau of Reclamation) are less common and add interest.
- **Christmas lantern**: a 1951 two-tone red-and-green Model 200 (a forum veteran describes it as red top and green bottom; TNTTT). Repainted two-tone "Christmas" lanterns are a known hobby project, so check the date stamp and factory paint.

### Quick ID checklist
- Fount bottom: date stamp format (A 51 / 3 56 / 41 7) and model stamp.
- Fount finish: nickel-plated brass, green paint, red, maroon (1961), chrome (202).
- Base rest/band: black (1950s 200A) vs aluminium (1960+).
- Mantles: one (200/200A/242/243/202) or two (220/228); ventilator size (228 big hat).
- Burner: brass U-tube (200) vs cast (242C); frame legs one vs two.
- Globe: Coleman-marked Pyrex matched to model; "Sunshine of the Night" on early ones.
- Decal present/absent; government stamp or engraving.
- Rust, dents, and whether it holds pressure and lights.

- Nickel-plated brass founts (242B 1937–41, 242C, 220D/228D to 1948–51, Quick-Lite/Arc 1920s–30s) — $125–300 vs $20–50 painted-steel 1960s–80s (~5×) — bright nickel fount (not paint), year-left date stamp ("41 7") or company-name stamp "Coleman Lamp & Stove Co." (entry comp)
- Unused boxed 200A even from 1968 — $135 vs $20–50 used (~3×) — original box with matching model number, mantles still tied, no soot on the globe (https://www.heraldnet.com/?p=177599)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Painted steel founts (red/green), nickel-plated brass founts (earlier), chrome (202). Original decals, original Coleman Pyrex globe matched to the model, and a crisp date stamp add value. Repainted founts (powder-coat or rattle-can) lower collector value though they sell to users (general knowledge, verify).
- Nickel-plated brass founts dominate pre-1951; painted brass then painted steel from 1951 (terry-marsh).
- Valve-wheel material: early metal wheels; brown plastic by A 51 on 220D/228D (terry-marsh).
- Many Pyrex globes cracked, so an undamaged early embossed Coleman Pyrex globe is notable (terry-marsh on 242A).
- Repaint tells: paint over the date stamp, overspray on the collar or base rest, too-glossy finish, colours like Krylon hunter green (forum members use these for refinishes; TNTTT).

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust on the fount, missing/cracked globe, dented ventilator, stuck valve, leaking pump leather, missing bail. Rebuilt and working (new pump cup and generator) is good for user buyers. Ask for the fount-bottom date stamp, globe markings, and whether it holds pressure/lights.
- Married parts: a 242C collar on a "9 59" 200A fount is a documented example (TNTTT) — the fount date wins.
- Pressure test overnight after any rebuild (TNTTT).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Reproduction decals and globes exist; modern Coleman retro reissues (e.g. anniversary editions) are dated recent (general knowledge, verify). Married parts (wrong globe or ventilator for the model) are common.
- Repainted "Christmas" and custom-colour lanterns (terry-marsh has a "Custom lanterns" section) — not factory.

## Market
Large, liquid collector and camper market; active Coleman Collectors Forum and FB groups. Common lanterns are everywhere, so only early dates, rare colours and models make money. Mild spring/summer camping bump (general knowledge, verify).

## What sellers usually miss
Date stamp meaning (early = money), black band, model 200 vs 200A, nickel/chrome founts, matching original globe. Also Coleman Canada and Sears-branded ("Sears 72325") variants.
- The 1947–53 letter-number stamp ("A 51", "B 47") — sellers misread it.
- Government stamps (WPA, USFS "F.S.", "U.S."), OCD engraving, presentation engraving.
- Maroon 1961 200A as a one-year colour.

## Shipping / handling
3–5 lb with globe – pack the globe separately; $15–25. Fuel residue: drain before shipping. eBay ~13%.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Model and date stamp (month/year), fount colour/finish, globe markings, working status (lit, holds pressure), original vs replaced parts, rust. Photograph fount bottom stamp, globe logo, decal, lit lantern. Title: "Coleman 200A Lantern dated 1/51 black band original globe".

### What to tell the buyer
Quote the model and the exact fount stamp and decode it ("stamped 'A 51' = first half of 1951"; "'3 56' = March 1956"). Give fount finish (nickel-plated brass, painted steel, chrome), base-rest colour, mantle count, globe markings, and whether parts are original or replaced (generator, pump cup, globe, ventilator). State whether it was lit and held pressure, and that it ships drained of fuel. Mention any government, OCD or presentation markings, and disclose repaint.

## Buy rule
Buy when: pre-1952 date, black band, model 200, 202, 242/243 with nickel fount, or any 1920s–30s, at ≤ $50. Pass when: 220/228 or red 200A later than 1955 at any price over $25 (sold $45–100, profit never clears $50). Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after ~13% fees and shipping both ways (Dylan's rule). Must fit a passenger seat.
Note: "black band" alone does not make a 1951 lantern — terry-marsh shows the black base rest on 200As by 1953, through 1959 (aluminium from 1960); a black-band 200A sold at a $86 median does not clear the profit rule at $50, so for black band require a 1951–52 date stamp.
