## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Domestic sewing machines 1880s–1980s, in cases, cabinets or treadle bases. Value comes from a handful of models: the **Singer Featherweight 221** (small, black glossy, gold scroll decals, folding bed extension, square tan or black carrying case, about 11 lb) and **222K** (free-arm, UK made), the **Singer 301** (long-bed slant-needle, black or tan/beige two-tone), and Swiss machines (Bernina, Elna). Brown, beige or mint mid-century "streamliner" machines branded for department stores (Montgomery Ward, Sears Kenmore, Spiegel, Firestone) are common and cheap.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Singer**: 221 Featherweight (1933–64; white 221K 1960s also collectible), 222K (1953–61), 301/301A (1951–57), 201 (heavy, gear-driven), 15-91, 99K, 66 "Red Eye" (decal collectible), 128 "Sphinx" decals. Serial prefixes (AE, AF, AG, AH, AJ, AK, AL…) date the machine.
- **Montgomery Ward**: Model 30 / "Signature" / "Supreme Reversible" streamliners made by National Sewing Machine Co. (Belvidere, IL) or Japanese contractors; $25–65 (comps).
- **Kenmore (Sears)**: 158-series (Japan), 117-series (made by White) — workhorses $40–150, cam-based models higher.
- **National / Free / New Home / White / Eldredge**: US 1900s–1950s; mostly low.
- **Necchi (Italy), Pfaff (Germany), Bernina (Switzerland), Elna (Switzerland), Viking Husqvarna (Sweden)**: quality European; value depends on model.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
All-metal internals and gear drive = good; plastic gears (later Singers, 1970s+) = low. Original decals intact, bright chrome handwheel, original case with key, bobbins and accessory box raise value. Crinkle-paint brown/beige streamliners are mass-market.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Frozen mechanisms from old oil, cracked/brittle wiring (rubber cords, a fire hazard), dead motor brushes, worn decals, missing bobbin case or slide plates, rotten case hinges. "Runs" ≠ "sews" — ask for stitched sample.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Chinese "Featherweight" lookalikes exist mainly as toys/miniatures and decor replicas; check Singer serial plate on the bed. Singer reissued no Featherweights; Japanese clones (Brother, Pinnock, "Deluxe") copy Singer 15 forms.

## Market
Featherweights sell daily and fast to quilters. Common machines barely sell shipped (20–35 lb, $40+ shipping) — most sell local pickup.

## What sellers usually miss
A Featherweight in a closet labelled "old Singer"; the 222K free-arm; Singer 301 in tan. They over-value heavy cabinet treadles and brown streamliners.

## Shipping / handling
Featherweight in case ~13 lb boxed, $25–40. Full-size machines 25–40 lb, $40–70, frequent cracked castings in transit — double-box, remove from case.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Serial number and decade, stitch sample photo, note rewiring, accessories included, case condition.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ship to buyer − ship to Dylan − price ≥ max($50, price). Full-size machines fail on shipping alone. Featherweight 221 at ≤ $150 with seller video sewing: resale ~$400 × 0.87 = $348 − $35 − $35 − $150 = $128 → buy. No untested electronics: require proof it sews.
