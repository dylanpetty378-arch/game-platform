## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hand kitchen tools. Dating tells:
- **Bakelite/catalin handles** (1930s–50s): red, green, butterscotch, cream; slightly translucent edges, heavier; often with a painted "stripe" (Androck, A&J). Plastic 1960s+ handles are lighter, uniform colour, moulded seams.
- **Painted wood handles** (1920s–50s): red/green/blue/yellow paint with a cream band, often chipped.
- **Cast iron mechanisms** (1850–1920): apple parers (Reading Hardware, Goodell "White Mountain", Lockey & Howland), cherry stoners (Enterprise Mfg Philadelphia), Dover egg beaters ("Dover Stamping Co. Boston, Pat. May 31 1870") – raised patent dates in the casting.
- **Blister-carded gadgets** (1970s–2000s): printed card, barcode, "Made in China/Taiwan/Hong Kong" – the candy thermometer in pid 7970938827 is this (Robinson Knife Co. item 8570, 1990s–2000s, China).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Androck** (Washburn Co., Worcester MA, 1920s–60s) – Bakelite/wood handle utensils, sifters, toasters.
- **A&J** (Edward Katzinger Co. → Ekco, 1920s–50s) – beaters, spatulas, red/green handles.
- **Ekco** (Chicago, 1888–) – utensils, "Flint" stainless (Flint 1900 series 1950s, $10–30 each).
- **Foley** (Minneapolis, 1926–) – food mills, sifters, pastry blenders.
- **Edlund** (Burlington VT) – can openers, "Top Notch" beaters.
- **Taylor Instrument** (Rochester NY) – candy/deep-fry thermometers; mid-century Taylor in original box $10–25.
- **Robinson Knife Co.** (Buffalo NY; now Robinson Home Products) – 1970s–2000s carded gadgets, candy/meat thermometers ("Chef'n'Craft", "Robinson" brand) – common, $5–12.
- **Wear-Ever, Mirro** (aluminium cookie presses, jello molds), **Nutbrown** (UK), **Bromwell** (sifters, Cincinnati), **Hazel Atlas/Federal glass** reamers, **Rival/Dazey/Swing-A-Way** can openers.
- **Pampered Chef / Tupperware** (1980s–2000s) – $3–15.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Quality: heavy stamped steel, riveted (not crimped) handles, maker stamp on shank, original paint. Cheap: thin steel, plastic, glued handles, "Made in China". Old vs. new: patent dates, Bakelite (hot-pin test smells of formaldehyde – not possible from photos), cream-band painting, wear patina on wood.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust on blades/beater wheels, paint loss on wood handles, cracked Bakelite, missing gears/crank on mechanical pieces, mismatched replacement parts. Owners repaint handles – sold "as is" at lower value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Cast-iron apple parers and cherry pitters have been reproduced (lighter, rough casting, no patent dates). Retro "Bakelite-look" plastic utensils sold at craft stores (2000s). Modern "farmhouse" enamel/wood utensils marketed as "vintage".

## Market
Very high supply. Individual common gadgets sell slowly at $5–15 with $6–8 shipping – margins vanish. Sets and mechanical cast iron sell to collectors and farmhouse decorators steadily.

## What sellers usually miss
Patent-dated cast iron mechanisms in "old kitchen junk" boxes; complete boxed Bakelite sets; early Taylor or Tel-Tru thermometers with figural dials. Sellers over-call modern carded items "vintage".

## Shipping / handling
Small items in poly/padded mailer $5–8; cast iron 3–8 lb $12–20. Glass thermometers need bubble wrap.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker + item + handle material/colour (red Bakelite, green wood) + "set of N" + era + patent date; mechanical: "Dover egg beater", "Reading 78 apple parer", "Enterprise cherry stoner". Junk: "kitchen junk lot", "retro", "farmhouse" on modern carded. Example: `Vintage Androck Red Bakelite Handle Kitchen Utensil Set of 6 1940s Matched`.
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for sets and mechanical cast iron (collectors and farmhouse decorators); Etsy at the same price for decorative sets; single common gadgets in a FB local lot, since $6–8 shipping eats them.
- **Price position:** list at the sold median (single Bakelite/wood-handle $5–20, matched sets $25–80, Dover/Enterprise/Reading mechanical $30–150, patent-era parers $100+); accept −15%, floor −25%; OBO; cut 10% after 30 days.
- **Photos:** 1) set laid out in a row or the mechanism in profile; then handle close-ups, maker stamp and patent date, mechanism gears/blades, box if present, chips and rust, ruler; video of the beater or parer turning.
- **State:** maker, era, material (Bakelite tested or "appears to be"), mechanism works, measurements, count and matching, maker stamps and patent dates by photo number, chips/rust/missing parts.
- **Pack / haul:** small items in a padded mailer, $5–8; cast iron 3–8 lb, mechanism wrapped so the crank cannot bend, single box, $12–20; glass thermometers bubble-wrapped.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "A matched [Androck red Bakelite set of 6 / Reading 78 parer with patent date], stamps in photo [3] — the carded 1990s gadgets are the $5 ones; complete [sets / working parers] sell for [$X]."

## Buy rule
With typical resale $5–20, the formula (resale × 0.87 − ~$8 out − ~$8 in − price ≥ max($50, price)) fails for single gadgets – pass. Buy only matched sets or patent cast iron where conservative resale ≥ $90 and price ≤ $25. Always passenger-seat sized.
