## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- Wood case with dividers for 24 bottles (or 12 big bottles), metal-banded corners, stencilled brand. About 18 × 12 × 4–5 in. Plastic crates and carriers are 1970s+. Date stamps (e.g., "1968") on end boards; bottler city stencils.
- **Six-pack carriers:** smaller open carriers with a centre handle: wood (planks or bent wood), cardboard (1930s, Art Deco styling), metal/aluminium (1940s–50s, often embossed "Six Bottles 25¢") and wire with wood grips (Collectors Weekly).
- **Where to look for marks:** the long sides carry the brand script; the **end boards** carry bottler name/city ("Coca-Cola Bottling Co. — [city, state]"), capacity, and often a year stamp; **interior bottoms** of Pepsi crates often carry a plant code plus year; route marks ("Rte 5", "Truck 12") occur (Appraisily).
- Sellers' title words: "Coke crate", "soda crate", "bottle case", "24 slot", "wood carrier", "six pack carrier", "with bottles".
- Look-alikes: fruit/produce crates (no dividers), milk crates (wire/plastic, dairy names), modern decor "Coca-Cola" boxes sold in craft stores.

## Makers, brands and variants
- Coca-Cola (yellow/red), Pepsi (blue), Dr Pepper, 7-Up (green), RC Cola; carrier makers (Ohio Box, etc.).
- **Coca-Cola wooden cases:** yellow cases with red lettering are generally the earlier style; red cases with white lettering became common from the 1960s on (eathealthy365; verify exact years). Finger/box-jointed corners point to pre-1960s.
- **Pepsi-Cola logo eras** (Appraisily): 1930s–early 1940s double-dot "Pepsi:Cola" script with long tails, red or black stencil on natural wood, often serif bottler names; late 1940s–mid 1950s heavier red script with navy outline, "Bigger Better" slogans; post-1958 blockier "Pepsi" with wave/bottle-cap medallion; a red-white-blue medallion on a masonite end panel suggests late 1960s or a replacement panel.
- **Coke six-pack carriers:** 1930s cardboard (Art Deco), wood plank/bent-wood carriers, 1940s–50s aluminium/steel carriers stamped "Six Bottles 25¢" or similar, wire carriers with wood grip (Collectors Weekly; plusmodels.com listing of a 1950s aluminium carrier).
- **Regional and small-brand crates** (Nehi, Orange Crush, Canada Dry, Grapette, Moxie, NuGrape, small-town bottlers) carry the scarcity premium; the bottler's town stencil is the hook for local buyers (general knowledge, verify).

### Quick ID checklist
- Corner joints: finger/box joints (older) vs plain butt joints with nails/staples (later or repro).
- End boards: bottler name and city, capacity, any year stamp; zoom the photo.
- Logo style: Pepsi double-dot script = 1930s–40s; "Pepsi" in blocky type with cap = post-1958.
- Coke colour: yellow with red lettering (earlier) vs red with white lettering (1960s+).
- Fasteners: cut nails / slotted screws (older) vs staples, Phillips screws, bright nails (later or repro).
- Dividers: all present and original? Missing dividers roughly halve the buyer pool (Appraisily).
- Carrier: metal with "Six Bottles 25¢"-type embossing, or wood with bottler stencil?
- Plastic = 1970s+ and near-worthless.

### Golden-egg tells
- **Pre-war double-dot Pepsi:Cola crate with bottler name** — ~2–5× a 1960s Pepsi crate ($45–120+ vs $20–40) — double-dot script with long tails, red/black stencil on bare wood, finger joints.
- **Dealer-marked / near-mint regional Pepsi crate** — $125–220+, outliers ~$300 (Appraisily) — crisp complete stencils, all dividers, original straps.
- **Early Coca-Cola wooden six-pack carrier with bottler city** — ~$50–200 vs $25–60 for a 24-bottle case — small open carrier with centre handle, city stencil on the end.
- **1940s–50s metal Coke carrier stamped with a price ("Six Bottles 25¢")** — ~2–3× a common wood case (verify) — embossed price/slogan on the aluminium/steel side.
- **Small-brand/regional bottler crate (Nehi, Grapette, Moxie, local town bottlers)** — ~$40–150 vs $25–60 — unfamiliar brand name or small-town stencil on ends.
- **Yellow Coca-Cola case with finger joints and town stencil** — upper end of $25–60 and above for decor buyers — yellow paint, red script, box-jointed corners.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Real crates show wear, nail rust, metal edging; reproductions have uniform fresh stencils and staples.
- Genuine lettering is painted or screen-printed on the wood and fades/chips unevenly; vinyl-sheen, sticker-like or laser-etched/burned logos are repro tells (eathealthy365).
- Construction dating (Appraisily): finger-jointed or dovetailed corners with cut nails = pre-war small-bottler work; stapled metal straps across the top by the mid-1950s; staples alone point post-1960.
- Carved hand-hold slots are original; plastic/vinyl handles are later additions; metal swing handles came from vendor coolers.
- Wear: real crates show darkened, smoothed hand-hold edges, slight warping, varied dings; fake distressing is repetitive. Thin pine or plywood and a fresh-lumber or chemical smell = new.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Missing dividers, split boards, paint wear (accepted).
- Modern screws or bright nails cut value ~15–20%; glossy polyurethane or sealants read inauthentic; over-cleaning that raises grain or blurs ink hurts (Appraisily).
- Masonite end panels may be replacements.
- Ask: are all dividers present, any replaced boards, has it been sealed or repainted.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Decor-store repro crates common.
- Repro tells: butt-jointed corners, staples/Phillips screws, shiny galvanised straps, bright uniform logos, laser-burned logos, light thin wood.
- Coca-Cola licensed modern "vintage style" crates and carriers exist (general knowledge, verify).

## Market
- Decor market; strong locally, shipping heavy.
- Buyers: farmhouse/man-cave decorators, Coke/Pepsi advertising collectors, bottle collectors who want a period display case.

## What sellers usually miss
- Small-town bottler names; early carriers.
- Year stamps on ends/bottoms and Pepsi double-dot script.

## Shipping / handling
18 × 12 × 4 in, 6–8 lb: $20–30.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- Brand, date stamps, bottler city, dividers complete.
- Photograph both ends, the interior bottom, corner joints and any stamps.

### What to tell the buyer
"Vintage [Pepsi-Cola / Coca-Cola] wooden 24-bottle delivery case, about [18 × 12 × 5] in. End boards stencilled [bottler name, city]; [year stamp if present]. [Finger-jointed corners / metal corner straps]; all dividers present. Original paint with honest delivery wear; not repainted or sealed. Bottles [included / not included]." Add the logo era where it applies (e.g. "double-dot Pepsi:Cola script, used c. 1930s–40s").
