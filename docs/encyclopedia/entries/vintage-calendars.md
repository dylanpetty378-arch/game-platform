## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A printed calendar that a business (feed store, grocer, gas station, bank, funeral home, insurer) bought from a calendar company and imprinted with its name for customers. A stock artwork "top" (landscape, kids, dogs, hunting, pin-up girl) sits over a tear-off monthly pad. The artwork and the age are what sell. The local business name adds only regional-history interest.

How to date it from a photo:
- **Pre-1920:** chromolithography (rich, grainless colour), embossed die-cut cardboard, a ribbon hanger, often a small pad, ornate Victorian or Art Nouveau framing. The year is printed on the pad.
- **1920s–40s:** halftone colour printing, oil-painting prints by named calendar artists (Maxfield Parrish, R. Atkinson Fox, Hy Hintermeister, Philip R. Goodwin, Frances Hook) with the artist's name in the margin.
- **1950s–60s:** photo-litho landscapes and children, the store's name and phone exchange ("Phone 40", "TE 8-") in a block under the picture, and a stapled tear pad. Calendar-maker credit in tiny print: Brown & Bigelow, Shaw-Barton (Coshocton OH), Osborne, Thos. D. Murphy (Red Oak IA), Kemper-Thomas, Gerlach-Barklow (Joliet IL).
- **1960s–80s:** spiral or wire-bound wall calendars (gas stations, Hallmark), a glossy coated stock.
- **Reprints:** calendars from years with matching day patterns get reissued. 1915 artwork was reprinted for 1971 (1915 and 1971 share the same weekdays), and those reprints were sold as "nostalgia" gifts or imprinted by realtors. A "1915" calendar that says the year twice, or carries a poem about the old year coming back, is a reprint.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Brown & Bigelow** (St Paul, MN, 1896–): the largest US advertising-calendar publisher. It published Gil Elvgren and Rolf Armstrong pin-ups, Norman Rockwell Boy Scout calendars, and C.M. Coolidge dogs-playing-poker. The name is in the bottom margin.
- **Thomas D. Murphy Co.** (Red Oak, Iowa, 1889–): art calendars and the Red Oak calendar-printing industry. Also **Osborne Co.** (Clifton NJ) and **Kemper-Thomas** (Cincinnati).
- **Shaw-Barton** (Coshocton, Ohio), **Gerlach-Barklow** (Joliet IL): pin-ups and landscapes.
- **Louis F. Dow** (St Paul): Earl Moran and Zoë Mozert pin-ups.
- **Hallmark / Coutts (Canada):** 1960s–70s greeting-card-company art calendars. Low value.
- **Company-published:** Coca-Cola (pre-1940s calendars with "Coca-Cola girls" bring $300–5,000), Edison-Mazda / General Electric (Maxfield Parrish 1918–1934), Hood's Sarsaparilla (1890s die-cuts), Winchester and Peters Cartridge, DuPont powder, Sunoco/Mobil/Texaco station calendars (1950s–70s station calendars are common).
- **Pin-up artists:** Gil Elvgren, Alberto Vargas (Esquire 1940s), George Petty, Rolf Armstrong, Earl Moran, Art Frahm, Zoë Mozert. Also the 1950s Marilyn Monroe "Golden Dreams" nude calendar, which is often reproduced.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Lithographed or embossed heavy card, gilt edging, die-cut shapes and a silk ribbon mark the quality, pre-1920 pieces.
- An artist's signature or a credit line ("From a painting by…") adds value. A photo landscape adds little.
- A complete pad matters most on pre-1940 calendars. On 1950s+ local calendars the pad is often trimmed or missing, and that matters less because the value is low anyway.
- Check the size. Wall-sized tops (16x33") are harder to sell and ship than 8x12".

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Water stains (tide-lines), foxing, tack holes, torn hangers, trimmed margins and fading from sun.
- Missing months, pages crossed off or written on (the 1959 Austin Grocery calendar has marker notes on January), tape repairs.
- Mould and mildew in basement or attic finds. Smell is a deal-breaker for buyers.
- Trimming or framing that hides the year or the advertiser lowers the value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- **Matching-year reissues** (1915/1971, 1931/1987, 1959/2015, and so on). They are sold sealed with poems about the year returning, like the Sybil "1915" reprint.
- Coca-Cola, pin-up (Elvgren, Monroe) and Parrish calendars have been heavily reproduced as prints. A repro shows a modern dot screen, bright white paper and no age toning.
- Calendar "tops" cut down and framed are often sold as original art prints. Fine, but they're worth less than a complete calendar.

## Market
This is a big, slow market. Most local 1950s–80s calendars sit unsold or go for under $20. Demand concentrates on pin-ups, petroliana, Coca-Cola, sporting and firearms, and pre-1920 chromolithography. Q4 and January see a slight bump (gift buyers and "your birth-year calendar" buyers, who look for matching-year calendars).

## What sellers usually miss
- **Artist credits.** A small "Elvgren", "Rolf Armstrong", "Philip R. Goodwin" or "Maxfield Parrish" in the margin of a calendar listed as "old calendar" is the find.
- **Advertisers with collector bases:** ammunition, seed corn, Coca-Cola, tractors and dairy, beyond the store name.
- **Matching-year demand.** Sellers don't know that some buyers want any calendar whose days match a current or upcoming year. This is low value, though, never the arbitrage.
- The reverse: sellers think any 1950s–70s calendar is valuable because it's old. It isn't.

## Shipping / handling
Flat, light (under 1 lb), but large. Ship flat between corrugated boards in a rigid mailer or a flat box, $8–15. Rolling causes cracking on old card. Wall calendars over 20" need oversize boxes ($15–20).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- State the year, the advertiser and the town exactly as printed, the calendar company and the artist credit.
- Say whether the pad is complete, which months are missing, and whether there are writing, tack holes, stains or odour.
- If it's a reprint (for example "1915 artwork reissued for 1971"), say so in the title.
- Photograph the full calendar, a close-up of the art, the artist's signature, the maker credit, and every flaw.

## Buy rule
Buy only when the photo shows a pre-1930 lithographed or embossed calendar, a named pin-up artist (Elvgren, Vargas, Petty, Armstrong, Moran, Mozert), Parrish/Mazda, Coca-Cola, or a firearms or seed-corn advertiser, the image is clean, and the price is ≤ 25% of the estimated sold value. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$10) − shipping to Dylan (~$10) − price, and it must be ≥ max($50, price). In practice the category only works on calendars that resell for $100 or more, bought for $25 or less. Pass on 1950s+ local-store, gas-station, Hallmark and reprint calendars at any asking price. Beware Coca-Cola, Monroe and Parrish reproductions; they need print-method verification, so if the photos can't show it, pass.
