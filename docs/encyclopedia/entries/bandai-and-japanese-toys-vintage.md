## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Chogokin** ("super alloy") die-cast robots, 1974 onward: Popy GA-series (GA-01 Mazinger Z, 1974), Bandai later. Heavy die-cast metal torsos, plastic limbs, spring-launched fists/missiles. Markings: "POPY" or "BANDAI", "Made in Japan", ©Dynamic Planning / ©Toei, ©Sunrise.
- **Jumbo Machinder** (Popy, 1973–): ~24 in hollow blow-molded polyethylene robots with launching fists.
- **Shogun Warriors** (Mattel import line, 1976–1980): Popy chogokins and Jumbo Machinders sold in the US with Mattel boxes. 24 in figures: Great Mazinga, Raydeen, Gaiking, Dragun, Combatra, Daimos, Poseidon, Godzilla (19 in, with launching hand and rolling tongue), Rodan. Diecast: 3.5 in and 5 in versions plus Dragun/Combatra/Gaiking/Daimos etc. Marked "© Mattel Inc. 1976/77 Made in Japan".
- **Godaikin** (Bandai America, 1982–1984): imported Bandai DX chogokin (Voltron, Gordian, Daimos).
- **Post-war tin toys** (1946–1970s): lithographed tin friction cars, wind-ups and battery-operated robots/space toys. Marks: maker logo stamped on tin – Alps (ALPS), Bandai (B in a circle), Yonezawa (Y), Nomura (TN in diamond), Masudaya / Modern Toys (TM), Marusan (SAN), Horikawa (SH), Yoshiya (KO), Asakusa (ASC), Ichiko, Linemar (Marx Japan), Cragstan (importer). "Made in Occupied Japan" = 1945–1952.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Popy** (Bandai subsidiary, 1971–1983; merged back into Bandai). GA, GB, GC, GD series chogokin; Jumbo Machinders.
- **Bandai** – also 1950s–60s tin cars (big friction cars of US models are valuable), later DX chogokin, Godaikin.
- **Mattel** – Shogun Warriors US distribution; parts (fists, missiles, swords, spinners) often missing.
- **Tin makers** – Nomura and Masudaya robots, Horikawa Space Explorer series, Yonezawa cars, Alps battery animals (bartender, drinking bear – common).
- **Reissues**: Bandai **Soul of Chogokin (GX series, 1997–)** – modern premium reissues, marked "Bandai 1997+" and "Made in China"; **Shogun Warriors 2017 Toynami/Jumbo reissues**; Japanese **retro reissue tin robots** (Metal House, Osaka Tin Toy) – nice but not vintage.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Original chogokin: Japan-made, heavy zinc die-cast, period stickers, copyright dates 1970s, chrome parts that may have wear. Missing parts drive value: fists, missiles, weapons, stickers, box inserts. Jumbos: original fists/launchers, decals, no sun fading. Tin: bright litho, intact tabs, original key, rubber tires not melted. Battery-op: corrosion in the battery box is the killer.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing fists/missiles (most common), broken springs, chrome wear, faded/yellowed Jumbo plastic, cracked Jumbo shoulders, missing stickers. Tin: rust, dents, bent tabs, missing key, battery corrosion, dead motors. Many Shogun Warriors parts are reproduced (see below).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Repro **Shogun Warriors fists, missiles, swords and stickers** (3D-printed or recast) – common; they lower value if not disclosed. Tells: lighter plastic, slightly different color, rough mold lines.
- **Chinese tin robot reproductions** (1980s–2000s, "Ha Ha Toy" etc.) – thin litho, "Made in China"; worth $15–40.
- **Bootleg chogokin** (Hong Kong/Taiwan 1970s–80s) – collectible in their own right but lower value.
- Reissues as listed above – not vintage; worth their own (often decent) secondary market.

## Market
Strong, active collector market with Gen-X nostalgia; Japanese and US buyers. Complete Jumbos and chogokin sell in days to 2 weeks. Tin robots trade at auction (Morphy, Bertoia). Moderate-high volume.

## What sellers usually miss
"Big plastic robot" in a garage lot can be a 24 in Shogun Warrior ($100–300). Die-cast "old robot toy" listings often hide Popy chogokin – look for heavy chrome/metal torso, Japanese markings. Sellers often don't know accessories matter, and may have the fists/missiles in a separate bag – ask.

## Shipping / handling
Chogokin: small, heavy, $10–15. 24 in Jumbos: large but light; 26 × 14 × 10 box, $25–40 (dimensional weight). Tin: pad tabs and litho; $12–20. All fit a passenger seat (Jumbos just fit).

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Exact name, maker (Popy/Bandai/Mattel), year, made in Japan, completeness (list every accessory present and missing, note any reproduction parts), sticker condition, box. For tin: maker logo, power (friction/wind-up/battery), working or not, battery box condition.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ship (~$15–35) − ship to Dylan (~$15–35) − price ≥ max($50, price). A 24 in Jumbo at $200 median works up to ~$60 (shipping both ways ~$60 combined). Popy chogokin at $150 median works ≤ ~$50. Battery-operated tin = untested electronics → pass unless clearly sold as a non-working display piece at ≤ $20 with a $100+ display value. Reissues (GX, 2017 Jumbos) are fine buys only at modern-reissue comps. Insist on photos of markings (underside/back) and accessory list.
