## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
A chess set = 32 pieces (16 per side) plus optionally a board. Key value families:
- **Staunton pattern** (standard tournament design 1849): Jaques of London (stamped "Jaques London" under the king; red-stamped boxes), 1930s–60s Drueke (Grand Rapids, MI), Lardy (France), Soviet "Baku"/"Tal"/"Latvian" tournament sets (weighted, often with felt bases).
- **Soviet/Russian painted folk sets**: matryoshka-style turned pieces painted as warriors, e.g. Russian knights vs Tatar/Mongol ("Golden Horde") with crescent rooks, from Khokhloma/Semenov workshops or Uzbekistan; 1970s–90s.
- **Carved figural sets**: Indian sandalwood/rosewood, Chinese bone/"ivory" (illegal to sell ivory interstate in many cases), Anri/Italian, Mexican onyx/marble, Philippine wood.
- **Themed resin sets**: Franklin Mint, Danbury Mint, Civil War, Lord of the Rings, Studio Anne Carlton — mostly low value.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Jaques of London** (1795–): highest value; king stamped "JAQUES LONDON", crown stamp on knights; boxwood/ebony.
- **Drueke** (USA, 1914–1990s): walnut/maple sets and folding boards, often branded.
- **Soviet**: "Made in USSR" stamps or paper labels; Tournament/Grandmaster weighted sets (1950s–80s) with distinctive knight carving; painted folk sets with paper labels in Cyrillic and price stamps ("Цена").
- **Franklin Mint / Danbury Mint**: pewter/porcelain themed sets $40–150 complete with board.
- **Anri** (Italy): carved/painted figures, $100–300.
- **Mexican onyx**: $15–40, heavy.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Boxwood/ebony (Jaques), weighted lead bases with felt (quality), hand-carved knights (best indicator of quality), hand-painted fine detail vs stencilled; Soviet painted sets: lacquered tempera, signatures/labels on bases. Cheap: thin lacquer, glued plastic felt, uniform machine carving, Indian "Kashmir" painted.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Missing pieces (pawns most often) kill value — demand a count; chips in paint, broken crowns/crosses, cracked bases, warped boards. Replacement pieces unacceptable to collectors.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Indian factories make "Jaques-style" and "Soviet replica" weighted sets; modern Russian souvenir painted sets (post-1991) are common and worth less than Soviet-era ones. Fake Jaques stamps exist.

## Market
Strong steady chess interest (post-2020 boom). Soviet tournament sets sell fast; painted folk sets moderate (weeks).

## What sellers usually miss
"Made in USSR" bases, Jaques stamps, weighted Soviet tournament knights; full set with original box.

## Shipping / handling
Shoebox to carry-on size, 2–6 lb; wrap pieces individually; board separately. $12–25.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Count and photograph all 32 pieces, king height, base diameter, labels/stamps, board size, damage.

## Buy rule
Profit = median × 0.87 − ~$18 − ~$18 − price ≥ max($50, price). Soviet painted sets (median ~$120) only qualify at ≤ $25; tournament weighted sets (~$200) at ≤ $60. Need a confirmed complete count before buying. Fits a car seat.
