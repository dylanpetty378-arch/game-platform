## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glass with decoration cut and polished by a wheel into a thick (usually lead) blank. "Brilliant" period (c. 1876–1917): deep miter cuts, hobstars (multi-point stars with a cut centre), fans, strawberry diamond, cane, notched prisms; heavy, ring like a bell when flicked. Pressed imitation: pattern formed in a mould — rounded, soft edges; mould seams; star bases in the mould; light weight.
pid 7975219263: ~5–6 in. clear round ashtray with jagged scalloped/saw-tooth rim, all-over "diamond point"/faceted pyramid pattern with rounded edges and a moulded starburst base; three rest notches — pressed glass (1960s–80s), possibly imported lead crystal, not hand cut.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Cut: sharp crisp edges you can feel, polished (glossy) cuts, slight irregularities in symmetry, no mould seams, heavy, lead crystal rings. Pressed: rounded "pillowy" edges, satin/dull inside facets, seams, uniform. Signatures are faint acid-etched — tilt in light.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Chips and flakes on hobstar points (most common), ground rims, cracks (bell test dull), sickness/cloudiness, haze inside vases. Repairs by grinding reduce value.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Forged acid signatures (Hawkes, Libbey) on unsigned or later glass; modern Asian/European cut pieces in ABP patterns; pressed glass described as "cut crystal" everywhere.

## Market
Pressed glass: oversupplied, slow. ABP: collector market depressed since 2000s (prices down 50–70% from peak) but signed quality sells steadily.

## What sellers usually miss
Acid signatures on ABP (Hawkes/Libbey); cut-to-clear coloured pieces; sterling-mounted cut glass (sterling rims add value). Sellers usually OVER-call pressed glass "cut crystal".

## Shipping / handling
Heavy and fragile: double box, $10–20 for small pieces.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State cut vs. pressed honestly, dimensions, weight, signature photo, chips under magnification, ring test.
