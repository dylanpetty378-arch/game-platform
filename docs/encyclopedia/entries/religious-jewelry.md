## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pendants, chains, rings, rosaries and medals with Christian or Jewish symbols.
- **Jerusalem (Crusader) cross**: a large cross potent with four small Greek crosses in the angles. Israeli souvenir versions (1948–1980s) in silver filigree/cannetille wirework with beaded borders and a coloured glass or synthetic stone centre (red "ruby", blue). Stamps: "Sterling", "925", "Israel", "Made in Israel", Hebrew characters, or none.
- **Rosaries**: sterling links (Creed, Chapel, HMH, Bliss), crystal or semi-precious beads; 14K gold rosaries rare.
- **Medals**: Miraculous Medal, St. Christopher, St. Benedict; sterling (stamped) vs. base metal (common).
- **Designer**: James Avery (marked "JA" in a shield / "James Avery 925"), Tiffany (Elsa Peretti cross, "Tiffany & Co 925"), David Yurman crosses (auth needed), Taxco Mexico sterling.

### Golden-egg tells
- Bezalel (Jerusalem, c. 1908–1929) filigree pendants/brooches — ~10–20× ($200–600+ at Judaica auction) vs the $20 median souvenir Jerusalem cross — stamp reads "Bezalel" + "Jerusalem", sometimes "935" or "Made in Palestine"; Ze'ev Raban biblical figures (Ruth) or Star of David set in beaded filigree, pre-state wording not "Israel" (https://www.kedem-auctions.com/en/content/five-bezalel-objects-%E2%80%93-jerusalem)
- Georg Jensen crosses and pendants (Torun no. 301, Mikkelsen no. 261A) — ~15–60× ($300–1,400) vs a plain sterling cross — impressed "925 S Denmark Georg Jensen" in a beaded oval plus a model number on the back (https://www.wright20.com/auctions/2021/06/scandinavian-design/117)
- James Avery retired designs (Jerusalem cross, Mission cross with enamel, floral crosses) — ~4–8× ($80–200) vs the $20 median — "JA" in a candlestick/shield mark with "925"; retired pattern names searched by buyers; weight 6–7 g for the Jerusalem cross (https://www.ebay.de/itm/286654161389)
- Creed sterling rosaries with crystal or semi-precious beads — ~3–5× ($40–120) vs base-metal/plastic rosaries — "Creed" maker's mark plus "Sterling" on the back of the centre medal and crucifix; sterling-link chain, not plated wire (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/more-jewelry-watches/more-jewelry/vintage-creed-rosary-sterling-silver-crystal-beads-religious-faith-catholic/id-j_13624712)
- "Made in Palestine" / pre-1948 Jerusalem silver and mother-of-pearl crosses — ~3–5× ($60–150) vs 1960s–80s "Israel"-stamped souvenirs — the word "Palestine" or an Old City shop name engraved on the back, 800/900 silver, carved mother-of-pearl inlay (entry comp)
- Stamped 14K/18K crosses sold as "gold tone" — ~5–10× (melt $100–400+) vs a plated cross — "585", "750", "14K" or "18K" on the bail; warm, even colour with no brassy wear at the edges; weigh in grams (entry comp)
- Byzantine/Russian enamel crosses with Cyrillic or Greek inscriptions — ~25× ($500–5,000) vs a sterling cross — champlevé enamel in blue/white cells, 84 zolotnik or Greek silver marks; authentication-tier, pass unless cheap (entry comp)

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Sterling tarnishes grey-black evenly; base metal shows green/brown corrosion, yellow brass where plating wore through. Hand filigree: twisted wire scrolls soldered inside a frame, slight irregularity; cast "filigree look" has blobby, flat-backed shapes. Stones: glass (bubbles, rounded facet edges), synthetic ruby (crisp, uniform). Weight: sterling cross 1.5 in. ~6–10 g.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Crushed or separated filigree wires, missing beads, lost or chipped stones, broken bails, solder repairs (lumpy silver solder on back), over-polishing. Rosaries: broken links, missing Our Father beads.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Modern Chinese/Indian cast copies of Israeli filigree crosses sold online for $10–20, often stamped "925" falsely. James Avery and Tiffany fakes common. Test: acid/XRF needed for certainty — magnet test only rules out steel.
