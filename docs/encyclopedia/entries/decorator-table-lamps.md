Buy rule (short): Only buy lamps whose maker is provable from a label/marks or unmistakable design (Chapman, Frederick Cooper, Stiffel, Murano, Fenton) and whose sold median clears profit after ~$25–35 shipping each way; skip resin, faux stone, fillable glass and anything needing rewiring.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
- **Resin/composite import lamps (1990s–2010s):** urn and "antique bronze" shapes, faux alabaster, faux stacked stone, beaded shades, scroll handles. Light, hollow, with moulded "carving" that repeats exactly; brands Uttermost, Coaster, Stylecraft, Hampton Bay, Pottery Barn copies. Near zero resale.
- **Stiffel (Chicago, 1932–2000s):** heavy solid brass column/urn lamps, often with a cast trophy-urn or torch shape, 3-way sockets and a pull-through "Stiffel" socket shell or foil SLC label. Heavy is the tell (a hollow-light lamp is not Stiffel – 1stDibs).
- **Frederick Cooper (Chicago, 1923–2000s):** high-end brass, porcelain and plaster lamps (ginger jars, tobacco-leaf, Asian, neoclassical). Look for "FC"/"F" on the base, switch or socket (1stDibs) and a heavy, finely cast or hand-painted body.
- **Chapman (Long Island, 1960s–1990s):** cast brass Hollywood Regency, bamboo, candlestick and bouillotte lamps; usually dated and marked on the base or socket ("Chapman 1972").
- **Aladdin (Mantle Lamp Co.) electric lamps (1930s–50s):** Alacite (ivory opaque glass) and pressed-glass figural lamps; marked Aladdin on the socket.
- **Hand-painted glass and milk glass:** 1940s–60s boudoir and "parlor/hurricane" lamps; Fenton (painted and hobnail) and Czech/Bohemian cased glass are the better ones.
- **Murano/Venetian glass (1960s–70s):** mushroom lamps, teardrop/"root beer" bodies; many modern copies.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- **Weight and material:** lift test is impossible from a photo, but look for: cast brass bases with crisp edges and screw-assembled parts (good) vs seamless moulded resin with painted "bronze" (bad). Faux alabaster with orange swirls and "carved" resin acanthus feet = 2000s import.
- **Sockets and switches:** vintage brass sockets with maker stamps (Leviton, Bryant, Stiffel, Aladdin) vs modern plastic sockets and in-line or touch switches.
- **Cords:** cloth or old rubber cords = old (and may need rewiring); clear plastic cords with polarised plugs = post-1980s.
- **Glass:** cut or pressed crystal columns should have sharp, polished facets; moulded glass has rounded edges and seams.
- **Shades:** original silk/linen shades add little; stained or torn shades are thrown out. Don't pay for shades.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Corroded metal (green/rust bloom, as in our first example), bent harps, missing finials, cracked glass bodies, chipped ceramic, worn gilt.
- Wiring: brittle cords, loose sockets – a lamp that needs rewiring falls under "no repairs" for Dylan.
- Shades: stains, splits, yellowing.
- Pairs: a single from a pair loses ~40% per lamp; matched pairs sell best.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
- Tiffany-style leaded lamps (1970s–today) – not Tiffany; sold $40–145. Real Tiffany/Handel/Pairpoint need professional authentication (outside Dylan's rules).
- "Murano-style" mushroom lamps from Amazon/Temu are listed as vintage.
- Frederick Cooper and Chapman names get attached to unmarked brass lamps in titles ("Chapman?"); without marks, value drops to generic ($30–125).

## Market
- Lamps are a slow, shipping-heavy category on eBay; most generic lamps sell locally (Facebook Marketplace) for $20–60. Designer lamps sell nationally within weeks.
- Pairs sell faster and for more than 2× a single at the designer tier.

## What sellers usually miss
- Chapman and Frederick Cooper marks on the base or socket of a lamp described only as "brass lamp" or "Asian lamp".
- Stiffel pull-through socket shells.
- Aladdin electric glass lamps (often called "milk glass lamp").
- Real Murano labels/foil stickers and the weight and depth of hand-blown cased glass.
- Conversely: sellers price resin "antique" urn lamps from Lens matches at $100–1,000; these are near-worthless.

## Shipping / handling
- Remove shade and harp; ship body double-boxed with 3" of padding; table lamps 24–32" ship as oversized boxes $25–45 Ground Advantage/UPS. Shades ship separately or not at all.
- A 30" lamp lying down fits a passenger seat; pairs and floor lamps usually don't fit well.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
- **Title words:** maker (Stiffel / Frederick Cooper / Chapman / Marbro / Murano) + "table lamp" + style (brass bouillotte / tobacco-leaf ginger jar / cased glass) + height + "pair" if pair + "Hollywood Regency"/"MCM". Junk: "beautiful", "antique" (for resin), "vintage lamp" alone. `Chapman 1972 Solid Brass Bouillotte Table Lamp 28in Hollywood Regency Signed`
- **Where:** eBay BIN with offers for designer lamps (pairs sell for more than 2× a single); FB Marketplace local for generic and resin lamps ($20–60, no shipping risk).
- **Price position:** at the sold median for the maker (Stiffel ~$100; Chapman ~$180; FC ~$225), accept −10%, floor 80%; OBO; cut 15% after 45 days. Generic local at $20–60 firm.
- **Photos:** lamp lit without shade on plain background; maker mark/label and socket shell; base underside; body close up for chips/dents; switch and cord; shade only if original; next to a tape.
- **State:** height to socket and to finial, base footprint, material, maker marks as photographed, socket type (3-way), "tested working, original cord" or rewired, shade included or not, flaws by photo number.
- **Pack / haul:** passenger-seat (30" lying down fits); remove shade, harp and finial, double-box the body with 3" padding, $25–45 oversize; shades separate or not at all.
- **Quote to a buyer:** "[Maker] [style] lamp, [dated/marked] on the socket, solid cast brass with the original finish, [height]; the same model lists at [$] on Chairish and generic regency brass goes for [$]."

## Buy rule
- Price ≤ $200; fits a passenger seat. Profit = sold median × 0.87 − shipping to buyer (~$30) − shipping to Dylan (~$30, or $0 local) − price ≥ max($50, price).
- Shipped both ways, a lamp needs a sold median ≥ ~$185 at a $20 price – only Frederick Cooper/Chapman/real Murano-class lamps.
- Local pickup lowers the bar to ~$120 median at $20.
- No rewiring, no corroded/needs-restoration lamps, no untested lamps; ask the seller to confirm it lights and to photograph the base/socket marks.
- Tiffany/Handel/Pairpoint-type claims need professional authentication – pass.
