## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Home-movie cameras from about 1932 (Regular/Double 8) and 1965 onward (Super 8 cartridge). Photo tells:
- **Regular 8 / Double 8 (1932–1960s)**: boxy metal body, wind-up key or crank on the side, no battery door, turret of 1–3 lenses or a single lens. Kodak models carry a red Kodak badge, a "Cine-Kodak" lens and an exposure-guide plate on the side (Brownie, Reliant, Magazine 8, Royal, Medallion). Bell & Howell (Sportster, 134, 220/252 "Electric Eye", Filmo), Keystone (K-series, brown/grey crinkle paint), Revere (88/Eight, turret models), Wollensak.
- **Super 8 (1965–1980s)**: pistol grip with batteries in the handle, plastic body, square cartridge door, zoom lens. Cheap ones: Kodak Instamatic M-series/XL, Bell & Howell 1970s, Sears, GAF, Keystone, Chinon, Sankyo, Minolta Autopak, Yashica. Better: Canon 310XL, 514XL, 814/1014 Electronic and XL-S; Nizo (Braun) S-series silver/black slab; Beaulieu 4008/5008/6008 (long rectangular, Schneider/Angenieux zoom); Nikon R8/R10; Leicina Special; Bolex 480/680 Macro-Zoom; Elmo Super 110/1012S.
- **Junk to recognise instantly**: film reels and reel cans (e.g. a "SAV-ON" drugstore-branded aluminium reel can lid), splicers, viewers, light bars, plain projectors.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Eastman Kodak (Rochester, NY)**: Cine-Kodak Eight models 20/25/60 (1932+), Magazine 8 (1940–55), Reliant (1949–55, roll-load, Cine-Ektar 13mm f/1.9 lens), Brownie Movie Camera (1951–60s, mass-market), Medallion, Zoom 8, Instamatic M2–M30 (Super 8), XL-series (1971–). Nearly all are $8–30.
- **Bell & Howell (Chicago)**: Filmo/Sportster 1930s–50s, Two-Twenty/Two-Fifty-Two "Electric Eye" 1950s–60s, Zoomatic, 1970s Super 8 Autoload. Low value.
- **Keystone, Revere, Wollensak, Argus, DeJur, Sekonic, Yashica**: low.
- **Bolex (Paillard, Switzerland)**: H8 Reflex (Regular 8) and Bolex B8/C8/D8L; H8 and the 16mm H16 are the money pieces ($150–600); Bolex 8mm "B8/C8" $40–100 (general knowledge, verify).
- **Canon**: 310XL (low-light), 514XL, 814/1014 Electronic and XL-S. Tested ones sell $250–900 because indie filmmakers shoot Super 8 again; comps.jsonl shows Canon 310XL $554.99 (Sep 2026) and 814XL Electronic $551.99 (Apr 2026).
- **Nizo (Niezoldi & Krämer / Braun, Germany)**: S560, S800, 6080, Professional: $200–700 tested (general knowledge, verify).
- **Beaulieu (France)**: 4008ZM2/ZM3, 6008 Pro, 7008: $400–1,500 working (general knowledge, verify).
- **Nikon R8/R10, Leicina Special, Logmar**: $400–2,000+.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
- Pre-war and 1940s–50s: die-cast metal, crinkle paint, wind-up; heavy for size but cheap to buy new then.
- Pro Super 8: metal body, long zoom (6–80mm), "XL" low-light, interchangeable lens mounts (Beaulieu C-mount), sound-recording models are heavier.
- Lens glass condition matters more than body wear; fungus and haze cut value in half.

## Condition, damage and repairs
- Wind-up springs often still run; electronics (Super 8) frequently have corroded battery contacts, dead light meters and broken zoom motors. Untested = parts value.
- Fungus/haze in zoom lenses; cracked eyecups; missing cartridge doors.
- Sellers rarely can test without film; a camera that runs with batteries and has a live meter needle is the minimum.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
None to worry about. New-production Super 8 (Kodak Super 8 camera 2023, Logmar) are modern and expensive, never on Craigslist for cheap.

## Market
eBay is flooded with Kodak/Bell & Howell wind-ups that sell for $10–25 or don't sell. The live market is filmmakers buying tested Canon/Nizo/Beaulieu Super 8 — fast sellers (days) at $250+. Accessory parts (eyecups) also sell steadily.

## What sellers usually miss
- They over-price common Kodak/Bell & Howell wind-ups as "antique".
- They under-price pro Super 8 because it looks like "old camcorder junk": a Canon "XL" badge, "Nizo" or "Beaulieu" name plate, a long black zoom lens with a C-mount, or "Macro" engraved on a Bolex barrel are the tells.
- Original film cartridges with exposed home movies sometimes sell to collectors (tiny niche).

## Shipping / handling
Shoebox size, 1–4 lb; $10–15 to ship padded. Remove batteries. Pro cameras need lens caps and double boxing.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
State format (Regular 8 vs Super 8), lens and focal range, whether it runs (wind-up runs / motor runs on batteries / meter works), lens clarity with a flashlight photo, and that it has not been tested with film unless it has.

## Buy rule
Profit = sold median × 0.87 − ~$12 to buyer − ~$12 to Dylan − price; pass unless ≥ max($50, price).
- Common wind-ups ($17 median): net $15 − $24 shipping = loss at any price. **Pass.**
- Pro Super 8 (Canon XL, Nizo, Beaulieu, Nikon) median ~$300: buy at ≤ $80 only if seller video shows the motor and meter working (rule: no untested electronics). At $80: 261 − 24 − 80 = $157 profit (Tier A).
- Wind-up Bolex H8 Reflex at ≤ $60 is acceptable (mechanical; test by winding).
