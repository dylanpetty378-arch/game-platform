## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Sphere with printed map gores on papier-mâché, cardboard, tin, or plastic, on a stand with a half or full meridian. Dating clues: country names (USSR 1922–91; East/West Germany 1949–90; "Persia" until 1935; "Siam" until 1939; "Belgian Congo" until 1960; "Zaire" 1971–97), ocean colour (blue oceans more common 1950s–80s; antique parchment/"tea" colour used both antique and modern "antique-look"), metal meridians with degree scales.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Old globes: papier-mâché/plaster sphere with hand-laid paper gores (visible seams, varnish yellowed and crazed), cast iron or brass bases. Modern: one-piece plastic or cardboard spheres, printed in two halves joined at the equator (equator seam band is a modern tell), lighter bases. Raised relief = textured plastic.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Dents and cracks in sphere, gore lifting, water stains, missing meridian/axis caps, faded or scuffed varnish, wobbly stands. Illuminated: wiring (no untested electronics rule — must light).

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
"Antique-look" modern globes (Replogle Heirloom, imports) with parchment ocean and old-style typography look antique but contain modern borders. Reproduction 18th c. globes (Smith's, Cary) sold as decor.
