# Telescopes (Edmund Astroscan, Celestron, Meade, Questar)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay/Cloudy Nights classifieds sold knowledge 2024–25 (Astroscan 2001 $150–250 complete; Celestron C8 orange tube $300–500; Questar 3.5 $1,500–3,500); craigslist listing body for 2889.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Edmund Astroscan: red plastic bowling-ball-shaped 4¼" reflector sitting in a three-point base, 1976–2010s – complete with base, RKE eyepiece, peep sight, strap and cap. Celestron C5/C8 orange-tube Schmidt-Cassegrains (1970s–80s, orange tube on a fork mount) $250–500. Meade/Celestron black department-store refractors on tripods: $20–60. Questar 3.5": small chrome/black Maksutov on a built-in fork with a leather case – $1,500+. Sellers write "telescope", often with brand.

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Tasco/Bushnell/Meade 60mm refractors, Celestron PowerSeeker | $20–60 | |
| Decent | Astroscan complete, Meade ETX-90, Celestron NexStar 4SE | $150–250 | |
| Collectible | Celestron C8 orange tube, Criterion RV-6 Dynascope, Unitron refractors | $300–800 | heavy |
| Top | Questar 3.5/7, Takahashi, Astro-Physics refractors | $1,500–10,000 | |

## What sellers usually miss
Astroscans are usually named (the shape is unique). Vintage orange C8s and Unitron refractors get listed as "old telescope". Mirror coating condition matters – ask for a photo down the tube.

## Shipping / handling
Astroscan 12 lb with base, awkward – $35–45 to ship, double-box. C8s 40+ lb – local only.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Astroscan complete ≤ $70, Questar/Unitron/C8 at any price under $200. Pass when: department-store refractors, Astroscan over $100. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.

# Tin wind-up and battery-operated toys (Marx, Japan 1950s–60s, Schuco)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay sold/active listings (Marx Funny Drummer tiger, Happy Drummer bunny Taiwan NOS $80 ask), Antiques Roadshow Marx appraisals, general collector pricing

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Lithographed tin bodies, a wind-up key on the side or a battery box underneath. Makers printed on the body/base: MARX (US, "Louis Marx & Co", 1930s–70s), Chein, Unique Art, Wolverine (US); Japan 1950s–60s: Alps, Masudaya (TM), Nomura (TN), Yonezawa (Y), Horikawa (SH), Bandai, Linemar (Marx's Japan line); Germany: Schuco, Lehmann, Arnold; Taiwan/Hong Kong/China 1970s+ = low value. The plush-over-tin "drummer"/"bear" battery toys (Funny Drummer tiger, Happy Drummer, Musical Bear) are 1960s Japan/Taiwan and sell $60–150 working. Robots and space toys (Horikawa, Nomura, Masudaya) are the money. Sellers' words: "tin toy", "wind up toy", "old mechanical toy", "battery toy".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | 1970s+ Taiwan/China, reproductions (Schylling), rusty non-working | $10–40 | |
| Decent | Marx wind-ups common (Drummer Boy, Merrymakers single), Japan plush drummers working | $50–150 | Funny Drummer tiger working ~$80–150 |
| Collectible | Marx Merrymakers band, Unique Art Lil Abner band, Japan tin cars (Cadillac, Lincoln), Linemar characters | $200–600 | |
| Top | Masudaya "Gang of Five" robots, Horikawa/Nomura robots, Schuco rare, pre-war Marx boxed | $1,000–10,000+ | |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2026 eBay active – 1970s Taiwan "Happy Drummer Bunny" NOS – $80 ask (not sold)
- 2026 eBay sold (typical) – Marx/Japan Funny Drummer tiger working – $75–150; non-working $30–50
- Reference – Marx Merrymakers mouse band working – $300–600

## What sellers usually miss
Maker marks (TN/TM/SH/Y logos) and country; whether it works (wind-ups usually do; battery boxes corrode). Boxes double the price. Robots and character pieces (Popeye, Disney Linemar) are the big misses.

## Shipping / handling
1–3 lb, pack carefully; $12–20. eBay ~13%. Dylan's rule: no untested electronics – ask for a video for battery toys.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Japan/Marx/Schuco marked, working, ≤ 35% of sold value, or any robot/space toy at ≤ $100. Pass when: Taiwan/China 1970s+, non-working battery toys, or priced at ≥ 60% of sold. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).

# Toy train accessories (Lionel / Marx / Noma postwar O-gauge)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: binnsroad.co.uk Lionel postwar accessory listings; OGR Forum threads on 154 crossing signal and 394 beacon; eBay sold-price knowledge (2024–25).

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Tinplate and bakelite trackside pieces: silver lattice towers with red/orange top (Lionel 394/494 rotary beacon), black semaphores with red/green lenses (Lionel 151), crossbuck crossing signals on a black base (Lionel 154), gateman shed (Lionel 45/145), water tower (Lionel 138), street lamps (Lionel 64/71), Marx lithographed crossing gates and signals (thinner tin, brighter litho), Noma "Lites" electric stations in a brown Noma box. # Trade violins (German/Czech "Stradivarius copy" fiddles)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: craigslist 1340 photos (two trade violins, "Amatus Cremona 1677" label, needs repair, $125), 1282 (Romanian "Euro Standard" w/ case $150), 1050 (1/4 size $100); eBay / Tarisio sold pattern (knowledge 2024–25)

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Paper label inside the f-hole reading "Antonius Stradivarius Cremonensis 1713", "Nicolaus Amatus", "Jacobus Stainer" = Markneukirchen/Schönbach factory copies, 1880–1930, millions made. Real tells of a better instrument: one-piece flamed maple back, hand-cut scroll with crisp turns, inlaid (not painted) purfling, labelled "Made in Germany/Czechoslovakia" by a shop (E.R. Pfretzschner, Roth, Heberlein, Juzek, Wurlitzer). Modern Chinese/Romanian student outfits (Cremona, Stentor, Euro-Standard) = $80–200 new. Sellers write "old violin", "antique fiddle", "Stradivarius violin" (never is).

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | unlabeled/"Stradivarius" trade fiddle, no strings, cracks, loose neck | $40–120 | most listings; set-up cost $150+ |
| Decent | playable trade violin w/ case & bow, Chinese/Romanian student outfit | $120–300 | |
| Collectible | Roth (1920s–50s), Juzek Master Art, Heberlein, Pfretzschner, French Mirecourt (JTL, Laberte) | $600–2,500 | the ones worth buying |
| Top | genuine Italian/early French/English makers; good pernambuco bows (Pfretzschner, Nürnberger, Bausch) | $2,500+ / bows $300–2,000 | needs expert appraisal — Dylan's authentication rule |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2025 – eBay – Stradivarius-label trade violin, playable w/ case – $150–250 (knowledge)
- 2025 – eBay – E.R. Pfretzschner 1960s 4/4 outfit – $400–700 (knowledge)
- 2025 – eBay – trade violin needing setup, no strings – $50–90 (knowledge)

## What sellers usually miss
The BOW is often worth more than the fiddle (silver-wound frog, octagonal pernambuco, stamped). Shop labels (Roth/Juzek) on the inside rib. Seller "Stradivarius" labels mean nothing.

## Shipping / handling
In case 6–8 lb, $30–40 each way; loosen strings. Reverb/eBay.

## Buy rule
Buy when: Roth/Juzek/Pfretzschner/French labelled, playable, ≤ $150, or a stamped silver-mounted bow ≤ $50. Pass when: "needs repair", no strings/bridge, unlabeled trade copy, or anything requiring an appraiser. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).

Wooden box calls (lidded, paddle), pot/slate calls, and duck/goose calls. Mass-market (Quaker Boy, Primos, Knight & Hale, Lynch pre-2000) = $10–30. Collectible = hand-made by named callmakers: M.L. Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay sold listings for "Victorian celluloid photo album", "musical photo album", Ruby Lane/Etsy sold

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Thick book-form albums with gilt-edged card pages cut for cabinet cards/CDVs. Covers: plush velvet with brass corners and a clasp; celluloid (early plastic) with lithographed Victorian ladies/flowers; some have a wind-up music box in the base (tune card on the bottom: "Blaze Away", "Irish Bells March" = c. 1900–10). Value drivers: celluloid cover art condition (no cracks/lifting), working music box, intact clasp, and above all the photos still inside (tintypes, identified people, occupational, post-mortem, Black Americana, Civil War soldiers add $5–100 per photo). Sellers' words: "antique photo album", "Victorian album", "velvet album", "music box album".

# Vintage electric fans (Vornado, Emerson, GE, Westinghouse)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: plusmodels.com / eBay 178148565519 (Vornado 16C2-1 seafoam, serviced, $109.99); eBay 406777501074 (Vornado 16C listing Oct 2025); chairish/1stdibs restored Vornado listings ($250–500 restored); eBay sold knowledge for brass-blade Emerson/GE.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Vornado (1945–59): streamlined "jet engine" shape – round shroud, three-blade propeller, metal body on a tilt stand; models B28C, 16C (16" desk), 20C (20"), D16C, in grey, green, red. Emerson/GE/Westinghouse 1910s–30s: brass blades, brass cage, cast-iron base – the ones with 6 brass blades ("Emerson 29646") are top tier. 1950s–60s Lasko/Eskimo steel fans are common. Sellers write "vintage fan", "antique fan", "Vornado desk fan", "doesn't work".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | 1950s–70s steel box/oscillating fans (Lasko, Galaxy, Eskimo) | $15–40 | |
| Decent | Vornado 16C/B28 non-working or rough; GE/Westinghouse steel-blade 1930s working | $50–90 | parts/restore buyers |
| Collectible | Vornado 16C/20C working, original paint; Emerson/GE brass-blade working | $110–250 | |
| Top | Vornado D16C/D28 in colour fully restored; Emerson 6-blade brass; Westinghouse "Tank"; Marelli/Jandus | $300–800 | restorers' market |

## What sellers usually miss
Vornado model (on the base plate) and colour: green/red bring 50% more than grey. Non-working Vornados still sell $50–90 to restorers because the motors are simple (oil, new cord) – but that is repair, which Dylan does not do.

## Buy rule
Buy when: working Vornado ≤ $40 or brass-blade Emerson/GE working ≤ $60. Pass when: "doesn't work" (repair), or steel 1950s+ fans over $15. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.

# Vintage marbles (jar lots, machine-made and handmade)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay sold knowledge 2024–25 (quart jar of mixed machine-made $30–70; Akro Agate corkscrews $5–20 each; handmade German swirls/onionskins $30–300 each); marblecollecting.com identification guides.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Jar lots are nearly always 1930s–70s machine-made: Akro Agate corkscrews/oxbloods, Peltier National Line Rainbos, Christensen (CAC) swirls, Marble King, Vitro, Master, plus 1970s cat's-eyes (worthless). Handmade German (pre-1920): visible pontil marks at both poles, latticinio cores, onionskins, sulphides (figure inside) – these are the money. Shooters (¾"+) bring more. Sellers write "vintage marbles", "jar of marbles", "old marbles".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Cat's-eyes, clearies, 1980s Chinese | $10–20 per lb | |
| Decent | Mixed 1930s–60s machine-made jar (Akro/Peltier/Vitro) | $30–70 per quart | |
| Collectible | Akro oxbloods, Peltier picture marbles (comics), CAC guineas, large shooters | $20–300 each | |
| Top | German sulphides, onionskins, lutz, large handmade swirls | $100–1,500 each | pontils visible |

## What sellers usually miss
Any handmade (pontil) marble hiding in a jar; Peltier comic marbles (Kayo, Moon Mullins) worth $50–150 each. Photos through a jar can't show this – pass unless a clear close-up exists.

## Shipping / handling
Quart jar ~4 lb; three jars 12 lb glass – $25–30 and breakage risk. Resell by sorted lot on eBay.

## Buy rule
Buy when: ≤ $15/quart of pre-1970 machine-made, or any lot with visible pontil/sulphide marbles ≤ $50. Pass when: cat's-eyes dominate, or $30+/quart. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.

# Violin bows (antique / trade bows)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: Brompton's auction archive (Mirecourt makers: Bazin, Ouchard, Cuniot-Hury, Morizot), eBay sold "antique violin bow", Maestronet forum rules of thumb

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Value sits in three things: the stick wood, the maker's stamp, and the mounts. Pernambuco sticks are dense, red-brown, often octagonal at the handle and round at the tip; brazilwood is lighter/orangey and cheaper; carbon/fibreglass is modern. Stamp: look just above the frog on the player's side ("BAZIN", "OUCHARD", "TOURTE" (generic German trade stamp, not a real Tourte), "GERMANY", "NÜRNBERGER", "PFRETZSCHNER", "GLASSER" (student)). Mounts: silver or gold winding and ferrule = better grade; nickel = trade grade; plastic/whalebone-imitation winding = student. Frog: ebony with Parisian eye (pearl dot in metal ring) is a French tell; plain pearl dot is generic; tortoiseshell/ivory frogs are top-tier and have shipping/legal issues. Tip plate: ivory/bone (old) vs plastic. Missing hair is normal and cheap to fix (~$60–80 rehair) but a warped or cracked stick is terminal. Sellers' words: "fiddle bow", "antique bow", "old violin bow".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Glasser fibreglass, brazilwood student, Chinese unbranded | $10–40 | most listings |
| Decent | unstamped German/Mirecourt nickel-mounted pernambuco trade bow, straight | $100–300 | |
| Collectible | stamped Mirecourt workshop bows (Bazin, Morizot, Cuniot-Hury, Husson), silver-mounted German (Nürnberger, Pfretzschner, Knopf) | $600–3,000 | auction estimates £600–1,600+ |
| Top | Ouchard, Sartory, Voirin, Peccatte, Tourte (real) | $5,000–$100,000+ | needs expert certificate (Raffin) |

## What sellers usually miss
The stamp (they never photograph above the frog), pernambuco vs brazilwood, silver vs nickel. A bow that "came with grandpa's fiddle" is usually a $30 trade bow but 1 in 20 is a $500+ workshop bow. Ask the seller for a close-up of the stick above the frog and the underside of the frog slide.
