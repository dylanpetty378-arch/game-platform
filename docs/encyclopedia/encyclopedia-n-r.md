# Nikon F / F2 / F3 professional film SLRs
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: gearfocus.com sold listings (F2 Photomic black, "Good", sold $270.58), eBay sold browsing, KEH price knowledge

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Big, square-shouldered 1960s–80s pro bodies with a removable prism. Tells: the F2 has a tall "Photomic" meter head with a round window on the front (the dome under the name plate) and an advance lever with a plastic tip; the F (1959–73) has a plainer prism and a "Nikon F" nameplate; the F3 (1980–2001) has a red stripe on the grip and an LCD meter. Black bodies are worth more than chrome. Sellers write "Nikon camera", "vintage Nikon 35mm", "old Nikon with lens" – they rarely name F2. Lenses: pre-AI Nikkors with scalloped metal focus rings; the "Nikkor-S/-H/-Q" names mean 1960s–70s glass.

## Comps (sold, dated)
- 2026 – Nikon F2 Photomic black body, "Good", new light seals – $270.58 (gearfocus sold)
- 2025–26 – F2 Photomic chrome bodies – $180–260 (eBay sold, recurring)

## What sellers usually miss
Colour (black > chrome), which finder is on it (DP-1 Photomic vs DP-12 "AS" – AS worth 2×), whether the meter works, and the lenses. A "lot of old cameras" from a retired photographer very often hides an F2 plus fast Nikkor primes (50/1.4, 85/1.8, 105/2.5 each $80–250).

## Shipping / handling
Body + lens 2–3 lb, box with foam: $15–20 ground. Resell on eBay (13%); mechanical shutter means no electronics-risk beyond the meter, which buyers accept as "meter untested".

## Buy rule
Buy when: an F2/F3/F body is visible in a lot under $120, or a black F2 body alone under $100. Pass when: Photomic head missing (body-only F2 ~$120), seller names "F2" and prices at $250+. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price) after fees and shipping both ways.

# Pachinko machines (vintage Japanese, 1960s–80s)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: pachinkoplanet.com/machine-values, eBay listings 2025

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Upright pinball-like board ~32"x20", chrome frame, hundreds of brass pins, flipper-less; balls fed from a top tray. Makers printed on the face or back: Nishijin, Sankyo, Sanyo, Daiichi, Heiwa, Ginza. 1970s "Super DX" style = ball-feed tray on top; 1960s single-shot = thumb lever, no tray; electro-mechanical (Nishijin Powerflash/Circuit, 1974–75) have lights/relays. Seller words: "pachinko", "Japanese pinball", "slot machine".

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)
| Tier | Examples | Typical sold price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | Non-working, rusted chrome, missing parts | $10–40 | parts only; restoration 70–80 h |
| Decent | Common 1970s Nishijin/Sankyo, complete, working | $100–200 incl. ship | ~95% of US machines |
| Collectible | Themed (dolphins, poker, sports), 1960s single-shot, restored | $300–600 | |
| Top | Pre-1960, electro-mechanical Powerflash/Circuit | $600+ | rarely trade |

## Comps (sold, dated)
- pachinkoplanet 2025: complete restorable 1970s machine $100–200 shipped; restored common model >$500; non-working ≤$10 value

## What sellers usually miss
Era. A single-shot 1960s or electro-mechanical machine listed as "old pachinko $80" is the only real upside. Themed faces are worth more than plain.

## Shipping / handling
~35 lb, glass face, pins bend – $60–90 to ship, high breakage risk. Local pickup only unless rare. Profit rarely clears Dylan's rule once shipping both ways is counted.

## Buy rule
Buy when: 1960s single-shot or EM model ≤ $100 and working, local. Pass when: "for parts or repair" (no repair rule), common 1970s at any price over $50 if it must ship.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Cast-aluminium/iron head with a glass or plastic dome, coin slot, dial showing minutes, a key lock on the back or side. Makers cast on the head: Duncan (Duncan-Miller, "Duncan 60/70/76" are 1960s–80s), Park-O-Meter (POM, Oklahoma, 1935 originator; "Park-O-Meter" script), Rockwell/Rhodes, M.H. Rhodes "Mark-Time", Dual (two-head). Early = 1930s–50s single dial, rounded "penny/nickel" slots, "Model M" POM. Value: original key + working mechanism + unmodified; lamp conversions (drilled, wired) are common decor and sell for less than an original meter. Pole-mounted with the original pipe adds a little. Sellers' words: "parking meter lamp", "old parking meter", "man cave".

# Paul Flato compacts (1948–1950s)
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: makeupmuseum.org (Flato compact history, patent Feb 1948, ads 1949–51), Heritage Auctions lot 668-39135 (signed Flato faux-pearl compact, c.1940), eBay.de active listing 126474240610 (Dogwood compact + lipstick w/ case, ask $350), Rau Antiques / Beladora (gold Flato compacts, four figures)

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Paul Flato was the Hollywood jeweler (Katharine Hepburn, Rita Hayworth); after prison he launched a costume compact line in 1948. Gold-tone (sometimes silver) square or round compacts with a small jewelled figural motif: dogwood blossom, kitty with green eyes, key, teardrop, circus, bunny, crown/"crown jewels", seashell, stars. Sold with a MATCHING LIPSTICK TUBE and a cream cardboard box printed "Paul Flato" in red script. Signed "Flato" inside the lid or on the hinge. Sellers write "vintage jeweled compact", "gold compact with rhinestones", sometimes "Paul Flato" (they often know the name but not the price).

## Value ladder (sold prices, not asking)

|---|---|---|---|
| Junk / common | unsigned rhinestone compacts (Majestic, Elgin American, Volupté) | $10–40 | most listings |
| Decent | signed Flato compact alone, worn plating, missing stones | $50–100 | |
| Collectible | signed Flato compact + lipstick, clean plating | $120–250 | the ones worth buying |
| Top | figural (kitty, circus, key) boxed set; precious-metal Flato jewelry pieces | $250–600 / $1,000s | verify signature before paying |

## Comps (sold, dated)

## What sellers usually miss
The "Flato" signature inside and the matching lipstick/box. A plain "jeweled gold compact and lipstick" listing at $20–40 is the arbitrage. Missing stones and plating wear halve the price.

## Shipping / handling
Under 1 lb; USPS Ground Advantage $6–10 each way. Sells on eBay (collectors) or Etsy. Fees ~13%.

## Buy rule
Buy when: signed Flato (or box visible) with lipstick for ≤ $60; compact alone ≤ $35. Pass when: unsigned, heavy stone loss, or price > $100. Profit must be ≥ max($50, price).

# Pokémon card lots & binders
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: sweep listings 2026-10 (XY-era binder 200+ cards $125 Klamath Falls; "Pokemon cards and collection" $175 Williamsport; "Pokemon cards OLD ONES" $1 St Louis; "Pokemon cards" $1 Atlanta); TCGplayer/PriceCharting market knowledge 2025–26.

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Binder pages or stacks. What sets era and value:
- **Card back**: all eras share the blue Pokéball back; **1999–2000 Base/Jungle/Fossil** have no set symbol (Base) or a Jungle/Fossil symbol, thick black border bottom text "©1995, 96, 98 Nintendo". **Shadowless / 1st Edition** stamps (left of the art box) on Base Set = the money cards.
- **Era by bottom-left/right symbols**: e-Reader bar (2002–03), EX era (2003–07) with "ex" cards, Diamond & Pearl (2007–10), HGSS/Black & White (2010–13), **XY (2014–16)** – "XY" set symbols, full-art EX and "Break" cards, Sun & Moon (2017–19) GX, Sword & Shield (2020–22) V/VMAX, Scarlet & Violet (2023+) ex.
- **Value lives in**: holo/reverse-holo rares, full-art, secret rares (card number higher than the set size, e.g., 110/108), "Rainbow", Gold, alt-art, and any **Charizard**. Commons/uncommons in bulk: $0.02–0.10 each.

# Royal Doulton character (Toby) jugs
Updated: 2026-10-12 · Sources: eBay sold/active listings Oct 2025 (Beefeater, Bobby, Guardsman D6755, Monty), general collector knowledge (D-numbers)

## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Glazed ceramic face jug with the handle forming a prop (Beefeater's halberd, Bobby's helmet strap, Guardsman's busby). Base is stamped "Royal Doulton" lion-and-crown with a D-number (D6xxx). Sizes: large (~7"), small (~4"), miniature (~2.5"), tiny. Sellers say "Toby jug", "Royal Doulton mug", "character jug".
