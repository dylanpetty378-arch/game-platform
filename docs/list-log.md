# L21 · Dimension Spaces

**Settled: five — `physical`, `mental`, `social`, `mystic`, `attempt`.** *(Mystic was added in a second pass; see the end of this entry.)*

## What was proposed

Three: `physical`, `social`, `attempt`. The reasoning was that physical is certain, attempt is not optional because every roll lives there, and social is the interesting decision — with `regard` and `resolve` as its two axes.

The framing offered with it was that under-separation is the more recoverable error, because you can always decline to author content that meets in odd ways, but you can never manufacture an interaction across a wall that already exists.

## What changed it

**Dylan proposed a fourth Space — `mental` — and the argument that settled it was psychic harm.**

Under three Spaces, a mind flayer's blast has to be `vital`, which is not a body injury, or social `resolve`, which is not social pressure either. Neither is honest. There was no correct home for a whole obvious category of effect.

He also named exhaustion and charm as things that would otherwise "roll up until attempt and have tags and stuff" — meaning they would be forced into States and Tags rather than being first-class vectors, which is a real loss of expressiveness.

**A second thing fell out of it.** Splitting `mental` off moved `resolve` out of the social Space, where it had only been sitting because there was nowhere else to put it. Social is now cleanly about **standing and obligation** — what others think of you and what they owe you — which is a better Space than the one originally proposed.

## The clarification the fourth Space depended on

Dylan's observation that *"poison would affect both the mental and physical spaces"* exposed something that had not been said plainly:

> **A Space limits a vector, never an ability. One ability places as many vectors as it needs.**

A poisoned blade is one thing in the fiction that places two vectors — a physical one and a mental one. They resolve independently, each meets its own Guards, and neither cancels the other. Which is correct: armour should not blunt a hallucination.

This was already legal in the model. Saying it out loud is what makes four Spaces comfortable rather than restrictive, because the walls turn out to be much cheaper than they look.

## The rule that keeps it from drifting

Four Spaces creates a recurring authoring question — *is intimidation mental or social?* — and without an answer it gets decided differently every time.

> **A vector's Space is decided by what it changes, not by what caused it.**

Intimidating a man in a tavern makes him afraid (**mental**) *and* makes the room see you as dangerous (**social**). One action, two vectors, no ambiguity. A charm spell is purely mental — it is not persuasion, it is tampering. Persuasion is purely social — you did not reach into their head, you changed what they owe you.

## What the split bought

- **Mental Guards become a real category.** *Mental fortitude* stops being a saving throw and becomes a Guard sitting on you, working exactly like armour. Wards, trained discipline, an iron will — all the same machinery.
- **The bard bolstering you against a dragon's terror now works arithmetically.** Both are mental vectors of opposite sign; they cancel. Even though one came from a friend talking and the other from a monster existing. That is the fire-and-ice property appearing in a completely different domain, and it is the best evidence so far that the model generalises rather than being a damage system in disguise.

## Rejected, and why

| | |
|---|---|
| **wealth** | Money is a **Resource**, not a direction. A bribe is a social vector *paid for* with a Resource. Making money a Space would require *"he gave me 40 gold"* to have a direction, which it does not |
| **knowledge** | Knowing a thing is a **Tag** or an Almanac entry. It is not a push with a magnitude and it does not cancel |
| **spirit / divine** | Not rejected as an idea — rejected from the **base Ruleset**. True only in some Settings, therefore a Component |

## The rule that came out of the last one

Dylan's phrasing was that Setting-specific-ness is *"the trademark to say something should be a component."* Generalised:

> **If it is only true in some Settings, it belongs to a Component.**
> **If it is true in all of them, it belongs to the base Ruleset.**
> **If the Substrate cannot function without it, it is a Socket.**

One question, three tiers. It is the sharpest statement of the Substrate line the project has, it settles `spirit` without argument, and it generalises far past Dimension Spaces. Now recorded in `dictionary.md` Part 1 and `CLAUDE.md`.

**The discipline that goes with it:** adding a Space later is free, because nothing existing interacts with it anyway. So the base four should carry almost everything, and a new Space needs a real argument — *this genuinely does not change a body, a mind, a standing, or how well someone did.* If every Setting adds its own Space, you end up with Settings that share no arithmetic.

## A correction worth keeping

The question *"is exhaustion a Dimension or a Resource?"* was put as either/or. Dylan's answer: **"Why just one?"**

He was right, and the framing was wrong:

> **Dimension and Resource are two ends of one pipeline, not alternatives.** A Dimension is the transient side — the axis a push travels along, where things cancel and combine. A Resource is the persistent side — what survives and accumulates. `temperature` is a Dimension; the injury it lands into is a Resource. **Anything that is a Dimension automatically also has a Resource, because that is what Landing means.**

Which leaves a narrower and better question, for L22:

> *Is there something that is meaningfully the opposite of this, that should meet it and cancel it **before it lands**?*

If yes, it earns a Dimension and gets the Resource for free. If no, it is only ever a Resource moved by Verbs.


## The fifth Space, added in a second pass

`mystic` was missed entirely in the first pass, and Dylan caught it: *"we forgot things like magic… That would allow for Ki, Magic, Mana, Cultivation, Divinity, and other mystical things that can't be explained with the scientific concepts."*

**The deciding case is counterspelling** — the same shape of argument that psychic harm made for `mental`. A counterspell meets a spell and unmakes it. That is the fire-meets-ice property, and there is no axis in physical, mental or social that can host it. Nor can they host dispelling, ward-breaking, severing a binding, or draining someone's power.

**The obvious objection is that magical fire is just fire**, and it is answered by the rule already in force — *a vector's Space is decided by what it changes*:

> A working's **effects** live in whatever Space they change. The working's **own existence** lives in `mystic`.

So magical fire burns you in physical `temperature`, and plate, water and being a fire elemental all help as much as they would against a torch. The spell's taking hold and persisting is the mystic part. **Which gives two independent defences against one fireball — dispel it, or survive it.** Settings with magic have always had both; no system models them as the same machinery in different Spaces.

### The Component test was consciously overridden here

Earlier in the same conversation, `spirit`/`divine` was rejected from the base Ruleset on Dylan's own test — *only true in some Settings, therefore a Component*. `mystic` fails that test too: a hard-science Setting has no mystic content.

It went in anyway, for two reasons that outweigh it:

1. **An empty Space costs nothing.** A Setting without magic simply has no Channels in it.
2. **If each magic Component published its own Space, no two could ever meet.** A druid's working and a necromancer's would be arithmetically unable to touch. Counterspelling across traditions would be impossible. For a platform whose central claim is that independently authored content composes, that is a large loss.

**The split that resolves it: the Space is infrastructure, the Channels are content.** The base ships `mystic` as shared arithmetic; Components fill it with ki, mana, cultivation, divinity. The same relationship `physical` has to `fire`.

Recording the override explicitly, because a test that gets quietly bent once gets bent silently forever after.

## Deliberately left open

The **Dimensions inside each Space** are L22's job, not L21's. The names floated during this conversation — `composure` and `clarity` for mental, `vigor` for exhaustion, a possible third mental axis of `will` — are notes for that list, not decisions.

# L29 · Attempt Domains and Dimensions

**Settled: seven Domains, fifteen Dimensions, plus a third layer of scoped Specialisations.**

## The correction that reframed the whole list

The first draft treated L29 as one list doing two jobs — *what a character is made of* **and** *the axes an attempt splits across*. Dylan rejected the conflation:

> *"The attributes for an entity are the absolute base level of what is used to define a thing. The attempt space holds the dimensions for what attempts can be made on things… Manipulate won't be a base attribute for an entity of any kind. It's going to be some combination or formula of things that get to a manipulate number. A relationship will never roll an attempt vector. A building will never attempt a grapple."*

**These are two lists, connected by formulas.** Entity Attributes are L2 and L3. Attempt Dimensions are L29, derived from Attributes by formula, and only for things that can attempt anything. The four-way test (person / ship / faction / storm) was therefore the wrong test for this list — a storm never attempts.

## The two-layer idea, and where it came from

Dylan proposed a tree: a broad category a GM can set a bar on, with several routes underneath it that a player allocates to.

> *"If a character is more quick or more strong, they can use what they're good at to succeed on the force roll. And if multiple characters go towards the capacity of the force threshold, they could each apply what they're good at instead of needing to all use the same thing. Like in D&D when somebody does a physical feat that requires a roll and it seems arbitrary if you use athletics or acrobatics."*

**A Domain is an outcome; its Dimensions are genuinely different routes to that outcome.** Not sub-categories — alternatives.

And a second correction fixed the shape: **a Domain stores no number.** It is a label, nothing more. That settles the arithmetic without a decision, because the Domain is definitionally the sum of its Dimensions.

## What the research changed

Three passes: skill lists across 25 systems, social mechanics, and two-tier resolution structures.

**The field has a consensus list of about eighteen**, modal count 15–20. `stealth` is the most lexically stable term in the hobby — that exact word in twenty systems. Named for legibility, not elegance.

**Social is the least converged area in the entire hobby: 0 to 25 skills.** GURPS 25, Draw Steel 13, D&D 4, Shadowrun 6e **2**, Daggerheart 0. Shadowrun consolidated nine social skills into two; Draw Steel shipped thirteen in 2025 and defended it in a sidebar. There is no right answer waiting to be found.

**The finding that reshaped INFLUENCE:** systems that hold up do not split social by rhetorical mode. They split by **precondition** and **residue**. Exalted 3e — *Persuade* requires an existing Intimacy; *Threaten* compels **and creates a fear Intimacy as a side effect**; *Bargain* requires no Intimacy at all because it trades value. Apocalypse World's *manipulate* requires **leverage** and holds only until the leverage is invalidated. GURPS separates Diplomacy from Fast-Talk purely by **failure consequence**.

So `appeal` / `pressure` / `bargain` are not three flavours of persuasion — they are three machines with different entry conditions and different marks left behind. **Which means the allocation decides not only whether you succeed but what you leave behind.**

**AWARENESS `senses` / `study` is the field's own split** — nine systems independently divided passive perception from active investigation. It also absorbs Knowledge, Investigation and Read People into one Domain, and lets *spot the tell* and *reason it out* count toward the same bar.

**Standing is never a skill.** Essentially no system rolls reputation. Ars Magica models it as a three-tuple — Score, Content, and **Type: who knows** (Local, Hermetic, Ecclesiastical) — which is exactly Dylan's `rapport → Local / Regional` idea, shipped in 1987. Every system that took standing seriously split it in two: Shadowrun *Connection* vs *Loyalty*, Blades *Tier* vs *Status*, Reign *Influence* vs *Sovereignty*.

## The warning, and why it was not decisive

**The recorded failure of every two-axis system where the player picks the axis is the dominant route.** Rob Donoghue, Fate co-designer: *"Spamming your best approach is the obvious abuse in FAE."* A designer built this exact structure — 9 skills × 6 approaches — playtested it and abandoned it: *"players exploited this system (not necessarily willingly!) to always pick the highest approach possible… I officially, and this time for real, give up."*

Dylan pushed back rather than folding, and was right. **The failure needs a free choice of axis.** In FAE the player scans six approaches, picks their best and argues the fiction backwards. Here the GM hands over the Domain and at least one point must be spent inside it, so the free choice never exists.

The deeper difference is the one he named: **in every failed system, one player made one roll.** The optimisation was *what is my best stat*. Here it is *who covers what*, distributed across a party against a shared budget with hidden bars. Different problem.

Residual risk — always `control`, never `timing` — is real, much smaller, and exactly what a Dimension-level bar punishes. **Instrument it:** count Dimension usage every session, and a Dimension nobody ever picks is the data saying the pair was never a real pair.

**One gap worth knowing:** every system surveyed picks exactly one cell. **No published system allocates points across cells.** Nobody has found the failure mode, and nobody has found the fix.

## The three ceilings

Dylan's worked lock scenario produced the sharpest result of the session. Three characters, one lock, GM naming PRECISION, hidden bars at PRECISION 10, GUILE 5, AWARENESS 5:

```
Vess  (burglar)    control 4 · stealth 2 · power 2
Kade  (duellist)   timing  5 · senses  1 · stealth 1
Oren  (scholar)    control 3 · study   1 · stealth 3

PRECISION 12 vs 10  CLEARED     the lock opens
GUILE      6 vs  5  CLEARED     nobody hears
AWARENESS  2 vs  5  MISSED      the needle gets somebody
```

*In, quietly, and one of them is poisoned* — chosen by nobody. Vess alone going all-in clears the lock on the nose at PRECISION 10 and scores zero on both the other bars.

His **200% attention budget** turned out to be a better mechanism than the headcount Participation Capacity we had, because it lets someone contribute *partially*. But it does not do the job alone:

```
Attempt Capacity 200%, ten people each spending 1 of 5 points  →  20% each, 200% total.  FITS.
```

> *"So we don't have a party of 10 people all allocate 1 point to six things and never fail at anything."*

**Hence both, both optional.** Attempt Capacity (a percentage) bounds accumulated attention; Participant Capacity (an integer) bounds headcount. Neither catches what the other does. With Participant 3 and Attempt 200%: three specialists all-in give PRECISION 18; ten dabblers give 3. Depth beats a crowd, and the crowd is turned away at the door rather than merely diluted.

**Attempt Capacity is measured in attention, not competence** — a master and a fumbler each consume the same budget at 100%. Space at the keyhole is space at the keyhole.

## The Threshold aggregation finding

The worked scenario exposed something that would have bitten in the first session: **three people's stealth summed to 6 and cleared a bar of 5.** Three people sneaking are not quieter than one.

A first proposal was a **lowest** mode — the weakest link gives you away. Dylan rejected it, and the reason is about incentives rather than simulation:

> *"Multiple people doing stealth should not hurt the party. It's saying that multiple people in the party are concentrating on making sure the party as a whole isn't seen. That's good… That would not encourage people to participate in the party stuff."*

**So three modes: sum, highest, each.** Effort accumulates on a lock; one person spotting the needle is enough for everyone; a fear aura is declared once and lands person by person. Individual jeopardy is **each**, usually with Participant Capacity one — not a weakest-link rule.

## The third layer

**A Specialisation is a scoped narrowing of one Dimension granting Bonus Points** — `appeal (among dockworkers)`, `stealth (in cities)`, `control (with locks)`.

It reuses Shaping, so it cannot inflate. The field says keep such bonuses small and flat: Shadowrun +2 dice, GURPS −2 for the wrong variant, Ars Magica +1-ish. **Nobody makes a specialisation a multiplier.**

Pathfinder 2e supplies the only anti-sprawl rule stated as a checkable invariant, and it is the one to copy: **strictly narrower than its Dimension, never able to substitute for one**, and it must name a scope — a place, a people, a kind of thing, or a condition.

Dylan's other idea — bonuses for particular *combinations* of Dimensions — has no precedent found. It fits as a Specialisation whose condition is an allocation pattern rather than a fiction scope, and it is a second novel element stacked on the first, so it wants hard playtesting.

## Deliberately left open

**How magnitude is produced.** *"It will be a combination of modifiers and a dice roll. We won't get more specific than that until all the lists are done and we figure out how the actual numbers will look on the lists we've made."*

The right call — the list does not depend on the formula, and the formula cannot be judged without the numbers the remaining lists will produce. But **every worked example in this project is provisional until it is settled**, so it should be the first thing picked up when the lists close.

## Flagged for playtesting

- `pace` is the thinnest Dimension and the first candidate to cut.
- Whether seven Domains is one too many — PRECISION/MOVEMENT and FORCE/MOVEMENT are the plausible merges.
- Whether concentrating within a Domain being weakly better feels right, or reads as a punishment for describing your attempt in detail.
- Dimension-usage counts, as the standing measure of whether each pair is a real pair.

# L22 · Dimensions, per Space

**Settled: fourteen across the four non-attempt Spaces** *(amended from twelve during L23 — see that entry)* — physical `temperature · integrity · vital · substance · vigor`, mental `composure · clarity · will`, social `regard · standing`, mystic `working · essence`. Accepted as proposed, in one pass.

## The test that did the work

It came out of the exhaustion correction in L21, and it turned out to be sharp enough to settle every candidate:

> **Is there something meaningfully the opposite of this, that should meet it and cancel it *before it lands*?**

**And an anti-test that catches most bad candidates before the test is even needed: a Dimension whose negative side you cannot describe is not a Dimension.** `piercing` has no opposite; it is a Channel placed inside `integrity`. `holy`'s only opposite is `unholy`, which is the same axis pointed the other way.

## Two things this list settled that were not really about Dimensions

**Wards and regeneration cancel; healing restores.** `vital`'s negative side is *mending in flight* — a regeneration aura standing on you, meeting incoming poison at R-1000. Restoration is a different mechanism at R-1250. Settings with magic have always drawn this line and never been able to say what the difference was; here it falls out of which layer the vector resolves at, and both are legal.

**A social Dimension is broadcast; a per-pair state is a Connection.** `trust` passes the opposite test cleanly — building confidence against sowing suspicion — and was still held back, because trust is inherently A-toward-B. That gives the Space a boundary it needed, and it keeps `social` from slowly absorbing Relationships.

## The asymmetry that kept the list short

**A missing Dimension is recoverable:** add one in year three, unspecified defaults to zero, and nothing that exists folds differently. **A Dimension that turns out to be two things is not:** splitting it changes what every existing Channel means.

So the bias is toward too few, and each entry has to earn a place against a real cost — every Dimension widens every vector in its Space, every Guard wanting full coverage, and every Channel's position statement.

## Where the doubt is

Dylan's own framing, and it is the right one:

> *"When I get around to actually making the items, characters, and magic systems and stuff then we'll find ways this needs to change."*

That is exactly what additive-only buys — the changes that arrive when real content is written will almost all be **additions**, which are free. The expensive discovery would be that one of these twelve was really two, and the shortness of the list is the defence against that.

**Flagged as least certain, in order:** `substance` (may be a Channel inside `integrity` rather than an axis) · `vigor` (may overlap `vital` at the edges) · whether `mystic` wants a third axis before v1 rather than after.

# L23 · Named Channels — and the L22 amendment it forced

**Settled: eighty-eight Channels, positioned in hundredths over all fourteen non-attempt Dimensions.**

Building this list changed L22 twice, which is the argument for building it early rather than late.

## What the research said

Three passes over damage-type and element systems, TTRPG and video game.

**The coordinate model is not novel in the risky way — it is shipped and load-bearing.** **EVE Online** has run damage as a normalised four-vector (EM / Thermal / Kinetic / Explosive) against a per-layer resistance four-vector for twenty years; the whole fitting metagame is reading those vectors. **Warframe** derives thirteen named damage types from seven basis vectors with a complete pairwise closure. **Path of Exile** gives converted damage a *provenance* — it keeps every type it passed through and is modified by all of them.

**What nobody does is a continuous space.** Every "elemental wheel" resolves to a lookup table with a circular picture.

**And the flat-list model is measurably broken.** D&D 5e's own glossary: *"Damage types have no rules of their own."* Across 799 monsters: **220 immune to poison** (a quarter of the bestiary), **five resistant to radiant**, and **force does not appear in the resistance table at all**. The analyst's own observation is the damning one — cold resistance was far higher than expected *"given how few cold damage spells there are."* **The resistance distribution and the offence distribution were designed independently and never met.**

Two designers, sixteen years apart, independently deleted the bludgeoning / piercing / slashing split — 4e in 2008, Draw Steel in 2025. The one dissenting argument found is the right one: the split only pays if **armour is modelled by type**. Ours is, which is why `pierce` and `rend` survive here.

**And the resistance dial has no comfortable setting.** Diablo 2 shipped near-total immunities with mitigations that worked at *"20% effectiveness against immune monsters"*; WoW built a mathematically careful resistance curve and **deleted it anyway** because resist gear became a per-encounter tax; Diablo 4 shipped resistances inert and reworked them. Pokémon's hard 0× survives for one reason only — **coverage is cheap**, because you carry six creatures with four moves each.

That last finding is the one that matters most here, and our answer is better than anyone's: **any interior Channel partially routes around any single-Dimension immunity, automatically.** A 100%-temperature-immune target still takes the vitality 40 of a `scald`. Coverage is cheap *by construction*.

## The first amendment: a Channel is not confined to a Space

Dylan: *"Why are channels specific to a space? Let's take fatigue for instance. That is vigor, but it's also a mental thing."*

He was right, and he proposed **100% of the magnitude to every Space a Channel touches**, while saying he suspected it would break. It does:

```
                                  mag   physical   mental
pure exhaustion (vigor only)       10       10        0
fatigue at 100% to both Spaces     10       10       10
```

Same magnitude, more landed — so every author writes multi-Space Channels, because breadth is free. **Guards do not fix it, because Guards are per-Space too.** And free breadth means breadth has to be priced by judgement forever, which is the exact thing a coordinate model exists to eliminate.

**The fix removes a concept rather than adding one:**

> **A Channel is a direction over every Dimension there is. Spaces are a property of Dimensions — which of them can cancel — not of Channels.**

There is no longer any such thing as *which Space is this Channel in*.

**And nothing is lost.** `fatigue` at 50/50 with magnitude 20 gives exactly what 100%/100% at magnitude 10 gives. The only difference is that the doubling appears in the magnitude, where a designer can see it. **Breadth means thin; a Channel's position prices itself.**

Two rules fell out: **a Channel's components must all land on the same target** (intimidation frightens *him* and raises *you* — two targets, so two vectors), and **"universal" Guards are universal within a Space** (armour must not blunt a hallucination).

## The second amendment: the signs were pointing two different ways

Dylan, on the first full table: *"concussion… +5 to kinetic, which makes sense. Also −5 to clarity, which makes sense. But if we look at them together, they do not make sense together."*

Exactly right, and the cause was in L22, not in the Channel. The physical axes had been named for **the harm that arrives**; the mental, social and mystic axes for **the property that is lost**. So two harms had opposite signs.

> **Every Dimension is a property of the target. Negative always means less of it. Positive always means more.**
>
> Two axes are **bipolar** — `temperature` and `working` — where displacement either way is what matters.

Which required renaming four axes, because their old names named the wrong thing: `thermal`→**`temperature`**, `kinetic`→**`integrity`**, `corrosive`→**`substance`**, `vital`→**`vitality`**.

**The proof it works:** of eighty-eight Channels, **only three have mixed signs** — `humble`, `menace`, `enthrall` — and every one is a genuine trade you would want to see at a glance.

## And two Dimensions were simply missing

Building the table found them, which is the whole reason to build it:

- **`mobility`** — slow, haste, entangle, root, pin, petrify
- **`acuity`** — blind, deafen, numb, dazzle, keen senses

Two of the most-used effect categories in any game, with no axis at all. They would have been faked as States, badly.

## Three iterations

1. The sign fix and the two missing axes.
2. Rebuild — which surfaced a **duplicate position** (`cripple` and `palsy` at identical coordinates, therefore the same Channel with two names) and a starved positive side, where every axis had one restorative pure and no interiors at all.
3. Added the positive-side interiors — `bolster`, `preserve`, `quicken`, `vigilance`, `freedom`, `hallow` — and the trade Channels.

## Two invariants worth having, both CI-checkable

**No two Channels may share a position.** Identical coordinates mean identical Channels. This is D&D's force-versus-thunder problem made *mechanically detectable*, which no other system in the field can do.

**Every Dimension must be used on both signs**, or an axis dies because nothing points at it. That is precisely how one bestiary ended with a quarter of its monsters immune to poison and nothing at all resisting force.

## Conditions are not Channels

Silence, invisibility, aging, knockback and size change are deliberately absent: States, Tags, the Place Socket and Scale respectively. **Five unrelated published systems reached this conclusion independently** — Monster Hunter separates elements from status ailments entirely, LANCER pulled burn out of the damage pipeline, PF2e made persistence a property rather than a type, GURPS routes fatigue to a different pool, PoE derives ailments per-component from the portion of the hit in each type. The systems that put conditions in the damage list are the ones with a dead type.

## Deliberately left open

**Expected to shrink** — Dylan's own framing: *"I think we'll end up reducing this amount, but let's go with it for now."* The tightest pairs are `blast`/`lightning`, `bleed`/`rend`, `rot`/`blight`, `pin`/`snare`.

**Something may still be missing**, and it will surface when spells and items are actually written. Additive-only makes adding free then.

# L27 / L28 — Sockets and the Economy

*The session that cut the Socket list from five to three, moved Time and Budget into the Substrate, and settled the shape of a cost. August 2026.*

## Where it started: five Sockets

Time, Place, Resolution, Landing, Budget. Each passed the Socket test cleanly — *can the Substrate function with this empty?* — and each had a Vocabulary/Behaviour split written for it.

Two were settled quickly.

**Place is a Socket**, and Dylan's argument for it was better than the one in the docs. The docs argued from distance, which is weak: a game about insects and a game about galaxies could be the same game with different numbers, and that is what most systems tell GMs to do. Dylan's version was that *"a game with bugs or a game with galaxies or with people who can all teleport at will or in the astral sea where everything floats in 5 dimensions"* are not one game scaled up and down, and the engine should not force them to pretend. A Setting with no distance at all — everything *here*, *adjacent* or *far* — is a legitimate occupant. The second argument is arithmetic: Scale belongs to parts as well as wholes, so something must know the door belongs to the ship, and containment is Place.

**Resolution is low-risk and may be one occupant with dials.** The only constraint added: the choice is pinned at Setting creation, never toggled live, or two people folding the same Ledger get different states.

## The Lens correction

The docs said *every* Lens must be Calibrated. Dylan: *"The lens should just be a view of the data, basically. Every lens is just a different view."*

He is right and the rule was overstated. Calibration is a claim about **odds** — a Lens that says *"you'll probably make this"* is asserting something about the Resolution Socket's distribution, and if it is wrong it lies to the player. A Lens showing raw numbers, or a filtered subset, or an in-fiction description, is just a view and needs nothing.

The narrowing kept the one clause that matters: **the Resolution occupant must publish its distribution.** That is decidable now even though the magnitude formula is still open, and without it nobody could write an odds-expressing Lens at all.

## Landing, explained

Dylan asked to verify his understanding, and had it right. Everything from R-100 to R-1100 is transient; what comes out is a set of signed integers, one per Dimension. **Landing (R-1200) is the step that turns those numbers into state that outlives the Moment** — and the Substrate cannot do it, because it ships no Resources and does not know a Setting has hit points. Empty Landing means the whole lattice runs perfectly and then nothing happens, which is the Socket test passing.

## The doubloon problem

The real work started here. Dylan's proposal: define the smallest possible unit, price everything in it, and let the economy define how those map to turns.

> *"if we had... maybe a doubloon (just a joke, i don't know what to name it)... then creating a spell might look like it uses 3 doubloons, requires a full round to activate, and impacts at the start of the target's turn... And the ecomoic unit could say that 2 doubloons is one action in this setting and you can three actions per turn? Then things mess up with rounding and stuff.. i don't know."*

Research went out on three fronts. It came back with two answers that appeared to contradict each other, and the contradiction was the finding.

**Question one — how fine should the *played* currency be? Coarse, overwhelmingly.** The decisive evidence is Larian's Nick Pechenin explaining why Divinity: Original Sin 2 cut action points from the teens to four: *"no matter the magnitude of action costs and budgets, designers end up using 'standard action' as the measure of all costs in the game… introducing a 7AP skill into this system would be awkward… despite AP cost increase of only 16.6%."* A fine unit does not buy fine pricing. It buys costs that don't line up and behave discontinuously.

The graveyard agrees. Exalted 2e's ticks — players built a physical rotating Battlewheel to track them, and 3e cut the system *"in order to make combat resolve much more quickly."* Rolemaster's percentage-of-round, abandoned because *"you're never left with 17% activity remaining in your turn, trying desperately to find your calculator."* The original X-COM's Time Units, replaced by two actions — and Julian Gollop, who **invented** Time Units, on why Phoenix Point doesn't use them: *"the most optimally effective play being very slow, and ultimately a bit boring."*

**But the failure is never the atom — it is the price list.** Feng Shui has run a fine-grained shot clock through two editions because essentially one price exists (3 shots; 1 to defend). Pathfinder 2e works because *"most things in the game now take one action."* What killed Exalted was a Speed table crossed with a DV-penalty table crossed with flurry Rate limits.

**Question two — how should content *name* a cost so it stays portable? Fine, integer, one atom, frozen.** The exact opposite. Forge Energy won modded Minecraft by defining four integer methods and **no unit at all**; IndustrialCraft's EU lost because supporting it meant implementing voltage tiers and machines exploding. Six renamed 1:1 aliases coexisted for a decade with zero problems; every rate ≠ 1 caused trouble.

## Dylan's objection, which broke the first answer

The first synthesis proposed a denomination ladder: content authored in either doubloons or named multiples, with a mandatory suffix. Dylan rejected it, twice, and both objections landed.

> *"if i say an action takes 10 atoms and a reaction takes 3 atoms and a bonus action takes four atoms... then i make an ability that can be done using 1 atom, then how do i make it so that it can't just be used as an action, bonus aciton, reaction, or object interaction whenever. Because those terms would be in the socket."*

**First: price alone cannot express opportunity.** True, and it is the thing categorical systems get right and fungible ones get wrong.

**Second, and sharper:** *"action" is an occupant word.* If content names it, content is not portable — and a different Budget occupant might have no such concept at all.

The attempted fix was a four-field cost with a hand-written eligibility condition. Dylan rejected that too:

> *"If i want something that enhances somebody's attack as a reaction, that might cost 10 doubloons, but I'm going to have to describe it in the most roundabout way in order to make it act how i want?"*

**The diagnosis: two vocabularies were doing overlapping jobs.** Denominations (`free`, `quick`, `action`) describe **size**. Eligibility conditions describe **when**. And "reaction" was a *when* sitting in a list of *sizes* — which is exactly D&D's fusion, and exactly why Mike Mearls's public verdict on his own mechanic is *"bonus actions are hot garbage that completely fail to fulfill their intended goal."*

**The fix is the pattern this project already uses everywhere: name the thing.** A Channel is a named position over fourteen Dimensions; nobody writes the coordinates. A timing is a named position in the time model; nobody writes the condition.

```
BEFORE                              AFTER
  amount    10                        cost    10
  eligible  Moment is not mine        timing  reaction
  pin       the current Moment
  cap       1 per round
```

## The move that changed the architecture

Then Dylan reframed the whole thing:

> *"maybe the game would be better if economic unit/action economy was defined in the substrate... that the time and economic units that are necessary to run a game being part of the substrate would maybe make the entire documentation around the game 1000% easier to understand."*

The first response resisted, on the grounds that a Setting might want week-long turns and freezing one turn model would make that impossible. Dylan corrected the category error:

> *"A component and a socket are different things... Turn-based stuff that is over the course of a month would inherently not use the abilities and spells that would when turns last seconds. So that would be fine. We should do what's necessary for time in the substrate and then allow for adding many time components."*

**That dissolves the objection completely.** A Component **adds**; it does not replace. Month-scale play and second-scale play never meet, so they do not need one shared machine. There was nothing to swap and therefore nothing to hold open.

**And it revealed a cost of Sockets that had never been priced: a Socket is a hole in the *explanation*, not just the code.** Every worked example had to caveat itself, which is why the Sunder example was hard to follow — that was not a writing problem.

**Consequences, all of which fall out at once:**

- Five Sockets become **three**.
- The cross-Socket vocabulary problem Dylan had flagged earlier **dissolves** — Time and Budget were the two everything else reached across.
- The rule "content may never name a denomination" **reverses**. No occupant, no problem. Frozen Substrate words may be named freely.
- The turn model becomes **the first game-design opinion frozen into the Substrate**, which is worth saying out loud: everything before it was machinery.

## What was decided, and what was deliberately not

Presented as four decisions with recommendations, after Dylan's correction that *"we make decisions based on you bringing something forward that needs answering and including a recommendation and then me reading it and deciding"* — a correction earned by a proposal that had quietly asserted "three actions per turn" as if it were arithmetic.

| | Decision | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Is `timing` a small closed named set, authored as one word? | **Yes.** Becomes L31 |
| **D2** | Does cost keep named rungs, or is it just a number? | **Just a number.** The ladder is cut. The word *action* may not exist in the game at all |
| **D3** | The turn allowance | **Left open.** A playtest number; nothing to price against yet |
| **D4** | Is `cap` a third field? | **Yes, optional and usually absent** — and *"I think there will be other fields as well"* |

**Deliberately open:** the allowance, whether a second non-convertible unit is ever needed, and the timing set itself.

## Two findings recorded against future lists

**The Budget must expose "doubloons spent this Moment" as readable state.** Every fungible economy in the research needs an anti-dumping device, and the survivors all landed on the same one — an escalating penalty for repetition (PF2e's −5/−10, Savage Worlds' cumulative −2). That belongs in a Component, but a Listener watches **state, never Verbs**, so unless spend-so-far is readable state the device is inexpressible.

**A shared pool between acting and defending is the highest-variance choice available.** It is the best thing about Mythras — every parry is an attack forgone — and the source of its worst exploit: the *Wrack* spell costs the caster 1 AP and forces multiple targets to spend several each, *"potentially enabling Total Party Kills."* These cannot be caught by reviewing effects one at a time. They need a property test: for every effect, doubloons-forced ÷ doubloons-spent under a declared bound.

## Three decisions taken after the research came back

**Anything may attempt.** The research's strongest precedent pointed the other way — Star Trek Adventures gives ships the same six attributes as characters and then *does not let them roll*; the ship assists and the crew acts. Dylan rejected the narrowing on the simplest possible grounds: *"No reason to restrict something that the GM doesn't have to allow."*

He is right, and the reason is structural rather than permissive. Under **absent is not zero**, an Entity with no points in any attempt Dimension already cannot attempt anything — nothing had to say so. Adding a gate would be adding a rule to enforce something the open-world reading enforces for free. And it leaves the Star Trek stance available as *content*: a Setting where ships assist but never act simply gives ships no attempt Dimensions.

**Points spent this Moment are readable state.** Confirmed, with a caveat worth keeping: *"there will also be more to it."* So the Substrate should not shape this as a single field. Whatever the economy exposes for the anti-dumping device, it is one member of a set that will grow.

**The contributor-breakdown question is open, with a recommendation.** See L25 in the dictionary for the worked example — three attackers landing −6, −5 and −4 in one Moment, against a Setting where a single landing over 10 maims. The packet arriving at R-1200 is `vitality −15` and has forgotten it was three blows, so the bar cannot be read as `highest`. Recommendation: carry the contributor list into Landing, because R-850 already works per contributing source, the Resolution Record already writes it down, and every good landing mechanism in the field reads `highest`.

# L32 — Moment kinds

*Settled in one pass. August 2026.*

## The proposal and what changed it

Ten candidates went in. Two collapsed immediately: *start of my turn* and *end of my turn* are just `turn start (E)` and `turn end (E)` with E = self. Four frozen words became two, and the *which one do I use* question disappeared with them.

Dylan took all three recommendations — the collapse, numeric rounds, and making `ordered entry`/`ordered exit` pinnable rather than merely watchable. On rounds he added the part that mattered: *"Initiation of turn-based stuff begins round 1. All moment kinds have at least the parameter for the round. A lot will probably be the current round."*

**That produced one detail worth recording.** Authors write relative — *three rounds from now* — and the Ledger stores absolute. That is safe in a way the Entity half of a reference is not, because **rounds never reorder**: round 7 always follows round 6, so resolving early costs nothing. Turn order *within* a round can change, which is exactly why the Entity stays symbolic and the round does not.

## The real question: conditional activation

Dylan pushed on the obvious weakness:

> *"Other round parameters and activation moments may be contingent on something. For instance, something could activate the next time somebody gets attacked. It could activate on initative 10. It could activate when a person takes their reaction... I think most things will be covered by the proposed eight. But.. the game just isn't flexible enough only allowing those eight."*

He is right that eight anchors cannot express those. He is looking at the wrong axis, and the fact that it *reads* as a weakness is a sign the two axes had never been stated side by side.

```
Listener(condition)   →   produces a Verb   →   pinned to a Moment
    WHETHER it fires                                WHEN it lands
    open, extensible forever                    closed, eight kinds
```

**The Moment list is small because it is a coordinate system, not a vocabulary of triggers.** Three of his four examples are Listeners on machinery that already exists — a Resolution Record matching a shape, doubloons spent this Moment with timing `respond`, a value crossing a bar. None needs a new Moment kind, and adding one would put permanent weight on a frozen list to do a job an open list already does.

**The rule that came out of it:** *a Moment kind says when; a Listener says whether. Never add a Moment kind to express a condition.*

## The one example that was neither — and the answer that was wrong

*"Activate on initiative 10."* The first answer was *a Component clock*: a position within a round independent of who occupies it only exists where turn structure is a **count** rather than an **ordering** — Shadowrun passes, Feng Shui shots, Exalted 2e ticks — and freezing *"a round has numbered slots"* would be a game-design assumption PF2e and 5e do not share.

Dylan overruled it, and he was right for a reason the original answer had missed entirely:

> *"the eight, plus turn-count, which is a moment happening at each numeric in the order - even if there's no entity at the spot."*

**A Component cannot publish a Moment kind.** Components add Channels, Tags, States, Listener forms and whole coarser clocks — but the set of things a vector may be *pinned to* is Substrate. Sending initiative counts to a Component would not have delegated the problem; it would have made an entire family of published initiative systems inexpressible until a Substrate change.

And the asymmetry is stark. **An inert Moment kind costs nothing** — in a Setting with a plain ordering, `turn count (n)` either maps to the ordinals or never occurs, which *absent is not zero* already covers, exactly as it covers `round start` in a Setting with no rounds. **Omitting one costs an Edition break, later, under pressure.**

Three details fell out: the Substrate does not need to know the direction (countdowns run high to low, ordinals low to high, and *count 10 is count 10* either way); the round parameter applies as it does to every kind; and when an Entity occupies position n, `turn count (n)` fires first and `turn start (E)` second, because the count is the coarser frame.

**Nine, not eight.**

## Two questions this handed to L26

1. **Conditions compose** — `and`, `or`, `not`. Accepted as proposed. `not` is safe despite the open-world rule, because that rule is about *fields being absent*, not about state being unknown, and the Fold has complete state at evaluation.
2. **Firing discipline is a required field, `once` or `while`, with no default.** Accepted as proposed.

## And the practice that came with it

Dylan closed with something larger than the list:

> *"for every single thing made, we'll need to verify it fits into the structure. And if it doesn't, that just means expanding the structure - especially during playtesting and before the platform is public."*

**That is the Substrate's real test, and it deserved writing down as a standing practice rather than an aside.** Nothing verifies a Substrate except trying to build content on it: the lists can be internally consistent, every CI gate can pass, the arithmetic can be provably deterministic, and the whole thing can still be unable to express a spell anybody wants. The only test that finds that is writing the spell.

Two things make it work. **Volume** — assets get generated in bulk rather than picked one at a time, because a structure that survives five hand-picked examples has proved nothing and one that survives four hundred generated ones has. And **mechanical checking**, because at that volume nothing else happens. That is a real deliverable and it belongs in L30: an **expressibility check** that either compiles an authored asset into Verbs, vectors, Channels, Tags and Listeners, or reports precisely what it could not express.

**The distinction the check must draw** is between an *additive* finding — a new Channel, Tag, State axis, Listener form, Moment kind — which is cheap, breaks nothing, and stays open for Components forever; and a *structural* one — a different Verb shape, a split Dimension Space, a redefined field — which is an Edition break and whose window **closes permanently at public launch**. From the authoring side those look identical. Underneath they are nothing alike.

Written up in full in `phase-map.md`, with the schedule: expand freely in Phases 2–4, deliberately and in writing during playtest, additive-only in open beta, and never after release. **The number worth tracking is the rate of structural findings** — additive ones never stop and should not, but structural ones should be falling toward zero, and if they are not then the Substrate is not ready whatever the calendar says.
