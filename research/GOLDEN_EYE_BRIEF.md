# Golden-egg eye brief — ShopGoodwill auction contact sheets

Dylan (Oct 13 2026): "A hundred dollars profit isn't a golden egg. It's a profitable lead." A **golden egg** is an
item worth **$1,000+ resale** (or ≥10× what it will realistically sell for here) that the listing does not recognise.
Profitable leads are still recorded, but they are not what we're hunting.

Each sheet: 12 × 10 thumbnails of ShopGoodwill auctions ending within 36 h, labelled `<cell> $<current bid>`.
Rows A–J, columns 1–12. Current bids are early; experienced bidders will push obvious things up. So the egg is
the thing **other bidders won't recognise from this photo and title**.

## Flag only real egg candidates — things that, if they are what they look like, are worth $1,000+
- Paintings/works on paper with real artistic quality: a confident hand, period frames, old canvases, regional
  schools (California plein air, Taos, Hudson River, Ashcan), mid-century abstraction, Old Master copies that might
  not be copies, folk art portraits. Most "oil paintings" are decor — flag only ones that look *good*.
- Sculpture with foundry quality (patinated bronze, signed bases), Asian works of art (jade, cloisonné, bronzes,
  scroll paintings, Imari/Satsuma of high quality), tribal art with real age.
- Native American: old pawn silver and turquoise with heft, pre-1950 pottery, baskets, beadwork, rugs/weavings.
- Jewelry that is actually fine: signed designer gold (Cartier, Tiffany, Van Cleef, David Webb, Buccellati), old-mine
  diamonds, large natural gems, heavy gold chains, gold watches (Patek, Rolex, Vacheron, Omega Constellation gold),
  vintage tool watches (Rolex Sub/GMT, Omega Speedmaster, Heuer chronographs).
- Rare cameras/lenses (Leica, early Nikon rangefinders, Hasselblad, Noctilux/Summilux), scientific instruments.
- Instruments: pre-1970 Gibson/Fender/Martin/Gretsch, old violins/bows with real figure, vintage saxophones (Selmer
  Mark VI), vintage amps/effects.
- Antiques: early American silver, Tiffany/Steuben/Lalique/Loetz glass, Rookwood/Grueby/Newcomb/Van Briggle art
  pottery, early clocks/automata, rare militaria (no Nazi items), early photography (daguerreotypes, tintypes of
  rare subjects), antiquarian books/maps/manuscripts, rare comics (Golden/Silver Age keys).
- Bulk lots where ONE piece visible might be any of the above.

## Do NOT flag
Ordinary decor, giftware, figurines (Lladró/Hummel/Precious Moments are not eggs), costume jewelry, modern watches,
common cameras, student instruments, prints/posters (unless clearly an original signed print by a major artist),
anything already titled with the valuable name *and* priced near value, anything big. Aim for **0–2 flags per sheet**.

## Output — one line per flag, appended after each sheet
`<sheet number><TAB><cell><TAB><what it might be, the $1,000+ version, ≤ 15 words>`
