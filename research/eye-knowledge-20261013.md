blue-note-jazz-lps: BST 84227 Mode For Joe with "Blue Note Records - A Division of Liberty Records, Inc." label = 1967-70 Liberty press, not the 1966 "New York USA" first press; Liberty label copies are the low-value tier.
vinyl-record-lots: Trout Mask Replica 2 STS 1053 on pink "Straight" label (Straight logo top) = 1969 US original; later reissues on Reprise.
wooden-crates: Paper cartridge boxes 1950s-80s (Remington Kleanbore green/red, Winchester-Western Super-X, Federal) are common $3-8 empties; value is in pre-war two-piece/picture boxes (Peters, UMC, US Cartridge, Winchester "Staynless" era).
vintage-fishing-lures: Arbogast Jitterbug with stamped "Fred Arbogast" lip and JITTERBUG printed on body side = common post-war plastic; wartime plastic-lip and wood-body versions are the valuable ones.
vintage-casting-reels: Pflueger "Quadruple" stamped endplate, non-level-wind nickel = common Akron-era reel, ~$20-45.
silverplate-hollowware: "Kendal & Dent 106 Cheapside" oval plaque with a 4-digit number = London retailer + pattern number on electroplate, not sterling.
militaria: German WWII helmet tells - crisp bright double decals + new yellow leather liner = restored/repro; settle by inside-skirt maker+size stamp (ET/EF/Q/NS/SE + 60-70) and lot number, M35 separate vent grommet vs M40 stamped vent. eBay restricts swastika items.
golf-clubs: Lens/eye "Scotty Cameron" on a Spider-shape putter was wrong - TaylorMade Spider Blade, ~$50-80.
comic-lots: yiayiascomics.com Craigslist posts use $1/$15 placeholder prices - dealer stock, kill.
vintage-art-posters: Craigslist seller "overkillpdx" (Portland) is a poster dealer with many listings - dealer stock.
vintage-fishing-reels: Lew's Speed Spool BB-1 was made by Shimano Industrial Co. Ltd, Japan (stamped on the frame/foot); BB-1 is the common version (~$40-70), so a "Shimano" title on a Lew's reel is not a mismatch signal.
roseville-pottery: Columbine (1941) shape 23-10 blue is a common line ($40-120 per entry); the base reads "23-10" impressed with Roseville USA; a base chip drops it to ~$50-60.
silverplate-flatware: Fontainebleau Miami Beach hotelware is International Silver "S.CO. XII" silverplate (mark "INTERNATIONAL S.CO. XII" + "The Fontainebleau" on handle); sellers quote $45+/pc retail but it is hotel plate, not sterling.
silverplate-flatware: "Melchior" (Russian мельхиор) flatware is nickel silver (cupronickel), often silver-coated; never sterling.
coins-and-bullion: 1986-S Statue of Liberty proof half dollar is copper-nickel clad (sells ~$6-10); only the 1986 Liberty dollar and gold $5 carry metal value.
militaria-medals: German "Volkslauf" (IVV) medals with a top bar naming the event and a maker bar (e.g. "DWB Pforzheim/Eisingen") are 1970s-80s walking-event participation medals, ~$5-10, not militaria.
staffordshire-figures: Royal Doulton "Honest Measure" toby (D6xxx, 1960s-80s) sells ~$20; a $25 ask leaves nothing.
american-art-pottery: McCoy aqua grape-and-leaf fan/cornucopia vase (1940s-50s) is often listed as "MCM" but is mass-market McCoy, ~$20-35.
costume-jewelry-lots: Christmas-tree brooches carded "Evergreen ... Made in China" are modern retail; signed vintage trees (Eisenberg, Weiss, Hollycraft, Pell) are marked on the back, not carded.
pressed-steel-toy-trucks: Smith-Miller GMC cabovers carry a script "Miller" decal on the cab door; 1980s Smith-Miller reissues exist (check underside stamp/date) - original vs reissue is the value split.
pressed-steel-toy-trucks: modern boxed reissues of Thimble Drome Champion / Real McCoy Midget Racer / Rodzy Standard tether cars come in white styrofoam trays with retro-art sleeves - these are repros, not 1950s originals.
pinback-buttons: "Dawn of a New Day - The Queens Museum 1939" button is a later Queens Museum commemorative, not an original 1939 NYWF pin (originals don't name the Queens Museum, which opened 1972).
islamic-metalwork: Persian qalamzani silvered/tinned copper figural trays are 20th-c. Isfahan tourist work despite "1200 century" claims; comp median $25.
designer-jewelry: Michael Valitutti (HSN) charms are two-tone sterling/vermeil, not 14k gold; ~$20-35 sold.
duck-decoys: Flambeau = modern plastic hunting decoys, $5-15 each.
movie-posters: Portal Publications (Sausalito CA 94965) 1970s reprints of 1930s Universal westerns (e.g. Buck Jones "Silver Spurs" 1936) carry "SAUSALITO CALIF 94965" in small type at the bottom-left margin and the film year alone at bottom-right; ~20x29 glossy stock. Originals are 27x41 with NSS number and a lithographer credit (Morgan Litho).
sterling-silver-jewelry: Trophy/loving cups stamped "STERLING WEIGHTED" (here with a "G" in the stamp) carry a filled foot; total scale weight overstates silver by ~50-60%, so price on ~40% of gross weight at most.
pocket-knife-lots: "Hammer Brand" with hammer logo on the blade = New York Knife Co., Walden NY (closed 1931), so pre-1931 by definition; small 3in 2-blade jacks in red candy-stripe celluloid are the common low variant (~$20-40); stag/bone whittlers and large patterns are the valuable NYK ones.
duck-decoys: Fruitland/Salisbury/Crisfield MD listings sit in Lower Eastern Shore carving country (Ward Bros, Lloyd Tyler, Sterling family); bluebill pairs there are worth asking for bottom photos even when unmarked-looking.
coins: Buffalo nickel 2x2 lots with coloured dealer price dots ("Group #N") are coin-dealer stock; a 1937-D at $2 has already been checked and is the normal 4-legged.
lionel-postwar-transformers: Blue tin transformer with lever handle in Lionel lots is a Marx, not Lionel; MPC 8304 (4-4-2) / 8902 (2-4-0) locos mark a 1970s set, not postwar.
star-wars-vintage: Hasbro Retro Collection Landspeeder (2019+) copies the 1978 Kenner toy; seller wording "updated version modeled after the vintage 1970s toy" is the tell.
cast-iron-toys: Skoglund & Olson (Sweden) cast iron toys carry a cast-in mark under the body reading "A/B SKOGLUND & OLSON GEFLE"; the red 10.5in bus is one marked model (seen Oct 13, PA, $100 dealer tag, no shipping).
animation-cels: Filmation feature cels for "Pinocchio and the Emperor of the Night" (1987) carry a round gold "Original Hand Painted Production Cel by Filmation / Royal Animated Art 1987" seal on the cel corner; minor non-Disney feature, low tier.
satsuma-pottery: Modern Chinese "Satsuma-style" moriage vases show a glossy white-glazed recessed base with sticker residue and no Shimazu mon or kiln mark; Meiji pieces have unglazed/crackled cream bases with painted marks.
pressed-steel-toy-trucks: "Tonka School District 277" 5in yellow van is the 1970s Tiny/Mini Tonka series, ~$10-15 worn; not the 1950s pressed-steel Tonka line.
native-american-turquoise-jewelry: Costume kachina-face brooch/cufflink sets in silver-tone base metal with faux matrix turquoise have "PAT. PEND." stamped on the cufflink toggles; real Southwestern/Taxco pieces are stamped STERLING or MEXICO 925 with a maker mark.
trade-cards: Chef & Brewer "British pub/inn sign" card sets are modern UK pub-chain promos, not antique tobacco cards.
vinyl-records: RCA Victor album P-282 "Yodelingly Yours Vol. 2" (Jimmie Rodgers) is a 1950 memorial reissue set, not original Victor 78s.
machinist-tools-and-boxes: Eye-screen read a black wrinkle-finish steel machinist chest of drill bits as a "flatware chest" - green-felt drawers with brass pulls are the machinist-chest tell.
vintage-action-figures: Loose lots of 1970s Fisher-Price Adventure People can hide a 1979 Kenner Boba Fett (blue-gray body, green chest); originals have a Hong Kong/Taiwan 1979 COO on the leg, knockoffs don't.
duck-decoys: "Raymond Lead Mfg Co Chicago" cast into a keel strip names the weight maker (a common 1930s-50s aftermarket keel), not the decoy carver/factory; don't read it as a maker attribution.
duck-decoys: black body + black head with white crown/nape patches + pale bill = surf scoter pattern (New England sea-duck decoy); sellers list them as just "duck"; worth checking against the $80 unmarked median.
vintage-microscopes: Leitz serials in the ~18,000s are about 1888-92; that era's stand has a black-japanned horseshoe foot, brass pillar with top-knob fine focus, and a sleeve-in brass draw tube, with the serial engraved on the tube/eyepiece.
opera-glasses: Tasco #560 "Act One" (yellow/black box) is the 1960s Japanese MOP opera glass; it is the common lookalike of 19th-c. French Lemaire/Chevalier MOP glasses (those have maker names on the eyepiece rim). Tasco sells about $25-45.
civil-war-relics: the US oval belt plate's correct construction (lead-filled back, two brass arrowhead hooks plus a bar/tongue) is copied by repros; bright crystalline "spangled" lead on the back suggests a recast; originals show dull, smooth grey oxidized lead. Ask for size and maker stamps (Boyd, J.H. Smith, etc.).
vinyl-records: Macola Record Co. (6209 Santa Monica Blvd) sealed deadstock 12in singles 1984-89 (Rapsur, Techno Hop, Techno Kut, Get Live, Hitt's, Flash, Sound of Sacramento) surface in multiples; the Dr. Dre/NWA-adjacent titles (Arabian Prince 'It Ain't Tough', Fila Fresh Crew 'Fear Of The Rap', Triple Threat Three 'MPG Rap') are the ones to comp.
champagne-capsules: no encyclopedia entry; plaques de muselet lots (100+ caps) are light and cheap to ship; the value is in Krug/Salon/Dom Perignon/Cristal, numbered artist series and pre-1980 caps.
political-memorabilia: the 1933 FDR "Inauguration March 4 1933 Washington DC" flag-reverse token (~32mm) is the souvenir piece ($10-25), not the official 2.75in Medallic Art inaugural medal.
ethnic-folk-dolls: kachinas with synthetic dyed fur, yarn ties, dyed feathers and a slab base are Navajo-made commercial pieces; Hopi tells are single-piece cottonwood root, a carved (not glued) mask, and the carver's signature + clan on the base.
