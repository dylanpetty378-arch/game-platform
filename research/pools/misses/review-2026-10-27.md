## What this means (main session)
- 138 of 300 are category-hot: instruments, watches, hi-fi, military gear, sports jerseys. Instruments and audio are on Dylan's never-show list (Oct 24), so they are not misses for us; removed from the SGW harvest and from the misses report (outcomes.py NEVER/BULK filters).
- 36 are by-the-pound grab bags (jewelry $27–45/lb, watches $15–20/lb, coins $45–100/lb): a market floor, not a find; now excluded from the report.
- The real hidden-maker misses (38) cluster in: French opaline glass, Bohemian jewelled enamel glass, Oaxacan master-workshop alebrijes, Tibetan inlaid metalwork, cylinder music boxes, Giuseppe Armani figures, WWI art medals, Haku Maki prints, estate briar pipe lots, Northwood ice-blue carnival, Victorian hairwork, Hudson River-style oils with art-store labels.
- Mislabel-cheap (11) is rarer than the brief assumes; 'style' (vintage style, Palais Royal style, shakuhachi style) is the recurring cheap word → added to DOWN and the eye brief.
- Melt under-weighted: piles of worn pre-1965 dimes/quarters with no copper edge; sterling cuffs sold by weight.
- Lots hiding a key piece (41): coins pulled into 2×2 holders inside a cent pile; jewelry boxes with a signed/sterling piece visible; Bakelite-colour bangles in a bulk box.

## Lessons as written by the agents
1. Biggest blind spot is category, not mislabel: 68/144 misses are category-hot (guitars, brass, violins, watches, sport jerseys). The ranker/eye skips Musical Instruments and Men's Watches. Harvest those SGW categories every day regardless of the "collector-object look" score.
2. Most misses named the maker in the title (Huss & Dalton, Gallagher, Reverend, Corum, Yema, Keilwerth). The eye brief's "out: title already names the maker" rule loses these. They are FB-style named-but-underpriced comps. For instruments and watches, score maker-in-title against a boutique-maker list and do not exclude them.
3. acoustic-guitars, Golden-egg tells: "boutique headstock (Huss & Dalton, Gallagher, Collings, Bourgeois, Santa Cruz) + all-solid satin top + fitted plush hard case = $2,000-4,000; small Guild with Hoboken NJ label = pre-1967, $800+".
4. modern-electric-guitars, Golden-egg tells: "Reverend with Formica/laminate top or 5-digit serial under 02000 = early Detroit-era run, $1,500-7,000; Godin slotted 12-string chambered body = Multiac/A12, $500+".
5. NEW: vintage-electric-guitars (Harmony, Univox, Teisco, Danelectro, Silvertone). Tell: "offset 60s-70s body + Japanese/Chicago headstock + original chipboard or hard case".
6. NEW: ww1-steel-helmets. Tells: "M16/M17 coal-scuttle with side vent lugs = German/Austro-Hungarian WWI shell, $450+; angular hand-painted ochre/green/brown camo panels (Mimikri) = $1,500+". Respect the no-Nazi rule: WWI is not Nazi.
7. NEW: chinese-bone-and-ivory-carvings (foo dogs, doctor's ladies). Tells: "creamy dense white with fine grain lines (Schreger cross-hatch = ivory), red glass eyes, rosewood fitted stand, reclining nude on leaf = medicine doll". SGW buyers bid $500-600 on titles that say "bone". Flag them, but Dylan cannot resell ivory, so make them reference-only.
8. NEW: estate-tobacco-pipes. Tells: "rows of straight-grain or sandblast briars, white dot on stem (Dunhill), silver bands, P-lip (Peterson), star (Castello); 20+ pipes laid out = estate collection, $30-60 per pipe".
9. vintage-wristwatches, Golden-egg tells: add "military marks on a diver dial (Marine Nationale / Navygraf), Corum pennant-flag markers, Hamilton Electric asymmetric case, Nivada F77 octagon, Zodiac Olympos faceted lugs". Each one is $400-950 and comes up a few times a week.
10. costume-jewelry-lots: the 15+ "24 Pound Mystery Box" SGW lots all clear about $27-45/lb ($630-1,100). That is a market floor, not an egg. Tag them bidding-war-noise and add a weight-x-$/lb comp line so the ranker stops treating them as misses.
11. Coin and currency lots by weight (world-banknotes, silver-certificates-and-coins) clear $60-100/lb. Add a melt rule: "pre-1965 US halves visible = 90% silver, about $11 a coin at $30 spot; coins in 2x2 holders pulled out of a wheat-cent pile = key dates".
12. trade-violins, Golden-egg tells: "named Italian/French maker label (Curatoli Naples, Breton Mirecourt) + one-piece flamed back + period coffin case = $600-1,100; the bow can be the value, so look for an ivory-tone or ebony frog with silver mounts".
13. NEW: vintage-synthesizers (CPB-2 bass pedals, Omnichord, Moog Taurus clones). Tell: "13-key pedalboard or strum-plate wedge = $350-450 even untested". Electronics rule conflict: these sold untested.
14. NEW: accordions (Scandalli, Hohner Panther / Corona Tex-Mex buttons). Tell: "Italian piano accordion with pearloid body in velvet case; Hohner diatonic button box = $350+".
15. original-paintings: add "heavy carved gilt period frame = $150-300 floor alone" and "Hudson River-style luminous autumn sky + 19th c. art-store label on the back (e.g. Zesbaugh's) = $500+".
16. NEW: victorian-hairwork. Tell: "looped fine brown/blond gimp-wire flowers under oval convex glass in a deep walnut shadowbox = $500-700".
17. carnival-glass, Golden-egg tell: "pale ice-blue/aqua-opal iridescence (not marigold) on a Northwood Peacocks or Peacock-at-Fountain bowl with beaded ruffle = 3-10x marigold".
