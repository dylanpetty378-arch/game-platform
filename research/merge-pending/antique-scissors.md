## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Pre-1940 cutting tools sold as decorative antiques: sewing and embroidery scissors (small, fine blades, figural handles — storks, cranes, busts), tailor's shears (large, asymmetric bows, often 10–14 in), buttonhole scissors (an adjusting screw sets cut length), grape/fruit shears (Victorian dining, silver or plate, vine-and-grape handles), candle wick trimmers/snuffers (a small box on one blade catches the wick; often on a stand or tray), and barber/paper shears.
**D. Peres Solingen** (Daniel Peres, founded 1792): decorative scissors with cast bust medallions between the handles, often a Kaiser-era uniformed bust, gilt or nickel finish; an omega-like mark below the pivot (triage).

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Germany (Solingen):** D. Peres, J.A. Henckels (twin mark), Gebr. Weyersberg, Gebrüder Stier, Böker; figural embroidery and medallion scissors, 1890s–1930s.
- **Sheffield:** Joseph Rodgers & Sons, William Whiteley & Sons, Robert Hinchliffe (cast steel from 1761), Wilkinson — tailor's shears and fine steel; Georgian cut-steel wick trimmers.
- **US:** Wiss (Newark, from 1848; tailor's and pinking shears), R. Heinisch (Newark, 1826), Clauss (Fremont, Ohio), Acme, Keen Kutter, Premax (buttonhole).
- **France:** Nogent cutlers; Thiers.
- **Sterling-handled:** Gorham, Unger Bros, Webster, Simons Bros, Wm. Kerr, Tiffany (American, 1890s–1910s, Art Nouveau handles); English hallmarked silver grape shears (Georgian–Victorian).

### Golden-egg tells
- English hallmarked silver grape shears (Georgian/Victorian) — ~5–15× plated grape shears ($150–400+, Appraisily range, verify) — vine-and-grape cast handles, lion passant + date letter + city mark on the handle.
- American sterling-handled embroidery scissors (Unger, Gorham, Webster, Kerr, Art Nouveau) — ~3–6× steel figural scissors ($80–250, general knowledge, verify) — "STERLING" stamped on the handle, repoussé or Art Nouveau women/flowers, steel blades.
- Georgian/Regency steel candle wick trimmers with box — $50–300 vs $10–20 for a modern copy (Appraisily, verify) — closed box on one blade, hand-forged steel or Sheffield plate, three small feet on some.
- Gold-mounted or gold-handled scissors (French/English sewing etuis) — scrap-plus value, several hundred dollars (general knowledge, verify) — karat marks, often in a fitted case.
- 18th-century hand-forged or cut-steel scissors — $150–500+ (general knowledge, verify) — faceted cut-steel studs, hammer marks, slight asymmetry, simple pivots.
- D. Peres/Henckels figural or bust-medallion scissors — ~2–4× plain steel scissors ($40–80, companion entry) — medallion between the finger bows, maker stamp and logo on the blade.

### Quick ID checklist
- Photo of each blade near the pivot: maker name, city (Solingen, Sheffield, Newark), logo.
- Handle marks: "STERLING", English hallmarks, karat stamps → silver/gold upgrade.
- Stork with a crown/"Germany" mark and gilt plating = common; stork marked "Italy/Pakistan/China" = modern.
- Box on one blade → wick trimmer; adjusting screw → buttonhole; long asymmetric bows → tailor's shears.
- Hand-forged look (file marks, uneven bows) → 18th–early 19th c.
- Rust, loose pivot, worn plating — no repairs allowed.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
Forged carbon steel, Sheffield cast steel, nickel or gold plating over steel/brass (figural handles), sterling (handles only), silver-plate (grape shears). Quality: crisp casting, tight pivot, blades that meet cleanly. Cheap modern: stamped, light, glossy gold plating, "China/Pakistan/Italy" stamps.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Rust pitting, loose or replaced pivot screws, worn plating, broken tips, bent blades, dented sterling handles, pivot replaced on grape shears. Sharpening that has changed the blade shape.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Stork scissors are reproduced in vast numbers (Appraisily warns of many reproductions of stork designs); "Victorian style" figural scissors; modern pewter "wick trimmers"; reproduction grape shears in plated alloy with fake hallmarks.

## Market
Niche and slow (4–8 weeks) except silver pieces, which sell to silver and sewing-tool collectors within 2–4 weeks.

## What sellers usually miss
Sterling handles listed as "brass/gold-tone"; English hallmarks on grape shears; a wick trimmer listed as "weird scissors".

## Shipping / handling
Pocket to shoebox; protect tips with cardboard; $5–8.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Maker mark close-ups, length, metal marks, pivot tightness, rust/plating condition.
### What to tell the buyer
"Antique [type], marked [maker/city], [length] in. [Sterling handles stamped STERLING / English hallmarks for (city, date letter)]. Pivot is [tight]; light age wear; marks pictured."

## Buy rule
Profit = sold × 0.87 − $6 − $6 − price ≥ max($50, price). D. Peres medallion scissors (~$40–80): ≤ $15 → generally pass. Sterling-handled or hallmarked silver grape shears: buy ≤ $25–60 when the sold median is ≥ $150. Pass on stork scissors and anything needing a pivot or plating repair. Pocket-size; fits easily.
