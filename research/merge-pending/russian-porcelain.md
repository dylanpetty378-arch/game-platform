## What it is / how to recognise it in a photo
Hard-paste porcelain from Russian and Soviet factories. The most common find in US sales is **Lomonosov Cobalt Net** (Кобальтовая сетка): white ground with a cobalt-blue lattice and small 22k gold accents at the crossings, designed by Anna Yatskevich (1940s), in continuous production since ~1950 and still made by the Imperial Porcelain Factory. Teapots in the "Tulip", "Bouquet" and "Banquet" shapes are common (general knowledge, verify). Also: LFZ animal figurines (polar bears, borzoi, cats, seals), Dulevo colourful folk figures and tea sets, Kuznetsov 19th–early-20th c. tea ware and plates, Gardner biscuit peasant figures.

## Makers, brands and variants
- **Imperial Porcelain Factory, St Petersburg (1744)** → State Porcelain Factory (GFZ) 1917 → **Lomonosov (LFZ)** 1925 → Imperial Porcelain (IFZ) 2005. Soviet LFZ marks include the stylised "ЛФЗ" logo with "Made in USSR" / "СДЕЛАНО В СССР" (pre-1991); post-1991 "Lomonosov – Made in Russia"; post-2005 "IFZ" / "Imperial Porcelain 1744" with a double-headed eagle (companion entry). The Russian Shop describes a red LFZ backstamp with "Made in USSR" on a pre-1991 Cobalt Net cup — mark colour varies by period.
- **Kuznetsov (1889–1917):** "Т-ва М.С. Кузнецова" with the double-headed eagle and factory name (Dulevo, Riga, Budy, Verbilki, Tver, Kuznetsovo).
- **Gardner (Verbilki, 1766–1891):** "Gardner"/"Гарднеръ" with St George.
- **Dulevo (DFZ):** Soviet marks with "Дулёво"; figurines and Agitka tea sets.
- **Riga (RPR), Konakovo (Kuznetsov faience), Verbilki, Polonne, Baranovka (Ukraine):** Soviet figurines and tea ware.

### Golden-egg tells
- Genuine 1920s agitprop (State Porcelain Factory, hammer-and-sickle overglaze on Imperial blanks) — $1,000–20,000+ vs ~$50 for a modern decorative plate (companion entry; auction only) — revolutionary slogans and imagery; double mark (Imperial cypher underglaze plus Soviet overglaze); fakes rife.
- Imperial-period (pre-1917) Imperial Porcelain Factory pieces — $1,000+ (companion entry) — imperial cypher under a crown in underglaze green/blue; museum-grade painting.
- Gardner biscuit peasant figures — $500–5,000+ (companion entry) — unglazed matte painted figures, St George / "Gardner" mark.
- Kuznetsov tea ware and plates with the eagle mark — $100–600 vs $25 for modern Russian (companion entry) — Cyrillic factory name with the double-headed eagle, heavier creamy body, transfer plus hand-colouring.
- Soviet LFZ/Riga/Dulevo figurines (1950s–80s), especially large animals and folk/ballet types — $60–300 vs $25–50 for current production (companion entry) — glossy slip-cast body, USSR-marked ЛФЗ logo, fine painted detail.
- USSR-marked Cobalt Net (vs "Made in Russia"/IFZ) — ~1.5× current production (general knowledge, verify) — "Made in USSR" / СДЕЛАНО В СССР with the LFZ logo on the base.

### Quick ID checklist
- Base photo: LFZ logo + "USSR" (pre-1991), "Made in Russia" (1991–2005), IFZ/eagle "1744" (2005+), Kuznetsov eagle, Gardner St George.
- Cobalt Net: are the gold accents hand-applied and the blue under the glaze? Printed decal net with no gold = Chinese copy.
- A scratched-through mark or "2" = factory second.
- Teapot: lid fit, spout tip chips, hairlines in the body.
- Figurines: ears, tails and fingers for breaks and restoration (UV).
- "Imperial"/"agitprop" claims → authentication needed; not a buy.

## Materials, finishes and quality tells
LFZ hard-paste: thin, bright white, crisp underglaze cobalt, hand-applied 22k gold that wears with use. Copies: printed net, flat or no gold, no underglaze mark. Kuznetsov: heavier creamy body with transfer prints. Second-grade marks lower value.

## Condition, damage and repairs
Gold wear, spout and lid-rim chips, hairlines in teapots, broken figurine extremities, restoration on figurines.

## Fakes, reproductions and reissues
Gardner and Kuznetsov marks faked on recent Chinese/Eastern European porcelain; Chinese Cobalt Net knock-offs; LFZ/IFZ still in production, so "vintage Cobalt Net" listings are often recent pieces; agitprop is heavily faked.

## Market
Steady niche (Russian/Ukrainian diaspora, porcelain collectors in US, Germany, Israel); post-2022 sanctions cut new supply to the West, supporting prices for pieces already outside Russia (companion entry). Cobalt Net sells in 1–4 weeks, figurines in 2–6.

## What sellers usually miss
LFZ animal figurines listed as "porcelain bear"; Cyrillic Kuznetsov marks unread; USSR vs Made in Russia difference. Conversely, sellers overrate current Cobalt Net as "antique".

## Shipping / handling
Teapots: box-in-box, lid wrapped separately, $12–15. Figurines: wrap extremities, $8–12.

## Selling it (for the buyer-facing listing)
Base mark photo, era from the mark, gold condition, chips/hairlines, dimensions.
### What to tell the buyer
"Lomonosov (LFZ) Cobalt Net teapot, hand-painted underglaze cobalt with 22k gold accents, marked [ЛФЗ / Made in USSR] — made before 1991. [Gold wear light]; no chips or hairlines found. Pattern designed by Anna Yatskevich and still in production; this is a Soviet-era example."
